This high attempt, as we would wish succeeds, What hosts have we overthrown, what Cities razed? Lo, populous Asia trembles at our deeds, And martial Europe does remain amazed; Greece, (which both Mars and Pallus did defend:) A humble supplicant before thee falls, Rebellious Thebes, which durst with thee contend; Lies now entombed within her broken walls; That Sea-commanding Tyre, reposing much In liquid Towers that Neptune reared in vain, Hath now confirmed thy forces to be such, That nothing can resist thy just, disdain. No doubt the ancient Grecians, Ghosts are glad To see the fierce Barbarians brought so low, Yet are for envy of thy fortune sad, And though unbodied blush at this overthrow: Miltiades by all men was admired, Who once in Greece their flying troops pursued, And he who with a stratagem retired, And Salamina's straits with blood imbrued; But yet for all the Captains of that age, The eastern Monarchs Empire was enlarged, Who in their Country (flaming all with rage,) The Sea with ships, the land with Armies charged He with more swarms of men then Autumns clusters, Drank Rivers dry, and marched on Neptune's back, By measure, not by number, made his musters, Did scourge the winds, strived mountains plains to make; All Europe feared then to be forced to bow, While the earth did groan to bear so great and host, But thou hast come, seen, and over-com'd them now, Even in the bounds wherein their power was most. That haughty foe, who vilipended oft Our predecessors force, and scorned our own, Now laid as low, as he was once aloft, With his disgrace, must make thy valour known; He does by this acknowledge his distress, In labouring thus to have his friends restored, This message (mighty Prince) imports no less, By his request thy conquest is decored; For the recovery of his captived Queen, He offered hath innumerable gold; A mass so great, that such was never seen, More (as they brag) then Maccdon can hold: My counsel is, that you aceept his offers, And with his daughters render her again, "Who would make war, must not have empty coffers, "Where one for glory, thousands sighed for gain; And if those Ladies guarded Captives stay, It cost and trouble breeds to fit their state; Thus more to charge, or charges to defray; To vex or ease, advice, and not too late. "More hard it is, and does deserve more praise "To guide, then get, to keep, then conquer Crowns: In Fortune's spheres chief height your glory placed, Can now not move unless it be more low, And if it once descend, then quite disgraced, Each Artisan your Statues will overthrow; For in the war as you may well perceive, There does no little partly depend on Fame; If we but once the least small check receive, The world will gather to procure our shame; Then tempt not Fortune further then you needs, Your rashly mounting thoughts let reason rain, Lest whilst your Hope's with Trophies feigned you feed, A moment loose what many days did gain. Plot summary

Plot — Darius
Vanquished by Alexander, Darius fears for his mother, wife, and children, who have been taken captive. He offers a ransom for them, and Parmenio encourages Alexander to accept (the money can be used to finance future campaigns), but Alexander refuses. The women assume that Darius is dead and ask leave to bury him. Alexander gives them his personal assurance that Darius is alive and that they v.ill be treated honourably. Bessus and Narbazanes plan to betray Darius to Alexander in the hope of getting kingdoms for themselves. Darius learns that his wife has died, but is impressed by Alexander's honourable treatment of her. Narbazanes encourages him to resign the crown to Bessus, while others advise him not to trust the two traitors, and to rely on his mercenary troops if necessary. Darius meditates on tl1e inevitable fall of princes and accepts the coming of death. The traitors spread a rumour that he has committed suicide, then take him prisoner and offer him to Alexander. Darius dies with a speech honouring Alexander for his magnanimity in victory. Alexander vows to hunt down and dismember the traitors.
