Full speech

Entheus and Orpheus names both deer to me, In equal balance I your Third will be In this nights honour, view these heaven born Stars, Who by my stealth are become Sublunars. How well their native beauty's fit this place, Which with a choral danee they first shall grace, Then shall their forms to humane figures turn, And these bright fires within their bosoms burn. Orpheus apply thy music for it well Helps to induce a Courtly miracle. Entheus, Thy counsels are divine and joust, Let Orpheus deck thy Hymn, since pray we must. So, pause awhile, and come ye fiery spirits, Break forth the earthlike sparks t'attend these Knights. WAit spirits wait, while through the clouds we And by descending gain a hire place. (pace, The good old Sage is silenced, her free tongue That made such melody, is now unstrung: Then grace her Trophy with a dance triumphant, Where Orpheus is none can fit music want. So be it ever, joy and peace, And mutual love give you increase, That your posterity may grow In fame, as long as Seas do flow. Cease, cease your wooing strife, see Jove intends, To fill your number up, and make all friends, Orpheus, and Eutheus, join your skills once more, And with a Hymn the Diety implore. Powerful Jove, that hast given four, Raise this number but once more, That complete, their numerous feet May aptly in joust measures meet.

Plot summary

Plot — The Lord's Masque (Wedding Masque of Stars and Statues Made Human)
Entheus, representing poetic fury, has been imprisoned by Mania, but Jove needs a wedding masque to be written and sends Orpheus to command his release. Mania protests that all the frantics are confined in the same cave, and she cannot let one out without releasing all the others; a mad dance ensues, but she succeeds in confining the non-literary lunatics. Entheus calls on Prometheus, who introduces eight stars. Orpheus’ music transforms them into human beings, the male masquers. Jove, who turned Prometheus’ first female creations into statues, rescinds his decree and makes them women again; at first there are only four women, but Jove creates four more to even up the numbers of male and female 1613 masquers. Dancing ensues. Sibylla speaks Latin prophecies about the bride and groom’s future, and Orpheus, Entheus, and Prometheus wish them well The masquers dance away.
