Another of these Students at that time, Was there with him, as I have herd a truth. Berowne they call him, but a merrier man, Within the limit of becoming mirth, I never spent and hours talk withal. His eye begets occasion for his wit, For every object that the one does catch, The other turns to a mirth-mouing jest. Which his fair tongue (conceits expositor) Delivers in such apt and gracious words, That aged ears play treuant at his tales, And younger hearings are quite ravished. So sweet and voluble is his discourse. Did not I dance with you in Brabant once? How needless was it then to ask the question? It's long of you y to spur me with such questions. Not till it leave the Rider in the mire. The hour that fools should ask. Fair fallen the face it covers. Amen, so you be none. Shall I teach you to know. Why she that bears the Bow. Finely put off. Well then, I am the shooter. If we choose by the horns, your self come not near. Finely put on indeed. Shall I come upon thee with and old saying, that was a man when King Pippin of France was a little boy, as touching the hit it. Thou canst not hit it, hit it, hit it, Thou canst not hit it my good man. Madam, came nothing else along with that? That was the way to make his godhead wax: For he hath been five thousand years a Boy. Come on then, wear the favours most in sighed. What would these strangers? Know their minds Boyet. If they do speak our language, it's our will That some plain man recount their purposes. Know what they would? Why that they have, and bid them so be gone. It is not so. Ask them how many inches Is in one mile? If they have measured many, The measure then of one is easily told: How many weary steps, Of many weary miles you have overwent, Are numbered in the travel of one mile? My face is but a Moon, and clouded too. Oh vain petitioner, beg a greater matter, Thou now requests but Moonshine in the water. Play music then: nay you must do it soon. Not yet no dance: thus change I Like the Moon. You took the Moon at full, but now she's changed? The music plays, vouchsafe some motion to it: Our ears vouchsafe it. Only to partly friends. Curtsy sweet hearts, and so the Measure ends. We can afford no more at such a price. Your absence only. Then cannot we be bought: and so adue, Twice to your Visore, and half once to you. Not one word more my maids, break off, break off. Well-liking wits they have, gross, gross, fat, fat. They were all in lamentable cases. The King was weeping ripe for a good word. Good Madam, if by me you'll be advised, Let's mock them still as well known as disguised: Let us complain to them what fools were hear, Disguised Like Muscovites in shapeless gear: And wonder what they were, and to what end Their shallow shows, and Prologue vildly penned: And their rough carriage so ridiculous, Should be presented at our Tent to us. Madam speak true. It is not so my Lord: My Lady (to the manner of the days) In courtesy gives undeserving praise. We four indeed confronted were with four In Russia habit: Here they stayed and hour, And talked apace: and in that hour (my Lord) They did not bless us with one happy word. I dare not call them fools; but this I think, When they are thirsty, fools would feign have drink. Help hold his brows, he'll found: why look you pale? Seasick I think coming from Moscovy. Sans, sans, I pray you. We did not coat them so. You'll never be friends with him, a killed your sister. What's your dark meaning mouse, of this light word? You are attaint with faults and perjury: Therefore if you my favour mean to get, A twelvemonth shall you spend, and never rest, But seek the weary beds of people sick. Oft have I herd of you my Lord Berowne, Before I saw you: and the world's large tongue Proclaims you for a man replete with mocks, Full of comparisons, and wounding flouts: Which you on all estates will execute, That lie within the mercy of your wit. To weed this Wormwood from your fruitful brain, And therewithal to win me, if you please, Without the which I am not to be wone: You shall this twelvemonth term from day to day, Visit the speechless sick, and still converse With groaning wretches: and your task shall be, With all the fierce endeavour of your wit, To enforce the pained impotent to smile. Why that's the way to choke a gibing spirit, Whose influence is begotten of that lose grace, Which shallow laughing hearers give to fools: A jests prosperity, lies in the ear Of him that hears it, never in the tongue Of him that makes it: then, if sickly ears, Deaft with the clamours of their own deer groans, Will hear your idle scorns; continue then, And I will have you, and that fault withal.
