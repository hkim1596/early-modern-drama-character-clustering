I've herd a better Character of both, Such, as to the young Count, Wit, and valour gives: Unto Forest, honest Spirits. Why dost thou spend thy gall in secret thus? A pox vpon't: turn thoughts to Actium: Heaven knows, I had rather enrich my self, Then envy others wealth. Employ thy brain. Get the Dukes fist to this; and thou shalt share Five hundred Crowns. The old business. His Man's as full of Proverbs As a Constable: he coins they himself. He walks Like a Zealand-storke. Why sir; this dignifies the Jest. They scarce Ere saw the Duke: and are less known Unto the world. His Grace well apprehends, These voluntary mistakes of Nature, In preservation of their intellects, Are fitter subjects for accidental mirth, Then a Comical continuance. It is A levity too humble in a Prince To heed such trifles. This disgrace, makes thy Cousin boil his heart In his own blood. And I with all celerity. How? will Borachio leave the Court? Then get a Patent to survey Brine-pits. Or else for casting Ordinance in Loom. A joust exception: for going hastily To kiss his whore; he could not find her Mouth. Now when he hath provided some high toy For the Press; he thinks on worser dic cion, Strait chooses one of the faction; who must Not Patronise, but buy what he makes vendable, With praise in the Epistle. I will be bold to think so. Known, by whom by City wits! Good! they have left the Garden door un locked I'll venture in to help discovery. Castruchio is graced with rare employment: The Duke and he do here consume the Night. These are hours for Ghosts, Adulterers And Thieves. The slave is Haggard. At Supper Being full of gold: his vain Appetite Fed at Nero's rate; I was discarded With a frown: shaken Like a Burre from's sleeve. As if my closure heretofore had been Impertinent. Ambition lessens all Beneath it self to nothing: the higher We do stand: so much less those men appear Whom we behold below—Hark! Kind Fortune Lend me thy Ears— And nothing makes Learning so cheap; but that Every writer sells his works. So swift of foot! I must overtake you. Signior, I knew you a brave Commander Under the great Petruchio; and since That time your constant virtues have deserved More recompense, then Fate will minister By me. My kindness is no miracle: Since gratitude is only sick, not dead. But pray believe what I have said is truth. I prithee stay, why slip but here asid And thou shalt see the most resplendent Fopp, That ever did discredit Nature. Sigmor. Lothario; a Country Gentleman But now the Court Baboon: who persuades himself, (Out of a new kind of madness) to be The Dukes favourite. He comes. Th'other is A bundle of Proverbs: whom he seduced From the Plough; to serve him for Preferment. I know him. He sings Like Phalaris Bull. But that's contingent now: Forest Being made Secretary of State. Why about two days since: one of the sect Sent me a Challenge. Because my sister Drunk his Lords health, with her Quoif on. Each hour These giddy Participles do embark Themselves for Duels. The one is a kin To my honourable Lady. Ih'other To my very good Lord. Good morrow to the Court Satirist. We shall have your Thrush, in a Cage shortly. Remember, who you deal withal. Prithee (sweet Castruchio ) leave thy barkeing. It'll bet treason shortly for any man, To carry ears, within three, miles of thy Tongue. Not of your faction (Sir) if none return Unto the prison for your libeling. You remember your Vices-strip'd, and whipped. Your trim Eclogues, the fulsome Satyr too, Written to his Grace. Wherein you flatter, Whine, and damn your self to get a pardon For what seems there a resolute offence. Satyrs, are more useful, now then ever. Nor grieves it me to see the humour used, But thus abused. To see a Barred still reach At ho Baize, Passion o'me! I'll tell thee. Thy R mes I clude not so much Brains, as would Suffice to fill a Cherrystone. I, with thy self. Step aside. They are as mad as thy Cousin. All this is Alcaron. Hold! Signior Lothario, hold! Mercy Becomes the powerful, Lets relieve Borachio, or all our Comic Scenes Are at and end. Who never saw verse, but what their Suitors writ, Which they read Like Prose too. They say you are particular with a Great Lady. Some lose thing (belike) yet will be at charge, To secure her fame from noise. For thou prayst Against all lechery but thine own. I, for then she had lived chaste. He grows angry, His eyes look read. T'were fit they would employ their modesty At home. For thou art a fool in print. True, for every Poet thinks himself The best Poet in the world. A precious Satirist! This surly Dog, Inveys against lechery in others, because He would engross all Women to himself. Oh damned villainy: Is this th'imployment That does make you proud? I will haunt you still, To strengthen my intelligence. Cry mercy Signior: the day's blear Eie'd yet, And my own hast made me unmannerly. Faith Signior; the Count being out of Town I though Forest would have more leisure To peruse my new Suit. He's early up; Which caused my vigilance. Then Signior, you will oblige my prayers. The Profit of the day be yours. These tricks Shall make me wear him in my Eyes. The slave Does usher out his breath in state; as if His honours had out grown his own knowledge. Yet but a tame Pande . The beauteous Corsa Is ravished by the Duke. Oh black horror. Arise my soul, inspire my industry With noble purpose. Something I'll do That shall proclaim my Spirit. I give you real grounds for my suspense. Reward (sir) may make a Villain bloody Though it cannot make him valiant. The Duke Will let him want no Gold. Noble Signior, I am yours, My thoughts continue in the former sense: I have a chaste, and virtuous wife; however You desired assurance from a trial So unkind as this. Beside our obligations to your worth, Even both our honours would impose it As a virtue, not a trouble. We are Your humble servants. Bring the slaves in, their deeds will soon convince Their faint denial, where did you leave they Lady? Hold the Lady there: Oh dire spectacle. the Duke, Lucio, Forest, and Lothario Lie here breathless. I did suspect some black Conspiracy. Which made me haunt them two Unto the Palace, but I did lose they By the Chapel stairs; bloody dogs, what Devil Prompted thee to this Actium. Take em away, and close their wounds, though there Be some mercy shown, by thus deferring That reward which your black souls shall receive In Hell. Yet know the Law will here on Earth Provide such tortures as shall make your death's Exemplary to all succeeding times.— Gentlemen, your silence may be excused. Where, theres so much cause of admiration. Some help transfer the dead from hence, others Call up the Councillors of state. So intricate is Heavens revenge gainst lust. The righteous suffer here, with the unjust. Dost here? Cosimo.

Plot summary

Plot — The Cruel Brother
As a favour to Lucio, to whom he is devoted, the Duke appoints the low-born Foreste his secretary, the first port of call for ali suitors seeking monopolies and preferment. Lucio also wants to marry Foreste’s sister, 1627 Corsa, arguing that she is ennobled by her virtue, though not her ancestry. He goes through with the marriage secretly, without the Duke’s permission; but the Duke is impressed by Corsa when they are introduced, and has her brought to court so that the marriage can be properly celebrated with masques and revels. Foreste humiliates the satirist Castruccio, but his elevation to the secretaryship prevents Castruccio from challenging him to a duel. The poet initially takes a literary revenge, but, when the Duke asks about his satires, he is too cowardly to admit that Foreste is the target. The rustic Lothario comes to court, dressing foppishly in the hope of becoming a ducal favourite. Castruccio persuades him that his lack of success is Foreste’s fault. Lothario accosts Foreste offensively and finds himself imprisoned in a tiny cell. The Duke’s real intention in bringing Corsa to court is to seduce her. He engages Foreste’s wife as an unwitting go-between, then sends Lucio away overnight on a diplomatic mission. With Castruccio’s assistance, he visits Corsa, and rapes her. Foreste learns of the rape and slits the dishonoured Corsa’s wrist; she bleeds to death. On his return, Lucio takes a more lenient view than Foreste — he places more weight on the compulsion than the sexual penetration — but both agree to take revenge on the Duke, and visit him in bed. In the end, they decide to spare him, but he has already arranged assassins to dispose of them: the job was given to Castruccio, who subcontracted it to Lothario. Lothario kills the Duke by mistake; Castruccio and his accomplice kill him, then finish the job by killing Foreste and Lucio. The conspirators are kept alive to answer questions, and then suffer a death of exemplary horribleness.
