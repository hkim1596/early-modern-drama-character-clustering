Naw bay may Priest hade God give you ten far ene, They sir that ay de, give yowll give me troothing, When ay? well a near ay swer bay the Sacrament, Ay had rather han a cup of nale then a Testament. It is the least thing ay car far bay may charge, Far see Long as thena han Images wharon to luck, What need thena be distructed avut of a Book. They, ay my sen, bay experience thot con show, Far in may Portace the tongue ay de nat know, Yet when ay see the great gilded letter, Ay ken it sea well, as nea man ken better: As far Example, on the day of Chra stes Natyuitie, Ay see a Bab in a Manger, and two Beasts standing by The Service whilk to Newyeares day is assaygnd, Bay the Paicture of the Circumcision ay feigned. The Service, whilk on Twalfth day must be done, Ay seek ay the mark of the three kings of Colon. Bay the Devil tentyng Chraist, ay feigned whadragesima, Bay Chraist on the Cross, ay search out Good frayday: Pasch for his mark, hath the Resurrection, Against Hally Thursday, is pented Chraistes Ascension, Thus in main own book, ay is a Good Clarke, But give the Sents war gone, the Cat had eat my mark See the sandry mairacles, whi ilk Sent have done, Bay the Picttures on the walls sal appear to them soon Bay the whilk thena ar learned in every distress, What Sent thena must prea to far succour doubtless: Sea that all Lepers to Sylvester must prea, That he wawd free tham, there disease take away. Layk ways, thena that han the falling saicknes, To be eased therfre, thena m n prea to St. Cornelis: In contagious air, as in plague or pestilence, To hally Sent Ruke, thena must call far assistance. From parill of drawning, Sent Carp keep the Mariners From dayng in warfare, Sent George guard the Soldiers, Sent Job heal the Pox, the Ague, Sent German, Far to ease the toothache, call to Sent Appollyne, Give that a woman be barren and childless, To help her herein, she must prea to Sent Nicolas. Far women in travail, call to Sent Magdalene Far lawlynes of mind, call to Sent Katherine, Sent Loy save your Horse, Sent Anthony your Swine. Bay may trooth, in reading any other, ne taym do I spend Far that ay ken, bay gene all caused ̄ fell, is canonized And bay the hely Pope himself is authorized: That Book farther, is wholly permitted, Wharas, the Bayble in partly is prohibited. And therefore, give it be lawful to utter my conscience, Before the new Testament ays give it credence. They mara is there a very busy body, When will jest with me and call me fool and noddy, And sets his Lads to spowt Latin against me, But ay spose then with Deparfundis Clam avi, And oftentimes he will reason's with me of the Sacarment, And say he can prove bay the new Tastament, That Chraystss body is in Heaven placed, But ays not believe him, ay will not be avut faced, He says besayed that the Pope is Anticraist, Fugered of John bay the seven headed beast, And all awre religion is but mons invention, And with Gods ward is at utter dissension, And a plaguy déel mare of sayk layk talk, That ay dar not far may nars bay his yate walk, But ay wawd he were brunt that ay mawght be whaiet, They sir that ay ken he is léped Phailelegoos. They or else ay wawd may sawl war in Hell, To de him a pleasure ay wawd gang a whole year, Give it war but to make him a Fadocke to bear. Ay shall follow after, God boy you good Gentleman.

Plot summary

Plot — The Conflict of Conscience
Satan, concerned about the spread of godliness on Earth, procures Hypocrisy, Tyranny, and Avarice to counteract it by propagating vice through the Roman Catholic church. Philologus and Mathetes discuss the danger to Protestantism, and conclude that worldly torments are sent by God to test men’s faith. After arguing about their mutual precedence, the vices resolve to help the papal legation, and adopt innocuous aliases. Tyranny is commissioned as an inquisitor to hunt heretics, while Avarice accepts their bribes to save them from arrest. Caconos alerts the vices to the heresies of Philologus, who is arrested and tried by the Cardinal in consistory court. He is examined on the topics of papal authority and transubstantiation, and found guilty. His property is confiscated and his family’s distress used to put psychological pressure on him; he is also threatened with imprisonment, torture, and execution, but promised money and power if he recants. He does, persuaded by the glass of vanity given by Sensual Suggestion. Spirit and Conscience call on Philologus to renounce his apostasy and embrace martyrdom, but he rejects them. In prosperity, he is visited by Horror, who torments him with the knowledge of his own damnation. Theologus and Eusebius urge the continuing availability of God’s grace, but Philologus blasphemes and curses God. Despairing of salvation, he hangs himself. (In the B-text, he is given grace to repent at the last moment.)
