Thou shouldst sooner have it I dare take in hand, For among us now, such is our country zeal, That we love best with strangers to deal. To fell a lease deer, whosoever that will, At the french, or dutch Church let him set up his bill. And he shall have chapmen, I warrant you good store, Look what and English man bids, they will give as much more. We brokers of strangers, well know the gain, By them we have good rewards for our pain. Therefore though thou be strange, the matter is not great, For thy money is English, which must work the feat. ¶I dare say each man would be glad at his heart, To have all his Neighbours such as thou art. What matter is it, if thou thy self be sped, Though thou take thy neighbours house over his head. ¶It is time I trow. Exiunt. ¶So are we in deed, and what of that? Who is it that with by would any thing have? ¶Therein we can help you if your pleasure it be, And will do or else we were greatly to blame, Provided always that to our pains you do see, And also put in a good pawn for the same. ¶You shall have it sir so soon as you will, And therein you shall be friendly used, For in friendly using this fellow hath skill, pointing to Fur theraunce. Therefore his counsel must not be refused. He is servant unto a Merchant man, Who is partly ruled after his mind. ¶Why sir no diligence in us shall be lacking, For we are ready, if that you be so. ¶Nay now let him shift for himself if he will, Since I am paid the thing I did seek: Alas good Gentleman, he is served but ill, In faith he is in now by the week. Plot summary

Plot — The Tide Tarrieth No Man
Courage advises a variety of characters to embrace short-term gain, since the tide waits for nobody: he encourages Greediness to ignore spiritual advice, helps No Good Neighbourhood to acquire a neighbour's house unjustly through bribery, recommends Willingto-Win-Worship to get into debt rather than leave the court and his ambitions, and advises Wantonness to get married against her mother's wishes. The results are bad: Willing-to-Win-Worship finds himself worse off than before; Greediness's tenant, No-GoodNeighbourhood's rival, is evicted and left homeless in his old age; Greediness oppress~:s his debtors, one of whom is arrested at a sermon; and Wantonness marries Wastefulness. Christianity is corrupted by riches and policy, and Greediness dies in despair. Despair encourages Wastefulness, who has spent all his money, to commit suicide, but Faithful Few saves him through prayer. Finally Authority intervenes, Courage is arrested by Correction, and Christianity is reformed.
