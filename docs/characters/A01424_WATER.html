The pure stream of my affection: o how I am troubled! I am not yet recovered. Do not talk of fighting, is it not time that Uva ter should come to quench the fire of such contention. I tell you, the care of your preservation made me break my banks to come to you, that you might see the overflowing love I bear you: your quarrel hath echoed unto me; I know your ambition for superiority: you are all my kinsmen, near all lied to Water, and though I say it, sometime not a little be holding to Water, even for your very makings. Will you refer your selves to me, and wade no further in these dis contentment's, I will undertake your reconcilement and quam lification. Then without further circumlocution or insinua cion, Water runs to the matter: you shall no more contend for excellency, for Water shall allow each of you a singulari tie. First, you Wine, shall be in most request among Cour tiers, Gallants, Gentlemen, and Poetical wits, Qui melioris luti homines, being of a refined mould, shall choose as a more nimble and active watering, to make their brains fruitful, Fecundi calices quem non? but so as not confined to them, nor limiting them to you, more then to exhilarate their spirits, and acuate their inventions. You Beer, shall be in most grace with the Citizens, as being a more stayed Liquour, fit for them that purpose retire meant and gravity, that with the Snail carries the cares of a house and family with them, tied to the attendance of and illi berall profession, that neither trot nor amble, but have a sure pace of their own, Bos lassus fortius figit pedem, The black Ox has trod upon their foot: yet I bound you not with the City, though it be the common entertainment, you may be in credit with Gentlemens Sellers, and carry reputation before you from March to Christmas—tide I should say; that Water should forget his Tide. You Ale I remit to the Country, as more fit to live where you were bred: your credit shall not be inferior, for people of all sorts shall desire your acquaintance, specially in the mor ning, though you may be allowed all the day after: the Parson shall account you one of his best Parishioners, & the Church wardens shall pay for your company, and drawing their Bills all the year long, you shall be loved and maintained at the Parish charge till you be old, be allowed a Robin-hood, or Mother Red-cap, to hang at your door, to beckon in Custo mers: and if you come into the City, you may be drunk with pleasure, but never come into the fashion. At all times you shall have respect, but ith Winter Morning's without comparison. How do you Like my censure now? Let my fear ebb a little. And yet the world says sometime Water is shallow: nay, I'll see you shake hands, and tie a new knot of friendship. What's the matter? Godamercy Sugar with all my heart, I shall love thy company, for I am solitary, and thou wilt make me plea sant. Stay. Hark Music? O some friends of mine, I know they, they often come upon the Water: let's entertain the air a little, never a voice among you? Hold, hold, hold. I, I, all friends on my word, Tossed. Why, now could I dance for joy.

Plot summary

Plot — Wine, Beer, and Ale Together by the Ears (Wine, Beer, Ale, and Tobacco Contending for Superiority)
There is dissension among the servants of Wine, Beer, and Ale: the drunken, quarrelsome Toast picks a fight with Sugar, who is also in his master’s bad books for excessive womanizing. The masters also bicker: Beer wants to take the wall, and Wine asserts his social superiority. Ale arrives in time to see Wine challenge Beer toa duel, and tries to make the peace. Unfortunately he does it by insisting that he is their better, on grounds of antiquity, learning, and property (for there are many houses named after him). This only exacerbates the situation. Water arrives and asks to adjudicate the argument and reconcile them. He allows them all their own distinctive application: Wine for courtiers and gallants, Beer for citizens, and Ale for country people. The altercation is resolved, though it takes a little more persuasion to get Toast, who has brought a knife in his master’s defence, to put it away. Wine, Beer, and Ale sing a song, and the characters dance. B-Text In this version, business in which Wine smokes a pipe of tobacco is omitted, and Tobacco introduced as a character: he interrupts the festive ending before the characters can dance, insisting that he too is a kind of drink and is the best of all. Afraid of his power to seduce men against them, the other drinks invite him to join them on equal terms, and Tobacco agrees, In the dance, each of the servants is appropriately applied to his master: Sugar is shaken ver Wine, Nutmeg grated over Beer, and Toast has his backside warmed and placed over Ale’s mouth.
