Hearts of most hard temper Melt and lament for her. Alas poor Lady, She's a stranger now again. Our content Is our best having. Yes troth, & troth; you would not be a Queen? How tastes it? Is it bitter? Forty pence, no: There was a Lady once ('tis and old Story) That would not be a Queen, that would she not For all the mud in Egypt; have you herd it? What do you think me — Beshrew me, I would, And venture Maidenhead for't, and so would you For all this spice of your Hypocrisy: You that have so fair parts of Woman on you, Have (too) a Woman's heart, which ever yet Affected Eminence, Wealth, Sovereignty; Which, to say sooth, are Blessings; and which gifts (Saving your mincing) the capacity Of your soft Chiuerell Conscience, would receive, If you might please to stretch it. 'Tis strange; a three pence bowed would hire me Old as I am, to Queen it: but I pray you, What think you of a Duchess? Have you limbs To bear that load of Title? Then you are weakly made; pluck off a little, I would not be a young Count in your way, For more then blushing comes to: If your back Cannot vouchsafe this burden, 'tis too weak Ever to get a Boy. In faith, for little England You'd venture and emballing: I my self Would for Carnarvonshire, although there longed No more to the Crown but that: Lo, who comes here? Why this it is: See, see, I have been begging sixteen years in Court (Am yet a Courtier beggarly) nor could Come pat betwixt too early, and too late For any suit of pound : and you, (o fate) A very fresh Fish here; fie, fie, fie upon This compelled fortune: have your mouth filled up, Before you open it. With your Theme, I could O're-mount the Lark: The Marchioness of Pembroke ? A thousand pounds a year, for pure respect? No other obligation? by my Life, That promises more thousands: Honours train Is longer then his fore-skirt; by this time I know your back will bear a Duchess. Say, Are you not stronger then you were? I'll not come back, the tidings that I bring Will make my boldness, manners. Now good Angels Fly over thy Royal head, and shade thy person Under their blessed wings. I, I my Liege, And of a lovely Boy: the God of heaven Both now, and ever bless her: It's a Girl Promises Boys hereafter. Sir, your Queen Desires your Visitation, and to be Acquainted with this stranger; it's as Like you, As Cherry, is to Cherry. And hundred Marks? By this light, I'll would more. And ordinary Groom is for such payment. I will have more, or scold it out of him. Said I for this, the Girl was Like to him? I'll Have more, or else say't: and now, while it's hight, I'll put it to the issue.

Plot summary

Plot — Henry the Eighth (All Is True)
The Kings of England and of France meet for a magnificent summit conference organized by Cardinal Wolsey. The English nobles resent Wolsey’s influence with the King, but Wolsey strikes first against the ringleader, Buckingham: evidence from his former surveyor, who bears him a grudge, secures his arrest; conviction for treason and execution follow. Queen Katherine persuades the King to repeal taxation which is causing hardship; Wolsey has it put about that his intercession secured this. King Henry attends a banquet at Wolsey’s house, performs in a masque of shepherds, and meets Anne Boleyn. Rumours circulate that he and the Queen are at odds: he claims to be troubled by the fact that she was originally married to his brother, but others notice that he is keen to marry Anne. Wolsey counsels divorce. A legatine court is summoned to judge the case, but Katherine refuses to co-operate and the court breaks up without reaching a decision. The King secretly marries Anne. The nobles conspire to overthrow Wolsey. Documents proving his corruption and treachery accidentally fall into the King’s hands, and Buckingham’s surviving allies serve him with articles of impeachment. He falls sick and dies. Anne is crowned Queen. Katherine too falls sick. Before her death she is visited by benevolent spirits, and by Caputius, the Imperial Ambassador, by whom she sends the King a request to love their daughter and provide for her former servants. Anne goes into labour and produces a daughter. A cabal of councillors attempts to overthrow Archbishop Cranmer, whom they consider a heretic. Before the hearing, Henry gives Cranmer his ring; the Archbishop produces it at a crucial moment, ensuring that the case is referred to the personal judgement of the King, who rebukes the conspirators. The vindicated Cranmer christens Henry’s new daughter, Elizabeth, and prophesies a golden future for England under her and her eventual successor.
