Enough, enough; Come let's away, my heart is winged with haste That outflies thought or motion; Egypt (sweet) Hath safety in it, not Jerusalem. There's pregnant reason for it, and our lives Are marks that Herod shoots at: Who but sees The woeful state of sad Jerusalem, And how this Tyrant (Like and angry Boar) Roots up the goodly Pines should cover him? Hath he not slain Antigonus, destroyed Thy Father and thy Grandsire: (Oh my Lords, My deer loved Lords, my Father and my Husband;) Worthy Hircanus, noble Alexander; And at this instant lies he not in wait For our destructions? Beldame that I am To prate at such a season; (holy Sweet) Come let's away, our flight is so secure, No Art can undermine it; any pause Opens our graves before us: fly, Oh fly. Hark, I hear The steps of some pursues us; prithee come, Let Egypt and not Juda be our Tomb. Rather think, How sharp a plague is Tyranny: Oh King, Remember it's the fiercest Beast, of all That are accounted savage; yet delights In Flattery, which is the worst of them That are tame and domestic: With these Fiends My life can find no pleasure; do not then Blame me to seek my freedom. But your Tyranny Shall outrun all example: Sir, Despair Arms me with truth and boldness; I dare now Tell you, of Kings, you are the wickedest; And I, that in the ruins of my blood, Read your destroying nature, and collect Into a short brief many Tragedies, Acted upon our family; what hope Is left, that can assist us? Truth hath no needs of figures: was't not you That did betray Hircanus in his flight To the Arabian Monarch; and when laid In harmless sleep then slew him? Did not you Hire the bloody Cassius to cut off My father's head, (the loved Antigonus? ) Have you not killed my Husband, troad my Sons Into the mire, that you might safely walk Over their heads unto Ambition? And can you hope, that we have any hope In you, but desolation? Passed belief, Nor shall the memory loose me; this not feigned, I'll fix my prayers upon him. Oh cease my Marriam, tears can do no good; This Murder's passed example; to be drowned, Drowned in a shallow murmur where the stones Chid the faint water for not covering them. Oh, 'twas a plot beyond the Devil sure; Man could not have that malice. Good Sweet, unlock these counsels. Antipater; he drown him! Yet I fear, None that can draw more vengeance or despair. Come, you're a barbarous Creature. Slanderous Grecian. Young Cockatrice. Do but (This) that motion shall destroy thee. No more; I that but now shed tears, now laugh: Oh God! To see so brave a Masterpiece of Villainy By such a Bastard Issue be compacted: Thou make atonement? No; go lea ne to drown The Lords elected people; here stands she That looks to taste thy poison. You may report our speeches; say, our joy Is, we have left no more he can destroy. You can dissemble royally; but that Cannot cure mine Impostume. Of accident? of plotted Massacre; Murder beyond example: but there's left A Hell to reckon with. Funerals are But wretched satisfactions. Would! is't so, Hath Mischief God the Conquest; then 'tis time To change my disposition, and deceive Those which would else deceive me; in this kind, It skills not whom we injure, whom we blind. Impudent! Is that all thou canst utter? Not any Sir. I do confess them; Here is the Viol and the drops: from this, What can your malice gather? Sir, 'tis false I never did intend your injury. 'Tis welcome Sir; a sudden death, I know Is terrible and fearful; but indeed, To those which do attend it, and do stand Constantly gazing on it; who do live, Where it scars none but Cowards; those can meet, And kiss it as a sweet Companion: 'Tis unto those a Bugbear, who do think Never on Heaven, but for necessity. Your Tyranny hath taught me other rules; And this guest comes long looked for: here's a health To all that honour Virtue; let suffice, Death does oretake; but it does not surprise. Utter it; For Heavens sake utter it, noble, worthy Lord.

Plot summary

Plot — Herod and Antipater
Queen Alexandra attempts to flee to Egypt with her son, the High Priest Aristobulus, after King Herod commands a bloody purge of their family. Herod’s troops arrive in time only to seize their luggage, but they are found hiding inside. Herod makes a public gesture of sparing them, but has his bastard son Antipater arrange an ‘accidental’ drowning for Aristobulus during a swimming contest. Herod backed the wrong side in the recent Roman civil war between Mark Antony and Augustus. He goes to Rome to make his peace with Augustus, the new master of the Empire, leaving Antipater with instructions to kill Mariam, the Queen, in the event of his failure. When Herod presents himself, Augustus offers two slaves the chance to murder him: the Briton conscientiously refuses, whereas the Roman eagerly accepts the job. But Augustus was only toying with Herod: he rewards the British slave, has the Roman thrown into the sea, and confirms Herod in his kingdom as an ally of Rome. Mariam learns that Herod has ordered her death, which compounds his crime in killing Aristobulus. Antipater, who seeks the crown, draws inspiration from Hell, and is shown a vision of the murder of Agamemnon. He procures false witnesses to accuse Mariam of attempting to poison her husband in order to facilitate an incestuous liaison with his brother. Herod sentences them both to death; he later changes his mind and tries to reprieve Mariam, but is too late. His two sons, Alexander and the younger Aristobulus, raise rebellion against him to avenge their mother. War is averted when Augustus intervenes: Herod’s authority is reinforced, the rebel princes are forgiven, and they are given nominally equal status with Antipater, though Herod gives precedence to the bastard. Antipater receives further hellish inspiration in a vision of Jugurth’s murder of his half-brothers. More false evidence is arranged, implicating Alexander and Aristobulus in plots to assassinate Herod and sow sedition in the army: a mason working on the rebuilding of the Temple is induced to say that he was bribed to drop a stone on Herod as he came to inspect the works, and Herod’s barber is asked to cut his throat. The barber shows the letter to Herod, who kills him to be on the safe side, and then has Alexander and Aristobulus strangled. Antipater is now the only surviving son.
