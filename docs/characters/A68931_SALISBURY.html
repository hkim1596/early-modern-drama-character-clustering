And thou brave Warwick, my thrice valiant son, Thy simple plainness and thy housekeeping, Hath won thee credit amongst the couunon sort, The reverence of mine age, and Neuels name, Is of no little force if I command, Then let us join all three in one for this, That good Duke Humphrey may his state possess, But wherefore weeps Warwick my noble son? Come sons, away, and look unto the main. Come leave your drinking, and fallen to blows: Sirrah whats thy name? Peter, what more? My Lord, the Commons sends you word by me, That unless false Suffolk here be done to death, Or banished fair England's territories, That they will err from your highness person, They say, by him the good Duke Humphrey died, They say, by him they fear the ruin of the Realm: And therefore, if you love your subjects weal, They wish you to banish him from forth the land. Well hast thou fought this day, thou valiant Duke, And thou brave bud of Yorks increasing house, The small remainder of my weary life, I hold for thee, for with thy warlike arm, Three times this day thou hast preserved my life. Saving your tale my lord, as I have herd, in the reign of Bullenbrook, the Duke of York did claim the Crown, and but for Owen Glendor, had been King. Thump, then see that thou thump thy master. I will son. See how the pangs of death does gripe his heart, So bade and end did never none behold, But as his death, so was his life in all.
