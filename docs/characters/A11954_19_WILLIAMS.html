We see yonder the beginning of the day, but I think we shall never see the end of it. Who goes there? Under what Captain serve you? A good old Commander, and a most kind Gentleman: I pray you what thinks he of our estate? That's more then we know. But if the Cause be not good, the King him self hath a heavy Reckoning to make, when all those Legs, and Arms, and Heads, chopped off in a Battle, shall join together at the latter day, and cry all, We die ed at such a place, some swearing, some crying for a Sur gean; some upon their Wives, left poor behind them; some upon the Debts they owe, some upon their Children rawly left: I am afeared, there are few die well, that die in a Battle: for how can they charitably dispose of any thing, when Blood is their argument? Now, if these men do not die well, it will be a black matter for the King, that led them to it; who to disobey, were against all pro portion of subjection. It's certain, every man that die ill, the ill upon his own head, the King is not to answer it. I, he said so, to make us fight cheerfully: but when our throats are cut, he may be ransomed, and we never the wiser. You pay him then: that's a perilous shot out of and Elder Gun, that a poor and a private displeasure can do against a Monarch: you may as well go about to turn the Sun to ye, with fanning in his face with a Peacocks feather: You'll never trust his word after; come, it's a foolish saying. Let it be a Quarrel between us, if you live. How shall I know thee again? Here's my Glove: Give me another of thine. This will I also wear in my Cap: if ever thou come to me, and say, after to morrow, This is my Glove, by this Hand I will take thee a box on the ear. Thou darest as well be hanged. Keep thy word: fare thee well. An't please your Majesty, 'tis the gage of one that I should fight withal, if he be alive. Under Captain Gower, my Liege. I will my Liege. I warrant it is to Knight you, Captain. Sir, know you this Glove? I know this, and thus I challenge it. Do you think I'll be forsworn? I am no Traitor. My Liege, this was my Glove, here is the fellow of it: and he that I gave it to in change, promised to wear it in his Cap: I promised to strike him, if he did: I met this man with my Glove in his Cap, and I have been as good as my word. All offences, my Lord, come from the heart: ne ver came any from mine, that might offend your Ma iestie. Your Majesty came not Like your self: you appeared to me but as a common man; witness the Night, your Garments, your Lowliness: and what your Highness suffered under that shape, I beseech you take it for your own fau to, and not mine: for had you been as I took you for, I made no offence; therefore I beseech your Highness pardon me. I will none of your Money. An't please your Majesty, a Rascal that swag ge ed with me last night: who if alive, and ever dare to challenge this Glove, I have sworn to take him a box of that ere: or if I can see my Glove in his cap, which he swore as he was a Soldier he would wear (if alive) I will strike it out soundly. So, I will my Liege, as I live.
