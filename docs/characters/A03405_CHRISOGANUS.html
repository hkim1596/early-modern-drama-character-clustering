The mysteries that Art hath found therein: It is distinguished into Regions, Those Regions filled with sundry sorts of stars: They (likewise) christened with peculiar names, To see a daily use wrought out of them, With demonstrations so infallible, The pleasure cannot be, but ravishing. Why you shall meet with projects so removed From vulgar apprehension, (as for instance,) The Sun here riseth in the East with us, But not of his own proper motion, As being turned by primum mobile, (The heaven above Coelum stellatum ) Whereas his true assent is in the West, And so he consummates his circled course In the Ecliptic line, which parts the Zodiac, Being born from Tropic to Tropic: this time We call a year; whose Hierocliphick was (Amongst the Egyptians figured in a Snake Wreathed circular, the tail within his mouth: As (happily) the atines (since) did call, A Ring, (of the word Annus) Annulus, The Mathematicques are the strength of truth, A Magazine of all perfection. If then there be no shadow, no pretext, To veil their loathed bodies; what should make Men so enamoured on this Strumpet war. To dote upon her form? when (in her self) She's made of nothing, but infectious plagues. Witness the present Chaos of our Scene, Where every street is chained with links of spoil, Here proud Ambition rides; there Fury flies, Here Horror; and there ruthless Murder stalks, Led on by Ruin, and in Steel and fire, That now on tops of houses; now in vaults, Now in the sacred Temples; here, and there Runs wild. Thou want's a Solan to consort with thee, To prove affliction is the perfect way That leads to Jove's tribunal dignity; Ill hast thou governed thy prosperity, That canst not smile in mere adversity. Look upon me (the poorest slave in show, That ever fortune buried in mishap:) Yet this is Nature's richest lewell-house And teaches me to weep at all your wants. Seest thou this poor and naked bosom here? Dost thou behold this scorned uncovered head? When thou wast rich and Peerless in thy pride, Content did never harbour in thy breast, Nor ere had love, her residence in thee, (I mean the love of perfect happiness) But skillesse grudging from a haughty spirit Did blind thy senses with a slender merit. Whilst I (poor man) not subject to such thought Gave entertain to those sweet blessed babes, Which Sapience brought from Wisdoms holy breast, And thought me rich to have their company. By nursing them in Peace I shunned all Sloth, Nor yet did Plenty make me prodigal: Pride I abhorred and termed the Beggars shield: Nor ever did base Euuie touch my heart. Yet always Love to bear (as Solon said,) A Turtles eye within and Aspics head: Nor could the rattling fury of fierce war Astonish me more then the midnight clock, The Trumpeter to Contemplation: For Poverty, I shake her by the hand, As welcome Lady to this woeful Land. First entertain submission in your souls To frame true concord in one unity. Behold the fair proportion of a man, Whom heavens have created so complete, Yet if the arm make war against the head, Or that the heart rebel against the brain, This elemental body (thus compact,) Is but a scattered Chaos of revenge; Your laws appointed to be positive, (By War confounded) must be brought again. For law is that which Love and Peace maintain. Follow, and I'll instruct you what I can: Why that I will demonstrate to you, thus; Turn a huge wheel: contrary to the sway Place me a fly upon't: the fly (before It can arrive the point from whence it went) Shall sundry times be circumvolved about; Even so the Sun and the affinities: For if you wonder how at one self hour, Two of discordant nature's may be born, As one a King, another some base Swain, One valiant, and the other timorous, Let but two drops of ink or water fallen Directly on so swift a turning wheel, And you shall find them both cast far in sunder. Even so the heavenly Orbs, whirling so fast And so impetuously (project men's fates) Most full of change and contrariety. Proud Lord, poor Art shall wear a glorious crown, When her despisers die to all renown. The more your blame.

Plot summary

Plot — Histriomastix, or The Player Whipped
Under the benevolent rule of Peace, various orders of society turn to learning in their different ways: Mavortius and his fellow noblemen and gentlemen become students of the liberal arts, professional people appoint the scholar Chrisoganus as their lecturer, and some artisans organize themselves as a company of players, Sir Oliver Owlet’s Men. However, the new reign of Plenty tempts many of them away from their studies, leaving only Chrisoganus loyal. While the lawyers and merchants take advantage of the leisure of the vacation to go hunting, Mavortius entertains his guests with a play given by Owlet’s Men. Plenty is succeeded by Pride, who encourages a dedication to fashion and a neglect of proper decorum: the lawyers and merchants enrich themselves unjustly, Mavortius dismisses many of his servants to release more money to spend on pleasure, and both Chrisoganus (who has turned playwright) and Owlet's Men price themselves out of the market. Next, Envy comes to power, causing class resentment in the realm: the citizenry’s adoption of the latest fashions causes resentment among the nobility, envious behaviour disrupts the players’ rehearsal, and Mavortius becomes belligerent. The next ruler is War, under whom the players are conscripted as soldiers, Mavortius fights with his friends, the common people rise in rebellion against their social superiors, and the Spanish invade the realm. Out of this chaos comes Poverty, leading aristocrats, merchants. and peasants alike to debt, bankruptcy, and famine. Owlet’s men, unable to pay their taxes, are banished from the realm. Finally Poverty and her associates are expelled by the return of Peace and Plenty. Queen Elizabeth also appears, in the guise of Astraea, and the play ends with the five lesser rulers resigning their sceptres to Peace.
