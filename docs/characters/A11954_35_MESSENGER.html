News (my good Lord) from Room. Labienus (this is stiffe-newes) Hath with his Parthian Force Extended Asia: from Euphrates his conquering Banner shook, from Syria to Lydia, And to Ionia, whilst — O my Lord. At your Noble pleasure. In Scicion, her length of sickness, With what else more serious, Imports thee to know, this bears. Thy biddings have been done, & every hour Most Noble Caesar, shalt thou have report How it's abroad. Pompey is strong at Sea, And it appears, he is beloved of those That only have feared Caesar: to the Ports The discontents repair, and men's reports Give him much wronged. Caesar I bring thee word, Menacrates and Menas famous Pirates Makes the Sea serve them, which they ear and wound With keels of every kind. Many hight inroads They make in Italy, the Borders Maritime Lack blood to think on't, and flush youth revolt, No Vessel can peep forth: but it's as soon Taken as seen: for Pompey's name strikes more Then could his War resisted. Madam, Madam. First Madam, he is well. Good Madam hear me. Wilt please you hear me? Madam, he's well. And Friends with Caesar. Caesar, and he, are greater Friends then ever. But yet Madam. Free Madam, no: I made no such report, He's bound unto Octavia. For the best turn in the bed. Madam, he's married to Octavia. Good Madam patience. Gracious Madam, I that do bring the news, made not the match. He's married Madam. Nay then I'll run: What mean you Madam, I have made no fault. I have done my duty. He's married Madam. Should I lie Madame? I crave your Highness pardon. Take no offence, that I would not offend you, To punish me for what you make me do Seems much unequal, he's married to Octavia. Most gracious Majesty. I dread Queen. Madam in Room, I looked her in the face: and saw her led between her Brother, and Mark Anthony. She is not Madam. Madam, I herd her speak, she is low voiced. She creeps: her motion, & her station are as one. She shows a body, rather then a life, A Statue, then a Breather. Or I have no observance. And I do think she's thirty. The News is true, my Lord, he is descried, Caesar ha taken Toryne. The Emperor calls Camidius. Anthony is come into the Field. Fulvia thy Wife, First came into the Field. I: but soon that War had end, And the times state Made friends of them, jointing their force against Caesar, Whose better issue in the war from Italy, Upon the first encounter drove them. The Nature of bade news infects the Teller. Madam, she was a widow. Round, even to faultiness. Brown Madam: and her forehead As low as she would wish it. The man from Scicion, Is there such and one? He stays upon your will. Fulvia thy wife is dead.

Plot summary

Plot — Antony and Cleopatra
Antony dallies with Cleopatra in Egypt while the Roman world collapses into faction. His wife and brother make war on Caesar, and Pompey takes advantage of his absence to foment a sea war with the state and avenge his murdered father. At first Antony ignores Caesar’s messages, but his wife’s death draws him back to Rome, where he and Caesar are reconciled; to seal the league, Antony marries Caesar’s sister, Octavia. A soothsayer, who has told the fortunes of members of Cleopatra’s court, warns him that, although he is a better man than Caesar, Caesar will always prove the more successful of the two. Antony, Caesar, and Lepidus meet Pompey with an army, but war is averted. Lepidus gets egregiously drunk at the feast on Pompey’s galley to celebrate the treaty. Pompey refuses his ally Menas’ dishonourable proposal to kill the triumvirate and make him master of the world. Antony’s lieutenant Ventidius defeats the Parthians in battle, but is cautious about taking too much of the credit for himself. Meanwhile, news of Antony’s marriage reaches Cleopatra; she does not take it well. Antony leaves Rome with his new wife, but absence breeds suspicion between him and Caesar. Caesar breaks the truce with Pompey and deposes Lepidus on a pretext. Antony, in turn, sends Octavia home and returns to Egypt, where he creates Cleopatra queen of several dominions. War between east and west follows. Antony perversely chooses to fight by sea, and Cleopatra insists on being present at the battle. They are routed when Cleopatra flees and Antony follows her. Antony’s followers begin to defect to Caesar, but Enobarbus remains loyal. Caesar attempts to divide the lovers by offering Cleopatra security if she will banish or kill Antony. Antony has the messenger whipped for over-familiarity with Cleopatra, and determines to fight to the bitter end. Enobarbus decides finally to go over to Caesar, but leaves behind his treasure; Antony sends it after him, and the guilt-stricken Enobarbus dies. Antony defeats Caesar in a land battle, but loses the next day at sea. Spurned by Antony, Cleopatra takes refuge in her monument and sends him false news of her death. He stabs himself and is pulled up, dying, to join Cleopatra in the monument. Caesar hopes to display Cleopatra in his triumph at Rome, but she frustrates him by smuggling an asp past her guard and committing suicide.
