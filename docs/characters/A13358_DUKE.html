Thus all alone from Cestus am I come, And left my princely court and noble train, To come to Athens, and in this disguise, To see what course my son Aurelius takes, But stay, heres some it may be Travels thither, Good sir can you derect me the way to Athens? Fair lovely maid young and affable, More clear of hew and far more beautiful, Then precious Sardonix or purple rocks, Of Amithests or glistering Hiasinthe, More amiable far then is the plain, Where glistering Cepherus in silver boures, Gazeth upon the Giant Andromede, Sweet Kate entertain this lovely woman. I think the man is mad he calls me a woman. What is she mad to? or is my shape transformed That both of them persuade me I am a woman, But they are mad sure, and therefore I'll begun, And leave their companies for fear of harm, And unto Athens hast to seek my son. I am glad sir that you would be so frank, Are you become the Duke of Cestus son, And revels with my treasure in the town, Base villain that thus dishonourest me. Hers no villain: he will not know me now, But what say you? have you forgotten me too? With thyson? no trust me if he be thine, I pray you sir who am I? Peace villain: lay hands on them, And send them to prison straight. Ah treacherous boy that durst presume, To wed thy self without thy father's leave, I swear by fair Cintheas burning rays, By Merops head and by seven mouthed Nile, Had I but known ere thou hadst wedded her, Were in thy breast the world's immortal soul, This angry sword should rip thy hateful chest, And hewed thee smaller then the Libian sands, Turn hence thy face: o cruel impious boy, Alfonso I did not think you would presume, To mach your daughter with my princely house, And never make me acquainted with the cause That damned villain that hath deluded me Whom I did send guide unto my son, O that my furious force could cleave the earth, That I might muster bans of hellish fiends, To rack his heart and tear his impious soul. The ceaseless turning of celestial orbs, Kindles not greater flames in flitting air, Then passionate anguish of my raging breast, Aurelius stand up I pardon thee, I see that virtue will have enemies, And fortune willbe thwarting honour still, And you fair virgin too I am content, To accept you for my daughter since 'tis done, And see you princely used in Cestus court. Thanks good Alfonso: but I came alone, And not as did beseem the Cestian Duke, Nor would I have it known within the town, That I was here and thus without my train, But as I came alone so will I go, And leave my son to solemnise his feast, And ere't belong I'll come again to you, And do him honour as beseems the son Of mighty Ierobell the Cestian Duke, Till when I'll leave you, Farewell Aurelius.

Plot summary

Plot — The Taming of a Shrew
Forcibly ejected from an alehouse, Sly is sleeping off his drunkenness when he is found by a lord, who brings him home and tricks him into thinking himself a nobleman. A play is performed for him: The Taming of a Shrew. In the play, Aurelius of Sestos, newly arrived in Athens, falls in love with Philema; his friend Polidor already loves her sister, Emelia. To pursue his love, Aurelius pretends to be a wealthy merchant’s son and has his man Valeria assume his aristocratic identity. However, the girls’ father Alfonso has decreed that his eldest daughter Kate, a shrewish scold, must be married before them. The two wooers decide to persuade Ferando to marry Kate, but Alfonso has already promised him six thousand crowns to undertake the same task He and Kate meet unchaperoned, and they wrangle, but the marriage is arranged for the following Sunday; Kate seems outwardly unwilling but consents in an aside. Ferando goes to prepare his country house. Valeria is sent to teach Kate the lute, but she behaves aggressively. On the wedding day Ferando arrives in base clothes and forces Kate to leave with him immediately after the ceremony. When they arrive in the country, he claims that the dinner is burnt, and beats his servants. She is kept hungry, and denied her choice of clothing. Aurelius has Philotus pose as his father to persuade Alfonso that he is wealthy enough to marry Philema. He and Polidor are married to Kate’s younger sisters. Kate and Ferando visit them, after Kate submits to Ferando’s will. On the way, they meet the Duke of Sestos (who has come to Athens in disguise to spy on his son), and mock him. The Duke exposes Aurelio’s pretence and proposes to have those involved sent to prison, but Sly intervenes in the performance to prevent this. The three new husbands bet on whose wife will come when called. Ferando wins, and Kate explains to her sisters their duty as wives. Sly falls asleep during the performance. The lord puts him back where he was found. He returns home intending to tame his own wife.
