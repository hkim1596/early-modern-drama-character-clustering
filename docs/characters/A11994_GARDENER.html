Go bind thou up young dangling Aphricokes, Which Like unruly children make their sire, Stoop with oppression of their prodigal weight, Give some supportance to the bending twigs, Go thou, and Like and executioner Cut off the heads of two fast growing sprays, That look too lofty in our commonwealth, All must be even in our government. You thus employed, I will go root away The noisome weeds which without profit suck The soils fertility from wholesome flowers. Hold thy peace. He that htah suffered this disordered spring, Hath now himself met with the fallen of leaf: The weeds which his broad spreading leaves did shelter, That seemed in eating him to hold him up, Are plucked up root and all by Bullingbrook, I mean the Earl of Wiltshire, Bushy, Green, They are. And Bullingbrook hath ceased the wasteful king, O what pity is it that he had not so trimde, And dressed his land a we this garden at time of year Do wound the bark, the skin of our fruit trees, Lest being ver proud in blood, With too much riches it it self Had he done so to great and growing men, They might have lived to bear and he to taste Their fruits of duty: supe fluous branches We lop away that bearing boughs may live: Had he done so, himself had born the crown, Which waste of idle hours hath quite thrown down Depressed he is already, and deposed 'Tis doubt he will be. Letters came last night To a deer friend of the good Duke of The kes, That tell black tidings. Pardon me Madam, little joy have I To breathe this news, yet what I say is true: King Richard he is in the mighty hold Of Bullingbrook: their Fortune's both are weyde In your Lo. scale is nothing but himself, And some few vanities that make him light: But in the balance of great Bullingbrook, Beside himself are all the English peers, And with that odds he weighs King Richard down; Post you to London and you will find it so, I speak no more e than every one does know. Poor Queen, so that thy state might be no worse, I would my Skill were subject to thy cum see: Here did she fallen a tear, here in this place I'll set a bank of Rew sour herb of grace, Rew even for ruth here shortly shall be seen, In the remembrance of a weeping Queen.
