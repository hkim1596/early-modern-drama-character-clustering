What wilt thou make bequeathe of others lands: Why man he lives again. Thou must restore to him his goods again. Nay force perforce he shall restore thy own. Phillida behold, thy uncle lives: See my Lords, no care of kindred holds her, She runs to meet Orphinio, love conducteth her. Knit up that knot within with jollity, And Register record this commodity. Thanks good Sempronio for this worthy skill, To register the memory of this, Henceforth where ere this history is herd, The world shall praise thee, in whose life began, The perfect knack to know and honest man. Servio stand forth, if thy important wrongs be such, Discourse to me and to these aged peers, Thy cause of grief, and what thou dost require. Servio come hither and possess thy place, We will consider of thy injuries. This homely tale does savour of truth. A lawful plea which may not be denied, Thy suit is granted, register it down. Now let our guard beset the city round, Search every house to find out Lelio: Mean while graven Senators, bethink your selves, Of some strict law against our Sessions, That may repulse these insolent debates. Exeunt omnes Bring tortors forth, bring me a cord, Stretch me the villains limes, force him to confess. What, have I made thee the marquess of Saint Marks And gave thee charge of all the City keys, And hast thou played me such a partly, To let those caitiff boys escape my hands. Who doubts but God beholds thy treachery, And therefore the minister of God, Will punish thee and make thee to confess Whither corruption or affliction Made thee discharge the prisoners of my wrath. If you be she that set my prisoners free, I am the Judge, and sentence thee to die: Slaves strangle her, let Servio be released. Fortunio what my son, what means these bans? Servio and Phillida, your judgement is, To lose Fortunios bands: Fortunios pains is to embrace old Corodinos neck: I thank my God that hath reclaimed thee, And made thee fly the vanities of youth, Now without fear shall I encounter death, When I am sure thy wanton days are passed. But thou ungracious man, pass from my Court, And exiled to the world: Come my Fortunio let us enter in And establish this perpetual law hence forth, That but in causes merely capital, A noble man submitting of him self, And after being reconciled to God, Shall have his pardon without prejudice. Usher let those that seek for audience Enter the Court, and move the Senators. Go, since you long to die, dispatch them two, Lelio for murder censured by himself, These for assaulting my Fortunio. By breach of prison they have forfeited. Bear hence the prisoner, we dissolve the court. Lords, let not Lelios honour blear your eyes, Speak sirrah, Did Lelio kill Sempronio. My son, my silence tells me many things, By it I find the depth of each men drift, And gathering things by certain circumstance, Am better able to discern the truth, Lords take your places, and resolve on this, That Lelio by approved truth is found To be the murderer of Sempronio, His lands and goods be seized to public use, We doom him dead. Plot summary

Plot — A Knack to Know an Honest Man
Sempronio has attempted to commit adultery with Lelio’s wife, so Lelio fights and wounds him in a duel. The affray is witnessed by three shepherds, who pursue him as a murderer, leaving Sempronio dying. He is found by an old hermit, Philip, who attempts to heal his wounds. His uncle, Servio, appeals to the Duke of Venice for justice against Lelio - though, glad to inherit Sempronio’s estate, he is motivated more by malice than moral outrage. The Duke pronounces Lelio guilty of murder and sentences him to death in his absence; his estate is confiscated, but his wife and daughter are allowed to keep his house. Lelio’s father-in-law Brishio helps him to escape from Venice, but Servio sees him being winched into the ship. Brishio is arrested and banished, and his sons, Zepherius and Orphinio, are left destitute. Philip revives Sempronio, whose near-death experience has made him chaste and ascetic. Philip disguises him as an old man, and he swears not to reveal his true identity until the hermit permits it. He assumes the alias Penitent Experience, and meets Fortunio and Marchetto, who are discussing their interest in Lelio’s family and estate: Fortunio wants the love of Lelio’s daughter, Lucida, but Marchetto is more interested in the money. Sempronio tells them that both gold and beauty are evanescent, and offers to show them how to recognize an honest man; Fortunio accepts him into his service. Marchetto seizes Lelio’s property, and offers to give some of it back to Annetta if she will grant him sexual favours; Fortunio also propositions Lucida. Refused, the men plan to return that night and rape them. Sempronio notifies Zepherius and Orphinio, who intervene and wound Fortunio as he is trying to force an entry. They are imprisoned and Servio tries to have them executed for. murder; but his daughter, Phillida, is in love with Orphinio and releases them, on condition that he will marry her should he ever return to Venice. Servio is arrested and sentenced to death for letting the brothers escape; but he is pardoned after Fortunio recovers, newly penitent, and admits that the brothers were right to stop the attempted rape. Lelio and Brishio flee to Florence and Milan ‘ respectively; but the two dukes are on the point of war over a dowry dispute, and the two refugees are appointed their champions.
