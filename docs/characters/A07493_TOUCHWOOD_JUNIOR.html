My Knight with a brace of Footmen, is come and brought up his Ewe Mutton, to find a Ram at London, I must hasten it, or else pick a Famine, her Bloods mine, and that's the surest. Well Knight, that choice spoy is only kept for me. Turn not to me till thou mayest lawfully, it but whets my stomach, which is too sharp set already. Read that note carefully, keep me from suspicion still, nor know my zeal but in thy Heart: read and send but thy liking in three words, I'll be at hand to take it. How strangely busy is the Devil and riches, Poor Soul kept in too hard, her Mother's Eye, is cruel towards her, being to him, 'twere a good mirth now to set him a work to make her wedding Ring. I must about it. Rather then the gain should fallen to a Stranger, 'twas honesty in me to enrich my Father. Oh nothing now, all that I wish is present. I would have a wedding Ring made for a Gentlewoman, with all speed that may be. Of some half ounce, stand fair and comely, with the Spark of a Diamond. Sir 'twere pity to loose the least grace. So is the Mistress. Yes sure I think I have her measure about me, good faith it's down, I cannot show't you, I must pull too many things out to be certain. Let me see, long, and slender, and neatly jointed, Just such another Gentlewoman that's your Daughter Sir. I protest I never saw two Maids handed more alike I'll never seek farther, if you'll give me leave Sir. I, and I'll bide all lose Sir. Shall I make bold with your finger Gentlewoman? That fits her to a hair Sir. Mass that's true, Posy I faith eene thus Sir. Love that's wise, blinds Parents Eyes. What Sir? Pardon you? I Sir. Yes faith I will. Thy doom take hold of thee. Being so it's soon, thanks, and your leave sweet Gentlewoman Brother I have sought you strangely. With all speed thou canst procure a Licence for me. Cuds-foot she's lost else, I shall miss her ever. Thanks by hundreds. The hapiest meeting that our souls could wish for Here's the Ring ready, I am beholding unto your Father's hast, has kept his hour. Back, be silent. It vows your service Sir. Sir I obey your time. Oh Sir, if ever you felt the force of love, pity it in me. Here it's ready Sir. The Ring and all things perfect, she'll steal hither. Oh here she's come Sir. My honest Brother. Hard fate. And you cannot see with those two Glasses, put on a pair more. I think you did, you received money for't. Like enough Sir, you have taken me at the worst time for words that ever you picked out, faith do not wrong me Sir. You're in the happiest way to enrich your self, And pleasure me Brother, as Men feet can tread in, For though she be locked up, her vow is fixed only to me; Then time shall never grieve me, for by that vow, E'ne absent enjoy her, assuredly confirmed that none Else shall, which will make tedious years seem gamefull To me, In the mean space loose you no time sweet brother, You have the means to strike at this Knights Fortune's, And lay him level with his bankrout merit, Get but his Wife with Child, perch at tree top, And shake the golden fruit into her Lap, About it before she weep her self to a dry ground, And whine out all her goodness. Nay and you'll follow my suit, & save my purse too Fortune dotes on me, he's in happy case Finds such and honest friend i'the Common place. Why there thou hast it right, I look for her this Evening Brother. I will deliver you of the wonder straight Brother, By the firm secrecy, and kind assistance Of a good Wench i'the House, who made of pity, Weighing the case her own, she's led through Gutters, Strange hidden ways, which none but Love could find, Or have the Heart to venture, I expect her Where you would little think. Hope tells me so, But from your love and time my peace must grow. I love them with my Heart for't. Barne-Elmes. Go, and you honest watermen that stay, Here's a French-crown for you, There comes a Maid with all speed to take water, Row her lustily to Barne-Elmes after me. What made you stay so long? Away quick, there's a Boat waits for you, And I'll take water at Pauls-wharfe, and overtake you. Oh thy news splits me. Enough, leave me Like my Joys, Sir saw you not a wretched Maid pass this way? Heart Villain, is it thou? I must break through thee then, there is no stop That checks my Tongue, and all my hopeful Fortune's, That Breast excepted, and I must have way. Sir you'll gain the Heart in my Breast at first? Oh now it's quit Sir. No longer Slave? But one bout? I'll follow thee to death, but have't out. My joy wants utterance.
