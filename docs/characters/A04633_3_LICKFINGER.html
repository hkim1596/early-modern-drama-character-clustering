To wish them you, I confess, That ha'them already. The pox! Or I, till now? And it's but half and hour. Why? you think I can run Like lightfoot Ralph, Or keep a wheelbarrow, with a sail in town here, To whirl me to you: I have lost two stone Of suet i'the service posting hither, You might have followed me Like a watering pot, And seen the knots I made along the street; My face dropped Like the skimmer in a fritter pan, And my whole body, is yet (to say the truth) A roasted pound of butter, with grated bred in it! He sweeps his face. A shilling or two cheaper, That's your jealousy. And bating two i'the hundred. Sir, take heed What you give out. You can spare them nothing. As good as nothing. Beware those worshipful ears, Sir, be not shortened, And you play Crop i'the fleet, if you use this licence. I am Lickfinger, Your Cook. I know his gift, he can be deaf when he list. Yes Sir, I have spared them Out of the custard politic for you, the Majors. They are a little crooked. Because you do indent. Rather then meat, I know it: you are joust still. By the mouldy signs? When you & your dogs have dined. A sweet reversion. Nothing but a young Hair in white-broth, I know his diet better then the Doctor. I must needs, You see who drives me, gentlemen. News, news my boys! I am to furnish a great feast to day, And I would have what news the Office affords. Sir, for that I will not urge, but for the fire and zeal To the true cause; thus I have undertaken: With two Lay-brethren, to my self, no more, One o'the broach, th'other o'the boiler, In one six months, and by plain cookery, No magic to't, but old Jap ets physic, The father of the Europaean Arts, To make such sauces for the Savages, And cooks their meats, with those enticing steems, As it would make our Caniball-Christians, Forbear the mutual eating one another, Which they do do, more cunningly, then the wild Anthropophagi ; that snatch only strangers, Like my old Patrons dogs, there. A little of the Duke of Bauier, and then— It expected More then it understood: for, they stand mute, Poor innocent dumb things; they are but wood. As is the bench and blocks, they were wrought on, yet If Mayday come, and the Sun shine, perhaps, They'll sing Like Memnons Statue, and be vocal. I'll ha'that news. Heretic, I see Thou art for the vain Oracle of the Botle. The hogshead, Trismegistus, is thy Pegasus. Thence flows thy Muses spring, from that hard hoof: Seduced Poet, I do say to thee, A Boiler, Range, and Dresser were the Fountains, Of all the knowledge in the universe. And they are the Kitchens, where the Master-cook — (Thou dost not know the man, nor canst thou know him, Till thou hast served some years in that deep school, That's both the Nurse and Mother of the Arts, And hearest him read, interpret, and demonstrate!) A Master-cook! Why, he's the man of men, For a Professor ! he designs, he draws, He paints, he carves, he builds, he fortifies, Makes Citadels of curious fowl and fish, Some he dri-dishes, some motes round with broths. Mounts marrowbones, cuts fifty angled custards, Rears bulwark pies, and for his outerworkes He raiseth Ramparts of immortal crust ; And teaches all the Tactics, at one dinner: What Ranks, what Files, to put his dishes in; The whole Art Military. Plot summary

Plot — The Staple of News
. _ Pug the junior devil is keen to visit earth. Satan svarns him that his ideas of effective devilment are half a century out of date, but grants him leave to spend the day in London, made flesh and subject to all the frailties and limitations of the human body. A ready-made body is available, in the shape of a recently hanged cutpurse, but Pug will have to make his own arrangements for clothes. Another proviso is that he must serve the first man he meets. Unfortunately for Pug, he turns out to be a twit: Fabian Fitzdottrel. Fitzdottrel longs to see the devil, and agrees to accept Pug as an unpaid servant because he gives his mame as Devil. The same obsession makes Fitzdottrel eager to see a new play, The Devil is an Ass, but he needs some finery to show off as he sits on the stage. Wittipol is eager to sleep with Fitzdottrel’s wife, and offers him a suitable cloak in exchange for fifteen minutes’ uninterrupted conversation with the lady. He uses the time to proposition her, and engages her interest even though she has been forbidden to reply. Fitzdottrel is introduced to Merecraft, a purveyor of imaginative financial schemes, who offers to make him rich and a duke through land reclamation in the eastern fens. Fitzdottrel agrees to meet with him, leaving Pug to ensure that nobody attempts to visit his wife. Pug instead brings her a follow-up message from Wittipol, then makes advances to her on his own account, but is caught by Fitzdottrel and gets a beating. Wittipol’s friend Manly has a room which abuts Mistress Fitzdottrel’s gallery, which he uses to have a second meeting with her. This is much more intimate than the first, despite being conducted through the windows of adjacent buildings. Pug gets back into favour by tipping off Fitzdottrel, who puts a stop to the assignation and threatens to take up another woman to share his expectations and be his Duchess. Merecraft suggests that what Fitzdottrel’s wife needs is to be taught proper manners, and recommends a widow who knows the ways of the Spanish court, who might be persuaded to undertake the task if sent an expensive ring as a gift, and who is entirely fictitious. His accomplice Engine proposes hiring an actor to impersonate her, but Merecraft worries that his business methods might be leaked to a playwright and end up on stage; in the event, Engine finds a gentleman to take on the role instead, who happens to be Wittipol.
