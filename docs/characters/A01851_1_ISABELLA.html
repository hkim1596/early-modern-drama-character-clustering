It's your uncle The noble Prince, Ascanio. Herd you that groan my Lord—o he is dead; Crack then you tardy heart strings, quickly crack, And give me leave to overtake the flight Of my dead husband. Be brief Thou art a villain, and a poisoner then, Hast thou a drug for us? I do: and more, how thou could purchase this Without a gilded. Thus we learn Murder from thy Felony: But what should make me trust a confessed rogue? I must beleev't: but darest thou poison well? Commit a crime, which thou mayest glory of? Nay I care not whom. But I can give reward to a wise crime. Now should he poison Sforza. Oh found hope! That makest us think all true that we desire. Should he betray us now? for what? that we Entreated him to kill, we knew not whom. By this expression thus much I have won: I may be made, but cannot be undone. FOrtune is merry, and the heaven disposed To play with me, I am turned Favourite. Me thinks my hairs ashamed of their white hue Should blush to youth: Oh how I could look big, Take Giant strides, dote on my lovely self, And talk as fillily as any Lord. To see the prettiness of Actium, Of State employment: Sforza's to be crowned, And I must win the popular suffrages. Good Heavens! was ever such a merry load Imposed on Man: some cry the times are ill, Others could wish them better, and a third Knows how to make all well, but tells not how, And, cause that he is silent, would be wise.
