Sir, my mother prays— She is your wife, my mother, Sir. Nothing, but that you wrong her, o my conscience. O were you not my father, I would let This passion out of your impostumed heart— Why should not I forget, that your blood moves In any veins of mine; when you forgo The reason of a father, husband, man? And stick degeneration on your name? If I sail ill, know your example steered My voyage and my vessel. Father's are more Then private men: their lives are the set copies. Their children write by; and should there give Their imitation patterns how to live. Hell's a sad place, they say:—O, I'll dare never To follow my own father leading thither. Looseness Sir? O hell! She is my mother; pray you, use her well. I cannot Sir. I must obey. Thou comest upon thy death, infectious issue of the world's plague; if thy blood stained foot enter these doors. Our parents are from home. Till their return, I'll keep possession. Or loose it with my life. Right noble son of so professed a foe, Heaven be as kind to you, as you to our woe. Hold. Here take your kindness back: Though we are poor, My sister was not bred to be a whore. Forbear to touch her. Gout, dropsy, lameness, rotten legs can hasten T'vndoe the poor. Usurers that fit Bound to their chairs with charms, & cannot move But by their porters, can to ill bestir them. He needs make haste, that is at hell before them. Fit still you lethargy: you had better drop— You are my stern Sir, at your pleasure guide This tempest-beaten vessel. Sir, be not so incensed: resume your son Into your former love, and I resign All right, that his free promise hath made mine. COme, sister to my sorrows, and my self. They say, society in woes does lighten Our pressures; but I find the contrary. My woes are heavier by thy company My grief for thy distress, doubles mine own. I should be far less wretched, if alone. Little once thought thy mother, that thy fate Should stoop to service to relieve thy state: We are not tried, but in our misery. He is a cunning Coachman that can turn Well in a narrow room. To manage plenty In a right form, commends the state, not person. He's blessed, that to be rich can give consent With honesty, or rest poor with content. I wonder, Benjamin does not visit us. His last relief is done, if that spring dry, We faint for succour, and must fainting die. See, here comes Bromloy, once our father's Steward: Sure, he'll support us. Sister, cry his kindness: thy speech is more pathetical. The Statute, Judas? with are no Beggars, though We tried thy courtesy. Cursed be thy fate, Thou from our father gottest thy whole estate. Yet grudgest us some fragments. Hence, out Dog: If thou stayest miscreant— Do thy worst, devil. And insatiate worm strike deep in to thy conscience, sile thy heart strings with rubbing flets: And turn thy derogated name, On foggy blastings of eternal shame. Ingratitude is gone; and in his room, Extortion and a fiend is hith or come. Go thou accursed Cain: in misery, When thou beggest mercy, be't as far from thee. Heaven be as deaf to thee, when thy soul breath Shall beg some respite at thy violent death. This fellow sure will succour us. Now I remember, when I went to school, I read of one Vespasian a good Emperor, That told his Courtiers if a day out-slipt him, Wherein he did nor good, that day was lost. The next he would redeem't with double cost. Ill coloured sin, how shameful dost thou look, In them that plead thy warrant from their book! Thus much: a broken head. Hence, skie-consulting Gypsy: men commit Sins dark as night, and blame the stars for it. Another passenger—O this is Sager. His wife was once a servant to our mother. Plot summary

Plot — The Honest Lawyer
Richard Vaster’s lands are mortgaged to Gripe the usurer for £300. Jealously believing himself to have been cuckolded, he sells his wife Florence to the bawd and innkeeper Marmaid, claiming her to be his sister. He then challenges Benjamin Gripe, the usurer’s son, to a duel, and pretends to be killed; the remorseful Benjamin is determined to make amends by looking after Vaster’s family. Valentine sets himself up as a phoney doctor and is asked to cure Gripe’s gout, which entails driving a large nail through the affected toe. While Gripe is inconvenienced by the nail, Valentine steals his purse and keys. Gripe also sends Benjamin to foreclose on Vaster’s mortgage; when Benjamin asks him to give the bereaved family a three-month respite, he is disinherited. Surprisingly, the nail does indeed drive out the gout. Curfew has passed himself off as the deputy Abbot of Newnham after the death of the real deputy, his brother, and he uses the abbey as a base for highway robbery. His first two victims are Valentine and the disguised Vaster, who both prove as rascally as he; the three of them end up going into partnership. They proceed to rob travellers: Vaster happily relieves Gripe of his money, but lets Benjamin off with an injunction to be good to the Vaster family. Whenever they leave anyone tied up on the road, Florence comes out of the nearby inn and releases them. She is proving a 1614 disappointment to Marmaid ~ all her clients seem to end up with a new moral outlook on prostitution — so the bawd allows Benjamin, the landlord, to take her away. He lodges her at his father’s house under an assumed name, Widow Sorrow. Valentine makes an affray at the inn, and is arrested. The justice before whom he is brought happens to be Gripe, who lets him off, because he has developed another medical complaint: inability to urinate. Valentine proposes using gunpowder to blow open the urinary tract, and again the treatment is surprisingly successful. Robert and Anne, Vaster’s son and daughter, are destitute, but nobody will give them any money, not even Bromley, Vaster’s former steward who owes everything to the family. Benjamin sends Robert to see ‘Widow Sorrow’, and falls in love with Anne. Robert soon identifies the widow as his mother, but suspects that her presence in the usurer’s household confirms his father’s suspicions of her adultery.
