Fie Antonia, You play the wiredrawer with her commendations. And so long since married? You amaze me. Lights there, lights. Have you prepared Those Ladies to attend her? and procured That politic safe conveyance for the Midwife Your Duchess plotted. Make use then of this forced occasion: Give out that Bosola hath poisoned her, With these Apricots: that will give some colour For her keeping close. For that you may pretend She'll use some prepared Antidote of her own, Least the Physicians should repoyson her. How fares it with the Duchess? Speak to her all happy comfort. Do not doubt me, Believe it, It's but the shadow of your fear, no more: How superstitiously we mind our evils? The throwing down salt, or crossing of a Hare; Bleeding at nose, the stumbling of a horse: Or singing of a Criket, are of pour To daunt whole man in us: Sir, fare you well: I wish you all the joys of a blessed Father; And (for my faith) lay this unto your breast, Old friends (Like old swords) still are trusted best. I was bold to come and see you. Do you lie here? Very well: I have brought you no commendations from your husband For I know none by him. I never knew man, and beast, of a horse, and a knight, So weary of each other, if he had had a good back, He would have undertook to have born his horse, His breech was so pitifully soar. Lady, I know not whither You want money, but I have brought you some. No, from mine own allowance. Look on't, it's gold, hath it not a fine colour? Try the found on't. With good speed, I would wish you (At such time, as you are nonresident With your husband) my mistress. Very fine, Is this her wit, or honesty that speaks thus? No employment (my Lord) A marginal note in the muster-book, that he is A voluntary Lord. He ha worn gunpowder, in's hollow tooth, for the toothache. I herd so, and Was armed for't ere I came: Let us make noble use Of this great ruin; and join all our force To establish this young hopeful Gentleman In's mother's right. These wretched eminent things Leave no more fame behind they, then should one Fallen in a frost, and leave his print in snow, As soon as the sun shines, it ever melts, Both form, and matter: I have ever thought Nature does nothing so great, for great men, As when she's pleased, to make them Lords of truth: "Integrity of life, is fame's best friend, Which nobly (beyond Death) shall crown the end. Plot summary

Plot — The Duchess of Malfi
There are two new arrivals at the Duchess of Malfi’s court. One is Antonio, her steward, who has returned from an extended visit to France. The other is Bosola, an ex-convict who committed a murder for the Cardinal, and who is now placed at court by the Duchess’s brother Ferdinand, with instructions to supply information. Ferdinand and the Cardinal leave the court, enjoining their sister not to marry again, but she defies them and secretly woos and marries Antonio. She clandestinely gives birth to a son, which Bosola discovers after Antonio accidentally drops the baby’s horoscope. The news is duly reported to the infuriated Ferdinand. The Duchess has two more children. Ferdinand visits Malfi, planning to marry her to Count Malateste. He visits her private chamber and catches her talking to Antonio, whose identity remains unknown because he has hidden as part of a practical joke. She banishes Antonio as a pretext to get him out of court, and follows him on a feigned pilgrimage to the state of Ancona. Bosola wins her confidence by speaking well of Antonio, unlike the other court flatterers; she entrusts him with the secret of their marriage, which he duly reports to his paymaster. The Cardinal is commissioned to fight in the war, and ceremonially puts off his ecclesiastical office. By papal decree, the Duchess and Antonio are banished ===== PAGE 347 ===== 1613 from Ancona and the Duchy of Malfi is seized by the Holy See; Antonio flees to Milan with his eldest son, and Bosola arrests the Duchess. Acting on Ferdinand’s orders, he imprisons and torments her, shows her the dead bodies of Antonio and her son (which turn out to be waxworks), and finally supervises her and her younger children’s murder. Ferdinand disowms him, then goes mad with lycanthropic delusions. The Cardinal schemes to have Antonio’s lands confiscated, poisons his mistress, and inadvertently reveals to Bosola his part in the Duchess’s death. Seeking revenge, Bosola mistakenly kills Antonio in the dark. In his madness, Ferdinand kills the Cardinal and Bosola, who kills him in turn. Delio decides to advance the rights of Antonio’s surviving son.
