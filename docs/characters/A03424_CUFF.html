Here at hand. Good Band, do not fret so. Nay: if you be so broad with him, Band, we shall have a fray presently. It was fit time for me to stay you up, for I am sure you were a falling Band. Sfoot, if these two should go together by the cares and hurt one another, Ruff would be in a fine plighted: would he not? If they talk of lacing, I were best look about my self. Did you not hear of the great Bans went over of late? It may be so; for I am sure he is a Hol land Band. Truly, his Laundress will bear him witness thereof. Now I perceive, the Spaniard and the Hollander will to it roundly. No, I dare warrant you, there is no man more careful of the time then he: for I am sure he hath always at the least a dozen Clocks about him. Nay, you shall not partly so, you will go into the fields, and know not what fighting means: a couple of white livered fellows, your Laundress will make you both as white as a clout it she list; If you lack beating, shee'l Beatrice you I'll warrant you, shee'l so clap your sides toga there, that they shall be beaten out in once or twice would ̄ dling; why? I have known her leave her mark behind her a whole week after, sheell quickly beat you Black and Blue, for I am sure shee'll scarce wash white before she starch. Well, remember your sclues and Mi steris Stichwell, one to whom you have been both beholding in your days. No, nor you neither. I thought so, why its the Semster, one that both you had been undone had it not been for her, but what talk you of undoing? I say Mister is Stichwell the Semster was the very maker of you both, yet thus little do you re guard her, but it is the common custom of you all, when you come to be so great as you are, you forget from what house you come. Well Band and Ruff, you were best both of you to take heed of her, you know she set you both in the Stocks once before, and if she catch you again, it is a hundredth to one, if she hang you not both up, for she hath God strings already. Since you will go, go; but hear me, if you go, look at me well; as little a fellow as I am, I will come and Cuff you both out of the fields; if I do not, say, Cuff is no man of his hands. If you go, you shall never say that Cuff came of a sleeveless errand, I'll bind your hands (I warrant you) for striking. And you be so choleric, I'll even pin you both in, as soon as I come home: can you not decide the quarrel between your selves without a field? I had thought you had been a little more mild, Ruff. You were a horrible Puritane the other day, a very precise Ruff. Come, you shall be friends, Band. Nay, surely he is his Master, at least his Maker: for Bans, make rags; Rags make Paper; Paper makes Pasteboard; and Pasteboard makes Collar; and I think that this is a stiff argument that he is his Master. But you shall not fight: have you not Friends & Neighbours enough to end this con trouersie, but you must go into the fields, and there cut the thread of your lives? we'll have none of that, come choose you and Umpire, Band, for it shall be so. Ruff, you shall be content. Nay, stay there, he is a most filthy Sni veling fellow, and he will wipe your Nose of all, if you put the case to him, but what say you to Shirt? Why, then go to Master Cap, the head man of the Town. Here's a do with you, and my Lord Corpus, indeed I would you were both hanged about his neck for me, but I see, this strife will ne ver be ended, till I be Arbitrator my self, you know, I am equally allied to you both: shall I be Moderator between you? Well then, Ruff shall be most accoun ted of amongst the Clergy, for he is the graven Fellow (although I know, the Puritans will not greatly care for him, he hath such a deal of set ting, and they love standing very well.) As for you, Band, you shall be most made of amongst the young Gall ants, although sometime they shall use Ruff, for a fashion, but not otherwise; how ever, you need not regard the gid die hea dead multitude, let them do as they list, some times respecting one, sometime the other: but when you come to the Counselor, and men of Law, which know right from wrong, acknow ledging Master Worths to be equal, they shall prefer neither, but use the kindness of you both, wearing both a Band and a Ruff; how say you, are you both content? Then go before me to the next town, and I'll follow after with a Band of your friend ship drawn, which I hope, these Gentlemen will seal with their hands. Plot summary

Plot — A Merry Dialogue between Band, Cuff, and Ruff
Ruff and Band quarrel over which of them is the more modish, and make a date to fight a duel the following day. Cuff tries to keep the peace, threatening them with a beating from the laundress and hanging by the seamster, but is unable to dissuade them. They disagree about who should umpire the fight, but finally agree to appoint Cuff moderator. Cuff defuses the situation by arguing that both are the height of fashion with different people: ruffs are favoured by clerics (other than Puritans) and bands by gallants.
