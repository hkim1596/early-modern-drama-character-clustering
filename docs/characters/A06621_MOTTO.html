Thou knowest I have taught thee the knack king of the hands, the tickling on a men hairs, Like the tuning of a Cittern. Beside, I instructed thee in the phrases of our eloquent occupation, as how sir will you be trimmed? will you have your beard Like a spade, or a bodkin? a penthouse on your upper lip, or and ally on your chin? allow curl on your head Like a Bull, or dangling lock Like a spaniel? your moustaches sharp at the ends, Like shoemakers aules, or hanging down to your mouth Like Goats flakes? your lovelocks wreathed with a silken twist, or shaggy to fall on your shoulders? Well for all this I desire no more at thy hands, than to keep secret the revenge I have prepared for the Pages. Indeed thou shouldst be no blab, because a bar ber, therefore be secret. Was it not a good cure Dello, to ease the toothache, and never touch the tooth? I did but rub his gums, and presently the rheum evaporated. It may be that it is only the breeding of a beard, and being the first beard, you shall have a hard travel. As likely sir, for any thing I know, as on your chin. May I but touch them Dello, I'll teach his tonge to tell a tale, what villainy it is to cousin one of a beard, but stand not thou nigh, for it is ods when he spits, but that all his teeth fly in thy face. I, so did you overhear me, when you cose ned me of a beard: but I forget all. How now Petulus, do they still ache? Let me rub your gums with this leaf. Nothing in the world, but to let me lay a gol den beard to your chin. You are Like to fetch it out with your teeth, or go without your teeth. Guilded, nay boy, all my beard was gold. It was not gilded, I will not be so overmatched. I am content, but I will have the beard in my hand to be sure. Agreed. I am as melancholy as a cat. What a world it is to see eggs forwarder than cocks? these infants are as cunning in diseases, as I that have run them over all, backward and forward. I tell you boys, it is melancholy that now troubles me. What meanest thou Dello? Because all the Court is sad. Because the King hath a pain in his ears. It may be, for his ears are swollen very big. 'Tis a lie; and yet if I had, he might well spare and inch or two. Let me alone. As as between and asses ears and mine. Nay, I mean the Kings are asses ears. Perii, unless you pity me, Motto is in a pit. Your servants, or handmaidens. Out villain, thou wringst too hard. Thou seest boy we are both mortal. I enjoy mine ears, but durante placito; nor thou thy finger, but favente dento. Alas, he that hath drawn so many teeth, and never asked Latin for a tooth, is ill brought up. Plot summary

Plot — Midas
Offered anything he wishes by way of reward for entertaining Baéehus) KingiMidas seeks advice. Two of his lords suggest that he request love and empire respectively; the third proposes gold, which can buy both, so Midas asks that everything he touches should turn to gold. The gift proves to be an inconvenience, turning Midas’s food and even his beard to gold. (A BaBE steals the golden beard from the FSyalibarber, but the barber gets his own back by giving him toothache.) Regretting his folly and his imperialistic designs on other nations, Midas seeks a remedy from the oracle, and washes off the golden touch in a nearby river. On the way home, Midas goes hunting in the woods, gets lost, and encounters Ajpoll6 and Pan ‘The two gods are in dispute over which is the best musician; Midas is asked to judge, and, perversely, chooses Pan. Apollo gives him ass’s ears in reprisal, and he becomes reclusive while rumours spread; even the reeds whisper about his ears. Meanwhile his empire begins to disintegrate and he is unable to achieve his overriding objective, the conquest of Lesbos. Finally he seeks a remedy from the oracle of Apollo, and also proclaims that any further talk about his ass’s ears will cost the speaker his own. The pages inveigle Motto the barber into flouting the edict, and thereby force him to return the golden beard. Midas makes his obeisance at Apollo’s oracle, and is ordered to give up his imperial ambitions; in particular, his ears will only get longer if he continues to covet Lesbos. He agrees, and promises to make annual sacrifices at the oracle. The ass’s ears fall off, and the play ends with praise for Apollo.
