Eye spread thy snares To catch poor innocent Maids: and having taken them In the Like pitfall, with their shipwracked honours, Make seizure of their lives. I'll call him back: if Stroza be no villain, He is not worth my clamour. What was that startled Within me? O I am dishonoured Perpetually; for he hath left behind That pledge of his acquaintance, that will for ever Cleave to my blood in scandal, I must now Sue, send, and crave, and what before I scorned By prayers to grant, submissively implore. Haste Stroza, unto the Prince his chamber, Give him this letter, it concerns my honour, My state, my life, all that I can call good Depends upon the safe delivery Of these few broken Letters. What stays she to outface me? Speak, will he come? It is well: If prayers or tears can move him, I'll make way To save my own shame, and enforce his stay. That hand in Contract you so long have had, Should not seem strange to you now. O Sir, you come to know whither Tobacco be good for you or no; I'll tell you, if you never took it, never take it then, or it you ever used it, take it still; Nay, I'm and excel lent Philistian grown of late I tell you. Love me thou dost not. I say thou dost not: lay no wager with me, For if thou dost, there will be two to one On my side against thee. Do show your self a Prince: let her no longer Live, that hath thus disgraced your Royal Blood. Strike, I'll stand. You are not true to your own honour Father. To let me longer live. It is my Lord. Durst I presume my Lord, to know Whither you have send my son? Whom but Parma's Prince. I have done. Since I have lost the name of Child, I am a servant now and must obey. My blushes must speak for me. And yet but what's your own Sir, I am serious, And it will ill become your Oaths and Vows To jest at my undoing. In doing thus, you should undo me quite. Her father the Duke learns of the situation, and sends Stroza to fetch Parma back, dead or alive. Julia’s unborn child saves her from execution for her misdeed, and she goes into hiding until the baby is born. When it is, the Duke orders Stroza to dispose of it, and plans to conceal her shame with a hasty marriage to another foreign prince. Stroza leaves the baby in a grove to be found by passers-by; sure enough, when he returns a few hours later, it has gone, found by Parma and recognized as his own true issue. Lauretta and her mother are found in the forest by the Prince of Florence, and installed by him in a ducal hunting lodge. She falls in love with him, but, knowing herself too low-born, gives cryptic answers to his questions about her love-life. Stroza arrives in Florence as an ambassador to negotiate a marriage between the Prince and Julia. The Duke of Florence accepts, and the Prince is obliged to consent. When he tells Lauretta about it, she initially assumes herself to be the bride, and continues her cryptic answers, telling him that she too will be marrying a prince at the exact same time; on learning he is to wed Julia, she faints. The Prince receives a letter from Parma warning him that Julia is unchaste, and he interrupts the wedding to raise the matter; only on receiving assurances of the bride’s virginity does he allow the ceremony to be completed.
