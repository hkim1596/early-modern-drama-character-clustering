Full speech

O, sweet Doctor Almanac, I have lost a deer old Master, beside Sir, I have been out of service, all this Kersmas; Nobody minds Fasting day, I have scarce been thought upon of Friday nights; And because Kersmas this year fell vpon't, The Fridays have been ever since so proud They scorn my company, the Butchers boys At Templebar, set their great Dogs upon me, I dare not walk abroad, nor be seen yet, The very Poulters Girls throw rotten Eggs at me, Nay Fishstreet loves me, even but from ̄ teeth outward, (The nearest Kin I have) looks shy upon me, As if t'ad forgotten me, I met Plumporridge now, My big-swollen Enemy, he's plump and lusty, The only man in place, sweet Master Doctor, Prefer me to the New-Yeere, you can doo't. Till Lent, you kill my heart, sweet M. Doctor, Thrust me into Candlemas Eve, I do beseech you. Why th'are fat enough. I, he's sure of welcome; me thinks he moves Like one of the great Porridge Tubs, going to the Counter. Why thou whoreson Breakfast, Dinner, Nuntions, Supper and Beaver, Cellar, Hall, Kitchen, and Wet-larder. Would he were gone once, I should be more respected. A Fasting-day? I served him honestly, and cost him little. Those were the Times, sir, That made your Predecessors rich, and able To lay up more for you, and since poor Fasting-dais Were not made reckoning on, the pampered flesh Has played the knave, Maids have had fuller bellies, Those meals that once were saved, have stirred, & leapt, And bègot Bastards, and they must be kept, Better keep Fasting-dais, your self may tell you, And for the profit of purse, back and belly? Well, better late then never. This is my comfort, I shall come to make All the Fat Rogues go to bed supperless, Get dinners where they can.

Plot summary

Plot — The Inner-Temple Masque, or The Masque of Heroes
The Old Year is dead. After attending his funeral, Dr Almanac examines Christmas’s water, and finds that he too is dying. The Old Year’s servant, a Fasting Day, looks for a new employer, and gets Candlemas Eve. The New Year seeks the doctor’s advice for his health. A set of festival days come in an antimasque, seeking their places in the new year; so do the good, bad, and indifferent days. Harmony arrives and sings to New Year about the relationship between virtue and prosperity. Heroes, deified for their virtues, appear and dance.
