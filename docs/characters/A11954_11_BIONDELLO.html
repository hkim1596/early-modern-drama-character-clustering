Where have I been? Nay how now, where are you? Master, ha my fellow Tranio stolen your clothes, or you stolen his, or both? Pray what's the news? I sir, never a whit. The better for him, would I were so too. He that ha the two fair daughters: is't he you mean? Master, master, news, and such news as you never herd of, Why, is it not news to herd of Petruchio is coming? Why no sir. He is coming. When he stands where I am, and sees you there. Why Petruchio is coming, in a new hat and and old jerkin, a pair of old breeches thrice turned; a pair of boots that have been candle-cases, one buck led, another laced: and old rusty sword taken out of the Town Armoury, with a broken hilt, and chapelesse: with two broken points: his horse hip'd with and old more thy saddle, and stirrups of no kindred: beside possessed with the glanders, and Like to mose in the chine, trou bled with the Lampasse, infected with the fashions, full of Windegalls, sped with Spavins, rayed with the Yel lowes, passed cure of the Fives, stark spoiled with the Staggers, begnawne with the Bots, Waid in the back, and shoulder-shotten, near leg'd before, and with a halfe-chekt Bit, & a headstall of sheeps leather, which being restrained to keep him from stumbling, hath been often burst, and now repaired with knots: one girth six times pieced, and a woman's Crupper of velure, which hath two letters for her name, fairly set down in studs, and here and there pieced with packthread. O sir, his Lackey, for all the world Capari soned Like the horse: with a linen stock on one leg, and a kersey boothose on the other, gartered with a read and blue lift; and old hat, & the humour of forty fancies pricked in't for a feather: a monster, a very monster in apparel, & not Like a Christian footboy, or a gentleman's Lackey. Why sir, he comes not. Who, that Petruchio came? No sir, I say his horse comes with him on his back. Nay by S. Iamy, I hold you a penny, a horse and a man is more then one, and yet not many.
