Full speech

If you love us my lord, hate Gaveston. Wiltshire hath men enough to save our heads. How now, why droops the earl of Lancaster? We may not, nor we will not suffer this. Does no man take exceptions at the slave? Is it not strange, that he is thus bewitched? What man of noble birth can brook this sighed? Quam male conveniunt: See what a scornful look the peasant casts. Lay hands on that traitor Gaveston. We are no traitors, therefore threaten not. Urge him my lord. Be it or no, he shall not linger here. Why? he is gone. Plead for him he that will, I am resolved. Not I against my nephew. But nephew, do not play the sophister. But how if he do not Nephew? And I. In this your grace hath highly honoured me, For with my nature war does best agree. Nephew, I must to Scotland, thou stayest here, Leave now to oppose thy self against the king, Thou seest by nature he is mild and calm, And seeing his mind so dotes on Gaveston, Let him without controlment have his will, The mightiest kings have had their minions, Great Alexander loud Ephestion, The conquering Hector, for Hilas wept, And for Patroclus stern Achillis drooped, And not kings only, but the wisest men, The Roman Tully loved Octavis, Grave Socrates, wild Alcibiades: Then let his grace, whose youth is flexible, And promises as much as we can wish, Freely enjoy that vain lightheaded earl, For riper years will wean him from such toys. But nephew, now you see the king is changed. And with the earl of Kenned that favors him. And Earl!
