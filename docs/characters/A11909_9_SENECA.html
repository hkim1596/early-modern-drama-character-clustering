Gainst lineage nought should rashly pointed be. A sovereign salve for fear is for to bear Your self debonair to your subjects all. A worthier virtue 'tis in countries sire, His people to defend with sword and fire. May, rather frolic youthful blood appears, To have more need of counsel wise and graven That heavenly powers your doings may allow. So much the more because so much you may. Give slender trust to Fortune's flattering face: She topsy turuy turns her wheel apace. A Princes praise I counted have always, To do that same which with his honour stood, Not that which frantic fancy counteth good. And whom they hate, with force they overquell. But faith more sure defence does seem to me. More meet of subjects for to be beloved What so by force of arms you do wring out, A grievous work it is to bring about. Will nothing then, but that which well you may. What peoples voice does jointly bind or loose. Let that confirmed stand. God Shield, and far that fact from you remove. It is (Oh Prince) a worthy famous thing, Amids redoubted Lords alone to ring: And wisely work your countries praise to save: And well your self to captive folk behave: From cruel brutish slaughter to abstain, And void of mood to wreak your angry pain: And to the world a quiet calm to give, That all your age in peace their lives may live. This is a Princes praise without all crime: This is the path to heaven whereby we climb. So is Augustus prince and father called Of country first in starbright throne ystald. Whom as a God in ministers we adorn, Yet troublous fortune tossed him before, A great while long on lands and ruffling seas, Until his father's foes he could appease, And brought wars diverse course could quel them quite. To you did fortune yield her power, and might, And reins of rule without all blood, and fight. And to your beck both land, and seas hath bent. Grim deadly envy daunted does relent. The Senate Lords gave place with free consent: The battailous rout of knights with willing hearts (That same decree from sager sire's departs) Unto the lay men's choice do well agree. Your grace the spring of peace they count to be. And chosen Judge, and guide of mortal stock. Your grace, your countries' sacred sire, does rock And rule with princely gorgeous title bright, The circled world in rundel wise ydight. Which mighty mounting name to keep so great, This noble city Room does you entreat: And does commend unto your royal grace Her lively limbs in charge for your lives pace. Your noble spouse, sprung forth of sainted peer Of Claudius stock, the starbright diamond clear, That Goddess Juno wise her brother's bed Partaking, pressed down with buttocks read, Your graces princely court shall garnish gay, With wondrous heavenly fair descended stay. In tender budding years, when love suppressed With blushing hides the flames of burning breast, Scant plain appears the love they bore indeed. Faith, meekness, manners mild, & bashful shame Of spouse, those ought and husband to reclaim. The pearls of judging mind, alone remain, Not subject once to any rulers reign. The passing pride of beauties numbing grace Each day appals, and bleamisheth apace. Oh noble prince such blind unlawful love, (Do rashly credit nought) from you remove. Swift winged love, men's fancy found, in vain A mercy wanting God to be, does fain: And arms his hands with wounding weapons keen And bows with burning brondes, for lovers green: Of Venus to be sprung they all accord, and blindly forged of thunders limping Lord. Bland love the minds great torment soar appears, And buddeth first in frolic youthful years. Who while we drink of Fortune's pleasant cup, With laysie pampering riot, is nes led up: Whom if to toster up you leave at length It fleeting, falls away with broken strength. This is in all our life (as I suppose) The greatest cause how pleasure first arose. Which sith mankind by brooding bideth aye, Through gladsome love y fierce wild beasts does sway It never can from manly breast depart. The peoples grief might never yield to it: Nor for we can the same at all permit. No trifling toys the people looks to have Of him, that ought to rule with wisdom graven. Seek rather for to please and calm their mood. When nought that they require they can obtain, They justly then aggrieved are again. And hard thing 'tis the people not to have That of their Prince, which they do justly crave. Let not your subjects then so soar be reigned. That no man trusts that is of credit light. With country peers to meddle it is afraid, Your grace may easily couch that budding bruit Let Sainted sire's deserts with pliant suit, Your graces mind remove: let spouses age, And courteous bashful shame disrumpe your rage.
