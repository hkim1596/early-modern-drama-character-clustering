I, and she knows so well Her own deserts, that (when I strive enjoy them) She weighs the things I do, with what she merits: And (seeing my worth outweighed so in her graces) She is so solemn, so precise, so froward, That no observance I can do to her, Can make her kind to me: if she find fault, I mend that fault; and then she says, I faulted, That I did mend it. I will strive faith, To be more strange and careless: yet, I hope I have now taken such a perfect course, To make her kind to me, and live contented, That I shall find my kindness well returned, And have no needs to fight with my affections. She (late) hath found much fault with every room Within my house; one was too big (she said) Another was not furnished to her mind, And so through all: all which, now, I have altered. Then here, she hath a place (on my backside) Wherein she loves to walk; and that (she said) Had some ill smells about it.
