Such laughter may have little mirth in it, And I shall have more comfort in a tear. Herd you the general whispering The Duke is ill.— Yes: and, they say, he has strange fits. But stay, my Picinino, who are here? I, thats the man. But why is this employment? why these Arms? When all but Naples are our friends, and they Not able now to show themselves our foes, Engaged, and almost lost in the French war. WHat will become of this declining state? Can we believe that the yet patient heaven Will any longer suffer? and not give Destruction as notorious as our crimes. Awake, stern Justice, and unsheath thy sword, The Scabbard will not heal us, but the edge, Nor is't enough to brandish, but to strike: Let then thy terror give us innocence, That mildness may no longer injure man. You are a worthy Advocate, and here Comes your great Patron: go and ask your ee: Yes, do good Aeolu —how he blows them hence! How clears his passage with a lusty frown! And yet it may be that despised wretch Worn out of clothes, and flesh, whom his high scorn Would not vouchsafe once more to look upon, Durst in the field do more, then he durst see, Then he would there vouchsafe to look upon. I fear our Climacterical is now: When all professions turn to soldier, To that eurst Art that thrives by Destiny. The sithes are straighted into swords, and earth Being not wounded is undone, where once Stood buildings, which and humble Poetry Without too bold a swelling might give Kings: Whole Mines undone to beautify one roof, Now only Desolation dwells: weak grief, To say Corn grows, where once a City stood, That sustenance is there where no men are, This is a trifling, and halfe-misery: Our Lands now only furnish us with Graves, Can hide us, but not feed us; we would think Our Cities standing, though the buildings fell, If we had no grief, but Fertility. I'm lost in this confusion: one reports We have lost all; another instantly Kill all the French at once: thus every street Is filled with w worser king people; some cry Arm, Others run crying, to persuade a slight: All have and earnest bus esse in the stir, But in the help not any. How quickly we learn misery? no ghost Would have so courteously relinquished Hell To teach us happiness: if a kind star Had cast a Fortune on us beyond wish, We might expect the story from the star Assoon as the deer benefit: but when grief That against which we would seal up our ears, When that is meant us, we shall surely hear, Though heaven do strain for a new Miracle, So to amaze us to a certainty: Though rotten carcases regain a voice, And hell is bounteous of intelligence, To give us tears. Are you to seek for the collection? Why, has not Sforza made himself our Sun? Are not his favours our refreshing showers? Tell me, good Picinino, does the Sun Spend all his rays upon one Continent? Or have you ever seen the partial Heavens Upon one A e lavish all her showers, While the rest moulder with dry barrenness? It's the use, The popular folly to admire events, And those low souls think that the sword is joust, Proportioning the reason by the end Of the chief acts, of the best enterprise, And so by folly run into a crime.

Plot summary

Plot — Lodovic Sforza, Duke of Milan
Sforza is ambitious to rule Milan even though primogeniture has passed him by, leaving him as merely the Protector to his nephew, the Duke Galeazzo. He invites the King of France, who covets the throne of Naples, to invade Italy to press his claim by force, relying on French support for his own usurpation of Milan. He then poisons Galeazzo, and attempts to dispose of the two poisoners; but one of them, Vitellio, survives and plans revenge using forged documents which implicate Sforza in the crime. After the coronation, rumours of foul play begin to circulate. Vittellio is caught lurking around Sforza’s bedchamber, intending to murder him, so Sforza has him strangled. Caiazzo plans to murder Isabella, Galeazzo’s widow, as she visits his tomb, but realizes that he could do better for himself by marrying her. She refuses him, but he promises her revenge on Sforza as a mark of his good faith, When she remains resolute, he threatens to ===== PAGE 305 ===== 1628 rape her, but is prevented by Sforza’s arrival. He claims she tried to persuade him to assassinate Sforza, but the Duke takes no reprisals. Sforza is visited by Galeazzo’s ghost, who foretells that he will fall from power and die in prison, Meanwhile, the French take Naples with ease, frustrating Sforza’s exit strategy: he was expecting them to be tied down in a long war, giving him time to co-ordinate Italian forces to expel them. The French King is taken ill at a tennis match and dies. The deposed Neapolitan ruler retakes his throne, so the French invade Italy again. When they reach Milan, Caiazzo defects with his soldiers, the senators surrender the city, and Isabella hands herself over, only to have her baby son taken from her and sent to be brought up in France. Sforza strengthens his army with Swiss mercenaries, but their captain is persuaded to transfer allegiance to the French; he offers to help Sforza escape disguised as a common soldier, but they are betrayed. The French conquer Milan, and, as the ghost foretold, Sforza spends the rest of his life in captivity.
