Full speech

No marry Sir-An Albanois, I pray be apert and plain. Propone it Sir, I'll solve it as I can. Umh: ya're full of Ambage: I answer as my spirits lead me, thus, I would not do it. Because I hold, Quod non omninò Licet. Sir I see to'th bottom, Of this deep well you dive in: I do arm you, In this strong fight, joust with the self same weapons Which I would wear to guard me, and those are My readings and belief settled by reading, And this I find- Quod non sunt fancy ̄ Dam mala, ut we jant For good; (how great soever) must be done, no ill how small soever. All good must not be done, but only that- Quod benè & legitimè fieri potest: For Sir I know, that Deus magis amat adverbia quam nomina. Quia in actionibus magis et Placent Benè & legitimè quam bonum. Ita ut nullum bonum Liceat sacere, nisi been & legi ime fieri potest. Quod in hoc Casum fieri non potest. Those learned men perhaps may hold it fit, That to save many, they to one men danger, (Referring all to the depth inscrutable) May allow of a particular; on no warrant That they can show me written, but being stirred, With a human compassion to men's lives: And less you revelation have divine, That bids you do, do not; Thus you have mine.
