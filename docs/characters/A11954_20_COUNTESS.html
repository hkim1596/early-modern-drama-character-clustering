Full speech

Porter, remember what I gave in charge, And when you have done so, bring the Keys to me. The Plot is laid, if all things fallen out right, I shall as famous be by this exploit, As Scythian Tomyris by Cyrus death. Great is the rumour of this dreadful Knight, And his achievements of no less account: Fain would mine eyes be witness with mine ears, To give their censure of these rare reports. And he is welcome: what? is this the man? Is this the Scourge of France? Is this the Talbot, so much feared abroad? That with his Name the Mother's still their Babes? I see Report is fabulous and false. I thought I should have seen some Hercules, A second Hector, for his grim aspect, And large proportion of his strong knit Limbs. Alas, this is a Child, a silly Dwarf: It cannot be, this weak and writhled shrimpe Should strike such terror to his Enemies. What means he now? Go ask him, whither he goes? If thou be he, then art thou Prisoner. To me, bloodthirsty Lord: And for that cause I trained thee to my House. Long time thy shadow hath been thrall to me, For in my Gallery thy Picture hangs: But now the substance shall endure the Like, And I will chain these Legs and Arms of thine, That hast by Tyranny these many years Wasted our Country, slain our Citizens, And sent our Sons and Husbands captivate. Laughest thou Wretch? Thy mirth shall turn to moan. Why? art not thou the man? Then have I substance too. This is a Riddling Merchant for the nonce, He will be here, and yet he is not here: How can these contrarieties agree? Victorious Talbot, pardon my abuse, I find thou art no less then Fame hath bruited, And more then may be gathered by thy shape. Let my presumption not provoke thy wrath, For I am sorry, that with reverence I did not entertain thee as thou art. With all my heart, and think me honoured, To feast so great a Warrior in my House.

Plot summary

Plot — 1 Henry the Sixth
King Henry Vis dead, and England's French lands are lost. After a military setback, the French accept the offered aid of Joan Pucelle , who claims supernatural powers from the Virgin Mary; but they also release Talbot in an exchange of prisoners. With Joan 's help the French recapture the occupied town of Orleans, but the English take it back in a night attack led by Talbot. The Countess of Auvergne invites Talbot to visit her. intending treachery, but he second-guesses her and brings soldiers with him. In England, feuds brew between Gloucester and Winchester, and between Richard Plantagenet and the Duke of Somerset, whose supporters show their allegiance by picking different coloured roses. Richard , who has been deprived of his titles through the attainder of his father, learns from the imprisoned and dying Mortimer that he has a claim to the throne . King Henry restores him to his inheritance, creating him Duke of York. Joan takes Rouen by a ruse , but Talbot recaptures it the same day; she then persuades England's ally Burgundy to join the French. After King Henry's coronation in Paris, York is left in command of Prance, but personal tension between him and Somerset means that troop reinforcements arrive late; in consequence, Talbot is defeated, surviving only long enough to learn that his son has perished in battle. Joan conjures devils, but they will not help her: she is captured by York and burned at the stake. The French and English agree a peace which will entail King Henry making a diplomatic marriage, but this is pre-empted when Suffolk captures Margaret and (having fallen in love with her but being alr eady married) plans to install her as Queen of England . Henry agrees to marry her, and Suffolk goes to France to mili the arrangements.
