What art thou, that darest bar me of my way? See in how short a while a quiet Soul, Hid from this world five hundred years and more, May be forgetful of great Times control, By such gay sights as never I saw before. My self year while could tell this worthy Lord, Time had revived me, to attend this day: Pardon me then, that I durst breath a word In contestation, where all ought obey. needs must these gaudier days yield greater crime, When long graned Ghosts dare thus contend with Time. Time, that in this days honour raised us both, Means not (I trust) so soon to sunder us: To see that separation, I am loath, Be then to both so kind and gracious; That we may wait upon this worthy man: And do him yet what service else we can. THus (honoured Lord) have we dutiously at tended you, till Time appoints our departing, who hath tutourd Leofstanes tongue how to take his leave, with some remarkable observations, not altogether unfitting your attention. First, the day of your Election, falling out in such strange manner as it did, exceeded the memory of Man to speak the Like. And yet, notwithstanding, so great a Snow, Fleet, and rough wind; at the we ry instant of your choice, the Sun did as readily thrust forth his Golden beams, to guild the in stant of your Inauguration, as hearts and hands did cheerfully applaud it with free and full confirma cion. Next, three Names, all of equal syllables and found, to happen in the immediate choice; is a matter deserving regard, and (from the Maioral ties first beginning) never was the Like. Pemberton, Swynnerton and Middleton, Names of three most worthy Gentlemen, but of much greater worth in sense and significancy, as your own (my Lord, for brevity) may yield and instance. Pemberton derives it self from the ancient British, Saxon, and eldest English, each Sillable suited with his apt meaning. Pem, implieth the head, chief or most eminent partly of any thing; Bert, bears the Character of bright shining and radiant splen dour; and Tun, hath continued the long known Word for any Town or City, as most Shires in England (to this day) doth deliver the expression of their Towns; As London, sometime tear med Ludstun, or Luds-Towne, may serve as and ex ample. A bright head of this famous City, in terpreting it self in the Name of Pemberton, and he being enstaulled in the government, to be her bright-shining Head under his Sovereign, Leof stanes desire is; Since Pemberton does bear so bright a Name, And that from gold & Goldsmiths grew his fame, His deeds may prove to be Like burnished Gold: By no dim darkness any way controulde. This Orferie, so styled by the ancient Epithet of your Companies profession, or Gold-Smi thery, does speak it self unto your honour in this manner. Here do the Miners and Pioneers delve into the Earth's entrails, to gain the Oar, both of Gold and Silver; and having attained thereun to in plentiful measure, the ingenious Fyner pur geth him from his first Nature, and shapes him in to to Ingots of variable form, according as his Art best guides and enstructs him. From him, these pre cious Metals descend to diverse other dexterious Artezans; as the Mint- Master, his Coiners, and diverse others, who make them to serve in public passage for general benefit, both in Coin, Plate, and Jewels, as occasion best discovereth the joust necessity. Being brought into these variant sub stances, to preserve those pure refined bodies from base adulterating, the Essay- Master or absolute Tryer of eithers virtue, makes proof of them in his Furnaces, and of their true worth or valve. So are they commended to Sovereign Justice, atten dead by her gracious Daughters, Gold and Silver, who bountifully hurl abroad their Mother's trea sures, after she hath (yet once again) tried them, by the Touch of undeceivable perfection. The rest, that do sufficiently speak themselves in their distinguished places, as the Jeweller, Lapidary, Pearle-Driller, Goldsmith, and such Like, your eye of heedful observation may spare their fur there relating. Only I may not omit these two for tuous Ladies, Antiquity and Memory, who, present (in apt place) Grave Judgement or Experience in the Golde-Smiths ancient faculty, by the suppo said shape of venerable Dunstan, a man, sometime very practic, & so well skilled in Gold-Smithery, as (notwithstanding his more Divine profession) being Bishop of Worcester, London, and lastly Archbishop of Canterbury, delighted therein to his ut most date of life.

Plot — Chruso-Thriambos: The Triumphs of Gold
[The first pageant is lost. The King and Queen of India have come to England, bringing with them ingots of Indian gold, to see the country’s beauty and to witness the ceremonial of the new Lord Mayor's day. What they actually encounter is a series of sea-fights and skirmishes en route to and from the Lord Mayor's oath-taking at Westrninster.} When the Lord Mayor returns to dry land, he meets Leofstan, the first Provost of London and a fellow goldsmith, who leads him to a tomb. There the procession is halted by Time, who introduces the two kings who secured London’s civic autonomy: Richard I, who instituted the office of Lord Mayor, and King John, who made it an electable office rather than a royal appointment. Time summons another former goldsmith Lord Mayor, Nicholas Faringdon, out of his tomb. At first, Faringdon is peeved to be raised from the dead, until he realizes the occasion; Time suggests that he should take pride in the achievements of his livery company, since not only the new Lord Mayor but also one of the new sheriffs are members. Time drives Faringdon and the kings in a triumphal chariot to Lawrence Lane, where Leofstan presents the principal pageant, an orphrey representing the mining and refining of gold, with Gold and Silver on top, chained to the throne of their mother, Justice; all three throw coins to the crowd. Time remarks on the traditional friendship between the Goldsmiths’ and Fishmongers’ companies, but emphasizes that the pageantry has been paid for entirely by the Goldsmiths. That evening, the speakers take their leave of the Lord Mayor at his house. Leoftsan explains the etymology of his name, Pemberton, and Time declares that he is expected to rule virtuously. Faringdon wishes him a year in office noted for justice, charity, and the absence.of corruption.
