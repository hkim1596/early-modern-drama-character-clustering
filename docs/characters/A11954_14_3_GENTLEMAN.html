Full speech

Most true, if ever Truth were pregnant by Circumstance: That which you hear, you'll swear you see, there is such unity in the proofs. The Mantle of Queen Hermiones: her Jewel about the Neck of it: the Letters of Antigonus found with it, which they know to be his Character: the Majesty of the Creature, in re semblance of the Mother: the Affection of Nobleness, which Nature shows above her Breeding, and many oh there Evidences, proclaim her, with all certainty, to be the Kings Daughter. Did you see the meeting of the two Kings? Then have you lost a Sighed which was to be seen, cannot be spoken of. There might you have be held one Joy crown another, so and in such manner, that it seemed Sorrow wept to take leave of them: for their Joy waded in tears. There was casting up of Eyes, hol ding up of Hands, with Countenance of such distraction, that they were to be known by Garment, not by Favour. Our King being ready to leap out of himself, for joy of his found Daughter; as if that Joy were now become a Loss, cries, O, thy Mother, thy Mother: then asks Bohemia forgiveness, then embraces his Sonne-in-Law: then again worries he his Daughter, with clipping her. Now he thanks the old Shepherd (which stands by, Like a Weather-bitten Conduit, of many Kings Reigns.) I never herd of such another Encounter; which lames Re port to follow it, and undo's description to do it. Like and old Tale still, which will have matter to rehearse, though Credit be asleep, and not and ear oh pen; he was torn to pieces with a Bear: This avouches the Shepherds Son; who ha not only his Innocence (which seems much) to justify him, but a Handkerchief and Rings of his that Paulina knows. Wracked the same instant of their Master's death, and in the view of the Shepherd: so that all the Instruments which aided to expose the Child, were even then loft, when it was found. But o the Noble Combat, that betwixt Joy and Sorrow was fought in Paulina. She had one Eye declined for the lose of her Husband, anon there elevated, that the Oracle was fulfilled: She lifted the Princess from the Earth, and so locks her in embracing, as if she would pin her to her heart, that she might no more be in danger of losing. One of the prettiest touches of all, and that which angled for mine Eyes (caught the Water, though not the Fish) was, when at the Relation of the Queens death (with the manner how she came to't bravely con fessed, and lamented by the King) how attentiveness wounded his Daughter, till (from one sign of dolour to another) she did (with and Alas ) I would feign say, bleed Tears; for I am sure, my heart wept blood. Who was most Marble, there changed colour: some swownded, all sorrowed: if all the World could have seen't, the Woe had been universal. No: The Princess hearing of her Mother's Statue (which is in the keeping of Paulina ) a Piece many years in doing, and now newly performed, by that rare Italian Master, Julio Romaeno, who (had he himself Eater nitie, and could put Breath into his Work) would be guile Nature of her Custom, so perfectly he is her Ape: He so near to Hermione, hath done Hermione, that they say one would speak to her, and stand in hope of answer. Thither (with all greediness of affection) are they gone, and there they intend to Sup.

Plot summary

Plot — The Winter's Tale
Polixenes of Bohemia is coming to the end of a nine-month stay at the Sicilian court of Leontes, his childhood playmate. Leontes cannot persuade him to extend his stay, but the pregnant queen, Hermione, has more success. Leontes conceives a suspicion that Polixenes and Hermione have committed adultery, and that her unborn child is a bastard. He commands Camillo to poison Polixenes, but instead Camillo warns the intended victim and escapes with him to Bohemia. Leontes arrests Hermione and sends to the oracle of Apollo for confirmation of her adultery. She gives birth to a daughter in prison, and Paulina hopes to bring Leontes to his senses by showing him the baby; instead, he orders her husband, Antigonus, to take it out of his dominions and abandon it. Hermione is tried for adultery and treason. The oracle, when read, exonerates her and foretells that, unless the lost be found, Leontes will have no heir. Leontes refuses to believe it, whereupon his son Mamillius, who fell sick when his mother was arrested, dies. Hermione collapses in court and is later reported dead. Antigonus abandons the baby on the coast of Bohemia, and is eaten by a bear. The child, Perdita, is found and adopted by an old shepherd. Sixteen years pass. The penitent Leontes, still mourning Hermione, attempts to recall Camillo to Sicily, but Polixenes will not let him go. Worried by rumours that Prince Florizel is courting a shepherd’s daughter, Polixenes attends the sheep-shearing feast in disguise. When it becomes clear that Florizel intends to marry Perdita without consulting his father, Polixenes intervenes and threatens awful reprisals. Camillo helps Florizel and Perdita to abscond to Sicily, knowing that Polixenes will follow them, enabling him to return home at last. Leontes welcomes the newcomers, who include not only the Bohemian royal family but also the old shepherd, who produces the objects found with baby Perdita; these establish her identity as Leontes’ long-lost daughter. The two kings are reconciled, and Polixenes withdraws his objection to his son’s marriage. The court visits a newly sculpted statue of Hermione in Paulina’s chapel. It turns out to be Hermione herself, still alive and awaiting the return of her daughter.
