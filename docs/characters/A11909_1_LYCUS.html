Oh Queen that name renowned hie And title takste of regal stock full gentle and easily A little while receive and hear my words with patient ear, If always men eternal hates should one to the other bear, And rage be gone out of the heart should never fallen away, But happy still should armour hold, unhappy still obey, Then shall the battles nothing leave: with wide fields then the land Shall lie vntild, with underlaid to housen fiery brand Then ashes deep shall overwhelm the buried people all. Expedient is to conqueror to wish that peace befall: To conquered needful partner of the kingdom come to me: Let's toyne our minds, take here this pledge of say and truth to thee. My right hand touch. Why whishtest thou with cruel face and mood? God to, these fierce and furious words thou woman mad refrain, And imperyes of princes learn of Hercles to sustain. Though I the sceptres got by the force of war do bear, In conquering hand & all do rule without the law his fear. Which arms subdue, a few words yet to thee now speak I shall For this my cause thy father did in bloody battle fallen: Thy brothers fell, the weapons keep no measurable stay. For neither easily tempered be, nor yet repressed may The drawn swords ire, the battles does the blood delight out shed. But he yet for his kingdom fought, we altogether led With wicked lust: yet th'end of war is now complained, lo, And not the cause, but now let all remembrance thereof go: When conqueror hath weapons left, the conquereds partly should be To leave his hates. Not I that thou with lowly bent knee Me reigning worship shouldst, require: even this does me delight. That thou thy miseries dost bear with mind so stout upright. Thou for a king a spouse art meet, let's join our beds anon. Because that stubbornly thou dost refuse my wedding so, And fear'ste a king, thoushatt know what the Sceptres now may do. Embrace thine altars, yet no God shall ever take away Thee from my hands: no not although with world upturned, may Alcides victor yet again to Gods above return. The woods on heaps together cast, let all their temples burn Even thrown upon their heads: his wife, and all his flock at last With underlaid fire, let one wood pile consume and waste. Does then thy husband droun'de in hell give thee this stomach hic? The heavy paise oppresses him of all the earth full great. Thou shalt be forced. Speak, what may rather I prepare then wedding new for thee. More royal gift? Thou shalt mad woman die. More then my Sceptors is to thee a servant loved so? Why does he yet a king then serve, and still sustain his yoke? Dost thou it virtue count, to be to beasts, and monsters cast? Him great things braggi ̄ g, darkness deep of tartare press full low. Of whom begotten, the housen then of Gods through pierceth he? Why do'ste thou slander Jove? No mortal kindred ever may be mixed with heaven above. But were they ever servants yet, before they Gods were made? But through all coasts he wandered not abroad as banished. Yet Phœbus did no monsters fear, or beasts of cruel might. Whom thou a miser seest, thou mayest know him a man to be. Call we him stout, from shoulders hie of whom the Lion thrown A gift for maiden made, and eke his Club from hand fell down, And painted side with purple weed did shine that he did wear? Or may we him call stout of heart, whose staring locks of hear With ointme ̄ to slowde? who hands renownde & known by praises hie To found unmeet for any man of timber did apply, With barbarous mytar cloasting in his forehead round about? Of this the house of Euritus destroyed does witness bear, And virgins flocks that brutishly by him oppressed wear. No Juno did command him this, nor none Eurystheus lo. But these in deed his own works are. What is to Jove, to king is leefull thing: To Jove thou gau'ste a wife, thou shalt now give oue to a king. And even of thee she shall it learn to be a thing not new, Her husband even approving it the better man ensue. But if she stubborn to be matched with me deny it still, Then even by force a noble child of her beget I will. Who all appoyncts with present death to have their punishment, He tyrunt wats not how to be: more sundry grieves invent. Restrain the wretched man from death, command that happy die. I, while with beams prepar'de to burn the pile increases hie, Will him with vowing sacrifice that rules the seas entreat.
