Sir we are come to know your pleasure. I heartily thank you, and look what I can do, It shall be always ready to pleasure you. Come, To follow our counsel your charge and promise was, Good sir be ruled and leave this peevish self. No nor so many more. For my partly, I know I can do you no good. Well since it will none otherwise frame. Let v twain Study, & return from whence we came. What Will, we may not miss thee, for no money. Thou shalt watch to take him at certain bays, Come not in the throng, but save thy self always. You twain on either side, first with your sword and buckler. After the first conflict, fight with your sword and daggers, You sir with a Javelin and your Target in your hand, See how you can, his deadly strokes withstand. Keep at the soyne, come not within his reach, Until you see, what good advantage you may ketch. Then hardly leave him not, till time you strike him dead, And of all other parts, especially save yonr head. Now when you please. I have no more to tell, But heartily to pray for you, and wish you well. Take breath and change your weapons play the men, Some what it was that made thee come agaen, Thou stickest some what better to thy tackling I see, But what, no force you are but Jack sprot to me. Plot summary

Plot — The Marriage of Wit and Science
Hearing Science’s reputation, Wit falls in love with her. His mother sends him on a journey to Mount Parnassus, with Will as his companion. Preferring a bachelor master (because it means an easier life for a servant), Will argues against marriage, but delivers Wit’s suit to Science, including his picture. Science has decided not to admit any more suitors, because they have all come to harm, but tells Wit that he must take time to learn to fight, so that he can take on her enemy Tediousness. Reason gives him a mirror and Experience commends him to seek help from Instruction. However, he finds Instruction boring, and he is killed in the battle with Tediousness. Recreation revives Wit with a song, but he is tempted by another sung by Idleness, and sleeps with her. She exchanges his coat for that of Ignorance, and he is not recognized by Science and Reason. He only realizes what has happened when he sees his own face in Reason’s mirror. Reason rebukes him and Shame whips him, but then, at Science’s request, he is given another chance. By following Instruction’s advice, he defeats and beheads Tediousness, so that his marriage with Science can proceed.
