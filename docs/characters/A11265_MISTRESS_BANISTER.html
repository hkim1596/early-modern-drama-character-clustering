Full speech

Oh master Friskiball, pity my husbands case, He is a man hath lived as well as any, Till envious fortune and the ravenous sea, Did rob, disrobe, and spoil us of our own. What thou hast said, springs from and envious heart, A Canniball that does eat men alive, But here upon my knee believe me sir, And what I speak, so help me God is true, We scarce have meat to feed our little babes, Most of our Plate is in that Brokers hand, Which had we money to dephray our debt, Oh think we would not bide that penury: Be merciful, kind master Friskiball, My husband, children, and my self will eat, But one meal a day, the other will we keep and fell, As partly to pay the debt we owe to you: If ever tears did pierce a tender mind, Be pitiful, let me some favour find. My children in their prayers both night and day, For your good fortune and success shall pray. God save you sir, pray is your name master Cromwell. Know you not one Bagot sir, thats come to Antwarpe. Into decay indeed, long of that wretch, I am the wife to woeful Banister: And by that bloody villain am persu'de, From London here to Antwarpe, My husband he is in the governors hands: And God of heaven knows how heel deal with him, Now sir your heart is framed of milder temper, Be merciful to a distressed soul, And God no doubt will trebell bless your gain. Oh speak to Bagot that same wicked wretch, And Angels voice may move a damned devil. I hard he landed some two hours since. That mighty God that knows each mortals heart, Keep you from trouble sorrow grief and smart. Oh thou base wretch whom we have fostered, Even as a Serpent for to poison us, If God did ever right a woman's wrong: To that same God I bend and bow my heart, To let his heany wrath fallen on thy head, By whom my Hope's and joys are, butchered. Thy works are infinite, great God of heaven. Thanks to my God, next to my Sovereign King, And last to you that these good Hope's does bring. They shall be welcome, husband isle go before, But is not that man master Friskiball: If Banister should bear so base a heart, I never would look my husband in the face, But hate him as I would a Cockatrise. Sir my brother is his steward if you please, Weal go along, and bear you company: I know we shall not want for welcome there.
