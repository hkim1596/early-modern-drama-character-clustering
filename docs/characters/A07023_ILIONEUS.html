Full speech

Follow you Trojans, follow this brave Lord, And plain to him the sum of your distress. Wretches of Troy, envied of the winds, That crave such favour at your honours feet, As poor distressed misery may plead: Save, save, Oh save our ships from cruel fire, That do complain the wounds of thousand waves, And spare our lives whom every spite pursues. We come not we to wrong your Libian Gods, Or steal your household ears from their shrines: Our hands are not prepared to lawless spoil, Nor armed to offend in any kind: Such force is far from our unweaponed thoughts, Whose fading weal of victory forsook, Forbids all hope to harbour near our hearts. I hear Aeneas voice, but see him not, For none of these can be our General. He means Aeneas, let us kiss his feet. Blessed be the time I see Achate's face. Lovely Aeneas, these are Carthage walls, And here Queen Dido wears imperial Crown, Who for Troy's sake hath entertained us all, And clad us in these wealthy robes we wear. Oft hath she asked us under whom we served, And when we told her she would weep for grief, Thinking the sea had swallowed up thy ships, And now she sees thee how will she rejoice? Look where she comes: Aeneas viewed her well. Renowned Dido, 'tis our General: warlike Aeneas. This man and I were at Olympus games. Why, let us built a City of our own, And not stand lingering here for amorous looks: Will Dido raise old Priam forth his graven, And built the town again the Greeks did burn? No no, she cares not how we sink or swim, So she may have Aeneas in her arms. But what shall it be called, Troy as before?
