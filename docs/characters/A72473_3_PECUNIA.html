Why do you so my Guardian? I not bid you, Cannot my Grace be got, and held too, Without your selfe-tormentings, and your watches, Your macerating of your body thus With cares, and scantings of your diet, and rest? But my Reputation. May suffer, and the worship of my family, When by so servile means they both are sought. Oh no! I could endure to take a great deal more (And with my constitution, were it left) Unto my choice, what think you of it, Statute? Wait you here, Broker. Nay, of my lips, Sir, To him. And I have my desire, Sir, to behold That youth, and shape, which in my dreams and wakes, I have so oft contemplated, and felt Warm in my veins, and native as my blood. When I was told of your arrival here, I felt my heart Beatrice, as it would leap out, In speech; and all my face it was a flame, But how it came to pass I do not know. I will do all grace To my new servant. You are a Courtier, Sir, or somewhat more; That have this tempting language! Yes, Lickfinger, Lickfinger is challenged by Madrigal of and argu meant. And we shall thank you for't and reward you. Who hath changed my servant? Sweet Cousin Madrigal, and Cousin Fitton, My Cousin Shunfield, and my learned Cousin. Indeed therein, I must confess, deer Cousin, The Gallants are all about Pecuniam. I am a most unfortunate Princess. Why do you this, Sir? spill the wine, and rave? For Brokers sleeping? No Guardian, I do Like them very well. Nay, say her jail. Never unfortunate Princess, Was used so by a Jailer. Ask my women, Band, you can tell, and Statute, how he has used me, Kept me close prisoner, under twenty bolts— No, he would have done, That lay not in his power: he had the use Of our bodies, Band, and Wax, and sometime Statutes: But once he would ha'smother'd me in a chest, And strangled me in leather, but that you Came to my rescue, then, and gave me air. My Pedigree? I tell you, friend, he must be a good Scholar, Can my dissent. I am of Princely raze, And as good blood, as any is i'the mines, Runs through my veins. I am, every limb, a Princess! Duchess OF mines, was my great Grandmother. And by the Father's side, I come from Solemnizing. My Grandfather was Duke of Or, and matched In the blood-royal of Ophyr. I know it, if I hear the Blazon. What be these? Besants? That is our Coat too, as we come from Or. What line's this? This? But this, this little branch. The Welsh-myne that. I have Welsh-blood in me too, blaze, Sir, that Coat. Well, at better leisure, We'll take a view of it, and so reward you. I will, if you will have it. Cousin Pyed-mantle. And so Pecuniam her self does wish, That she may still be aid unto their uses, Not slave unto their pleasures, or a Tyrant Over their fair desires; but teach them all The golden mean: the Prodigal how to live, The sordid, and the covetous, how to die, That with found mind; this safe frugality.
