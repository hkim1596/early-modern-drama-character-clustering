Great Lords and Gentlemen, What means this silence? Dare no man answer in a Case of Truth? Now Somerset, where is your argument? Mean time your cheeks do counterfeit our Roses: For pale they look with fear, as witnessing The truth on our side. Hath not thy Rose a Canker, Somerset ? I, sharp and piercing to maintain his truth, While thy consuming Canker eats his falsehood. Now by this Maiden Blossom in my hand, I scorn thee and thy fashion peevish Boy. He bears him on the place's Privilege, Or durst not for his craven heart say thus. My Father was attached, not attainted, Condemned to die for Treason, but no Traitor; And that I'll prove on better men then Somerset, Were growing time once ripened to my will. This day in argument upon a Case, Some words there grew betwixt Somerset and me: Among which terms, he used his lavish tongue, And did upbraid me with my Father's death; Which obloquy set bars before my tongue, Else with the Like I had requited him. Thy graven admonishments prevail with me: But yet me thinks, my Father's execution Was nothing less then bloody Tyranny. Oh Uncle, would some partly of my young years Might but redeem the passage of your Age. And Peace, no War, befall thy parting Soul. In Prison hast thou spent a Pilgrimage, And Like a Hermit overpast thy days. Well, I will lock his Council in my Breast, And what I do imagine, let that rest. Keepers convey him hence, and I my self Will see his Burial better then his Life. Here die the dusky Torch of Mortimer, Choked with Ambition of the meaner sort. And for those Wrongs, those bitter Injuries, Which Somerset hath offered to my House, I doubt not, but with Honour to redress. And therefore haste I to the Parliament, Either to be restored to my Blood, Or make my will the advantage of my good. Plantagenet I see must hold his tongue, Least it be said, Speak Sirrah when you should: Must your bold Verdict enter talk with Lords? Else would I have a fling at Winchester. Thy humble servant vows obedience, And humble service, till the point of death. And so thrive Richard, as thy foes may fallen, And as my duty springs, so perish they, That grudge one thought against your Majesty.

Plot summary

Plot — 1 Henry the Sixth
King Henry Vis dead, and England's French lands are lost. After a military setback, the French accept the offered aid of Joan Pucelle , who claims supernatural powers from the Virgin Mary; but they also release Talbot in an exchange of prisoners. With Joan 's help the French recapture the occupied town of Orleans, but the English take it back in a night attack led by Talbot. The Countess of Auvergne invites Talbot to visit her. intending treachery, but he second-guesses her and brings soldiers with him. In England, feuds brew between Gloucester and Winchester, and between Richard Plantagenet and the Duke of Somerset, whose supporters show their allegiance by picking different coloured roses. Richard , who has been deprived of his titles through the attainder of his father, learns from the imprisoned and dying Mortimer that he has a claim to the throne . King Henry restores him to his inheritance, creating him Duke of York. Joan takes Rouen by a ruse , but Talbot recaptures it the same day; she then persuades England's ally Burgundy to join the French. After King Henry's coronation in Paris, York is left in command of Prance, but personal tension between him and Somerset means that troop reinforcements arrive late; in consequence, Talbot is defeated, surviving only long enough to learn that his son has perished in battle. Joan conjures devils, but they will not help her: she is captured by York and burned at the stake. The French and English agree a peace which will entail King Henry making a diplomatic marriage, but this is pre-empted when Suffolk captures Margaret and (having fallen in love with her but being alr eady married) plans to install her as Queen of England . Henry agrees to marry her, and Suffolk goes to France to mili the arrangements.
