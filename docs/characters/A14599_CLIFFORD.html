Its well done fellows. The daring Scot fuller of insolence then strength Stand forth to bid us battle. It a brave hunting. Bolt offers to lay his Coat under the king. Speak to your countrymen, Common and Mentith. There's drinking money, hence to your works. What is, what should, what can this Wallace be? Whom fame limbs out for such a gallant piece, And is so curious in her workmanship, No partly deforms him, Yet Wallace is a Rebel, his chief scandal Is poverty of Gentry, by my sword Wertenberg no impeach to my deer Ancestors, I well could spare him some of my unused titles, Or would at martial gaming so I might loose And Wallace win so much of Clifford's honour, Our stocks might be alike, but I exceed, This night he is betrayed, he shall not, I'll turn traitor first he shall not, Call Beaumont back, or else by Clifford's honour, And oath which I esteem above my life, I will turn traitor, and reveal your plots, Call him back. Where's noble Bruce? I have a message, but 'tis more honourable, sent to you too, The Herald says that Wallace dares you, his Spite is all at you, and if your spirit be great As his, you find him in the rear. I would not wish to meet with braver spirits. Take breath, I would not have the world robbed of two such spirits, post to the King, and tell him that the noblest Hearts of the whole herd are hunted to the toil, Ask whither they shall fallen, or live for gain. Charge them black day, The Lion Hunt's a Lion for his prey. A fight. Is not this he that killed Wallace? The pangs of war are Like to childbed throws Bitter in suffering, but the storm being passed, The talk, as of scaped shipwreck sweet, does taste, The death of the Scotch General went to my heart, He had in him of man as as any, And for ought I think, his blood was poorly sold By his own Countrymen, rather then sought by us. Had not the Rear where Wallace did command, Stood and given aim, it had been a day Bloody and dismal, and whose hard to say, Sir, you shall give me leave to drink a health To all the valiant Scots. Perhaps I cannot. The Lord Bruce, I mean. Do, sirrah be gone. There's great enquiry for you. The King has a fresh command for Bruce. True, and Huntington is one. No Subject? Peace. Bruce speaks not Like a subject. Keep him off. Who did this damned villainy? So much swears Clifford. Yet though a traitor, thus much let me speak For absent Wallace, were the case your own, Or one that's baser having any spirit, A murdered father and a bleeding wife, Mangled before him, would strike fire in snow, Make loyalty turn traitor, and obedience Forget all duty. Plot summary

Plot — The Valiant Scot
PLOT Scotland is under English rule. An attempt is made to dispossess Old Wallace of his office and lands; but Old Wallace partially circumvents it by transferring ownership of the land to his son. King Edward plans to make war on France, and appoints Bruce his general. Scotland is left in the charge of Queen Elinor and a panel of commissioners. Young Selby, son of one of the commissioners, abducts Peggy Graham, intending to marry her by force. Wallace intervenes and kills Young Selby in a duel. Commissioner Selby arrests Peggy and threatens her with execution for the murder. Wallace gives himself up, and Peggy is released. His own execution is forestalled when the Queen orders him brought to court for interrogation. He escapes en route, marries Peggy, and becomes a rebel. Friar Gertrid prophesies that he will be victorious until betrayed by his own blood. The English capture Peggy, Gertrid, and Old Wallace, and Selby has them killed. King Edward sends Wallace an ultimatum: if he will surrender and beg for mercy, he may be spared. Wallace sends an alternative ultimatum back: if Edward will admit himself to be a tyrant, he may be spared. Upon discovering that one of the ambassadors, the Queen’s nephew, has no diplomatic accreditation, he puts the young man to death. He then cuts out an ambassador’s tongue and blinds another, and accompanies them back to King Edward disguised as a lame soldier. The English plan to entice Wallace into a trap, and send another messenger to invite him to a parley; but the stratagem fails because the man appointed to escort the messenger to the Scottish camp is the lame soldier — Wallace himself. Wallace sails to seek help from France, but his ship is wrecked. On the shore, he meets first Bolt, from whom he forcibly takes possession of a washed-up chest of provisions, then Selby, who is now banished from England and penurious, and regrets the execution of Old Wallace. Wallace decides not to take revenge, but while he is away fetching food, another of the fallen commissioners turns up and kills Selby in an argument. Bolt returns and kills the murderer, taking him for Wallace. He is presented to King Edward and rewarded, but later discredited when Wallace turns out to be alive. The Scots prepare to fight, but Wallace is offended to be placed in the rearguard, and withdraws his services.
