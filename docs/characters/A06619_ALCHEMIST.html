What Peter do you loiter, knowing that every minute increases our Mine? But what stripling is this? Craft sir boy, you must call it mystery. Canst thou take pains? But thou must be sworn to be secret, and then I will entertain thee. When in the depth of my skill I determine to try the uttermost of mine Art, I am dissuaded by the gods, otherwise, I durst undertake to make the fire as it flames, gold, the wind as it blows, silver, the uva ter as it runs, led, the earth as it stands, iron, the sky, brass, and men's thoughts, firm mettles. Come in, and thou shalt see all. Exit. Rafe, my boy is run away, I trust thou wilt not run after. My boy was the veriest These, the aran test liar, and the vildest swearer in the world, other wise the best boy in the world, he hath stolen my appa rell, all my money, and forgotten nothing but to bid me farewell. Why thou hast not yet seen the end of my Art. I Rafe, the fortune of this Art consisteth in the measure of the fire, for if there be a coal too much, or a spark too little, if it be a little too hight, or a thought too soft, all our labour is in vain; beside, they that blow, must beat time with their breathes, as Musicians do with their breasts, so as there must be of the metals, the fire and workers a very harmony. So is it, and often does it happen, that the joust proportion of the fire and all things concur. Then away. Oh my child, Gryphes make their nests of gold though their coats are feathers, and we feather our nests with Diamonds, though our garments be but freeze. If thou knewest the secret of this Science, the cunning would make thee so proud that thou woul dest disdain the outward pomp. And ounce of Silver limde, as much of crude Mercury, of Spirits four, being tempered with the bodies seven, by multiplying of it ten times, comes for one pound, eight thousand pounds, so that I may have only Beechen coals.
