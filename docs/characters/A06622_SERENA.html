And I deer brother find my thoughts in tangled with affections beyond nature, which so flame into my distempered head, that I can neither without danger smother the fire, nor without more destie disclose my fury. Only to be content with our father's mean estate, to comhat against our own intemperate de sire's, and yield to the success of fortune, who though she hath framed us miserable, cannot make us mon strous. That which nature warrants laws forbid, strange it seems in sense, that because thou art mine, therefore thou must not be mine. They say there is hard by and old cunning woman, who can tell Fortune's, expound dreams, tell of things that be lost, and divine of accidents to come, she is call led the good woman, who yet never did hurt. Good brother let us. Good mother tell us, if by your cunning you can, what shall become of my brother and me. These doggrell rhymes and obscure words, come ming out of the mouth of such a weather-beat ̄ witch, are thought divinations of some holy spirit, being but dreams of decayed brains for mine own part, I would thou mightest fit on that stool till he & I mar tie by law. Come brother, let us to our poor home, this is our comfort, to bewray our passion, since we cannot enjoy our love. Heeres one hard by, I'll call him. And I, or it shall cost me the laying on freely of my maze. No more words at the wedding, if the Mayor should know it, I were in danger of mine office.
