Farewell sweet Mortimer, and for my sake, Forbear to levy arms against the king. Whither goes my lord? On whom but on my husband should I fawn? In saying this, thou wrongest me Gaveston, Is't not enough, that thou corrupts my lord, And art a bawd to his affections, But thou must call mine honour thus in question? Your highness knows, it lies not in my power. Villain, 'tis thou that robbest me of my lord. Wherein my lord, have I deserved these words? Witness the tears that Isabella sheds, Witness this heart, that sighing for thee breaks, How deer my lord is to poor Isabel. Oh miserable and distressed Queen! Would when I left sweet France and was embarked, That charming Circe's walking on the waves, Had changed my shape, or at the marriage day The cup of Hymen had been full of poison, Or with those arms that twinned about my neck, I had been stifled, and not lived to see, The king my lord thus to abandon me: Like frantic Juno will I fill the earth, With ghastly murmur of my sighs and cries, For never doted Jove on Ganymede, So much as he on cursed Gaveston, But that will more exasperate his wrath, I must entreat him, I must speak him fair, And be a means to call home Gaveston : And yet heel ever dote on Gaveston, And so am I for ever miserable. Ah Mortimer! now breaks the kings hate forth, And he confesses that he loves me not. No, rather will I die a thousand death's, And yet I love in vain, heel never love me. Oh never Lancaster! I am inioynde, To sue unto you all for his repeal: This wills my lord, and this must I perform, Or else be banished from his highness presence. I Mortimer, for till he be restored, The angry king hath banished me the court: And therefore as thou love and tenderest me, Be thou my advocate unto these peers. Oh Lancaster, let him dissuade the king, For 'tis against my will he should return. 'Tis for my self I speak, and not for him. Sweet Mortimer, fit down by me a while, And I will tell thee reasons of such waighte, As thou wilt soon subscribe to his repeal. Then thus, but none shall hear it but our selves. Yet good my lord, hear what he can allege. And when this favour Isabel forgets, Then let her live abandoned and forlorn, But see in happy time, my lord the king, Having brought the Earl of Cornwall on his way, Is new returned, this news will glad him much, Yet not so much as me, I love him more Then he can Gaveston, would he loved me But half so much, then were I Triple blessed. Hark how he harps upon his minion. My gracious lord, I come to bring you news. That Gaveston my Lord shall be repealed. But will you love me, if you find it so? For Gaveston, but not for Isabel. No other jewels hang about my neck Then these my lord, nor let me have more wealth, Then I may fetch from this rich treasury: Oh how a kiss revives poor Isabel. And may it prove more happy then the first, My gentle lord, be speak these nobles fair, That wait attendance for a gracious look, And on their knees salute your majesty. Now is the king of England rich and strong. Having the love of his renowned peers. Sweet husband be content, they all love you. Aye me poor soul when these begin to jar. Ah surious Mortimer what hast thou done? My lord, 'tis thought, the Earls are up in arms. Thus do you still suspect me without cause. Your pardon is quickly God of Isabel. No farewell, to poor Isabel, thy Queen? Heavens can witness, I love none but you, From my embracements thus he breaks away, Oh that mine arms could close this I'll about, That I might pull him to me where I would, Or that these tears that drissell from mine eyes, Had power to mollify his stony heart, That when I had him we might never partly. I Mortimer, the miserable Queen, Whose pining heart, her inward sighs have blasted, And body with continual Morning wasted: These hands are tired, with haling of my lord From Gaveston, from wicked Gaveston, And all in vain, for when I speak him fair, He turns away, and smiles upon his minion. What would you with the king, is't him you seek? He's gone by water unto Scarborough, Pursue him quickly, and he cannot escape, The king hath left him, and his train is small. That this your army going several ways, Might be of lesser source, and with the power That he intends presently to raise, Be easily suppressed: and therefore be gone. No Mortimer, isle to my lord the king. You know the king is so suspicious, As if he hear I have but talked with you, Mine honour will be called in question, And therefore gentle Mortimer be gone. So well hast thou deseru'de sweet Mortimer, As Isaebell could live with thee for ever, In vain I look for love at Edwards hand, Whose eyes are fixed on none but Gaveston : Yet once more isle importune him with prayers, If he be strange and not regard my words, My son and I will over into France, And to the king my brother there complain, How Gaveston hath robbed me of his love: But yet I hope my sorrows will have end, And Gaveston this blessed day be slain.
