Oh peace, Prince Dolphin, You are too much mistaken in this King: Question your Grace the late Ambassadors, With what great State he herd their Embassy, How well supplied with Noble Councillors, How modest in exception; and withal, How terrible in constant resolution: And you shall find, his Vanities forespent, Were but the outside of the Roman Brutus, Covering Discretion with a Coat of Folly; As Gardeners do with Ordure hide those Roots That shall first spring, and be most delicate. This becomes the Great. Sorry am I his numbers are so few, His Soldiers sick, and famished in their March: For I am sure, when he shall see our Army, He'll drop his heart into the sink of fear, And for achievement, offer us his Ransom. By my faith Sir, but it is: never any body saw it, but his Lackey: it's a hooded valour, and when it appears, it will bore. You have shot over. Then shall we find to morrow, they have only stomachs to eat, and none to fight. Now is it time to arm: come, shall we about it? And if he be not fought withal, my Lord, Let us not live in France: let us quit all, And give our Vineyards to a barbarous People. Dieu de Battles, where have they this mettle? Is not their Climate foggy, raw, and dull? On whom, as in despite, the Sun looks pale, Killing their Fruit with frowns. Can sodden Water, A Drench for sur-reyn'd ades, their Ba lie broth, Decoct their cold blood to such valiant heat? And shall our quick blood, spirited with Wine, Seem frosty? Oh, for honour of our Land, Let us not hang Like roping Isyckles Upon our Houses Thatch, while a more frosty People Sweat drops of gallant Youth in our rich fields: Poor we call them, in their Native Lords. Tut, I have the best Armour of the World: would it were day. It is the best Horse of Europe. Indeed my Lord, it is a most absolute and ex cellent Horse. Nay, for me thought yesterday your Mistress shrewdly shook your back. Mine was not bridled. You have good judgement in Horseman ship. I had as live have my Mistress a Jade. I could make as true a boast as that, if I had a Sow to my Mistress. Yet do I not use my Horse for my Mistress, or any such Proverb, so little kin to the purpose. Stars my Lord. And yet my Sky shall not want. Even as your Horse bears your praises, who would trot as well, were some of your brags dismoun ted. I will not say so, for fear I should be faced out of my way: but I would it were morning, for I would feign be about the ears of the English. You must first go your self to hazard, ere you have them. I think he will eat all he kills. Swear by her Foot, that she may tread out the Oath. Doing is activity, and he will still be doing. Nor will do none to morrow: he will keep that good name still. I was told that, by one that knows him better then you. Marry he told me so himself, and he said he cared not who knew it. I will cap that Proverb with, There is flattery in friendship. Well placed: there stands your friend for the Devil: have at the very eye of that Proverb with, A Pox of the Devil. Who hath measured the ground? A valiant and most expert Gentleman. Would it were day? Alas poor Harry of England: he longs not for the Dawning, as we do. If the English had any apprehension, they would run away. Just, joust: and the men do sympathise with the Mastiffs, in robustious and rough coming on, leaving their Wits with their Wives: and then give them great Meals of Beef, and Iron and Steel; they will eat Like Wolves, and fight Like Devils. Hark how our Steeds, for present Service neigh. To Horse you gallant Princes, straight to Horse. Do but behold yond poor and starved Band, And your fair show shall suck away their Souls, Leaving them but the shales and husks of men. There is not work enough for all our hands, Scarce blood enough in all their sickly Veins, To give each naked Curtleax a stain, That our French Gallant shall to day draw out, And sheath for lack of sport. Let us but blow on them, The vapour of our Valour will overturn them. It's positive against all exceptions, Lords, That our superfluous Lackeys, and our Peasants, Who in unnecessary Actium swarm About our Squares of Battle, were enough To purge this field of such a hilding Foe; Though we upon this Mountains Basis by, Took stand for idle speculation: But that our Honours must not. What's to say? A very little little let us do, And all is done: then let the Trumpets found The Tucket Sonnance, and the Note to mount: For our approach shall so much dare the field, That England shall couch down in fear, and yield. They have said their prayers, And they ay for death. I stay but for my Guard: on To the field, I will the Banner from a Trumpet take, And use it for my haste. Come, come away, The Sun is high, and we outwear the day. Oh Diable. Why all our ranks are broke. Disorder that hath spoiled us, friend us now, Let us on heaps go offer up our lives.
