Full speech

Bacon, we hear, that song we have suspect, That thou art read in Magics mystery, In Piromancy, to divine by flames, To tell by Hadromaticke, ebbs and tides, By Aeromancy, to discover doubts, To plain out questions, as Apollo did. I tell thee Bacon, Oxford makes report, Nay England, and the Court of Henry says, Thart making of a brazen head by Art, Which shall unfold strange doubts and Aphorisms, And read a Lecture in Philosophy, And by the help of Devils and ghastly fiends, Thou meanest ere many years or days be passed, To compass England with a wall of brass. Is this possible? What be those? But Bacon roves a bow beyond his reach, And tells of more then Magic can perform: Thinking to get a fame by fooleries, Have I not passed as far in state of schools, And read of many secrets? yet to think. That heads of brass can utter any voice, Or more to tell of deep Philosophy, This is a Fable Aesop had forgotten. I will, ask what thou can. I was, what then? I, none at all, I read not there a line. I pass not of his frivolous speeches. I know not what we did, a pox of all conjuring Friars. The Devil take thee and Henly too. We must lay plots for stately Tragedies, Strange Comic shows, such as proud Rossius Vaunted before the Roman Emperors. Bacon, if he will hold the Germane play, We'll teach him what and English Friar can do: The Devil I think dare not dispute with him. A vengeance on the Friar for his pains, But leaving that, let's to Bacon straight, To see if he will take this task in hand. Whats the matter, fellow? Who is the master and chief of this crew? What are you?
