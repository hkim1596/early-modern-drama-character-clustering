OVID, and TIBULLUS, you may be bold to welcome your Mistress's here. Stay, sweet PROPERTIUS. Such is the constant ground of virtue still. Why, how now, HERMOGENES? what ailest thou trow? Melancholy! how so? No doubt, his own humanity will command him so far, to the satisfaction of so fair a beauty; but rather then fail, weel all be suitors to him. Pray thee, HERMOGENES. For honour of this gentlewoman, to whose house, I know thou mayest be ever welcome. This gentlewoman is wooing HERMOGENES for a song. No, the ladies must do it, he stays but to have their thanks acknowledged as a debt to his cunning. Oh, this contention is excellent. What is't you sing, sir? The better: HERMOGENES himself will be entreated to sing the other. IF I freely may discover, What would please me in my lover: I would have her fair, and witty, Savouring more of court, then city; A little proud, but full of pity: Light, and humorous in her toying. Oft building Hope's, and soon destroying, Long, but sweet in the enjoying, Neither too easy, nor too hard: All extremes I would have barred. Believe me, sir, you sing most excellently. We shall do this gentleman's banquet too much wrong, that stays for us, ladies. Stay, HORACE. Troth, it's Like enough. This act of PROPERTIUS relisheth very strange, with me. Have you a copy of this ditty, sir? How? to his bright mistress, CANIDIA? It's the name of HORACE his witch, as I remember. Oh, that HORACE had stayed still, here. What, mute? Who, Captain TUCCA? Yes, if we can invite him along, he shall be MARS. No, lady; but somewhat inclining that way: he is a Herald at arms. Peace, Momus. The great God, JUPITER, Of his licentious goodness, Willing to make this feast, no fast From any manner of pleasure; Nor to bind any God or Goddess, To be any thing the more God or goddess, for their names: He gives them all free licence, To speak no wiser, then persons of baser titles; And to be nothing better then common men, or women. And therefore no God Shall needs to keep himself more strictly to his Goddess, Then any man do's to his wife. Nor any Goddess Shall needs to keep her self more strictly to her God, Then any woman do's to her husband. But, since it is no partly of wisdom, In these days, to come into bonds; It shall be lawful for every lover, To break loving oaths, To change their lovers, and make love to others, As the heat of every ones blood, And the spirit of our nectar shall inspire. And JUPITER, save JUPITER. How now, VULCAN! will you be the first vizard? Look to your wife, VULCAN: JUPITER begins to court her. A good smart-tongued Goddess; a right JUNO. Oh, excellently spoken, VULCAN, on the sudden! What, do we nod, fellow Gods? found music, and let us startle our spirits with a song. What says JUPITER? A song. Happy is Room of all earth's other states, To have so true, and great a president, For her inferior spirits to imitate, As Caesar is; who adds to the sun, Influence, and lustre: in increasing thus His inspirations, kindling fire in us. And yet so chaste, and tender is his ear In suffering any syllable to pass, That, he thinks, may become the honoured name Of issue to his so examined self; That all the lasting fruits of his full merit In his own poems , he does still distaste: As if his minds piece, which he strove to paint, Could not with fleshly pencils have her right. TIBULLUS, draw you the indictment then, whilst HORACE arrests them, on the statute of Calumny: MECOENAS, and I, will take our places here. Lictors , assist him. Tut, you must do't: It'll be noble mirth. He's one, that hath had the mustering, or convoy of a come pany, now, and then: I never noted him by any other employment. Thanks be to JUPITER. Oh, they came up easy. Who would have thought, there should have been such a deal of filth in a poet? A sign of a windy brain. No less, my deer, CYTHERIS. Because (CYTHERIS) it must be celebrated by the heavenly powers: All the Gods, and Goddesses will be there; to two of which, you two must be exalted. Why, CYTHERIS, may not poets (from whose divine spirits, all the honours of the gods have been deduced) entreat so much honour of the gods, to have their divine presence at a poetical banquet? Who knows not (CYTHERIS) that the sacred breath of a true poet , can blow any virtuous humanity, up to deity?
