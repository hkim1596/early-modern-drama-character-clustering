UP from the Chaos of eternal night, (To which the whole digestion of the world Is now returning) once more I ascend, And bide the cold damp of this piercing air. To urge the justice, whose almighty word Measures the bloody acts of impious men, With equal penance, who in th'act it self Includes infliction, which Like chained shot Batter together still; though (as the thunder Seems, by men's duller hearing then their sighed, To break a great time after lightning forth, Yet both at one time tear the labouring cloud,) So men think penance of their ils is slow, Though th'ill and penance still together go. Reform ye ignorant men, your manless lives Whose laws ye think are nothing but your lustest When leaving but for supposition sake, The body of felicity (Religion) Set in the amid of Christendom, and her head Cleft to her bosom; one half one way swaying Another th'other: all the Christian world And all her laws, whose observation, Stands upon faith, above the power of reason: Leaving (I say) all these, this might suffice, To fray ye from your vicious swinge in ill, And set you more on fire to do more good: That since the world (as which of you denies) Stands by proportion, all may the ce conclude, That all the joints and nerves sustaining nature, As well may break, and yet the world abide, As any one good unrewarded die, Or any one ill escape his penalty. Danger (the spur of all great minds) is ever The curb to your tame spirits; you respect not (With all your holiness of life and learning) More then the present, Like illiterate vulgars, Your mind (you say) kept in your fleshes bounds, Shows that men will must ruled be by his power: When (by true doctrine) you are taught to live Rather without the body, then within; And rather to your God still then your self: To live to him, is to do all things fitting His Image, in which, Like himself we live; To be his Image, is to do those things, That make us deathless, which by death is only; Doing those deeds that fit eternity, And those deeds are the perfecting that Justice, That makes the world last, which proportion is Of punishment and wreak for every wrong, As well as for right a reward as strong: Away then, use the means thou hast to right The wrong I suffered. Plot — The Revenge of Bussy D'Ambois
Before the start of the action, the Duke of Guise had Bussy D’Amboise and his brother Clermont brought ===== PAGE 63 ===== 1610 to court. Montsoreau had Bussy killed in reprisal for his adultery with Tamyra, and he died prophesying the deaths of Monsieur and Guise; his ghost then appeared to Clermont demanding revenge. Their sister Charlotte also seeks to avenge Bussy, and made her marriage to Baligny conditional on his swearing to participate in the plot; but Clermont insists on doing it alone, as the ghost commanded. Baligny’s role is, therefore, to deliver Clermont’s challenge to Montsoreau; but he cannot get access because Montsoreau has fearfully barricaded himself into his house. Two allies help him. Renel, who is selling his lands to Montsoreau, enables him to get in. When Montsoreau refuses to accept the challenge, Baligny leaves it behind anyway and Tamyra, who has reluctantly gone back to her husband, promises to make him read it. Unknown to the others, Baligny is reporting to the King on potential troublemakers like Clermont and Renel. The King is concerned at the growing power of Guise’s faction, so he and Baligny arrange for Clermont to be arrested: Clermont is to travel with Baligny to inspect the troops at Cambrai, and there he will be taken into custody by Baligny’s lieutenant, Maillard; thus Baligny will not have to participate directly in his brother-in-law’s downfall. Clermont has to leave Paris before concluding his business with Montsoreau. In Cambrai, he receives an anonymous letter warning him of Baligny’s treachery, but refuses to credit it. Maillard arrests him, with difficulty, and he is taken back to the Bastille; but he is allowed to send a message to his lover, the Countess of Cambrai, who was expecting him to visit her that evening. ~ Guise pressurizes the King into signing an order for .
