That were pretty I faith, to see Honesty know a knave by his cap, 'Tis more than I can do with all the skill I have: But tell me I pray thee, how I should know a knave. You see father, this merchant will be witness That he saw so much money tendered, And you received it, being full satisfied, As the last payment for the farm he bought, And if this merchant take his oath against you, That seven days passed he saw the money tendered, I must pass sentence then against you needs. But will you swear on the bible this is true? God save you gentlemen, the king greets you, and at this time, Having some occasion, to use money, hath sent to know What you that be Knights and Squires will lend his Grace, And you master Farmer, be brief (sir) for I cannot stay. Very well, but what says the Farmer? What can he spare the King? Why thats well said, and I commend thy honest mind, Would all men were of thy mind: I warrant thee, thou art and honest man, & one that loves the king But tell me, what wouldst thou have me do? Sir, fear not, I will do it for you, I warrant you, For I tell you, I can do much with the King. I thank you sir heartily. Truly Honesty, if I were furnished with money, I would not stick to give thee thy dinner, But now thou seest I am but a guest my self. It is my gracious Lord and honest man, And one it seems that loves you Majesty: For as your Grace gave me in charge, I went about into the country, to see what sums of money I could make, Among the chiefest of the commonalty: And amongst the richest Knights that I could find, They would lend your Grace at most but twenty ponnd, And every Squire would lend you Grace but ten: Then came I amongst the rest to this plain man, And asked him what he would lend the king, He answered sir, you see I am but poor, Not half so wealthy as a Knight or Squire, And yet in sign of duty to his Grace, I will lend his Majesty two hundred pound. Plot summary

Plot — A Knack to Know a Knave
. . Concerned for the good order of his kingdom, King Edgar commissions Honesty to seek out and expose knaves. The dying Bailiff of Hexham advises his four sons to live by fraud and deceit in their various: professions, and to enrich themselves at the expense of the commonweal and the poor..He dies and is carried off by the devil. While Edgar deals with the case of 1592 Philarchus, a courtier who has abused his father, Honesty observes the corrupt dealings of the Bailiff’s sons: Cuthbert, a cony-catcher, who bears false witness in an attempt to take possession of a farm; Walter, a farmer, who oppresses his neighbours and exports corn to feed the enemy; John, a priest, who refuses to give to charity; and Perin, a courtier, who misuses the royal seal and sells Walter an export licence for his corn under pretext of raising revenues for the crown. Meanwhile honest members of the state, from knight to ploughman, complain of their economic decline. Edgar sends Ethenwald to woo Alfrida by proxy, but Ethenwald falls in love with her himself and marries her. The King forgives him and visits the couple (receiving a petition from the mad men of Gotham on the way), but the jealous Ethenwald is convinced that Edgar plans to cuckold him, and has Alfrida and the kitchen maid swap identities during the royal visit. An outraged Edgar sees through the subterfuge and plans to take revenge, but Bishop Dunstan, with the help of the devil, persuades him to pardon Ethenwald instead. Honesty exposes the various knaves he has observed, and, with royal permission, sentences them all to gruesome and unusual punishments.
