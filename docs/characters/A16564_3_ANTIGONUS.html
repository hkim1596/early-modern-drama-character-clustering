The Macedonians (swollen with wrath) would scorn, That to their King a stranger should succeed: Can men obey a Babe, a Babe not born? What fancies strange would this confusion breed? This could not well become our grave foresight, A doubtful birth so long to tend in vain, Which may abortive be, and brought to light, (Through nature's error) made not apt to reign. But if affection carry us so far, That of that raze we must be ruled by some, Though neither trained by time in peace, nor war, As those who must indeed by kind overcome: Then have we Hercules the eldest son, To our great Prince by fair Bar ines born, Who fourteen years of age, hath now begun His Princely birth by virtue to adorn. How fortuned you your forces to dispose, So well to escape that storm of threatened harms? For, then you had to deal with mighty foes, Who were in war grown hoary under arms. With one consent then let us all conclude, That Alexanders raze (when born) must reign: So shall we establish still that sacred blood, Which raised our state, and may it best maintain. And let us now (before we partly) appoint Who shall command till that the babe be born: And circumspectly ponder every point, That success so our Council may adorn. The humour of that man was too well known, Could he have parted other men from pride, Whose soul was sold a slave unto his own, And for the same (forced by his followers) died? Thus hastened was Perdicca's to his end. I wish that Soldiers never could be brought To prove so mut'nous as they oft have been, And that they durst not violate in ought, Those who by them (as sacred) should be seen: Nor Like I Captains who (Like blustering winds) Would over their troops insult (as tyrants still), Not weighing merits, nor respecting minds, As carried headlong with a blinded will. "Pride by presumption bred (when at a height) "Encountering with contempt both match in ire, "And betwixt them bring base cruelty to light, "The loathsome offspring of a hated Sire. Such of Perdicca's was the monstrous pride, (The vice from which that vice more vile proceeds) That it strange ways for his advancement tried, And did burst forth in most prodigious deeds; At first by Meleagers death when stained, He showed what tyrants harboured in his heart, To whom faith given, nor yet the Church he gained, (Though sacred both) no safety could impart. The Cappadocians (when all else was tried) Choosed (rather then his insolence to bear) By mass ring themselves to escape from pride: "Pride, spite and horror, death breeds only fear. Yet what against his foes he did perform, From martial minds might plead for some excuse, Since irritated thoughts which (wronged) do storm, In minds offended, fury do infuse: But yet why sought he in a servile sort, To play the tyrant, braving his best friends, Who with disdainful forms could not comport? "More then and enemies yoke, a friends offends. And when of late by Ptolemy constrained, He brought his bans with disadvantage back, How by the same his government was stained, The world can witness by his Armies wrack: "But hate made judge, each error seems a crime, "While present ils do aggravate things gone: His Soldiers moved by fortune and the Time, Did by his death venge all their wrongs in one. Lo, when prosperity too much prevails, "Above the judgement thus of vulgar minds, "As little Barges burdened with great Sails, "They move in state, all swollen with Fortune's winds "And as adversity the sprite refines "From abject: dross of pride, and passion base, "That in affliction, virtue clearest shines, "And makes one all the ways of wit to trace: "So does good success make the judgement die, "Then while the fortunate their ease do take, "And lulled asleep in pleasures meadows lie, "As for the Slaughter fat, and ripe to shake; "Yet this the nature is of gallant men, "To rest (as in no state too much involved) "When prospering wary, and most humble then, "If crossed courageous, when embarked, resolved. What though your first attempts renowned are, By which you in two fields victorious stood, And did overthrow two thunderbolts of war, Who lost their lives amidst a crimson flood: Yet is that course of victory controlled, And you have tried what force your force exceeds, Then, faded Laurels should not make you bold, As still reposing on your bypast deeds: For, by the same to indignation moved, The Macedonians all abhor your name, Who at that time so proud a Conqueror prov worser, That ir great men's slaughter having winged your fame. No vice then pride does greater hate procure, "Which foes do scorn, and friends cannot endure. A haughty gesture shows a tyrants heart; "All love a courteous countenance, void of Art. A humble port, kind looks, words smooth and soft, "Are means by which great minds may mount aloft. That which you speak of hate, in love I spy, "Love cannot find and imperfection forth, "But does excuse, extenuate, or deny, "Faults (where it likes) with shadows of no worth: I left Perdicca's, but did him no wrong, Who first to take my life, all means did prove; I told Antipater how he so long Had been abused by a pretended love: For, as I frankly love, while loved again, If the ingrate, ingrately me acquit, Straight kindling fury with a just disdain, I by love passed, proportion then my spite; And yet (Eumenes) I commend thy mind, Who to defend thy friend, hast proved so free, And since in love so constantly inclined, A friendship firm I would contract with thee; Then where that now thy state hath been brought low, (Since spoiled of him in whom thou didst repose) While aided by our power, thou great mayest grow, And raise thy Hope's of kingdoms to dispose.
