Here sir, put of the other half of your Prenticeship. I thank your Ladyship. No forsooth Madam of yours. What Potkinn? you must put off your Tankerd, and put on your blue coat and wait upon Mistress Toochstone into the country. Exit. But alas Frank, how will all this be maintained now? Well Frank, well; the Seas you say are uncertain: But he that sails in your Court Seas, shall find them ten times fuller of hazard; wherein to see what is to be seen, is torment more then a free Spirit can endure; But when you come to suffer, how many Injuries swallow you? What care and devotion must you use, to humour and imperious Lord? proportion your looks to his looks? smiles to his smiles? fit your sails to the wind of his breath? But he's worse then a Prentice that does it, not only humouring the Lord, but every Trencher bearer, every Groom that by indulgence and intelligence crept into his favour, and by Pandatisme into his Chamber; He rules the roast: And when my honourable Lord says it shall be thus, my worshipful Rascal (the Groom of his close stool) says it shall not be thus, claps the door after him, and who dares enter? A Prentice, quoth you? 'tis but to learn to live, and does that disgrace a man? he that rises hardly, stands firmly: but he that rises with ease, Alas, falls as easily. But sweet Frank, when shall my father Security present me? What I cannot do, Madam, I would be glad to learn. Not I truly, Madam, and if I had, it were but cold comfort, should come out of books, now. Which he cannot be forced out off, Madam. I do not know, Madam.
