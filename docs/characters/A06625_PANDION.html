Altered Trachinus, I say no more, and shame that any should know so much. Trachinus, what would you more, all true. A labour intolerable for Pandion. Because it is harder to shape a life to dissem ble, then to go forward with the liberty of truth. Do you know in court any that mean to live? Report hath not always a blister on her tongue. Yet hath Salurus blasts, and water boughs, worms and Caterpillars. Nor the excellency of Sapho the occasion: but the iniquity of flatterers, who always whisper in Prin ces ears suspicion and courness. Yea, & with the judgement of the world, that she is without comparison. I would I might return straight. But not still. Mine eye drinketh neither the colour of wine nor women. When I behold beauty before the sun, his beams dim beauty: when by candle, beauty obscures toarch light: so as no time I can judge, because at any time I cannot discern being in the sun a brightness to shadow beauty, and in beauty a glistering to extin guish light. I follow. And you sir boy go to Syracyse about by land, where you shall meet my stuff, pay for the carriage, and convey it to my lodging. Yea. A strange nature of cold, to driven one into such and heat. Indeed Lady, I have no more Physic then will purge choler, and that if it please you, I will practice up Pon you. It is good for women that be waspish. It is true, and are not you a female content. Of men we learn to speak, of Gods to hold our peace. Silence shall disgest what folly hath swallo wed, and wisdom wean what fancy hath nursed. If it were, what then? Why, in courts there is nothing more come mon. And as to be bald among the Micanyans it was ac counted no shame, because they were all bald: so to be in love among courtiers it is no discredit: for that they are all in love. As of the Seres wool, which being whitest & softest, fretteth soonestand deepest. I will go with you: but send our pages away.

Plot — Sappho and Phao
Menus plans to visit Syracuse to make SAppH6 fall in love, and travels there on PHa0’s ferry-boat. She makes Phao beautiful; and, unknown to her, @iipi@ makes him irresistibly attractive to women, even though he is not interested in them. Sibyllaj whom the gods have given long life but not eternal youth, counsels Phao not to waste his prime years. He falls in love with Sappho, and Sibylla advises him on the technique of wooing. Sappho is taken ill; Phao, who has some pharmaceutical knowledge, is summoned to minister to her. In fact, her malady is love for Phao, whose base origins make him unsuitable for her. Venus too falls for Phao, and procures from Wailea new arrows for Cupid, with which he can break her rival Sappho’s infatuation and turn Phao’s affections her way. Cupid carries out the first part of his mission, but also tells Sappho what he has done; so she persuades him to use another of the new arrows to make Phao hate Venus. Cupid decides to be Sappho’s son instead of Venus’, and gives her his arrows: now she, not Venus, will be the lady of love. Venus swears to get even, and, convinced of love’s pointlessness, Phao leaves Sicily.
