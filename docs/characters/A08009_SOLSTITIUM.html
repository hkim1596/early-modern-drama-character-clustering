Full speech

All hail to Summer my dread sovereign Lord. Alas, my Lord, what gave you me to keep, But a few days eyes in my prime of youth? And those I have converted to white hairs: I never loved ambitiously to climb, Or thrust my hand too far into the fire. To be in heaven, sure, is a blessed thing: But Atlas-like, to prop heaven on ones back, Cannot but be more labour then delight. Such is the state of men in honour placed; They are gold vessels made for servile uses, High trees that keep the weather from low houses, But cannot shield the tempest from themselves. I love to dwell betwixt the hills and dales; Neither to be so great to be envied, Nor yet so poor the world should pity me. Inter utrum que tene, medio tutissimus ibis. In them I weigh the day and night alike. This white glass is the hourglasse of the day: This black one the joust measure of the night; One more then other holds not a grain: Both serve times joust proportion to maintain. Nothing my Lord, nor ought more did I ask. Deserts, my Lord, of ancient servitors, Are Like old sores, which may not be ripped up: Such use these times have God, that none must beg, But those that have young limbs to lavish fast.
