Full speech

Madam, I will. My Lord, it good to make them friends For his oaths are seldom spent in vain. Nay for the Popes sake, and thine own benefit. What Peer in France but thou (aspiring Guise ) Durst be in arms without the Kings consent? I challenge thee for treason in the cause. But trust him not my Lord, for had your highness, Seen with what a pomp he entered Paris, And how the Citizens with gifts and shows Did entertain him and promised to be at his command: Nay, they feared not to speak in the streets, That the Guise durst stand in arms against the King, For not effecting of his holiness will. My Lord, I think for safety of your royal person, It would be good the Guise were made away, And so to quite your grace of all suspect. Mounted his royal Cabonet. And please your grace the Duke of Guise, does crave access unto your highness. My Lord her is his son. My Lord, see where she comes, as if she drooped to hear these news. Ah, had your highness let him live, We might have punished him to his deserts. Oh that that damned villain were alive again, That we might torture him with some new found death. Or else die Epernoune. Thou able to maintain and host in pay, That livest by foreign exhibition. The Pope and King of Spain are thy good friends, Else all France knows how poor a Duke thou art. I Like not this Friars look. It not amiss my Lord, if he were searched. Oh my Lord, let him live a while.
