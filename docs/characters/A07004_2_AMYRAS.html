Why may not I my Lord, as well as he, Be termed the scourge and terror of the world? And I would strive to swim through pools of blood, Or make a bridge of murdered Carcases, Whose arches should be framed with bones of Turks, Ere I would lose the title of a king. And here this mourful streamer shall be placed Wrought with the Persean and Egyptian arms, To signify she was a princess born, And wife unto the Monarch of the East. As is that town, so is my heart consumed, With grief and sorrow for my mother's death. And me another my Lord. Brother, ho, what, given so much to sleep You cannot leave it, when our enemies drums And rattling cannons thunder in our ears Our proper ruin, and our father's foil: What, darest thou then be absent from ̄ the fight, Knowing my father hates thy cowardice, And oft hath warned thee to be still in field, When he himself amid the thickest troops Beats down our foes to flesh our taintless swords. You will not go then: Were all the lofty mounts of Zona mundi, That fill the amid of farthest Tartary, Turned into pearl and proffered for my stay, I would not bide the fury of my father: When made a victor in these haughty arms. He comes and finds his sons have had no shares In all the honours he proposed for us. Shall we let go these kings again my Lord To gather greater numbers gainst our power, That they may say, it is not chance does this, But matchless strength and magnanimity. Good my Lord, let him be forgiven for once, And we will force him to the field hereafter. They will talk still my Lord, if you do not bridle them. See now my Lord how brave the Captain hangs. Alas my Lord, how should our bleeding hearts Wounded and broken with your Highness grief, Retain a thought of joy, or spark of life? Your soul gives essence to our wretched subjects. Whose matter is incorporoat in your flesh. Heavens witness me, with what a broken heart And damned spirit I ascend this seat, and send my soul before my father die, His anguish and his burning agony. Meet heaven & earth, & here let all things end For earth hath spent the pride of all her fruit, And heaven consumed his choicest living fire. Let earth and heaven his timeless death deplore, For both their woorths will equal him no more, Let me have coach my Lord, that I may ride, And thus be drawn with these two idle kings. With what a flinty bosom should I joy. The breath of life, and burden of my soul, If not resolved into resolved pains, My bodies mortified lineaments should exercise the motions of my heart, Pierced with the joy of any dignity? Oh father, if the unrelenting ears Of death and hell be shut against my prayers, And that the spiteful influence of heaven. Deny my soul fruition of her joy, How should I step or stir my hateful feet, Against the inward powers of my heart, Leading a life that only strives to die, And plead in vain, unpleasing soueranity.
