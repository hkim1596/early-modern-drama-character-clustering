Hearest thou Ned? nay look if he will speak to me. I pree thee tell me Ned, art thou in love with the Keepers daughter? Why then sirrah, I'll teach thee how to deceive Love. Marry sirrah Ned, thou shalt put on my cap, and my coat, and my dagger, and I will put on thy clothes, and thy sword, and so thou shalt be my fool. Why so thou shalt beguile Love, for Love is such a proud scab, that he will never meddle with fools nor children. Is not Raphes counsel good, Ned. Sirrah, Will Ermsby, Ned is deceived. Why is the Abbot a learned man, and hath he read many books, and thinkest thou he hath not more learning then thou to choose a bonny wench, yes warrant I thee by his whole Grammar. Sirrah Ned, wouldst feign have her? Why sirrah Ned, weell ride to Oxford to Fryer Bacon, o he is a brave scholar sirrah, they say he is a brave Nigromancer, that he can make women of devils, and he can juggle cats into Costermongers. Marry sirrah, thou shalt go to him, and because thy fa there Harry shall not miss thee, he shall turn me to thee; and I'll to the Court, and I'll Prince it out, and he shall make thee ei there a silken purse, full of gold, or else a fine wrought smock. Marry sirrah, if thou be'st a silken purse full of gold, then on Sundays she'll hang thee by her side, and you must not say a word. Now sir when she comes into a great press of peo ple, for fear of the Cutpurse on a sud ̄ she'l swap thee into her plackerd, then sirrah being there, you may plead for your self. Then she'll put thee into her chest and lay thee into Lavender, and upon some good day she'll put thee on, and at night when you go to bed, then being turned from a smock to a man, you may make up the match. God thank you when I have it on my back, Ned. And sirrah Lacy, buy me a thousand thousand million of fine Bells'. Marry every time that Ned sighs for the Keepers daughter, I'll tie a bell about him, so within three or four days I will send word to his father Harry, that his son and my master Ned is become Loves Morris dance. Where be these vagabond knaves, that they attend no better on their master? Sirrah Ned, I'll have no more post-horse to ride on, I'll have another fetch. Marry sir, I'll send to the I'll of Eely for four or five dozen of Geese, and I'll have them tide six and six together with whipcord. Now upon their backs will I have a fair field bed, with a Canopy, and so when it is my pleasure, I'll slee into what place I please; this will be easy. Wherefore have I such a company of cutting knaves to wait upon me, but to keep & defend my countenance against all mine enemies? have you not good swords and bucklers? Sirrah, I am the master of these good-fellows, mayest thou not know me to be a Lord by my reparrell? Ned strike him, I'll warrant thee by mine honour. Why servant Ned, will not the Fryer do it? Were not my sword glued to my scabbard by conjuration, I would cut off his head and make him do it by force. Faith Ned, and I'll Lord it out till thou comest, I'll be Prince of Wales over all the black pots in Oxford. I am, father Doctor, as a man would say, the Belwea there of this company, these are my Lords, and I the Prince of Wales. Sirrah Miles, bring hither the Tapster that drew the wine, & I warrant when they see how soundly I have broke his head, thei'le say 'twas done by no less man then a Prince. Doctors, whose doting nightcaps are not capable of my ingenious dignity, know that I am Edward Plantagenet, whom if you displease, will make a ship that shall hold all your Colleges, and so carry away the Niniuersity with a fair wind, to the Bankeside in Southwark, how sayest thou Ned Warraine, shall I not do it? What sayest, Miles, shall I honour the prison with my presence? And upon that I will led the way, only I will have Miles go before me, because I have herd Henry say, that wise dome must go before Majesty. Sirrah Harry, shall Ned marry Nell? Marry Harry, follow my counsel, send for Fryer Ba con to marry them, for heel so conjure him and her with his Nigromancy, that they shall love together Like Pig & Lamb whilst they live. I, so she will promise me two things. That she will never scold with Ned, nor sighed with me, Sirrah Harry, I have put her down with a thing unpossible. Why Harry, didst thou ever see that a woman could both hold her tongue and her hands? no: but when egge-pyes grow on Appletrees, then will thy grey Mare prove a Bagpiper. You love her? Madam Nell, never believe him you, though he swears he loves you. Why, his love is Like unto a Tapsters glass that is broken with every touch; for he loved the fair maid of From singfield once out of all hoe; nay Ned, never wink upon me, I care not, I. He says all England hath no such, and I say, and I'll stand to it, there is one better in Warwickshire. Why Ned I have laid the plot in my head, thou shalt have her already.
