Oh Nurse our dolours witness sure By curroll cheeks distilling rain, And heavy hearts complaint endure. That sends this guiltless ghost to graven In rule my state their destinies have, And not thy prayers, (Oh matron) joust. The Lion fierce I shall appeal, And sooner tame the Tiger stout, Then mankind Tyrants brutish breast, He spites the noble razed out, Contemmes high powers, disdains the least: Ne can well use that princely weed, Which venomous parent wrapped him in By huge unspeakable grisly deed. Although that wight unthankful, grynne, In Kingly throne that he does reign, Through cruel cursed mother's aid: Although he pay with Death again So great a gift, it shall be said And after fates in long spent age, That woman wight shall have alway, This eloge yet and saying sage, That he by her does bear the sway, Dispatch he me least with this hand he fallen. But wondrous dolour, great and wrathful ire, And Misery's will it grant without desire. What, that he will by gilded once slain before, Alive again my brother me restore? That court does wait another brood they say. And poor Britts death tugges me another way. Their minds so pressed to pleasure me, I know Great comfort brings: but do not slake my woe. But princes force does bear the greater sway. His Minion brave can not so lead her life. But dear to make Ere long she shall a mother eke be made. So far therein I dare most boldly wade. Nay, sooner shall the roaring frothy seas, And mounting flashing flaws ymatch the sky, And smoking, stifling parching fire dry With dankish pools agree and watery fen: And grisly Pluto's filthy feltered den, With starbright heaven shall sooner coupled be, And shining light with gloomy shades agree, And with the clear dry day the dewy night, Than unto servile lore of husband wight, That brutish wise in blood takes his delight, My heavy woeful mind can I address, While brother's death my heart does still possess. Oh that of heavenly powers the prince and sire, That shogges and shakes the earth with thu ̄ dring fire, And with his wondrous, fearful, cursed cracks, And strange mishapen monsters which he makes, Our fearful musing minds does soar amaze, Would coin some cureless burning wildfyre blaze, To pelt and pash with thumping fire bright, That devilish pate, that cruel cursed wight. We saw from heaven, with beams forthshoting far Doubtless a dreadful hairy, blazing star: That spouted out a mortal fiery flake, Whose force a princes blood can only slake: Even where that hayting carman sloe Boot With chilling cold all stark of frozen pole, Does guide aright Charles whirling running rote, In stead of night that never away does role. Lo now the open air in every street, With doggish tyrants breath, is poisoned, quite, And dreadful stars some sudden death do threat. To people ruled, by wicked Nero;s spright. So stern a freak, or mankind tyrant stout, Not Tellus with the Gods displeased brought out, When mighty Jove neglected she vphorlde Huge, ugly, monstrous Typhon to the world. A sorer plague, a cleaner scouring scourge, With bloody paws that cities bounds does purge, Is Nero dire, this cruel cursed wight. That does himself gainst God and man ydyght: And thrusts from sacred shrines their quiet port, And goodly temples gay he sancted sort: That cities dwellers puts from countries fort: That hath bereft his brother of his life, And launched his mother's sides with gory knife: Yet does this present lightsome day enjoy And leads his life, that does us soar annoy. Oh Father of heaven, in vain why dost thou throw Thy great unvanquished rattling thundering blow Upon the whistling woods and ample seas, With force of princely power thy wrath t'appease? Ou such and hurtful and pernicious freak, Thy endue and joust conceived ire to wreak. Why stay thy mighty puissant brands so long, Ere thou fling down thy rattling cracking throng? Oh Lord, that Nero once might pay the price, Of all his devilish deeds, and every vice, Whole wide world's tyrant stern where he a stroke Does bear: which he overlades with burdenous yoke Of princely sire born, but does defame, With beastly manners vile his princely name: Ah. no, now, long this court (alas) we see With heavy wrath of Gods displeased ire Hath overcharged be: which Venus dire With Messalina's monstrous ramping lust, She first hath brought adown into the dust. Who madly married to prince Claudius grace, But little myndful then of that same case, And not regarding much thappoynted pain, With cursed cressets married once again. To which unlucky incestuall brydall bed, That drosell dire that furious slut Erin, With hanging hair about her hellish head, And gyrt with snakes with deadly step went in. And flaming brands from spousal chamber catch, In both their bloods ybathd, hath quenched clean: And hath incensed prince Claudius burning thought In bloody thratling stroke to pass all mean. My mother first of wretches all the most, With stripe of deadly sword gave up her ghost. And now extinguished quite, left me forlorn, With dolours pining pangs and mourning worn. And after her in hellish term does hail, Unto the senseless souls of Pluto's jail Her make, and Brittannick her son that way: And first this ruinous court did she betray. Where pull you me poor wretch? alas, Into what banished exiles place, Would Nero have me for to pass, Or Fortune bids, with frowning face? If now with fainting strength quite coolde, And with my broils all wearied cease, And longer life she grant me woolde, If that she work for to increase, My sorrows great with deadly dart, Why is she then so much my foe, In country that I may not partly, And leave my life before I go? But now no help of health I feel, Alas I see my Brother's boat: This is the same, whose vaulted keel, His Mother once did set a clote. And now his piteous Sister I, Excluded clean from spousal place, Shall be so carried by and by: No force hath virtue in this case. No Gods there be my woes to wreck. The griefly, dreadful drab Eryn, Does wielded the world at nod and beck, Who can lament my state, wherein I am, alas, sufficiently? How can Aedon duly plain, My smarting streams at bears that I Do shed? whose wings I would be fame, If destinies would them grant, to wear. Then would I leave my mourning mates, As swiftly fled, as wings could bear, And so avoid these bloody pates. Then sitting sole in shirwood shirle, And hanging sure, by dandling twig With plaintive pipe I might out twarle My heavy tuned note so big. Lo me the tyrant stern will send To yrcksome shades and hellish spirits. Why wretch do I the tyiue thus spend? Draw me to death you to whose mights, False Fortune hath bequeathed me. I witness now the heavenly pour. What dost thou bedlam? leave to flee, With prayer to Gods, who on thee lower. I call to witness Tartar deep, And sprites of Hell revenging freaks Of heinous facts, in Worser geon steep, And Sire whom death deserved wreaks. I do not now repine to die, Deck up your Ship, and hoist your Sail, On frothing seas to winds on hie: Let him that guides the Helm not fail, To seek the shore of Pharian Land.
