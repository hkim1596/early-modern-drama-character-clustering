W Hat Snarl, my deer Democritus, how is't? You are a Courtier grown, I hear. I hope you do not share in their desires. Hold there, you'll run your self out of breath else: And now resolve me of the Lord Philautus: Is all that true that is reported of him? My sense will note be able to endure him. Is none his friend to tell him of his faults? Oh they are still in fashion: in them A wry neck is a comely president: Disorder, disagreement in their lives And manners is thought regular, their actions Are still authentic, if it be received; To be illiterate, is a point of state. But the worst thing which I dislike in him, Which he does more by words then Actium: He gives out that the Ladies dote upon him, And that he can command them at his pleasure, And swears, there's scarce and honest woman. How. You are deceived, There are a thousand chaste. 'Tis ill To be incredulous, when charity Exacts your belief: but let that pass: What will you say, if I find out a means To cure him of his folly? Nay, I'll do it: I'll make him in love, and do it. Suspend your judgement, till I confirm you. What is he? We all do wish, Your beauty, or your vanity were less. For by this means, that which would else commend you, Proves your disgrace, you take the edge of praise off, Is endue to you, by too much whetting it. You are the Centre of all women's love then. It seems that you are of opinion, There is no text of womankind so holy, But may be corrupted, though a Deity. But I think otherwise; and can justify it. What if I bring you now unto a beauty As glorious as the Sun, but in desire Cold, as the middle Region of the air, And free from all reflection of lust? You shall converse with her, and she shall feed Your sense with such discursive influence, And a voice sweeter than the Lydian tunes Jove would bow down his ear to, yet her blood Shall run as cold as Iulips through her veins: The springtide of her youth, shall swell with more Delights, then there be drops in April, yet she As chased as Salmacis, amid the streams. Plot summary

Plot — Holland's Leaguer
Six years ago, Faustina’s father sent her abroad to keep her away from her love, Fidelio, and made her swear not to marry for seven years. Now the old man is dead, she has returned to London, known only to Fidelio, but insists on keeping her oath for the last remaining year. Her brother Philautus is in love with himself, which distresses his friends and offends his wife, but gives his servant Ardelio the opportunity to steal from him by conveying away property into storage at an accomplice’s house. Fidelio plans to make him fall in love with Faustina; this will both cure him and test her fidelity. He explains it to Faustina as a plan to humiliate a lord who has cast aspersions on women. Philautus makes advances to Faustina on the assumption that all women are libidinous and enjoy being raped from time to time; but he is impressed by her militant chastity, and accedes to her demand that he prove his honour in battle. He later returns from the wars, cured of narcissism, and asks Faustina’s pardon for his earlier insolence. She identifies herself as his sister, and he gives his blessing to her marriage to Fidelio; so she agrees to abandon her oath before the seven years are out. On learning that Ardelio is a villain, Philautus dismisses him. He goes to collect the stolen goods, only to find that his partner in crime will not acknowledge that the items even exist. The ensuing argument is overheard, and the miscreants are both arrested, In the sub-plot, Agurtes intends to cheat the courtly name-dropper Trimalchio by introducing him to a young heiress - who is, in reality, his daughter and lifelong accomplice Millicent.
