Neither my lord, for as he was surprised, Begirt with weapons, and with enemies round, I did your highness message to them all, Demanding him of them, entreating rather, And said, upon the honour of my name, That I would undertake to carry him Unto your highness, and to bring him back. I found them at the first inexorable, The earl of Warwick would not bide the hearing, Mortimer hardly, Pembroke and Lancaster Spoke least: and when they flatly had denied, Refusing to receive me pledge for him, The earl of Pembroke mildly thus bespoke. My lords, because our sovereign sends for him, And promises he shall be safe returned, I will this undertake, to have him hence, And see him redelivered to your hands. From the lieutenant of the tower my lord. It shall be done my lord. My lord, be not pensive, we are your friends, Men are ordained to live in misery, Therefore come, dalliance dangereth our lives. Heeres channel water, as our charge is given, Fit down, for weel be Barbars to your grace. Why strive you thus, your labour is in vain? Betwixt theirs and yours, shall be no enmity, Come, come, away, now put the torches out, Weel enter in by darkness to Killingworth. Guard the king sure, it is the earl of Kenned. Keep them a sunder, thrust in the king. Edmund, yield thou thy self, or thou shalt die. The court is where lord Mortimer remains, Thither shall your honour go, and so farewell. Gurney, I wonder the king die not, Being in a vault up to the knees in water, To which the channels of the castle run, From whence a damp continually arises, That were enough to poison any man, Much more a king brought up so tenderly. He hath a body able to endure, More then we can enflict, and therefore now, Let us assail his mind another while. But stay, whose this? Gurney, it was left unpointed for the nonce, Edwardum occidere nolite timere, Thats his meaning. I stay a while, thou shalt have answer straight. This villain's sent to make away the king. And when the murders done, See how he must be handled for his labour, Pereat iste: let him have the king, What else, here is the keys, this is the lake, Do as you are commanded by my lord. I my good Lord, I would it were undone. Gurney my lord is fled, and will I fear, Betray us both, therefore let me sly. I humbly thank your honour. My lords, king Edward greets you all by me. My lords, it is his majesty's request, And in the honour of a king he swears, He will but talk with him and send him back. Then if you will not trust his grace in keep, My lords, I will be pledge for his return. My lord of Lancaster, what say you in this? His majesty, hearing that you had take ̄ Gaveston, Intreateth you by me, yet but he may See him before he die, for why he says, And sends you word, he knows that die he shall, And if you gratify his grace so far, He will be mindful of the courtesy. 'Tis very kindly spoke my lord of Pembroke, Your honour hath and adamant of power, To draw a prince. Yea my good lord, for Gaveston is dead. The earl of Warwick seazde him on his way, For being delivered unto Penbrookes men, Their lord road home, thinking his prisoner safe, But ere he came, Warwick in ambush lay, And bore him to his death, and in a trench Strake off his head, and marched unto the camp. Fear not my Lord, weel do as you come maund. I will madam. Very well. Fear not you that. I fear me that this cry will raise the town, And therefore let us take horse and away.
