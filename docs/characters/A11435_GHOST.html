Thou canst not fly me, There is no Cavern in the Earth's, vast entrails But I can through as pearcant as the light, And find thee, though thou were't entombed in stone, Thou canst not catch my unsubstantial partly, For I am air, and am not to be touched. From flaming fires of burning Phlegeton, I have a time limited to walk, Until the morning Cock shall summon me For to retire to misty Erebus. My pilgrimage has no cessation, Until I bring thee with me to the place Where Rhadamant, and sable Aeacus dwell Alive or dead, 'tis I that must enjoy thee, To tell the story where we spirits live Would pluck Vermilion from thy Rosy cheeks, And make them pale, as Snowy Apennines, And from thine eyes draw liquid streams of tears More full of issue then a steepy Fountain, Alive or dead I must, and will enjoy thee, Think on thy promise. Thy time is not yet come; i'm now exiled I may not touch thee while thou art with chilled. All things keep their time! All things keep their time. Death's eldest daughter sleep with silencies Has charmed yond beldames, no jarring clock Nor murmuring wind dares oppose just fate. Awake found mortal ne'reto sleep again, Now is the time I come to claim my promise, Alive or dead I must, and will enjoy thee. With expedition; The Ferryman attends thee at the verge Of Cocitus, and sooty Acheron, And he shall waft thee into Tartary, Where perjury, and falsehood finds reward There shalt thou read thy history of faults, And amongst the furies find just recompense, I'll bring thee over Turrets, Towers, and Steeples, Over shady Groves, brineish Mears, and Brooks, The flattering Sea to me is navigable, Over steepy Mountains, and the craggy Rocks, Whose heights Kiss Stars, and stop the flying Clouds We'll through as swift as Swallows in recourse. The Chauntecleere summons my retreat, Signing a perjod to my pilgrimage; From nipping frosts, and penetrating blasts Could Snows, black thaws, and misty killing dews. I'll led thee to the ever-flaming Furnace, That Like a Fever fed by opposite meats, Engenders, and consumes it self with heat. I'll Pierce the Air as with a thunder bolt, And make thy passage free; make speed away Thy broken contract, now thou goest to pay.

Plot — The Vow Breaker, or The Fair Maid of Clifton
Though Young Bateman is betrothed to Anne Boot and loved by her, her father reneges on the ===== PAGE 347 ===== 1628 arrangement. Meanwhile, war looms: England enters an alliance with a federation of Scottish lords hostile to the French Queen Regent of Scotland, and troops are being recruited to march north. Bateman joins up, hoping that his absence will make Old Boot’s heart grow fonder, and Anne vows to love only him; but her father puts pressure on her to marry German, and, seduced by his wealth, she agrees. The French are vanquished, and Bateman distinguishes himself in battle; but he is troubled by dreams about Anne, and returns home on the day of her wedding, just missing the ceremony. German goes away on a long business trip, leaving Anne in her father’s house. Old Bateman sues Boot for breach of the pre-contract; Young Bateman hangs himself outside Anne’s door, and his ghost haunts Anne, invisible to the rest of her family. Anne gives birth to a baby girl. While the women around her sleep, she sees the ghost and runs out of the house; following her footsteps in the snow, the household find her drowned in the river. The two bereaved fathers, Boot and Bateman, commiserate together. In Scotland, Sir Gervase Clifton insults the Queen Regent by drawing an unfavourable comparison with Queen Elizabeth, and captures Martigues in a single combat, but releases him with honour. Joshua the Puritan intends to hang his cat for Sabbath-breaking - she killed a mouse on Sunday - but the cat is saved by the arrival of a French ambassador. The English attack Leith, and Clifton captures a French colonel’s armour. Hostilities are ended by diplomatic means, and Scotland is to be demilitarized. The English and French lords feast together, and Joshua gets drunk. The English troops return south to meet the Queen at Nottingham; the common soldiers plan to put on a play for her, but decide on a morris dance instead. She meets the civic authorities and rewards her military commanders.
