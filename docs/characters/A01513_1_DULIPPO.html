In good time have I spied my good master. Yet sir let me sometime do my duty unto you, especially where no body hears. Good. Yea excellent, we have as good as wone the wager. Hear you me, yesternight in the evening I wall ked out, and found Pasiphilo, and with small entreating I had him home to supper, where by such means as I v said, he became my great friend, and told me the whole or der of our adversaries determination: yea and what Dam mon does intend to do also, and hath promised me that from ̄ time to time, what he can espy he will bring me word of it. I know him very well, he can not deceive me, and this that he hath told me I know must needs be true. That Damon had purposed to give his daughter in marriage to this doctor, upon y e dower that he hath proffered. Stay awhile, you will understand me before you hear me. I answered to that, I was ready to make his the like dower. Abide, you hear not the worst yet. Worse? why what assurance could you suppose that I might make without some special consent from Phi logono my father? In deed you have lost your time: for the books that you toss now a days, treat of small science. I said further, that I received letters lately from my father, whereby I understood that he would be here very shortly to perform all that I had proffered: therefore I required him to request Damo ̄ on my behalf, that he would stay his promise to the doctor for a fourthnight or more. Comfort your self man, and trust in me: there is a salve for every soar, and doubt you not, to this mischief we shall find a remedy. Well, harken a while then: this morning I took my horse and road into the fields to solace my self, and as I passed the Ford beyonds S. Anthony's gate, I met at the foot of the hill a gentleman riding with two or three men, and as me thought by his habit and his looks, he should be none of the wise . He saluted me, and I him: I asked him from whence he came, and whither he would? he answered that he had come from Venice, then from Padua, now was going to Ferrara, and so to his country, which is Scienna: As soon as I know him to be a Scenese, suddenly lifting up mine eyes, as it were with and admiration I said unto him, are you a Scenese, and come to Ferrara? why not, said he: quoth I, half and more with a trembling voice, know you the danger that should ensue if you be known in Ferrara to be a Scenese? he more than half amazed, de sired me earnestly to tell him what I meant. I believe you, but Harken to me. I answered him in this sort: Gentleman, because I have heretofore found very courteous entertainment in your country, being a student there, I account my self as it were bound to a Scenese, and therefore if I knew of any mishap towards any of that country, God forbid but I should disclose it: and I marvel that you know not of the injury that your countrymen offered this other day to the Ambassadors of County Hercule. If you will harken a while, you shall find them no tales, but that they appertain to you more than you think for. I told him further, these Ambassadors of County Hercule had dyuers Mules, Wagons, and Cham rettes, laden with diverse costly jewels, gorgeous furniture, & other things which they carried as presents, passing that way to the king of Naples the which were not only stayed in Sciene by the officers who ̄ you call Customers, but searched, ransacked, tossed & turned, & in the end exacted for tribute, as if they had be the goods of a mean merchant. Oh how impacie ̄ to you are: I pray you stay a while. You shall hear by and by a thing as fit for our purpose, as any could have happened. Plot summary

Plot — Supposes
Polynesta loves Dulippo, whose suit has been promoted by her nurse, and who is supposed a poor servant. In fact he is not Dulippo at all but Erostrato, who two years ago gained access to Polynesta by swapping identities with his servant and entering her father Damon’s household. Meanwhile Cleander seeks her hand, and the supposed Erostrato (the true Dulippo) becomes his rival for trust with him. Cleander appears to offer the best dowry, but Damon gives ‘Erostrato’ a fortnight’s grace pending his father Philogano’s arrival to underwrite his counter-offer. ‘Erostrato’ duly arranges to produce a false Philogano, recruiting a Sienese gentleman for the role by persuading him that a diplomatic incident has made Ferrara an unsafe place for a Sienese visitor. Overhearing an argument between two women servants, Damon discovers that ‘Dulippo’ and Polynesta have been sleeping together. ‘Dulippo’ is apprehended and imprisoned in the dungeon of his master’s house, and therefore unable to warn his servant of the arrival of the true Philogano: he misses his son and has come to take him back to Sicily. By chance, Philogano meets the true Dulippo, who refuses to acknowledge him. After the locals confirm that they know the young man as Erostrato, Philogano suspects that he has murdered his master and stolen his identity, and seeks legal assistance from Cleander. However, Dulippo turns out to be Cleander’s son, whom he lost as a child when evacuating Otranto during the Turkish conquest; he was later captured by Sicilians and sold to Philogano. The exchange of identities is revealed, allaying Damon’s worries about his daughter’s reputation. Cleander withdraws his suit: he no longer needs to marry and beget an heir now that his lost son has been restored to him. The true Erostrato is confirmed as Polynesta’s future husband.
