Full speech

Thanks Landoys, and here Earl Richmond's vows, If their kind promises take but effect, That as they have promised I be made King, I will so deal in governing the state, Which now lies Like a savage shultred grove, Where brambles, briers, and thorns, overgrow those sprigs, Which if they might but spring to their effect, And not be crossed so by their contraries, Making them subject to these outrages, Would prove such members of the Commonweal, That England should in them be honoured, As much as ever was the Roman state, When it was governed by the Counsels rule, And I will draw my sword brave countrymen, And never leave to follow my resolve, Till I have mowed those brambles, briers and thorns That hinder those that long to do us good. Yet good father, one word more ere you depart, What number do you think the kings power to be? And we hardly five thousand, being beset with many enemies, hoping upon a few friends, yet despair not Richmond, but remember thou fightest in right, to defend thy country from the tyranny of and usurping tyrant, therefore Richmond go forward, the more dangerous the battle is in attaining, it proves the more honourable being obtained. Then forward Richmond, God and saint George, for me.
