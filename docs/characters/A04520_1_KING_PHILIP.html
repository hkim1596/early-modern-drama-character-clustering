And in good time, welcome my Lord Chattil lion: What news? will John accord to our command? This bitter wind must nip somebodies spring: Sudden and brief, why so, 'tis harvest weather. But say Chattilion, what persons of account are with him? Me seems John, and over-daring spirit Effects some frenzy in thy rash approach, Treading my Confines with thy armed troops. I rather looked for some submiss reply Touching the claim thy Nephew Arthur makes To that which thou unjustly dost usurp. But here's no proof that shows your son a king. Lordings forbear, for time is coming fast, That deeds may try what words can not determine, And to the purpose for the cause you come. Me seems you set right in chance of war, Yielding no other reasons for your claim, But so and so, because it shall be so. So wrong shall be subornd by trust of strength: A tyrants practice to invest himself, Where weak resistance gives wrong the way. To check the which, in holy lawful arms, I, in the right of Arthur, Geffreys son, Am come before this city of Angiers, To bar all other false supposed claim, From whence, or howsoever the error springs. And in his quarrel on my princely word, I'll fight it out unto the latest man. Summon them, I will not believe it till I see it, and when I see it, I'll soon change it. Then hear me speak in the behalf of Arthur son of Geoffrey, elder brother to John, his title manifest, with out contradiction, to the crown & kingdom of England, with Angiers, & diverse towns on this side the sea: will you acknowledge him your liege Lord, who speaks in my word, to entertain you with all favors, as beseems a King to his subjects, or a friend to his wellwillers: or stand to the peril of your conte ̄ pt, when ̄ his title is proved by the sword.
