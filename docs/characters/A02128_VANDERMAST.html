That Lordly are the buildings of the Town, Spacious the rooms, and full of pleasant walks: But for the Doctors, how that they be learned, It may be meanly, for ought I can hear. I say, of Piromancy. The Cabalists that write of Magic spells, As Hermes, Melchre, and Pythagoras, Affirm that amongst the quadruplicity Of elemental essence, Terra is but thought, To be a punctum squared to the rest: And that the compass of ascending elements Exceed in bigness as they do in height; Judging the concave Circle of the Sun, To hold the rest in his Circumference; If then, as Hermes says, the fire be greatest, Purest, and only gives shapes to spirits: Then must these Demones that haunt that place, Be every way superior to the rest. Each Scholar in the Necromantic spells Can do as much as Bungay hath performed. But as Alemena's bastard raised this Tree, So will I raise him up as when he lived, And cause him pull the Dragon from his seat, And tear the branches piecemeal from the root, Hercules, Prodi, Prodi, Hercules. Now Bungay, if thon canst by Magic charm The Fiend, appearing Like great Hercules, From pulling down the branches of the Tree, Then art thou worthy to be counted learned. Cease Hercules, until I give thee charge. Mighty Commander of this English I'll, Henry, come from the stout Plantagenets, Bungay is learned enough to be a Fryer: But to compare with Jaques Vandermast, Oxford and Cambridge must go seek their Cells, To find a man to match him in his Art. I have given nonplus to the Paduans, To them of Sien, Florence, and Bologna, Rheims, Louvain, and fair Rotterdam, Franckford, Lutrech, and Orleans: And now must Henry, if he do me right, Crown me with Laurel, as they all have done. Now Hercules, I charge thee to thy task, Pull off the golden branches from the root. Never before was't known to Ʋ andermast, That men held Devils in such obedient awe. Bacon does more then Art, or else I fail. Wherein darest thou dispute with me? Before rich Europes Worthies put thou forth The doubtful question unto Ʋ andermast. Rather these earthly Geomantike spirits, Are dull and Like the place where they remain: For when proud Lucifer fell from the heavens, The spirits and Angels that did sin with him, Retained their local essence as their faults, All subjects under Luna's Continent, The which offended less, hang in the fire, And second faults did rest within the air, But Lucifer and his proudhearted fiends, Were thrown into the Centre of the earth, Having less understanding then the rest, As having greater sin, and lesser grace. Therefore such gross and earthly spirits do serve, For Jugglers, Witches, and vild Sorcerers, Whereas the Piromanticke Genii, Are mighty, swift, and of far reaching power. But grant that Geomancy hath most force, Bungay, to please these mighty Potentates, Prove by some instance what thy Art can do. What wilt thou do? Well done. Jove's bastard son, thou Libian Heroules, Pull off the sprigs from off the Hesperian Tree, As once thou didst to win the golden fruit. Hercules. Fiat. What art thou that questionst thus? Bacon, wilt thou dispute? Raised Hercules to ruinate that tree, That Bungay mounted by his Magic spells. By all the Thrones, and Dominations, Virtues, Powers, and mighty Hierarchies, I charge thee to obey to Vandermast. Lordly thou lookest, as if that thou Wertenberg learned? Thy countenance, as if science held her seat Between the circled arches of thy brows.
