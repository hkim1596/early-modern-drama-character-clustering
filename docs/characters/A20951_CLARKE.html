Why sir isle do it for you at any time, Provided as you have given your word, I may have Susan Mosbie to my wife: For as sharp witted Poets, whose sweet verse Make heavenly gods break of their Nector draughts, And lay their ears down to the lowly earth: Use humble promise to their sacred Muse, So we that are the Poets favourite, Must have a love, I, Love is the Painters Muse. That makes him frame a speaking countenance. A weeping eye that witnesses hearts grief, Then tell me Master Mosbie shall I have his? What is it M. Mosbie? How now Michael how does my Mistress, And all at home? Then brother to requite this courtesy, You shall command my life my skill and all. You know not me, that ask such questions: Let it suffice, I know you love him well, And feign would have your husband made away: Wherein trust me you show a noble mind, That rather then yowl live with him you hate, Yowl venture life, and die with him you love, The Like will I do for my Susans sake. Though I am loath, because it touches life, Yet rather or I'll leave sweet Susans love, I'll do it, and with all the haste I may. But for whom is it? Very easily, I'll tell you straight, How I do work of these Empoisoned drugs, I fasten on my spectacles so close, As nothing can any way offend my sighed, Then as I put a lease within my nose, So put I rubarbe to avoid the smell, And softly as another work I paint, Within this ten days, I How does she, and all the rest? Sick, of what disease? A fear, of what? A fever God forbid. M. Michael the spleen prickles you. Go too, you carry and eye over mistress susan. Why more from a Painter, then from a serving creature Like your self. What mean you by that? Such another word wilcost you a cuff or a knock Would susans love lay upon this stroke, I hear it is, the very touch is death. I know your mind, and here I have it for you, Put but a dram of this into his drink, Or any kind of broth that he shall eat: And he shall die within and hour after. Yonder's your husband, Mosbie isle be gone. You did. A match, I faith sir I the day is mine, The Painter, lays his cullours to the life, His pensel draws no shadows in his love. Susan is mine. I thank you mistress Arden, I will in, And if fair Susan, and I can make a gree, You shall command me to the uttermost, As far as either goods or life may stretch. Exit Clark.
