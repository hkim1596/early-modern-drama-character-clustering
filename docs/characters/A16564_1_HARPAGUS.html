Though all who partners are of the earth and air, Still whilst tapestred with this azure pale, If for nought else, yet for those gifts least rare, To serve the all-powerful powers, should never fail; Yet there are some whom success hath designed, Whose names are written in respected scrolls, WHom benefits (not ordinary) bind To love them more then life, yea, then their souls: Of those that you are one, your deeds declare, Of whom amidst innumerable broils, Even from your cradle they have had a care, And led you safe through many dangerous toils; Though of the troubles of your youth I see, You have not herd the wonderful discourse, I them remember, who did chance to be And actor in your Tragicke-Comicke course. Who would not wonder at thy wondrous fate, Whom (even or born) destruction did attend, Whilst ere thou couldst offend, pursued by hate, Even then to end what now shall never end? Your mother first her father's mind did sting, Whilst once he dreamed, which yet his soul confounds, That of a tree which from her womb did spring, Umbrageous branches, darkened Asia's bounds; Then to the Magies straight he gave in charge To try what this strange vision did presage, Who having studied their dark Art at large, Gave this response with a prophetic rage: That once his daughter should a son bring forth, Who should (by valour gaining great renown) Make vanquished Asia witness of his worth; But from his grandfather first reave the Crown. This to Astyages a terror bred, Who (vainly bent to scorn the heavens decree) His daughter (out of policy) would wed, To some weak stranger of no great degree. And to Cambyses, who of her made choice, He, for his Country (then contemned) gave ear, Whom by your birth the Princess did rejoice, And gave her father further cause of fear, "Thus tyranny (their brood whose courage fails) "Does force the Parents in despair to fallen, "To fight a dastard, proud when it prevails, "But yet (as feared of all,) does still fear all; "And tyrants no security can find, "For every shadow frights a guilty mind. This Monarch then who could not dream of harms, Whose guards did glance all still with steel arrayed, Then whilst he lived secure from foreign Arms, A babe, scarce born, and his, did make afraid. And whilst Lucina the last help did make, As if some ugly monster had been born, A Minotaur, a Centaur, or a Snake, The peoples terror, and the Mother's scorn; The Grandchild's birth, which justly should impart, To grandfathers the greatest cause of joys, Did (long ere wounded, making him to smart) Involve him in a maze of sad annoys; And to prevent what did him fondly fright, By giving cause of a deserved hate, He sought by robbing you the newfound light, To make your birth and burial of one date. Soon after this he sent for me in haste, Whom at that time (and not in vain) he loved, And told the sum of all things that were passed, By which his marble-minde seemed nothing moved; Yet in the same, as he would let me know, Though pity none, some horror did remain, Whilst damned in substance, to seem clear in show, Your blood his heart, but not his hand should stain. "Thus having lulled asleep their judgement still, "The wicked would extenuate their crimes, "Not knowing those who but allow of ill, "As actors guilty, differ but in times. With his vile fault he would have burdened me, Whom straight he charged and Innocent to slay; I promised to perform his rash decree, Well weighing whom, not what I should obey; When I had parted from his Highness face, And carried you (then swaddled) with me too, Whilst horror did congeal my blood, a space I stood perplexed, not knowing what to do, And as to purge my partly) even shedding tears, By troops of passion grief, my soul assailed, Thus (when distressed for easing others fears,) Th'intended death of you, your murderers wailed; For him I sent a servant of mine own, Who for the time was herdsman to the King, To whom I made all my Commission known, But as enjoined to him, showed every thing; Delivering you with and unwilling breath, WHom of pure gold, a glistering robe arrayed, I threatened him with many a cruel death, If that your death were any way delayed; Straight then to execute the Tyrants doom, He from my sighed did all astonished go, Too great a charge for such a simple Groom, The show of Majesty amazed him so; What man (not wondering) can by deeds behold The providence of all-commanding Jove, Whose brazen edicts cannot be controlled: "Firm are the Statutes of the States above: "That mortal whom a Deities favour shields, "No worldly force is able to confound, "He may securely walk through dangers fields, "Times and occasions are to serve him bound: For lo, before the herdsman was come home, His wife had chanced a breathless child to bear, Who wondered so to see her husband come, While by his conscience crushed, he quaked for fear; And straight she curious grew to know the form, How he a babe so beautiful obtained; Who her of all did suddenly inform, And to what cruelty he was constrained; She quickly then the occasion to embrace, (No doubt inspired by some celestial power) Prayed that her infant might supply your place, Yet where no beasts his body might devo re, So shall we have (says she) a double gain, Since our own child shall get a stately tomb, And we a Princely brood, which may remain, Still nursed with us as th'issue of my womb. The husband liked so well his wives design, That he performed all what she did require, And when I had directed one of mine, This Tragedies last act, who might inquire: My man who spied a babe there breathless lie, With that rich funeral furniture arrayed, Told what the fellow told, (a generous lie) So that thus tried, I trusted what they said. I end, Time (posting with houre-feather'd wings) Had given you strength, with others of your years You haunted games, not nephews unto Kings, But for that time admitted for your Peers, They fail call fortune blind, she sighed bewrayed, And your authority by lot enlarged, In pastoral sport, who still the Sceptre swayed, And as but born for that, that best discharged: With other children then, as once it chanced, A Nobleman of Medea's Son remained, Who swollen with envy to see you advanced, Your childish charge with scornful words disdained; You raging at that proud attempt of his, Did punish him, as it became a Prince, I doubt now (Sir) if that you think of this: The rest of rashness did your deed convince. The child's great Father did inform the King, How that so base a boy his Son abused, And of the guard one hasted you to bring, As for and odious crime to be accused; But when the King (expostulating long) By terrors strived to cast your courage down: You boldly said, that you had done no wrong, To punish one who had contemned your Crown; You so magnanimous amazed to find, Whilst pausing long with and attentive Eye, That speech imperious told the King your kind: Whose brood but eagles durst have soared so high? The feigned Father to the King was brought, Who (feared for torture) telling truth in time, Where he reward deserved, but pardon sought, As if the saving you had been a crime. Then (as it seemed) delighted with the rest, The King did cause a sumptuous feast prepare, And me desired as his most special guest, That with my Son I would to Court repair; When I was come, the King great joy disclosed, And soothed my words which did his chance applaud, But for another end then I supposed: "What fairer cloak then courtesy for fraud? When absence of the Sun did darkness breed, The Candles light inheriting his place, On my Sons flesh they caused my self to feed, Then did upbraid me with his bloodless face; What anguish, or what rage overflowed my soul, A loving Father may imagine best, Yet at that time I did my rage controull, But laid it high up in a stormy breast. "Lo how those wretches whom the heavens would wrack, "(To plagues expos ) of judgement are unarmed: "The King of me his Captain straight did make, "And look't for help of him whom he had harmed; "Yet was th'old wrong so rooted in my heart, "My Countries thraldom, and mine own disgrace, "That all the horrors mischief could impart "Seemed nought to me, so my disdain took place. This enterprise at first so well did speed, That since, your Greatness still began to rise, Which may by time to brave a story breed, As may be precious in all Princes eyes. No doubt that Dame this trouble hardly bears, Who only seemed for him to Like of life, I herd him (whilst she bathed his breast with tears) Oft wish by proof to merit such a wife. When their farewell was sealed, last speeches spent, She kissed the Coach that did contain her trust, And with eyes big with pearl, gazed where he went, Still till her sighed was choked with clods of dust. I'll raise a Pyramid's of Croesus spoils, Where of their worth each partly shall be comprised, But how to do in these tumultuous broils, Now time requires that you were well advised: Your adversary does attend your will; This haughty Town for fear to fallen does bow, And therefore pardon, ransom, quite, or kill, Do what you please, none can control us now.

Plot — Croesus Cw
Solon visits Croesus, hoping to persuade him to give up his immoderate love of gold and think about less transitory things. He explains that all mortality is subject to change, and tells Croesus stories about the importance of posthumous fame, but Croesus considers them irrelevant to his situation as a king. Solon realizes that Croesus wili change only by losing the power which has made him arrogant. Adrastus is banished from Phrygia after accidentally killing his brother. (His intended victim was the man who poisoned his girlfriend.) Croesus has a nightmare about the death of his son Atys, and becomes excessively protective: he makes Atys marry in order to secure the succession, appoints him a bodyguard, and bans the type of sharp implement which caused his death in the dream. Mysia is ravaged by a wild boar, and its people appeal to Croesus for help. He is happy to hear that they don’t want his money, but distressed that they ask instead that he send Atys to hunt the boar. Atys protests at his father’s reluctance, so Croesus accedes, assigning Adrastus to look after him during the mission. Atys is killed, and Adrastus kills himself in remorse. In the past, Astyages, King of Media and Croesus’ brother-in-law, was perturbed by a prophecy that he would be deposed by his grandson. When the child was born, he tried to have it killed; but the herdsman who was given the job spared its life and adopted it as a replacement for his own stillborn baby. During his youth, Cyrus’ princely behaviour led to his identification. Astyages served Harpagus a banquet of his own son’s flesh, so Harpagus contacted Cyrus and raised a rebellion. Now Cyrus has led the Persian army to depose Astyages. Against his counsellor’s advice, Croesus plans to intervene and conquer Persia, but Cyrus anticipates him and attacks pre-emptively, ordering that Croesus be taken alive. Croesus is nearly killed by a Persian soldier, but saved when his dumb son cries out and identifies him. Cyrus intends to burn him at the stake, but is impressed by Croesus’ acknowledgement that Solon was right to warn him not to trust in worldly things. Cyrus frees him, and orders restraint in his soldiers’ looting.
