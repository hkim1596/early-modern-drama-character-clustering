The sign of the more grace, but stay, here sits one methinks at his prayers, let us see who it is. So thinks me too, you sirrah, what victuals have you about you? Truth so it should be, but methinks I smell meat about Thee. O villain, see how he hath got victailes, bred, beef, and beer, where the King commanded upon pain of death none should eat for so many days, no not the sucking infant. Five days. Come lets draw him away perforce. I sir. 'Tis Adam, the Smith's man, how now Adam ? How devoutly he sits at his orisons, but stay, Me thinks I feel a smell of some meat or bred about him. Well, for all your words, we must search you. Villain thou shalt be hanged for it. I marry must thou. I warrant Thee, shalt want none of these. I marry. Come away knave, wilt thou stand feeding now? How duly the men of Nineveh keep the proclama cion, how are they armed to repentance? we have searched through the whole City & have not as yet found one that breaks the fast.

Plot summary

Plot — A Looking Glass for London and England
Triumphant over the King of Jerusalem, the Assyrian king Rasni declares himself divine and marries his sister, Remilia; a viceroy who objects is banished and the flatterer Radagon given his place. Inordinately proud of her beauty, Remilia is struck by lightning. Rasni is unable to revive her, and buries her in state; but is encouraged by Radagon to think of the living rather than the dead, and to take Alvida, wife of the King of Paphlagonia. When her husband objects to the adulterous liaison, she poisons him. Alvida continues to be unfaithful: her ambition attracts her to Rasni, but her sexual appetite favours the Viceroy of Cilicia, so she takes both. Thrasybulus and Alcon are both cheated by a usurer to whom they are trying to repay money: he keeps them talking beyond the due hour and then secures possession of Thrasybulus’ farm and Alcon’s cow as a penalty for non-payment. They attempt to sue him, but he bribes both the judge and their lawyer, and they lose their cases. Alcon appeals to Radagon, his son, to help his starving family, but Radagon is too proud to acknowledge his parent, and Rasni has him ejected from the court. Radagon’s mother curses him, and: he is swallowed by subterranean fire. An angel orders Jonah to preach against Nineveh’s wickedness. He is reluctant, and takes a ship to Tarshish instead. The ship encounters a storm, which Jonah identifies as a sign of divine displeasure at his disobedience. He has himself thrown overboard, and the storm abates. A whale swallows him, then belks him up on shore. He goes to Nineveh as ordered. These events are watched and commented on by the prophet Hosea, brought from Jerusalem by an angel.
