Full speech

Bravely resolved Ere long, assure thy self, Weel seat thee in thy ancient dignity, And force to Caesar homage, and to Room: And, though we fear not one particular man, Yet, for because we truly are informed, That Caradoc is strong and puissant, For ten days we intend to make a truce, And in the mean time to make strong our host: Which if he do refuse, the time expired, To render up thy right, which he detains; War, Like some gnawing vulture shall attend Unto their final ruin, and their end. And to that purpose, Marcus Gallicus Shall as a Legate both from Room and us, Instantly give them knowledge: the time's but short: And till the date's expire, prepare for sport. Exeunt. Yield thee, proud Welshman, or weel force thee yield. Assault him then. Hold, noble Welshman. Thou seest it is impossible to escape, Hadst thou the strength of mighty Hercules. If thou wilt yield, I vow by all the gods That do protect Caesar and mighty Room, By all the honours that the Roman power Have wone, since Romulus did built their walls, Because thou art a man vnparaleld, Of honourable courage, I'll engage My life for thine to Caesar for thy freedom. Caesar himself admires thy fortitude, And will with honour welcome thee at Room. He is a King, whom baseness never touched, And scorns to pluck a Lion by the beard, Being a carcase. Speak, will you trust our oath? Then, Manlius Valens, you shall bear him thither; And for your guard, take the nineth Legion, Surnamed, The valiant: and by the way, At London stays his daughter, wife and brother: Let them to Caesar bear him company. Exit Caradoc. Farewell, brave Prince. Now Romans once again, Sing the Welshmen's glory is eclipsed, Let us provide to meet Lord Morgan, And Lord Constantine, Venusius, and the rest that gather head, And seat Prince Codigune in what's his right, That now have gathered strong and fresh supply. This battle shall add honour to our name, And with triumphant Laurel crown our fame. Exeunt. Now Britain's, though the wrongs done to this Prince. And to our selves, deserve a sharp revenge; Yet, for we pity the effusion And havoc that these cruel broils intend, Once more in peace we crave this Princes right, Which your weak Army can no way detain. Perhaps you stand upon the idle Hope's Of Caradoc : Know then, you are deceived: For he's our prisoner, and to Room is sent With Manlius Valens to the Emperor. Then yield your selves, or try the chance of war. Now, valiant Romans, once more do we tread Upon the bosom of the Bryttish ground: And by the gods that do protect great Room, Weel now acquit great Caesars foul disgrace, Or die Like Romans in this foray ne place. Come, Lady, you must go along with us. Now Caradoc, thy life is in our hands: Behold, thou art in girded with a whole host. And couldst thou borrow force of beasts and men, Thou couldst by no means escape.

Plot summary

Plot — The Valiant Welshman (Caradoc the Great)
The Earl of. Monmouth seeks to usurp old King Octavian’s throne, and has won two significant victories over him. Cadallan and his sons come to support Octavian. Cadallan is killed in the ensuing battle, but his son Caradoc avenges him by killing Monmouth. Re-established on the throne, Octavian rewards Caradoc with marriage to his daughter and heir, Guinevere. This displeases his bastard son, Codigune, who had regarded Guinevere as his only obstacle to the succession. King Guiderius has failed to pay tribute to the Romans, so the Emperor Claudius invades Britain. Guiderius appeals to Octavian, and Caradoc is sent, fresh from his wedding, to offer military aid. In his absence, Codigune conspires with Gloucester and Cornwall: they poison Octavian and imprison Guinevere, and Codigune seizes power. Gloucester writes warning Guiderius not to trust Caradoc, so Guiderius, about to go into battle, orders the Welsh troops to observe from a hilltop and intervene only ifa British defeat seems likely. Caradoc disregards this, joins the battle in disguise, and bests Claudius in hand- to-hand combat; he refuses Claudius’ offer to buy his freedom, but accepts a token of the Emperor’s friendship, a golden lion. Prince Gald is impressed by Caradoc’s single-handed contribution to the British victory, accompanies him back to Wales, and marries his sister Voada. Finding Codigune installed as King of Wales, Caradoc offers to settle matters in single combat. Codigune is beaten, refuses an offer to make him Viceroy of North Wales, and flees to the Romans. Cornwall submits to Caradoc, who is crowned King. Gloucester flees to make more mischief: he persuades a witch to create a ravaging monster which Caradoc will be expected to fight, and which will kill him. The Romans send Caradoc an ultimatum, requiring him to resign the throne to Codigune; at the same time, he is summoned to deal with the monster, a giant serpent. He gives priority to the serpent, and defeats it with the help of a magic herb, then disposes of the witch and forces her son Bluso to swear that in future he will use his magic powers only for good. Gloucester hangs himself. While Caradoc is away, Cornwall hands over the town of Gloucester to the Romans. Gallicus, the Roman legate who brought the ultimatum, falls in love with Voada and abducts her. Bluso enables Gald to visit her bedchamber, magically unseen.
