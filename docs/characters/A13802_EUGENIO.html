Lelio, his desire Enjoy your sister Flaviam, begets hope, Which Like a waking dream, makes false appearance Lively as truth it self. Lelio, had I no interest in your sister, The holy league of friendship should command me, Beside the seconding Sulpitia's love, Who to your nobleness commends her life. Signior Antonio! Happily welcome. Nor I. And Astrologer. Thanks ever, and as long shall my best service Wait on your will. Cricca our hope's in thee, Thou must instruct us: How good Cricca ? Your servant, sir. CRicca commend my service to my Mistress. It's true. And as from nights of Storms the glorious Sun Break's from the East, and chases thence the Clouds That choked the Air with horror; so her beauty Dispels sad darkness from my troubled thoughts, And clear's my heart. How is't my dearest Flaviam? Sweet soul despair not, weep not thus, Unless you wish my heart should lifeblood drop, Fast as your eyes do tears. What is't you fear? Not love my Flaviam ? Wrong not your judgement: rip up this amorous breast And in that Temple see a heart that burn's I'th' vestal sacrifice of chastest love, Before your beauty's Deity. Consider sweetest, I have a father rival in my love; And though no duty, reverence, nor respect Have power to change my thoughts: yet it's not comely With open violence to withstand his will; But by fair courses try to divert his mind From disproportioned affections. And if I cannot, then nor fear of anger, Nor life, nor lands, shall cross our purposes. Comfort your self sweet Flaviam : for your brother Second's our Hope's with his best services. Farewell Sweet Flaviam, rest contented with assurance Of my best love and service. I rest at your dispose: what you determine, With my best power I ratify; and Sulpitia, I dare be bold to promise, says no less. Dreams flow from thoughts of things we most desire, Or fear, and seldom prove true Prophets, would they did. Then were I now in full possession Of my best Flaviam: as I hope I shall be.

Plot summary

Plot — Albumazar
The neighbours Pandolfo and Antonio, both widowers, have arranged to marry each other’s daughters; but before they can do so, Antonio is lost and presumed dead on an overseas voyage, and the plan goes into abeyance because his son Lelio disapproves of Pandolfo as a potential brother-in-law. Albumazar’s accomplice Furbo has gained access to Pandolfo’s household, posing as his daughter’s tutor, and has learned of his .- amorous aspirations, and also his credulity about. magic and astrology. Albumazar plans to trick him, and sets about demonstrating his occult power. Ronca shows Pandolfo some of the astrologer’s amazing inventions: a telescope and an ‘otacousticon’ (actually a pair of ass’s ears) which allow him to see and hear things at a distance. Albumazar then predicts Pandolfo’s marriage, and also a stabbing which his associates then enact in a brawl. Pandolfo asks the astrologer to raise Antonio’s ghost, but instead Albumazar undertakes to transform the tenant farmer Trincalo into Antonio’s likeness for one day, so that he can give Flavia in marriage to Pandolfo. Trincalo agrees because this will give him access to Antonio’s household, and therefore to the maid Armellina, whom he loves. Pandolfo is required to prepare a room for the metamorphosis, laid out with valuable possessions. When he begins to have second thoughts about paying Albumazar, his servant Cricca suggests that he should pretend to have been robbed of his valuables; but when they are indeed stolen, Albumazar pretends in turn that the transformed Trincalo has just locked them away for safe keeping. After undergoing the fake metamorphosis, Trincalo is told he may not look at himself in a mirror for fear of breaking the spell. The real Antonio returns unexpectedly, and is taken for the transformed Trincalo: he does not understand when Pandolfo asks after his missing property, and his son and daughter, forewarned of the planned trick, beat him out of his own house.
