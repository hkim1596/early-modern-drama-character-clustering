Full speech

Master Major! We thank you for your entertainment, And for your princely present, a cup of gold! In gratefullnes we back return the keys With all the emblems of your government; We in our progress, are a sojourner, Not and inhabitant, we will be so with you; A welcome fuller of bounty, virtue, love, We have not seen; therefore to gratulate As a small token of our princely love, On, to your former motion made for Trent. You'd have it navigable to Gainsborough So to Boston, Kingston, Humber, and Hull; But, what are the causes? I have tasted your welcome, and would feign Grant your design, so you give reason. Ya'r a merry man Master Major. On, to your Charter. You shall enjoy your wishes; We thank you; Welcome renowned John of Wilton, And you the war Like Heroes of his train, Warlike Clifton; fame has been before thee, And with her shrill Trump sent your praises home, Ever your arrival; rise noble John of Wilton; The only champion of Elizabeth. And they shall have them; welcome bold Arguile, Thank thou the God of batles, that hast given Prosperity to our first enterprise, Being the first Batle that we ever waged, Linked victory unto a virgin's arm, For which we render thee all attributes, Guarded by thee, and these our loving Subject's, We fear no Spanish force, nor Frenchmen's braves, Let Austria brag; and Room, and Italy Send out their poisoned Darts; dreadless we stand Protected by thy never failing power, Lord Gray, return you governor of Berwick, The Duke of Norfolk, for some special causes We must recall; Arguile shall have his pledges, We but reserved them to preserve our selves, Clifton; be thou our deputy Lieutenant, And Lord warden of Nottingham Castle, Our self will be Lieutenant of the County. For Howard, Pellham, Leak, and all the rest That in this victory shared with dangers, They shall participate our princely loves, I know not how to dignify your deeds Without a large premeditation; Gray, and Clifton, Clifton, and warlike Gray Fought for our father, brother, and sister At Dennis, Roan, Bullen, and at Calais The bloody sweat that Muslborough bred At Edinburgh, and now again at Leith, In all which we fortunately conquered, Thanks unto heaven, next your valiant hands. Master Mayor, and noble John of Wilton, And warlike Clifton with all your men of war We this night do invite you, for our gueasts, To sup with us; to morrow we'll survey The underminings, and unpaced greise That Mortimer, and Isabel did devise To steal their sportive daliancies in, Of whom your stately fortress does retain The Labyrinth (now called Mortimers hold) Heaven for our victory we first will pay, And praise our Subject's that redeemed the day, Proud France, and poysening Spain, if heave'n us bless A virgin's arm shall quell your mightiness.

Plot summary

Plot — The Vow Breaker, or The Fair Maid of Clifton
Though Young Bateman is betrothed to Anne Boot and loved by her, her father reneges on the ===== PAGE 347 ===== 1628 arrangement. Meanwhile, war looms: England enters an alliance with a federation of Scottish lords hostile to the French Queen Regent of Scotland, and troops are being recruited to march north. Bateman joins up, hoping that his absence will make Old Boot’s heart grow fonder, and Anne vows to love only him; but her father puts pressure on her to marry German, and, seduced by his wealth, she agrees. The French are vanquished, and Bateman distinguishes himself in battle; but he is troubled by dreams about Anne, and returns home on the day of her wedding, just missing the ceremony. German goes away on a long business trip, leaving Anne in her father’s house. Old Bateman sues Boot for breach of the pre-contract; Young Bateman hangs himself outside Anne’s door, and his ghost haunts Anne, invisible to the rest of her family. Anne gives birth to a baby girl. While the women around her sleep, she sees the ghost and runs out of the house; following her footsteps in the snow, the household find her drowned in the river. The two bereaved fathers, Boot and Bateman, commiserate together. In Scotland, Sir Gervase Clifton insults the Queen Regent by drawing an unfavourable comparison with Queen Elizabeth, and captures Martigues in a single combat, but releases him with honour. Joshua the Puritan intends to hang his cat for Sabbath-breaking - she killed a mouse on Sunday - but the cat is saved by the arrival of a French ambassador. The English attack Leith, and Clifton captures a French colonel’s armour. Hostilities are ended by diplomatic means, and Scotland is to be demilitarized. The English and French lords feast together, and Joshua gets drunk. The English troops return south to meet the Queen at Nottingham; the common soldiers plan to put on a play for her, but decide on a morris dance instead. She meets the civic authorities and rewards her military commanders.
