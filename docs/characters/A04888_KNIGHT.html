I sir, but he borrowed corn, and promised to pay you corn again, and you can have but so much as you lent: For if he should pay you at the rate you demand, You would have for the twenty bushels you lent, forty, Which were neither right nor conscience. But I will: sirrah Bailie, I will answer the poor men's debts, and come home for thy fee anon, go old men. Get you home and praise God. Sir, though housekeeping be some hindrance to my willing mind by reason it robs me of that, which should bewray my loving mind both to my prince & country, money I mean which at this time I stand in some want of, yet of that small store that I have, am willing to impart the lending of the king xx. pound, and more I assure you I am not able. Hear you sir, will it please you to take partly of a piece of beef with me, you shall be welcome. Yes Honesty, thou shalt be my brother's guest and mine. NEighbour Walter I cannot but admire to see How housekeeping is decayed within this thirty year, But where the fault is God knows, I know not: My father in his life time gave hospitality to all strangers, and Distressed traueillers, his table was never empty of bred, beef And beer, he was wont to keep a hundred tall men in his hall. He was a feaster of all comers in general, And yet was he never in want of money: I think God did bless him with increase, for his bountiful mind I tell you neighbour, my great grandfather, & all my pre decessors have be held in good regard for their good housekee ping, and (God willing) their good names shall never take and exi gent in me, for I will (God willing) keep such hospitality to my death, as my state can maintain, and I will rather fell my land, to maintain housekeeping, then keeping my land make sale of my good name for housekeeping, but stay, who comes here. Why neighbour, my goods are lent me to no other end But to relieve my needy brothers, but God I hope hath in store for me. But who comes her?

Plot summary

Plot — A Knack to Know a Knave
. . Concerned for the good order of his kingdom, King Edgar commissions Honesty to seek out and expose knaves. The dying Bailiff of Hexham advises his four sons to live by fraud and deceit in their various: professions, and to enrich themselves at the expense of the commonweal and the poor..He dies and is carried off by the devil. While Edgar deals with the case of 1592 Philarchus, a courtier who has abused his father, Honesty observes the corrupt dealings of the Bailiff’s sons: Cuthbert, a cony-catcher, who bears false witness in an attempt to take possession of a farm; Walter, a farmer, who oppresses his neighbours and exports corn to feed the enemy; John, a priest, who refuses to give to charity; and Perin, a courtier, who misuses the royal seal and sells Walter an export licence for his corn under pretext of raising revenues for the crown. Meanwhile honest members of the state, from knight to ploughman, complain of their economic decline. Edgar sends Ethenwald to woo Alfrida by proxy, but Ethenwald falls in love with her himself and marries her. The King forgives him and visits the couple (receiving a petition from the mad men of Gotham on the way), but the jealous Ethenwald is convinced that Edgar plans to cuckold him, and has Alfrida and the kitchen maid swap identities during the royal visit. An outraged Edgar sees through the subterfuge and plans to take revenge, but Bishop Dunstan, with the help of the devil, persuades him to pardon Ethenwald instead. Honesty exposes the various knaves he has observed, and, with royal permission, sentences them all to gruesome and unusual punishments.
