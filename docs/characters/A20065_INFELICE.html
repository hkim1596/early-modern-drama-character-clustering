Good Woman do. O sir, that cannot make me jealous. Here's honeyed poison, to me he never thus writ, But Lust can set a double edge on wit. From whom sayest thou? And whats your business? What would she have me do in this? Where lies this Land? Is she then married? why stirs not her Husband in it? Let her in writing draw the cause at large: And I will move the Duke. My Husbands name, and hand and seal at arms to a Love-letter? Where hadst thou this writing? My Lord turned Ranger now? This very purse was woven with mine own hands, This Diamond on that very night, when he Untied my Virgin girdle, gave I him: And must a common Harlot share in mine? Oh worser man, to quit thy pains, take thou the gold. Cupid himself was sure his Secretary, These nes are even the Arrows Love let flies, The very Ink dropped out of Venus eyes. Oaths, promises, preferments, Jewels, gold, What snares should break, if all these cannot hold? What creature is thy Mistress? What manner of woman? They are delivered better: Why should she send back these things? Strumpets Like cheating gamesters will not win At first: these are but baits to draw him in. How might I learn his hunting hours? Knowest thou what other gifts have passed between them? Him I'm examine presently. Be gone then, & what lies in me command. Come hither sirrah, how much cost those Satins, and cloth of Silver, which my husband sent by you to a low Gentlewoman yonder? She there, to whom you carried letters. Did your Lord never send you with a Ring, sir, set with a Diamond? Are you so close, you Bawd, you pandring slave? Out of my sighed, base varlet, get thee gone. If you'll needs know, it was about the clock: how works the day, my Lord, (pray) by your watch? How, two? I am scarce at one. Then sure it's you, Mine goes by heavens Dial, (the Sun) and it goes true. Set it to mine (at one) then. Faith then belike, Neither your clock nor mine does truly strike, And since it is uncertain which goes true, Better be false at one, then false at two. Yet not merry. Nothing my Lord, but my false watch, pray tell me, You see, my clock, or yours is out of frame, Must we upon the Workman lay the blame, Or on your selves that keep them? All is not well indeed, my dearest Lord, Lock up thy gates of hearing, that no found Of what I speak ma enter. Or if my own tongue must my self betray, Count it a dream, or turn thine eyes away, And think me not thy wife. Earth is sins cushion: when the sick soul feels her self growing poor, then she turns beggar, cries and kneels for help; Hipollito (for husband I dare not call thee) I have slolne that Jewel of my chaste honour (which was only thine) and given it to a slave. I beg but this, Set not my shame out to the world's broad eye, Yet let thy vengeance (Like my fault) soar hie, So it be in darkened clouds. Hipollito? The slave did woo me. 'Twas thus, he gave me this battery first. O I Mistake, believe me, all this in beaten gold: Yet I held out, but at length this was charmed. What? change your Diamond wench, the act is base, Common, but foul, so shall not your disgrace: Could not I feed your appetite? O Men, You were created Augels, pure and fair, But since the first fell, worse then Devils you are. You should our shields be, but you prove our rods. Were there no Men, Women might live Like gods. Guilty my Lord? Nay, you may laugh, but henceforth shun my bed, With no whores leavings I'll be poisoned. Here, sir. It's a good honest servant, that old man. And it may be my husband, Because when once this woman was unmasked, He leveled all her thoughts, and made them fit: Now he'd mar all again, to try his wit. Let me be gone, my Lord, or stand unseen; It's rare when a Judge strikes, and that none die, And it's unfit then, women should be by. Pray do so. It's my cue To enter now: room, let my Prize be played, I would lurked in Clouds, yet herd what all have said, What Jury more can prove, she has wronged my bed, Then her own husband, she must be punished; I challenge Law, my Lord, Letters, and Gold, and Jewels From my Lord that woman took. Plot summary

Plot — 2 The Honest Whore (2 The Converted Courtesan)
On his way to court, Hippolito receives petitioners, including the reformed courtesan Bellafront: she wants his help in petitioning the Duke to pardon her husband Matheo, who has been condemned to death for homicide. Hippolito solicits the help of Bellafront’s father Orlando, but he is still morally outraged at her former sexual misconduct. Nevertheless he secretly arranges for Matheo’s release, and enters his household disguised as a servingman, Pacheco. Matheo proves dissolute, and when ‘Pacheco’ gives him money for safe keeping, he gambles it all away. Bellafront is trying not to let poverty and Matheo’s prodigality force her back into prostitution. Hippolito, who converted her from whoredom, now tries to seduce her back again. She sends ‘Pacheco’ to return his love-tokens, but ‘Pacheco’ instead takes them to Infelice, who identifies them as her own gifts to Hippolito. She interrogates Hippolito’s go-between, Brian the footman, then tells her husband that Brian gave her the love-tokens and had an affair with her. Brian is dismissed and goes home to Ireland. Desperate for money, Matheo strips the gown from his wife’s back and sells it. Lodovico promises to furnish him as a gentleman, so he is properly dressed when Orlando visits in his own person. The old man feigns lack of sympathy, then returns as Pacheco in time to prevent Matheo assaulting Bellafront. Prompted by Orlando, the Duke takes a hand: he issues a warrant to arrest Matheo for robbing two pedlars (who were actually Orlando’s men); and, hearing that Hippolito is in love with a whore, but not knowing her identity, passes new anti-prostitution laws. Meanwhile, Candido is married a second time, but his bride tests his patience with unruly behaviour on their wedding day. He determines to tame her, and Lodovico helps in the guise of an apprentice; she submits herself after a mock duel with measuring sticks. Some gallants attempt to seduce her into a brothel, but fail, and Candido still keeps his temper when, drawn to an alehouse for a business deal, he is forced to drink to a bawd’s health. Matheo is arrested at the alehouse on the Duke’s warrant, and Candido too for receiving the cloth stolen from the pedlars. At Bridewell, Matheo admits the robbery, but attempts to incriminate Bellafront out of jealousy for her supposed relationship with Hippolito.
