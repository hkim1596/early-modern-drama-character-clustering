Full speech

God and his Angels guard your sacred throne, And make you long become it. The sin upon my head dread sovereign. For in the book of Numbers is it writ, When the son die, let the inheritance Descend unto the daughter. Noble Lord stand for your own, Unwind your bloody flag, Go my dread Lord to your great graunsirs graven, From whom you claim: And your great Uncle Edward the black Prince, Who on the French ground played a Tragedy Making defeat on the full power of France, Whilst his most mighty father on a hill, Stood smiling to behold his Lyons whelp, Foraging blood of French Nobility. Oh Noble English that could entertain With half their Forces the full power of France: And let and other half stand laughing by, All out of work, and cold for Actium. The Marches gracious sovereign, shall be sufficient To guard your England from the pilfering borderers. She hath been then more feared then hurt my Lord: For hear her but examplified by her self, When all her chivalry hath be in France And she a mourning widow of her Nobles, She hath her self not only well defended, But taken and impounded as a stray, the king of Scots, Whom Like a caitiff she did lead to France, Filling your Chronicles as rich with praise As is the owse and bottom of the sea With sunken wrack and shipless treasury. True: therefore does heaven divide the fate of man in diverse functions. Whereto is added as and aim or but, obedience: For so live the honey Bees, creatures that by awe Ordain and act of order to a peopeld Kingdom: They have a King and officers of sort, Where some Like Magistrates correct at home: Others Like Merchants venture trade abroad: Others Like soldiers armed in their stings, Make boot upon the summer's velvet bud: Which pillage they with merry march bring home To the tent royal of their Emperor, Who busied in his majesty, behold The singing masons building roofs of gold: The civil citizens lading up the honey, The sad eyed Justice with his surly humme, Delivering up to executors pale, the lazy caning Drone. This I infer, that 20. actions once a foot, May all end in one moment. As many Arrows losed several ways, fly to one mark: As many several ways meet in one town: As many fresh streams run in one self sea: As many lines close in the dial centre: So may a thousand actions once a foot, End in one moment, and be all well born without defect. Therefore my Liege to France, Divide your happy England into four, Of which take you one quarter into France, And you withal, shall make all Gallia shake, If we with thrice that power left at home, Cannot defend our own door from the dog, Let us be beaten, and from henceforth loose The name of policy and hardiness.

Plot summary

Plot — Henry the Fifth
Faced with the prospect of parliamentary legislation to sequester church lands, the Bishop of Canterbury encourages King Henry V to consider his claim to the French throne: he demonstrates that the Salic law debarring succession through the female line does not apply to France, and promises the church's financial support for a war. The Dauphin hears of Henry's ambitions and sends him a mocking gift of tennis balls; annoyed by the suggestion tl1at he should go away and play, Henry gathers his troops. The French bribe three noblemen to assassinate him before he leaves England, but he discovers the plot and has the traitors put to death. The French prepare for battle, but the Dauphin scornfully underestimates Henry. Exeter, sent as ambassador to the French King, demands surrender and delivers to the Dauphin Henry's personal contempt. The French offer a few dukedoms and a marriage alliance if Henry will withdraw. He considers this inadequate, and attacks Harfleur. The Dauphin and his troops fail to arrive in time to relieve the town, so its Governor surrenders to Henry. Exeter is left in command while Henry takes his disease-ridden troops back to winter in Calais, but the French force a battle en route at Agincourt. The night before, the French anticipate victory over the bedraggled English. Henry tours his army and listens to their worries about the battle. He quarrels with one of his soldiers, Williams, and they agree to settle their differences after their battle: they will know one another by the gloves they exchange. The French offer Henry the chance to surrender, but he refuses and battle ensues. The English do well, but when the French regroup for a new assault, Henry orders his troops to kill their prisoners. The French attack the baggage-train and kill the boys who are guarding it. The English are victorious, with remarkably few casualties. Henry persuades Fluellen to wear Williams's glove, claiming it is a battle trophy which will be challenged only by a traitor; the n-vo soldiers are about to fight when the King intervenes and reveals the truth. Henry later agrees a peace with France, is adopted as heir to the French throne, and marries Princess Catherine. In a subsidiary action, Henry's former lowlife associates do less well. Rejected by the King, Falstaff dies, broken-hearted. Pistol, Nim, and Bardolph join the expedition to France, accompanied by Falstaff's boy.
