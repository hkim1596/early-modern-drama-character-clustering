We know that since our Sovereign lost his breath, Earth hath been bathed with many a crimson flood; Perdicca's did procure Meleagers death, And his own Soldiers drowned his breath with blood, Th'Athenians prey Leonatus did remain, And (by Eumenes subtlety dismayed) Neoptolemus and Craterus were sl ine, Then by his own Eumenes was betrayed. And have the Heaven reserved more plagues in store? As if we yet were not enough accursed. Then wound our ears by hearing others wounds, That pity now may tread the steps of hate. Life is the subject of distress and grief, "That still affords us matter to be moan; "And we by death can only have relief: "To live, and to be wretched, are both but one. "Yet foolish worldlings tossed with endless care, "(Though at too deer a rate) would still buy breath, "And following feathers wavering through the air, "Love life (though wretched) more then a happy death. All as a pest them who are wretched do fly: "Eclipsed fortune threatens lose of friends. "Thus time and travel all things once may daunt. Yet might have many followers, and few friends: "Friends by the Touchstone of distress are tried. Durst Subject's damn their Sovereign? and not herd? So still may clouds obscure the world's bright eye. Oh strange barbarity, most monstrous deed! Could men a woman, Subject's kill their Queen: And could her fortune passed no pity breed? Who ever gave the wound, that not her seen. The ugly Author of those odious evils, (Of punishment afraid) must still be sad, His breast a hell, his thoughts all turned to devils, (Through horror of himself) must make him mad. "No end in sin, crimes are maintained by crimes, "Who fallen in depths, must touch the bottom once: "The path of honour hath but narrow bounds, "On which who step, attentive must remain: "Since raised so high above the vulgar grounds, "That who thence fallen can never rise again. Oh, how ambition does abuse the great! Who with enough not pleased, still strive for more: Lo, how our Sovereign seemed to raise his state, Yet made it but to fallen while starved with store. And since his Trophies reared in several fields, Both him and his have to confusion brought, "Then, what is all the good that greatness yields, "Which makes it self seem much to be made nought? "Thus, though the mountains make a mighty show, "They are but barren heaps born up aloft, "Where Plains are pleasant still, though they lie low, "And are most fertile too, though trod on oft.
