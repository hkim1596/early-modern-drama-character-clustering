Full speech

A faithful Brother, if it please you. I understand no Heathen language, truly. Heathen Greek, I take it. All's Heathen, but the Hebrew. Please you, a Servant of the exiled Brothers, That deal with Widow's, and with Orphans goods; And make a joust account, unto the Saints: A Deacon. From Tribulation Wholesome, Our very zealous Pastor. Of what kind, Sir? Were the Orphans Parents Sincere professors? Because We then are to deal justly, and give (in truth) Their utmost value. No surely. The Brothers bid me ay unto you, Sir. Surely, they will not venture any more, Till they may see projection. You have had, For the Instruments, as bricks, and ome, and glasses, Already thirty pound; and, for Materials, They say, some ninety more: And, they have herd, since, That one, at Hiedelberg, made it, of and Egg And a small paper of Pinne-dust. In pure zeal, I do not Like the man: He is a Heathen. And speaks the language of Canaan, truly. He bears The visible mark of the beast, in his forehead. And for his Stone, it is a work of darkness, And, with Philosophy, blinds the eyes of man. Which his cannot: The sanctified cause Should have a sanctified course. I have not aedified more, truly, by man; Not, since the beautiful light, first, shone on me: And I am sad my zeal hath so offended. The motion's good. And of the Spirit; I will knock first: Peace be within. Christ-tide, I pray you. I have done. Bells' are profane, a tune may be religious. It is indeed and Idol I hate Traditions. I do not trust them. They are Popish, all. I will not peace. I will not— Please the profane, to grieve the godly. I may not. About the second day, of the third week, In the ninth Month? It will be joyful tidings to the Brothers. Lawful? We know no Magistrate Or, if we did, This's foreign coin. It's, Sir. The Brothers shall approve it lawful, doubt not. Where shall't be done? Peace to the Household. Casting of Dollars is concluded lawful. What is the Motive. They are profane, Lewd, superstitious, and idolatrous Breeches. Avoid Satan, Thou art not of the light. That Ruff of pride, About thy neck, betrays thee: and is the same With that, which the unclean Birds, in seventy-seven, Were seen to prank it with, on diverse coasts. Thou lookest Like Antichrist, in that lewd hat. Depart, proud Spanish Fiend. Child of perdition. Sir, I know The Spaniard hates the Brothers, and hath Spies Upon their Actions: And that this was one I make no scruple. But the holy Synod Have been in prayer, and meditation, for it. And it's revealed no less, to them, then me, That casting of Money is most lawful. I will tell This to the Elders, and the weaker Brothers, That the whole Company of the Separation May join in humble prayer again, Yea, for some fitter place. The Peace of mind Rest with these walls. Come forth, you Seed of Vipers, Sons of Belial, Your wickedness is broke forth: Abomination Is in the House. The place, It is become a Cage of unclean birds. Wee'll join, to weed them out. Call her not Sister. She is a Harlot, verily. Satan, avoid, and hinder not our zeal. Locusts Of the foul pit Worsethen the Grasshoppers, or the Lice of Egypt. Scorpions, And Caterpillars. They are the vessels Of shame, and of dishonour. To bear away the portion of the Righteous, Out of this den of Thieves. The goods, sometime the Orphans, that the Brothers Bought with their Silver pence. I do defy The wicked Mammon, so do all the Brothers, Thou profane Man. I ask thee, with what conscience Thou canst advance that Nemrod, against us, That have the seal? Were not the Shillings numbered, That made the Pounds? were not the Pounds told out, Upon the second day of the fourth week, In the eight month, upon the table dormant, The year, of the last patience of the Saints, Six hundred and ten. Sir. I am strong, And will stand up, well girded, against and Host, That threaten Gad in exile. I will pray there Against thy House: May Dogs defile thy walls, And Wasps and Hornets breed beneath thy roof, This seat of falsehood, and this cave of cos'nage. My name is Ananias.

Plot — The Alchemist
With his master absent to avoid the plague in London, Face has set up an occult consultancy in his house in association with the formerly itinerant rogues Subtle and Doll Common: Face fetches in the clients and Subtle poses as the alchemist. They quarrel over the relative merits of their contributions to the enterprise, but resolve to collaborate competitively in the day’s ===== PAGE 80 ===== business. They are visited by successive clients: Dapper, who wants a familiar to help him win at gambling; Drugger, who wants advice on the most auspicious layout for his shop; Sir Epicure Mammon, who seeks the philosopher’s stone to fulfil his fantasies of wealth, luxury, and voluptuousness, and who brings along with him the sceptic Surly; and the religious sectarians Ananias and Tribulation, who want both the stone and Subtle’s claimed expertise in counterfeiting foreign coins. Dapper is persuaded that he is the Fairy Queen’s nephew and heir, and is promised an introduction to her. Mammon brings various domestic articles for aurification, which Subtle then sells on to Ananias and Tribulation, saying they belong to orphans. Clients come thick and fast. Drugger introduces his neighbours, Kastril (who wants te learn how to quarrel) and the wealthy widow Pliant (who wants her fortune told and whom Face and Subtle both want to marry). They are sent away so that the tricksters can deal with Dapper, who in his turn is secreted in the privy while they attend to Mammon. The knight courts Doll, who is presented to him as a lord’s sister prone to religious mania, which he activates by ill-chosen words; but he is then told that his lust has caused a setback in the alchemical process, which demands moral purity. Meanwhile, Surly returns, disguised as a Spanish count seeking sexual favours; but, since Doll is busy with Mammon, he is foisted on Dame Pliant, who is tempted with the chance of becoming a Spanish countess. His attempt to tell her the truth is outmanoeuvred when Face and Subtle convince her that he is an impostor and that the real Count is on his way; Drugger is persuaded to impersonate him and sent off to borrow a Spanish suit for the role. Lovewit, the master of the house, returns to hear Tumours of strange goings-on. Face’s explanation is confuted by the cries of Dapper, forgotten in the privy, so he confesses everything.
