How now Gossip! how doe's the Play please you? I, she is a proper piece! that such creatures can broke for. Or a begging scholar in black, or one of these beggarly Poets, gossip, that would hang upon a young heir Like a horseleech. I, these were tolerable Beggars, Beggars of fashion! you shall see some such anon! But they have no Fool in this Play, I am afraid, gossip. I remember it gossip, I went with you, by the same token, M rs . Trouble Truth dissuaded us, and told us, he was a profane Poet, and all his Plays had Devils in them. That he kept school upon the Stage, could conjure there, above the School of Westminster, and Doctor Lamb too: not a Play he made, but had a Devil in it. And that he would learn us all to make our husbands Cuckolds at Plays: by another token, that a young married wife i'the company, said, she could find in her heart to steal thither, and see a little o'the vanity through her masque, and come practice at home. Nay, Gossip, I name no body. It may be 'twas my self. As fine a gentleman of his inches, as ever I saw trusted to the Stage or any where else: and loved the common wealth, as well as ere a Pa triot of them all: he would carry away the Vice on his back, quick to Hell, in every Play where he came, and reform abuses. But there was one Smug, a Smith, would have made a horse laugh, and broke his halter, as they say. 'Twas his partly, Gossip, he was to be drunk, by his partly. I fear we are all such, and we were old enough: But we are not all old enough to make one witch. How Like you the Vice i'the Play. That was the old way, Gossip, when Iniquity came in Like Hokos Pokos, in a Jugglers jerkin, with false skirts Like the Knave of Clubs! but now they are attired Like men and women of the time, the Vices, male and female! Prodigality Like a young heir, and his Mi stress Money (whose favours he scatters Like counters) prank't up Like a prime Lady, the Infanta of the Mines. By whom is it so thought? or where lies the abuse? Take heed, it lie not in the vice of your interpretation: what have Aurelia, Claram, Pecuniam to do with any person? do they any more, but express the property of Money, which is the daughter of earth, and drawn out of the Mines? Is there nothing to be called Infanta, but what is subject to exception? Why not the Infanta of the Beggars? or Infanta o'the Gipsies? as well as King of Beggars, and King of Gipsies? That's but one Gossips opinion, and my Gossip Tatle's too! but what says Expectation, here, she sits sullen and silent. Or the butterbox, Buz, the Emissary. In Silver -street, the Region of money, a good seat for a Usurer. I, or humanity. Either would appear in this Peny-boy, and he were rightly distilled. But how Like you the news? you are gone from that. I, my Gossip Tatle knew what fine slips grew in Gardiners-lane; who kissed the Butchers wife with the Cowes-breath; what matches were made in the bowling-Alley, and what bettes won and lost; how much grieft went to the Mill and what beside: who conjured in Tutle-fields, and how many? when they never came there. And which Boy road upon Doctor Lamb, in the likeness of a roaring Lion, that run away with him in his teeth, and ha not devoured him yet. But very communicative, and liberal, and began to be magnificent, if the churl his father would have let him alone. With wine, gossips, as he meant to do, and then to de fraud his purposes? And lie so, in wait for a piece of wit, Like a Mousetrap? It was a plain piece of political incest, and worthy to be brought afore the high Commission of wit. Suppose we were to censure him, you are the youngest voice, Gossip Tatle, begin. Faith I would have him flat disinherited, by a decree of Court, bound to make restitution of the Lady Pecuniam, and the use of her body to his son. And dedicated to the sustaining of the Staple! Banckruptly, indeed! To scorn, of Mirth? Three or four: old Covetousness, the sordid Peny-boy, the Money-bawd, who is a flesh-bawd too, they say. Plot summary

Plot — The Staple of News
. _ Pug the junior devil is keen to visit earth. Satan svarns him that his ideas of effective devilment are half a century out of date, but grants him leave to spend the day in London, made flesh and subject to all the frailties and limitations of the human body. A ready-made body is available, in the shape of a recently hanged cutpurse, but Pug will have to make his own arrangements for clothes. Another proviso is that he must serve the first man he meets. Unfortunately for Pug, he turns out to be a twit: Fabian Fitzdottrel. Fitzdottrel longs to see the devil, and agrees to accept Pug as an unpaid servant because he gives his mame as Devil. The same obsession makes Fitzdottrel eager to see a new play, The Devil is an Ass, but he needs some finery to show off as he sits on the stage. Wittipol is eager to sleep with Fitzdottrel’s wife, and offers him a suitable cloak in exchange for fifteen minutes’ uninterrupted conversation with the lady. He uses the time to proposition her, and engages her interest even though she has been forbidden to reply. Fitzdottrel is introduced to Merecraft, a purveyor of imaginative financial schemes, who offers to make him rich and a duke through land reclamation in the eastern fens. Fitzdottrel agrees to meet with him, leaving Pug to ensure that nobody attempts to visit his wife. Pug instead brings her a follow-up message from Wittipol, then makes advances to her on his own account, but is caught by Fitzdottrel and gets a beating. Wittipol’s friend Manly has a room which abuts Mistress Fitzdottrel’s gallery, which he uses to have a second meeting with her. This is much more intimate than the first, despite being conducted through the windows of adjacent buildings. Pug gets back into favour by tipping off Fitzdottrel, who puts a stop to the assignation and threatens to take up another woman to share his expectations and be his Duchess. Merecraft suggests that what Fitzdottrel’s wife needs is to be taught proper manners, and recommends a widow who knows the ways of the Spanish court, who might be persuaded to undertake the task if sent an expensive ring as a gift, and who is entirely fictitious. His accomplice Engine proposes hiring an actor to impersonate her, but Merecraft worries that his business methods might be leaked to a playwright and end up on stage; in the event, Engine finds a gentleman to take on the role instead, who happens to be Wittipol.
