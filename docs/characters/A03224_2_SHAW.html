My Lord, I am going by commanndement, Unto the Marshalsea, to Captain Stranguidge, For Piracy of late condemnde to die, There to confess him and his company, That done, e come with speed back to the king, And make no doubt but e effect the thing. Farewell my Lord of Gloucester. Well met my good Lord Lovel. Why to the Tower, to shrive the Duke of Clarence, Who as I hear is salne so grievous sick, As it is thought be can by no means escape. Drowned in a butt of Malmesey? that is strange, Doubtless he never woned misdoe himself? O fowl words my Lord, no more of that, The world knows nothing, then what should I fear? Does not your honour seek promotion? O give the Doctor then a little leave, So that he gain preferment with a King, Cares not who goes to wrack, whose heart does wring. What else my Lord? That way the current of our fortune runs, By noble Richard gallant royal Richard, He is the man must only do us good, So I have honour, let me swim through blood. My Lord, be but at Paul's cross on Sunday next, I hope I have it here shall soundly prove, King Edwards chtldren not legitimate. Nay, and that for Edward ruling now, And George the Duke of Clarence so late dead: Their mother happened to tread the shoe awry, Why what is Richard then? Tut, lawful man, he says it so himself. And what he says isle be so hold to swear, Though in my soul I know it otherwise, Beware promotion while you live my Lord. Spuria vitulamina non agent radices altas. Bastardly slips have always slender grouth. Ah Shaw, this was the cursed theme, That at Paul's cross thou madest thy sermon of, To prove the lawful issue of thy King, God out of wedlock, illegitimate. Ah Duke of Gloucester this didst thou procure. Did Richard (villain) no it was thy fault, Thou wouldst be won to such a damned deed, Which now to think on makes my soul to bleed. Ah Friar Anselm, sleep among the blessed, Thy propheste thus falsely did I wrest. It did, it did. Here friend, what is thy will with me? I cannot come, I pray Thee take that Friar, For he can do it better far then I. Dost thou not? no, thy untainted soul Cannot discern the horrors that I do. With all my heart, would it were ended now, So it were done, I care not where nor how. Amen. My Lord of Gloucester, I recefude the same From old Friar Anselm of S. Bartholmews. My Lord of Clarence, I must tell your Lord , His Highness is much troubled in his sickness With this same prophesy of G. Who is this G? Oft times he will demand, then will he sighs, And name his brother George, your self my Lord, And then he strikes his breast, I promise you, This morning in th'extreamest of his fit, He lay so still, we all thought he had slept, When suddenly, George is the G. quoth he, And gave a groan, and turned his face away. This Christian Patience at the point of death, Does argue he hath led no wicked life, How ever heaven hath laid this cross on him, Well Matthew Flood. for so thou callest thy self, Finish a good course as thou hast begun, And cl re thy conscience by confession, What knowest thou of the Duke of Exceters death? More happy he, well Flood forgive the world, As thou wilt have forgiveness from the heavens. Then concerning this for which thou diet, knew Stranguidge of the league betwixt the Kings before he took that prize? Stranguidge what say you? You see theres but a turn betwixt your lives, You must be next, confess and save yeur soul, Concerning that wherein I questionde him: I am your ghostly father to absolve You of your sins, if you confess the truth. Plot summary

Plot — 2 Edward the Fourth
King Edward invades France; but the Duke of Burgundy and the Constable of France fail to supply the military aid they promised. Several English soldiers are shot by the Constable's men in a failed attempt to assassinate Edward; the Constable pleads an accident,The English herald sent to King Louis is offered peace terms, which Edward modifies: Louis must submit to his suzerainty, and pay both the expenses of the invasion and an annual tribute thereafter. Louis accepts the offer and a treaty is agreed. Burgundy and the Constable try to double-cross both Edward and one another: each writes independently to both Edward and Louis, offering Louis Edward's murder and Edward assistance against Louis. Edward feigns deafness, forcing the envoys to speak loud enough for the hidden Louis to hear; then confronts the envoys with their masters' treachery. He returns to England, leaving troops in France to help Louis rout the traitors. Jane Shore visits the London prisons, distributing alms to the prisoners. Brakenbury solicits her help to procure a pardon for his kinsman Strangwidge, whose actions against French shipping were committed in ignorance of the recent treaty which made them piratical. Also condemned is his passenger, known as Flood but actually Matthew Shore. The Queen first confronts, then forgives Jane for her adultery with Edward. The King pardons Strangwidge and his crew, and Jane pays their prison fees herself. Brakenbury employs 'Flood' at the Tower. Edward has fallen ill and is anxious about a prophecy that 'G' will dispose of his sons and usurp the throne. His brother Clarence is imprisoned and drowned in the Tower. Edward dies and Richard of Gloucester becomes Lord Protector. Jane is expelled from court, and goes to stay with Mistress Blague. Richard lodges the young King and his brother in the Tower and has them murdered. 'Flood' is wounded in an altercation with the murderers and seeks succour at Mistress Blague's inn, where he is tended by Jane. The ghost of Friar Anselm tells Dr Shaw that everyone involved in the death of the princes will come to a bad end; Richard will reign only three years. Richard issues a proclamation banning anyone from giving Jane food or shelter, and Mistress Blague turns her out; but not soon enough to evade being arrested. Jane does penance.
