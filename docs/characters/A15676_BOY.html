Full speech

'Tis but one and all sir. You have a good memory Sir, for they are five mi nutes ere windefall of your Glass. Your Tables are ready Sir, and all the men you keep which is indeed half a Boy, Scillicet, Videlicet. Your Glass may flatter you, but truly I will not, your head is not a hair better then it should be. Somewhat thin, and yet there is more hair then wit. Then wit of man can number sir, take it i'th right sense I pray ye. I have here two Mistress's, but if the best were chosen out, if Poliphemus tother eye were out, his choice might be as good as Argus broad waking, so difficult is the difference. Sir. Yes sir God be praised. The weather sir. That you are wise in trimming your hair Mai ster? True Master: but if yowl see a fool look in your Glass master. It amiable if it would not altar. Me thinks that should wear it the sooner. Thats easily discerned, for four wear breeches. Your pipe shall want no fire sir. You can correct no more then is your own, I am but half yours to command, if you steal away any part that is not your own, you are so far in danger as the striking of and other men servant.

Plot — Every Woman in Her Humor
Misogyny has made the normally jovial Acutus.- melancholy. After scaring some foolishly fashionable characters, he joins them at the Hobby tavern, where Gaetica has taken lodgings with her dog. Disguised as a © soldier, Acutus picks a fight with Scilicet and Servulus, © then exposes their hypocrisy by returning disguised as first a friend and then an enemy of the ‘soldier’, and eliciting inconsistent responses. Scilicet attempts to woo Gaetica; she is more concerned about the disappearance of her man Bos with her dog, but becomes betrothed to him. , Lentulus is in love, and asks Cicero to woo Terentia for him. They go to dine with Flaminius, Terentia’s father, and Cicero is left alone with the ladies of the house. The upshot is that Terentia falls in love — but -- with Cicero himself rather than Lentulus. Rumours circulate that she is to marry Cicero, but Flaminius has not given his consent and takes offence. Lentulus makes Cicero and Terentia admit wronging him, and. _ he sentences Cicero to death — but only if Terentia will kill him. He woos and wins Terentia’s sister Flavia, and plans a double wedding. Acutus gives Philautus a sleeping potion, and he appears to die. He is rumoured to have drunk himself to death, but the reported numbers of alcohol victims escalate as the news circulates. The drunken Bos has his clothes stolen, and is brought back to the tavern in a barrel. Gracchus agrees to give him a new suit if he can demonstrate that drunkenness is a virtue, which he proceeds to do in a debate. At the wedding, Caesar declares a general amnesty; Acutus and Gracchus have Lentulus intercede to procure Philautus’ release.
