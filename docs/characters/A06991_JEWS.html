A Fleet of warlike Galleys, Barabas, Are come from Turkey, and lie in our Rhode: And they this day fit in the Counsel-house To entertain them and their Embassy. Were it for confirmation of a League, They would not come in warlike manner thus. Why, Barabas, they come for peace or war. I know you will; well brothers let us go. I, that thou hast — I, that thou art a — I, thy daughter, — I, remember that — O good Barabas come to our house. O Barabas, their Laws are strict. They wear no shirts, and they go barefoot too. Good Barabas come to me. You hear your answer, and you may be gone. I will not go for thee. How, dost call me rogue? Fight. Come therefore let us go to Barrabas ; For he can counsel best in these affairs; And here he comes. I fear their coming will afflict us all. But there's a meeting in the Senate-house, And all the Jews in Malta must be there. Let's take our leaves; Farewell good Barabas. Barabas, thou hast — Thou art a — Thy daughter — Remember that — Thou hast committed — I, but Barabas remember Mathias and Done Lodowick. I will not say that by a forged challenge they met. O no, good Barabas come to our house. And Barabas, you know — I'll be with you to night. Not, then I'll make thee go. O yet be patient, gentle Barabas. Why, Barabas, as hardly can we brook The cruel handling of our selves in this: Thou seest they have taken half our goods. Yet brother Barabas remember Job, Come, let us leave him in his ireful mood, Our words will but increase his ecstasy. Good Barabas be patient. On then: but trust me it's a misery To see a man in such affliction: Farewell Barabas. Exeunt. And very wisely said, it may be so. Why go get you away.
