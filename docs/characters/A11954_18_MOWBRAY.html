I well allow the occasion of our Arms, But gladly would be better satisfied, How (in our Means) we should advance our selves To look with forehead bold and big enough Upon the Power and puissance of the King. Shall we go draw our numbers, and set on? Thus do the Hope's we have in him, touch ground, And dash themselves to pieces. The joust proportion that we gave them out. Let us sway-on, and face them in the field. I think it is my Lord of Westmoreland. Why not to him in partly, and to us all, That feel the bruises of the days before, And suffer the Condition of these Times To lay a heavy and unequal Hand upon our Honours? What thing, in Honour, had my Father lost, That needs to be revived, and breathed in me? The King that loved him, as the State stood then, Was forced, perforce compelled to banish him: And then, that Henry Bullingbrook and he Being mounted, and both roused in their Seats, Their neighing Coursers daring of the Spur, Their armed Staves in charge, their Beavers down, Their eyes of fire, sparkling through sights of Steel, And the loud Trumpet blowing them together: Then, then, when there was nothing could have stayed My Father from the Breast of Bullingbrook ; Oh, when the King did throw his Warder down, (His own Life hung upon the Staff he threw) Then threw he down himself, and all their Lives, That by Indictment, and by dint of Sword, Have since miscarried under Bullingbrook. But he hath forced us to compel this Offer, And it proceeds from Policy, not Love. Well, by my will, we shall admit no Parley. There is a thing within my Bosom tells me, That no Conditions of our Peace can stand. I, but our valuation shall be such, That every slight, and false-deriued Cause, Yea, every idle, nice, and wanton Reason, Shall, to the King, taste of this Actium: That were our Royal faiths, Martyrs in Love, We shall be winnowed with so rough a wind, That even our Corn shall seem as light as Chaff, And good from bade find no partition. Be it so: Here is returned my Lord of Westmoreland. Your Grace of York, in heaven's name then forward. If not, we ready are to try our Fortune's, To the last man. You wish me health in very happy season, For I am, on the sudden, something ill. So much the worse, if your own Rule be true. This had been cheerful, after Victory. Is this proceeding joust, and honourable?
