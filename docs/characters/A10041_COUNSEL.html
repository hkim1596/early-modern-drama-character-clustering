O pusant king, your blifful woordꝭ, deserves abundant praise That you in this do go about, your father's same to raise. O blifful day that king so yung, such profit should conceive: His father's praise & his to win, from those that would deceive. Sure my true and sovereign king, I fallen before you pressed: Answer to give as duty mine, in that your grace request. If that your heart addicted be, the Egyptians to convince: Through Marsis and the continued ̄ quest won, then deed of happy prince Shall pierce the skies unto the throne of the supernal seat: And merit there a joust reward, of Jupiter the great. But then your grace must not turn back, from this pretenced will For to proceed in virtuous life, employ endeavour still. Extinguish vice, and in that cup, to drink have no delight. To martial feats and kingly sport, fix all your whole delight. Permit (Oh king) few words to here, my duty serves no less: Therefore give leave to council thine, his mind for to express. Then will I speak unto your grace, as duty doth me bind. Your grace doth mean for to attempt of war the manly art: Your grace therein may bap receive with others for your part. The dent of death in those affairs, all persons are alike: The heart courageous often times, his detriment doth seek. Its best therefore for to permit, a Ruler of your land: To fit and judge with equity, when things of right are scanned. Pleases your grace I judge of him to be a man right fit: For he is learned in the Law having the gift of wit. In your graces presinct, I do not view for it a meeter man: His learning is of good effect. bring proof thereof I can. I do not know what is his life, his conscience hid from me: I doubt not but the fear of God, before his eyes to be. Behold I see him now agresse and enter into place:

Plot summary

Plot — Cambises, King of Persia
Gamibyses leaves Sisamimes in charge of his kingdom while he conquers Egypt. After a comic incident in which soldiers going to the war are beaten by a prostitute, the deputy, encouraged by AlmibiGextér, grows corrupt and takes bribes. Cambyses returns victorious and has him executed and flayed in the presence of Sisamnes’ son @tiaii, whom he appoints as his father’s successor. Praxaspes criticizes Cambyses’ drunkenness, so the King demonstrates his steady hand by shooting the courtier’s son dead. SHIEFAIE is also critical, but bides his time awaiting Cambyses’ death and his own succession to the throne; but Cambyses grows fearful of his ambition and has him murdered. After another comic incident in which Ambidexter first entraps two rustics into speaking ill of the King and then denounces them as traitors, Menus and @upid induce Cambyses into an_ incestuous marriage with a kinswoman. She criticizes him for the murder of Smerdis and he has her killed too. ===== PAGE 46 ===== Finally Cambyses accidentally impales himself on his own sword when leaping onto his horse, and dies. This is interpreted as divine punishment for his tyranny.
