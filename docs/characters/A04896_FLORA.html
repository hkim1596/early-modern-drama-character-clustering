Full speech

Put up those murdering blades on pain of my dis pleasure, Confine them to perpetual prison in the scabbard, That they may never come forth to manage civil broils. While in my flowery bowers I took repose, I herd the noise of these tumultuous broils, Which struck me with a wonderful amazement. Then hastily I left my banks of pleasure, And hither came to end these mortal jars; Therefore I charge you both on that llegance And respect which you do owe to me, Quite to dismiss your armed bans. And you Martagon, who have fair Violetta wronged, To her shall make and ample restitution, Of what y'have taken from her; And entertain a friendly league with Rhodon, Which you Cynosbatus must also condescend to: But as for you found Madam Eglantine, Since you have broke the sacred laws of love, And by unlawful means sought to accomplish Your designs, and make the Shepherd Rhodon Enamored on you: You to a vestal Temple shall be confined, Where with ten years penance You shall expiate your folly. But where be those two intruders Poneria and Agnostus. These that have crept in among us, and with false slights Sought to overthrow our state. We banish them quite Out of Thessaly for ever. What I have decreed you must assent unto. Rhodon, I here bestow on thee this noble shepherdess. And now since all things are reduced to joyful peace, Let us betake our selves to sweet delights, And solemnize with mirth your nuptial rites.

Plot summary

Plot — Rhodon and Iris
: Aiming to sow dissension among the flowers, Poneria and Agnostus disguise themselves as a virtuous maiden and a wise man. Rhodon has recently breken off his suit to Eglantine, and is now expected to marry Iris. His sister Violetta appeals to him for help against the marauding Martagon, who has trampled on her flowers and forced her to live away from her home. Martagon conspires with Eglantine’s brother to forestall Rhodon’s match with Iris. The jilted Eglantine grows melancholy, but Poneria stops her killing herself and gets her to tart herself up for an assignation with Rhodon. It becomes clear that Martagon has no intention of making restitution to Violetta, so Rhodon will have to take him on by force: a floral war seems inevitable. Iris gives Rhodon a gern which makes the wearer victorious in battle; Violetta gives him a lettuce which guarantees sustenance and prevents poisoning. Martagon suborns Poneria to kill Rhodon, so she gives Eglantine a poison, telling her it is a love-philtre. Posing as Iris, Eglantine gives it to him, claiming it to be a restorative, and expects him to fall in love with her.
