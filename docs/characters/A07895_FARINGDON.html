Full speech

AStonishment and frightful wonder, Shakes and splits my soul in sunder. Cannot graves contain their dead, Where long they have lain buried, But to Triumphs, sports, and shows They must be raised? Alack, God knows They count their quiet slumber hlest, Free from disturbance, and unrest. How? Whence? or where May I suppose my self? Well I wot, (If Faringdon mistake it not) That ancient famous Cathedral, Hight the Church of blessed Paul. And that this Ward well witness can, Once thereof I was Alderman, And gave it mine own proper name. I built these gates, the very same. But when I note this goodly train, (Yelad in Scarlet) it should sayen (And soothly too) that these are they Who watch for London night and day, Grave Magistrates; of which fair band, When second Edward swayed this land, Four several times the chief was I, And Lord of Londons Mayoralty. As by the bearing of that sword, It seems that you are Londons Lord: To whom becomes me lout full low, Old duty yet (methinks) I know. Turn now thy Glass to instant day, And let old Faringdon thee pray: Good Time, resolve him, what is he, Graced with this day of Dignity? Joy and gladsome jouissance, Does old Faringdon in-trance, To hear the tale that Time hath told, Since those reverend days of old, Unto this great Solemnity, For Brothers of my Company. Glad and golden be your days, Live in Prince and peoples praise: Honour London with your care, Study still for her welfare: And as Goldsmiths both you are, Such good and golden deeds prepare, That may renown our Mystery, To times of utmost memory. My minute calls, and Ghosts must go. Yet loath I am to leave you so. For I could well spend out this day, And do what service else I may; Were Time but pleased that I might stay. Now, as custom wills it so, On to Pauls Church must you go, To bless God for this bounteous day. Till you return, here will we stay, And usher then a gladsome guiding, Home to the place of your abiding. For such is your kind Brethrens will: And Time hath tied us thereuntill. So wish I too, with all my heart, And seeing now we needs must partly, From Faringdon one farewell take, Who gives it for the Goldsmiths sake. You are Lieutenant to your King, And 'tis a very worthy thing, To mind Gods blessing, and his grace, That brought ye to so high a place. O soil it not with any blame, That, may impeach it, or your name. For they have told you well before, That whereof I needs speak no more: You are a Goldsmith, Golden be Your daily deeds of Charity. Golden your hearing poor men's cases, Free from partial bribes embraces. And let no rich or mighty man Injure the poor, if help you can. The World well wots, your former care Forbids you now to pinch or spare, But to be liberal, frank, and free, And keep good Hospitality, Such as beseems a Mayoralty, Yet far from prodigality. To be too lavish, is Like crime As being too frugal in this time. I say no more, but God defend you, Many days of comfort send you, To whom (with all these) I commend you.

Plot summary

Plot — Chruso-Thriambos: The Triumphs of Gold
[The first pageant is lost. The King and Queen of India have come to England, bringing with them ingots of Indian gold, to see the country’s beauty and to witness the ceremonial of the new Lord Mayor's day. What they actually encounter is a series of sea-fights and skirmishes en route to and from the Lord Mayor's oath-taking at Westrninster.} When the Lord Mayor returns to dry land, he meets Leofstan, the first Provost of London and a fellow goldsmith, who leads him to a tomb. There the procession is halted by Time, who introduces the two kings who secured London’s civic autonomy: Richard I, who instituted the office of Lord Mayor, and King John, who made it an electable office rather than a royal appointment. Time summons another former goldsmith Lord Mayor, Nicholas Faringdon, out of his tomb. At first, Faringdon is peeved to be raised from the dead, until he realizes the occasion; Time suggests that he should take pride in the achievements of his livery company, since not only the new Lord Mayor but also one of the new sheriffs are members. Time drives Faringdon and the kings in a triumphal chariot to Lawrence Lane, where Leofstan presents the principal pageant, an orphrey representing the mining and refining of gold, with Gold and Silver on top, chained to the throne of their mother, Justice; all three throw coins to the crowd. Time remarks on the traditional friendship between the Goldsmiths’ and Fishmongers’ companies, but emphasizes that the pageantry has been paid for entirely by the Goldsmiths. That evening, the speakers take their leave of the Lord Mayor at his house. Leoftsan explains the etymology of his name, Pemberton, and Time declares that he is expected to rule virtuously. Faringdon wishes him a year in office noted for justice, charity, and the absence.of corruption.
