Hic sum doctissime & reverendissime Doctor. Ecce quam bonum & quam incundum habitare libros in unum. Marry sir, he does but fulfil by rehearsing of these names, the Fable of the Fox & the Grapes, that which is above us, pertains nothing to us. What of this, Master? why he does speak mystical lie, for he knows if your skill fail to make a brazen head, yet Mother Waters strong Ale will fit his turn to make him have a copper nose. I'll bring you two or three witnesses. Marry sir, three or four as honest Devils, and good companions as any be in hell. Marry sir, he'll straight be on your pick pack to know whither the feminine or the masculine gender be most worthy. Nay Master Burden, my master ere he hath done with you, will turn you from a Doctor to a dunce, and shake you so small, that he will leave you no more learning in you then is in Balams Ass. Nay, now my master goes to conjuration, take heed. O master cease your conjuration, or you spoil all, for hers a she devil come with a shoulder of mutton on a spit, you have marred the devils supper, but no doubt he thinks our College fare is slender, and so hath sent you his cook with a shoulder of mutton to make it exceed. I'll warrant you, Master, if Master Burden could con jure as well as you, he would have his book every night from Henly to study on at Oxford. Master, shall I make a good motion? Marry sir, now that my hostess is gone to provide supper, conjure another spirit, and send Doctor Burden flying after. So be it, & nunc & semper, Amen. No sir, yes what is this else; Ego sum tuiu homo, I am your man, I warrant you sir, as good Tully's phrase as any is in Oxford. Ego, that is I, marry nomen substantino. Why sir, let him prove himself and a will, I can be herd felt and understood. Not far from Copper-smiths hall. Nor I sir, but what would you at Brazen-nose? Whose men be you? Then here's good game for the hawk, for here's the master fool, and a covie of Coxcombs, one wise man I think would spring you all. A company of Scabs, the proudest of you all draw your weapon if he can. See how boldly I speak now my master is by. O I beseech you conjure his hand too, that he may not lift his arms to his head, for he is light-fingered. In faith my Lord, your manhood and your sword is all alike, they are so fast conjured that we shall never see them. Salve, Doctor Burden, this lubberly Lurden, Ill shaped and ill faced, disdained and disgraced, What he tells unto vobis, mentitur de nobis. Ecce asinum mundi, figuram retundi, Neat, sheat and sine, as brisk as a cup of wine. And yet, master Doctor, to speak Like a Proctor, And tell unto you, what is veriment and true, To cease off this quarrel; look but on his apparel, Then mark but my talis, he is great Prince of Walis, The chief of our gregis, and filius Regis, Then beware what is done, for he is Henries white son. And with scientiam and great diligentia, Will conjure and charm, to keep you from harm, That utrum horum manis, your very great navis, Like Bartlets ship, from Oxford do skip, With Colleges and schools, full loaded with fools, Quid dices and hoc, worshipful Domine Dawcocke? No, no, out with your blades, and hamper these Jades, Have a fiurt and a crash, now revel dash, And teach these Sacerdos, that the Bocardos, Like Peasants and clues, are meet for themselves. Salvete omnes Reges, that govern your Greges, in Saxony, and Spain, in England, and in Almain: for all this frolic rabble must I cover the table, with trenchers, salt, and cloth, and then look for your broth. My master hath made me sewer of these great Lords, and (God knows) I am as serviceable at a table, as a Sow is under and Apple tree: it's no matter, their cheer shall not be great, and therefore what skills where the salt stand before or behind? Spill, sir? why, do you think I never carried twopenny chop before in my life? By your leave, Nobile decus, for here comes Doctor Bacons pecus, being in his full age, to car ry a mess of pottage. Marry sir, I will, this day shall be a festival day with me: For I shall exceed in the highest degree. Here, sir. Think you that the watching of the brazen head craves no furniture? I warrant you, sir, I have so armed my self, that if all your devils do come, I will not fear them and inch. So, I thought you would talk your self asleep anon, and it's no marvel, for Bungay on the days, and he on the nights, have watched joust these ten and fifty days, now this is the night, and it's my task and no more. Now Jesus bless me, what a goodly head it is, & a nose! You talk of nos autem glori ficare; but here's a nose, that I warrant may be called nos autem po pelares for the people of the parish. Well I am furnished with weapons, now sir, I will set me down by a post, and make it as good as a watchman to wake me if I chance to slumber. I thought, goodman head, I would call you out of your moment passion a God, I have almost broke my pate: Up, Milet, to your task, take your brown bill in your hand, heres some of your Master's Hobgoblins abroad. With this, a great noise. Time is. Why, Master Brazenhead, have you such a capital nose, and answer you with syllables, Time is? is this all my Master's cunning, to spend seven years study about Time is? Well, sir, it may be, we shall have some orations of it anon; well, I'll watch you as narrowly as ever you were watched, and I'll play with you as the Nightingale with the Slowworm, I'll set a prick against my breast; now rest there, Miles, Lord have mercy upon me, I have almost killed my self: up, Miles, list how they rumble. Well, Friar Bacon, you have spent your seven years study well, that can make your Head speak but two words at once, Time was: yea marry, time was when my Master was a wise man, but that was before he began to make the Brazenhead. You shall lie while you arse ache, and your Head speak no better: well, I will watch and walk up and down, and be a Peripatetian and a Philosopher of Aristotles stamp. What, a fresh noise? Take thy Pistols in hand, Miles. Master, master, up, hell's broken lose, your head speaks, and there's such a thunder and lightning, that I war rant, all Oxford is up in arms; out of your bed, take a brown bill in your hand, the latter day is come. When spoke the Head? did not you say that he should tell strange principles of Philosophy? Why sir, it speaks but two words at a time. Oft, I marry hath it thrice: but in all those three times it hath uttered but seven words. Marry sir, the firs time he said, Time is, as if Fabuis Commentator should have pronounced a sentence: he said, Time was: and the third time with thunder and lightning, as in great choler, he said, Time is passed. Even, Time is. Why then, sir, you forbid me your service. 'Tis no matter, I am against you with the old proverb, The more the Fox is cursed, the better he fares. God be with you, sir, I'll take but a book in my hand, a wide sleeved gown on my back, and a crowned cap on my head, and see If I can want promotion. A Scholar, quoth you, marry sir, I would I had been made a bottle-maker, when I was made a scholar; for I can get neither to be a Deacon, Reader, nor Schoolmaster; no, not the Clarke of a Parish; some call me dunce: another says, my head is as full of Latin, as and eg's full of oatmeal: thus I am tormented, that the Devil and Friar Bacon haunts me. Good Lord, here's one of my Master's Devils I'll go speak to him: what master Plutus, how cheer you? Know you, sir, why are not you one of my Master's Devils, that were wont to come to my master Doctor Bacon, at Brazen-nose? Good Lord, M. Plutus, I have seen you a thousand times at my Master's, and yet I had never the manners to make you drink; but sir, I am glad to see how conformable you are to the state; I warrant you, he's as yeomanly a man, as you shall see, mark you Master's, here's a plain honest man, without welt or guard; but I pray you sir, do you come lately from hell? Faith, 'tis a place I have desired long to see, have you not good tippling houses there? may not a man have a lusty fire there, a pot of good Ale, a pair of cards, a swinging piece of chalk, and a brown toast that will clap a white waistcoat on a cup of good drink? You are for me, friend, and I am for you: but I pray you, may I not have and office there? By my troth, sir, in a place, where I may profit my self. I know hell is a hight place, and men are marvelous dry, and much drink is spent there; I would be a Tapster. There's nothing lets me from going with you, but that 'tis a long journey, and I have never a horse. Now surely here's a courteous devil, that for to plea sure his friend, will not stick to make a Jade of himself: but I pray you goodman friend, let me move a question to you. I pray you, whither is your pace a trot or and amble? 'Tis well, but take heed it be not a trot, But 'tis no matter, I'll prevent it. Marry, friend, I put on my spurs: for if I find your pace either a trot, or else uneasy, I'll put you to a false gallop, I'll make you feel the benefit of my spurs. O Lord, here's even a goodly marvel, when a man rides to hell on the Devils back.
