Always at hand, thy carefulness is great: Where is thy Lady? So early, then I see loves the best lark; For the Corn builder has not warbled yet His morning's Carol to the rising Sun. There's for thy pains. Our Father's present; Sweet, we are betrayed. If I could fear the waving of a Sword, Mine enemies had frighted me ere now; But I'm invaluable, Like my mind, Not to be wounded but with darts of love; And I as little estimate a Father In these Pathaires, as he esteems my grief. There's no preoritie in loves high Court Granted unto the Fathers' before the Son; But Like the purest government of all, Every men mind is his own Monarchy: Where reason never set foot to make a law, Shall common sense keep one, that were absurd. Not if you will forbear me; But in a war defensive I will stand Against and Army of my Ancestors, Did their enfranchised souls break open their tombs, And reassumed their bodies as they lived, In their full pride and youthful jollity. I live with joy, that I'm no Father-killer. This is extreme. How am I crossed! Yet I have all the world to traffic in, Except in England, and your Honoues house: But as the dissolution of the soul From such a body as desires to love, Is burdensome and grievous to the man; Such is my heart dissevered from my Love. Careless of foes, of father, or his curse, Come I again to challenge Floramell, Which I suspect the Steward has betrayed. He that is once false, will be never true. Well, proecede. Meet me at York house. I am yours in all things that are honourable. I do. Exit Young Fythwaters. Nor I. I need no buckler. Theyle never be honest. You are cruel to impose a curse upon me, That sentence is extreme, I feel it work. More deadly on my grieved faculties, Then to have died upon my father's sword: For now mine own woes must destroy my self. And thats a murder worse then parricide. Exit Y. Fit. The stay of my fair Mistress makes me wild, Sure I shall never more behold her face; The Stewards false, and Floramell may change. I'll therefore give a perjod to my grief, And in despair finish what life denies: Yet ere I die, let all the World this know, A Woman's love procured my overthrow. My Floramell, to Sea. Exeunt. Since I was cast upon this fatal Rock, And saw my Love dissevered by the waves, And my kind Steward in the Ocean drowned, Here I have lived, fed only with raw Fish, Such as the Sea yields: and each Ship I see, (As daily there are some furrow this way) I call unto for aid, but never the near. Once asked me, What I was? I answered him, And Englishman. Quoth he, Stay there and starve. To the next that passed, I said I was a French-borne. I'll aid no French quoth he. Unto a third, That I a Spaniard was. He bade me hang : So that I know not what I ought to say, Nor whom to speak to: but in happy time, From this high Rock, I see a tall Ship come, Furnished with all his Sails; and as it ploughs The Ocean up, it raises hills of snow, That fly on both sides as they did give way, To make a valley for the Ship to pass. Their Captain as I think looks upon me, And has took notice of my waving hand. Now the Ship turnesand this way ploughs amain, As if it meant to run it self aground: In happy time, now I shall be relieved. Your answer there? In the honourable intercourse of men I should do so, and were you in my case, You would enforce your own necessity. I would be as thou art, proud of nothing. As it anchors here It bears a goodly show; but launched again, And a stormerise, it may be cast aland As I have been: nay worse, it may may be sunk, And then what is't, but a fair something, nothing? Plot summary

Plot — The Hector of Germany, or The Palsgrave, Prime Elector
. Defeated in his attempt to seize power in Spain, Henry the Bastard attempts to get himself elected Holy Roman Emperor; he secures the support of three of the seven Electors, promising that Saxony will be the power behind his throne. But the other Electors, including the sickly Palsgrave, prefer the Duke of Savoy’s candidature, so the Bastard uses force: he defeats the opposing Electors in battle, imprisons two of them, and has himself crowned, then breaks his word and excludes Saxony from power. News of the ===== PAGE 311 ===== 1613 usurpation cures the Palsgrave, who prepares to mobilize an army. Spain is the theatre of war for their first confrontation. The Palsgrave is victorious, and the Bastard is captured, but rescued by Saxony. The warring potentates seek alliances against one another: the Palsgrave goes to England, the Bastard to France. The Palsgrave competes in an English tournament and is installed in the Order of the Garter. King John of France is reluctant to fight the English again (they have too good a track record of victory in France), and hopes to use diplomacy to secure Edward III’s neutrality. Refused the chance to conduct the mission, Saxony goes to England in disguise, but is identified by the Palsgrave, who dishonours him by pulling off a lock of his hair. Diplomacy fails, and Edward and the Palsgrave invade France, dispatching troops to free the imprisoned Electors. The Palsgrave defeats and kills Saxony in single combat. King John withdraws his support from the Bastard, and Savoy is formally crowned Emperor. In the sub-plot, Lord Fitzwaters is betrothed to Floramell, but discovers that she loves his son. He witnesses a secret assignation between the lovers in the garden of Lord Clinton, Floramell’s father. Clinton orders Young Fitzwaters off his land; Lord Fitzwaters goes further and banishes him from England. Floramell goes with him, leaving in her place a cross-dressed page, whom Lord Fitzwaters ‘marries’. The lovers are separated in a shipwreck: Floramell is washed up on the French coast and becomes a maid of honour at court; Young Fitzwaters is marooned on a barren rock and subsists on a fish diet until he is rescued by Saxony. King John is attracted to Floramell and offers to make way for her by having the Queen murdered. Floramell will not consent to adultery or murder, but agrees to give him a single kiss.
