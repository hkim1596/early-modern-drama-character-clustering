Faith lovely girl, I am of Beckles by, Your neighbour not above six miles from hence, A Famers son that never was so quaint, But that he could do courtesy to such Dams: But trust me Margret I am sent in charge, From him that reueld in your father's house, And filled his Lodge with cheer and venison, Tired in green, he sent you this rich purse: His token that he helped you run your cheese, And in the milkehouse chatted with your self. Women are often weak in memory. Not while I may have such quaint girls as you. Gramarcies Peggy, look for me ere long. Daphne the Damsel, that caught Phœbus fast, And locked him in the brightness of her looks, Was not so beauteous in Apollo's eyes, As is fair Margret to the Lincoln Earl, Recant thee: Lacy, thou art put in trust, Edward thy Sovereigns son hath chosen thee A secret friend to court her for himself: And darest thou wrong thy Prince with treachery Lacy, Love makes no exception of a friend, Nor deems it of a Prince, but as a man: Honour bids me control him in his lust, His wooing is not for to wed the girl, But to entrap her and beguile the lass: Lacy, thou love, then brook not such abuse, But wed her, and abide thy Princes frown: For die, then see her live disgraced. Thus watchful are such men as live in love, Whose eyes brook broken slumbers for their sleep. I tell thee, Peggy, since last Harlston fair, My mind hath felt a heap of passion. Peggy, I pleaded first to get your grace for him: But when mine eyes suruaid your beauteous looks, Love Like a wag, straight dined into my heart, And there did shrine the Idea of your self: Pity me though I be a Farmers son, And measure not my riches, but my love. For what, gentle Fryer? tell the news. The Earl of Lin olne? Fryer, thou art mad, It was some other, thou mistakest the man: The Earl of Lincoln? why it cannot be. Why speakest not Bungay? Friar, to thy book. He's dumb indeed: Bacon hath with his Devils Enchanted him, or else some strange disease, Or Apoplexy hath possessed his lungs: But, Peggy, what he cannot with his book, We'll betwixt us both unite it up in heart. Of courtesy, Margret, let us led the Friar Unto thy father's lodge, to comfort him With broths to bring him from this hapless trance. Truth all, my Lord, and thus I make reply, At Harlstone Fair there courting for your Grace, When as mine eye suruaid her curious shape, And drew the beauteous glory of her looks, To dive into the centre of my heart, Love taught me that your Honour did but jest, That Princes were in fancy but as men, How that the lovely maid of Fressingfield Was fitter to be Lacies wedded wife, Then Concubine unto the Prince of Wales. I have deserved it, Edward, act it well. If ought betides to lovely Margret, That wrongs or wrings her honour from content, Europes rich wealth, nor England's Monarchy, Should not allure Lacy to overlive. Then Edward, short my life, and end her loves. Nay, Edward, keep a love worth many friends. Humbly I take her of my Sovereign, As if that Edward gave me England's right, And rich't me with the Albion Diadem. I'll hasten, Peggy, to make short return. But please your Honour go unto the Lodge, We shall have Butter, Cheese, and Venison. And yesterday I brought for Margret, A lusty bottle of neat Clarret wine: Thus can we feast and entertain your Grace. Or else Lord Lacy is unfit to live. May it please your Highness give me leave to post To Fressingfield, I'll fetch the bonny girl, And prove in true appearance at the Court, What I have vouched often with my tongue. I go, my Lord. Come on my wags, we're near the Keepers Lodge, Here have I oft walked in the watery Meads, And chatted with my lovely Margret. 'Tis the same, Keeper, how farest thou holla man, what cheer, How does Peggy thy daughter and my love? Why how now Margret, what a malcontent, A Nun? what holy father taught you this, To task your self to such a tedious life, As die a maid? 'twere injury to me, To smother up such beauty in a Cell. Whence, Peggy, comes this Metamorphosis? What, shorn a Nun, and I have from the Court Posted with coursers to convey thee hence, To Windsor, where our marriage shall be kept? Thy wedding robes are in the Tailors hands. Come, Peggy, leave these peremptory vows. 'Twas but to try sweet Peggies constancy: But will fair Margret leave her love and Lord? Why then, Margret will be shorn a Nun. A good motion. Peggy, your answer must be short. Peggy, thy Lord, thy love, thy husband, Trust me, by truth of Knighthood, that the King Stays for to marry matchless Ellinor, Until I bring thee richly to the Court, That one day may both marry her and thee. How sayest thou Keeper, art thou glad of this? What have you fit for breakfast? we have hied and posted all this night to Fressingfield. And not a bottle of wine? Come, Sussex, let's in, we shall have more, for she speaks least, to hold her promise sure. WHY looks my Lord Like to a troubled sky, When heavens bright shine, is shadowed with a fog: Alate we ran the Dear and through the Iawnds Stripped with our Nags the lofty frolic Bucks, That scudded before the teisers Like the wind, Near was the Dear of merry Fressingfield, So lustily pulled down by jolly mates, Nor sharde the Farmers such fat venison, So frankly dealt this hundred years before: Nor have I seen my Lord more frolic in the chase, And now changed to a melancholy dump. I grant (my Lord) the Damsel is as fair, As simple Suffolks homely towns can yield: But in the Court be qainter Dams then she, Whose faces are enriched with honours taint, Whose beauty's stand upon the stage of fame, And vaunt their trophies in the Court of Love. Wonderfully wisely counseled, Raphe. I will, my Lord, so execute this charge, As if that Lacy were in love with her. What wilt thou do with them, Raphe? God send your Honour your hearts desire. But lovely Peggy Semele is dead, And therefore Phœbus from his Palace pries, And seeing such a sweet and seemly saint, Shows all his glory for to court your self. Then am I double prisoner to thy self, Peggy, I yield, but are these news in jest? Believe me, Lass, I am the Lincoln Earl, I not deny, but tired thus in rags, I lived disguised to win fair Peggies love. I meant, fair girl, to make thee Lacies wife. Say, shall I make thee Countess ere I sleep? The Lincoln Countess, for it shall be so, I'll plighted the bans and seal it with a kiss. Fryer, content, Peggy how Like you this?
