Nay, dearest Logicus, let not the excellency of your reason be so severe, but that it may admit a gracious appre hension of a smiling love; let not the exactness of your wise dome be so regulated, but that it may express a courteous ac ceptance of a Lovers admiration; let not— Why lo, now your self has made a set speech; and thus while you Reprehend, you Offend: while you Direct, you Neglect: while you Reform, you Deform: while you— Which is your way? Nay, do you speak first, you are the better Man. Why so does mine; weel go together. Why so must I. Why, and I must walk here alone a little first. Why, and I can very well think on my business by the way. Why, and I am going to a Feast. Why, and I am going to Ethicus. No, no; your lip is all that I desire. TAra, ding de ding, ding de ding, lan, tan, dan, dido. Historiam? why, ask Poeta. I, you; they say she's sick of love. And eloquent tongue is never dry, Astrologiam will pledge you for me. I'll in too, to fit and Talk with her, while she's sick. Exit Rhetorica. Yes, faith, you shall go further if you will, to the Infinitive: I am not in the Mood to be wooed now. And did you mark with what a Strength of Heat, his Cold Feebleness set upon me? and I was Mistress Tongue; and I was Nimble-tongued, and I had Four tongues. But if the Eye of Age be not so Dim, but that it may Re flect upon it self: if the Ear of Age be not so Perverse, but that it may Admit a free Attention; if the Reason of Age, will but yield to Reason; then shall his Eye, his Ear, his Rea son, bring in their several informations against his Age. If we should inquire with whom does reside the most refined Expolition of Language; would it be answered with Olde-folkes? if we should inquire with whom does abide the most nimble vigour of purest Apprehension; would it be answered with Old-folkes? if we should inquire who are most tried for Quick Dispatch of weighty Affairs, would it be answered your Old-folkes? whose Age brings Care, Care Weakness, Weakness Forwardness, Frowardness Distraction, Distraction Childishness: and thus running Round in the Circle of Time, growing Giddy, they fallen down upon all Four again, Like Children: Children I may call them for their Impotency, not Innocence: for their Perverseness, not Hopefulness; for their Impatience, not Tenderness; for then would they afford a more Tender censure, of our more Tender loves: but let's be gone, and though they Chide, yet will we Love; and I will sooner confess my Tongue to want Eloquence, then my Love of Logicus to want Reason. Why then, Grammaticus, at this double request, without any Circumlocutions or Figures, I plainly offer un to thee my love. Nay? nay? nay truly Ethicus, it's good manners, to let her answer in her own defence.

Plot summary

Plot — The Marriages of the Arts ~w,
Astronomia has several suitors: Geographus (whom her mother dislikes), Geometres (whom her friends favour), and Poeta (who has recently jilted Historia to pursue her). Against her kinsman Polites’ advice, Historia still wants to marry Poeta, consummating the long-standing association between history and the poetic imagination. Poeta quarrels with Grammaticus, and Logicus becomes involved in the ensuing affray. Unsure whether to fight a duel with Poeta or sue him for assault, Logicus sets both courses in train. Hearing that Poeta has been hurt in the fight, Historia sends to Medicus for help, but Magus, who is helping Geometres, sees an opportunity to dispose of one of his client’s rivals, and persuades Medicus to send poison instead. Medicus’ man, Sanguis, delivers the poison, but spots that it isn’t medicine. Poeta infers Historia's treachery, but persuades himself out of absolute misogyny by thinking of Astronomia. To reconcile Poeta with Logicus and Grammaticus, Ethicus arranges a banquet. Several of the wooers use magical help in their suits. Poeta meets two gipsies, who pick his- pocket and foretell that he will marry Astronomia. Using sieve- divination, Magus predicts that Geometres will marry Astronomia, and will achieve this mainly through hypnotism, with a little pharmaceutical help. (Geometres finds the third option, conjuration, too frightening to try.) However, when Astronomia drinks the supposed love philtre at Ethicus’ feast, it poisons her. Meanwhile, Poeta seems reconciled to his former adversaries, but gets drunk and tries to pick fights with Geographus and Grammaticus; Magus charms him to sleep. Causidicus accepts fees from both Logicus and Poeta, so when the case comes to trial, Polites dismisses him and forces the parties to speak for themselves. The trial is adjourned when news arrives that Astronomia, burning up with the poison, has fallen into a coma. Medicus attends her, and cures her by administering an enema. Meanwhile the gipsies and servants indulge themselves in country pastimes: morris dancing, barley-break, and riddles. Polites takes up Geographus’ and Historia’s love- suits: he persuades Astronomia’s mother that Geographus will be a worthy son-in-law, so long as he gets rid of his servant Phantastes, and the couple are married.
