No, my Lord. Umph, when ̄ I had lost my way to heaven, you shewedit: I was new born that day. I think he does, but such rude spots of shame Stick on my cheek, that he scarce knows my name. Matheo, prithee make thy prison thy glass, And in it view the wrinkles, and the scars, By which thou Wertenberg disfigured, viewing them, mend them. Thou dost not hear me? Thou hast been in the hands of misery, and taken strong Physic, prithee now be found. Nay see, th'art thirsty still for poison, come, I will not have thee swagger. It's that sharpened and axe to cut thy throat. Good Love, I would not have thee fell thy substance And time (worth all) in those damned shops of Hell; Those Dicing houses, that stand never well, But when they stand most ill, that four-squared sin Has almost lodged us in the beggars Inn. Beside (to speak which even my soul does grieve) A sort of Ravens have hung upon thy sleeve, And fed upon thee: good Mat. (if you please) so base as Scorn to spread wing amongst these; By them thy fame is speckled, yet it shows Clear amongst them; so Crows are fair with Crows. Custom in sin, gives sin a lovely die. Blackness in Moors is no deformity. One knocks at door. How wild is his behaviour! o, I fear He's spoiled by prison, he's half damned comes there, But I must fit all storms: when a full sail his Fortune's spread, he loved me: being now poor, I'll beg for him, and no wife can do more. My Father? any tongue that sounds his name, Speaks Music to me: welcome good old man. How does my father? lives he? has he health? How does my father? I so much do shame him, So much do wound him, that I scarce dare name him. Alas, Matheo, wilt thou load a back That is already broke? Thou villain, curb thy tongue, thou art a Judas, To fell thy Master's name to slander thus. Gentleman. Art not ashamed to strike and absent man thus? Play on thee? no, you two will fly together, Because you are roving arrows of one feather. Would thou wouldst leave my house, thou never shalt Please, me weave thy nets never so hie, Thou shalt be but a spider in mine eye. Th'art rank with poison, poison tempered well, Is food for health; but thy black tongue does swell With venom, to hurt him that gave thee bred, To wrong men absent, is to spurn the dead. And so didst thou thy Master, and my Father. Let him say worse, when for the earth's offence Hight vengeance through the marble clouds is driven, Is't fit earth shoot again those darts at heaven? He thinks a silver net can catch the poor, Here's bait to choke a Nun, and turn her whore. Wilt thou be honest to me? She (crowned with reverend praises) passed by them, I (though with face masked) could not escape the hem, For (as if Heaven had set strange marks on Whores, Because they should be pointing stocks to man) Dressed up in civilest shape a Courtesan. Plot summary

Plot — 2 The Honest Whore (2 The Converted Courtesan)
On his way to court, Hippolito receives petitioners, including the reformed courtesan Bellafront: she wants his help in petitioning the Duke to pardon her husband Matheo, who has been condemned to death for homicide. Hippolito solicits the help of Bellafront’s father Orlando, but he is still morally outraged at her former sexual misconduct. Nevertheless he secretly arranges for Matheo’s release, and enters his household disguised as a servingman, Pacheco. Matheo proves dissolute, and when ‘Pacheco’ gives him money for safe keeping, he gambles it all away. Bellafront is trying not to let poverty and Matheo’s prodigality force her back into prostitution. Hippolito, who converted her from whoredom, now tries to seduce her back again. She sends ‘Pacheco’ to return his love-tokens, but ‘Pacheco’ instead takes them to Infelice, who identifies them as her own gifts to Hippolito. She interrogates Hippolito’s go-between, Brian the footman, then tells her husband that Brian gave her the love-tokens and had an affair with her. Brian is dismissed and goes home to Ireland. Desperate for money, Matheo strips the gown from his wife’s back and sells it. Lodovico promises to furnish him as a gentleman, so he is properly dressed when Orlando visits in his own person. The old man feigns lack of sympathy, then returns as Pacheco in time to prevent Matheo assaulting Bellafront. Prompted by Orlando, the Duke takes a hand: he issues a warrant to arrest Matheo for robbing two pedlars (who were actually Orlando’s men); and, hearing that Hippolito is in love with a whore, but not knowing her identity, passes new anti-prostitution laws. Meanwhile, Candido is married a second time, but his bride tests his patience with unruly behaviour on their wedding day. He determines to tame her, and Lodovico helps in the guise of an apprentice; she submits herself after a mock duel with measuring sticks. Some gallants attempt to seduce her into a brothel, but fail, and Candido still keeps his temper when, drawn to an alehouse for a business deal, he is forced to drink to a bawd’s health. Matheo is arrested at the alehouse on the Duke’s warrant, and Candido too for receiving the cloth stolen from the pedlars. At Bridewell, Matheo admits the robbery, but attempts to incriminate Bellafront out of jealousy for her supposed relationship with Hippolito.
