But Sack is the life, soul, and spirits of a man, the fire which Prometheus stole, not from Jove's Kitchen, but his Wine-Cellour, to increase the native heat and radical moisture, without which, we are but drowsy dust, or dead clay: this is Nectar, the very Nepenthe the gods were drunk with, it's this that gave Ganymede, beauty, Hebe youth, to Jove his heaven and eternity; do you think Aristotle drank Perry, or Plato Cyder? do you think Alexander had ever conquered the world, if he had been sober? he knew the force & valour of Sack, that it was the best armour, the best encou ragement, and that none could be a good Commander, that was not double drunk, with Wine and Ambition. Therefore the Garrisons are all drinking Schools, the Soldiers trained up to the mustering of pewter pots day lie, learning to continued temne death, by accustoming to be dead drunk: scars do not so well become a Captain, as Car buncles. A read nose is the grace of a Sergeant Mayor, and they unworthy the place of Ancients that have not good continued lours, the best shot to be discharged is the Tavern bill, the best Alarm is the sounding of healths, and the most abso lute March is reeling. Thus you may easily perceive the profit of Sack in military discipline, for that it may justly seem to have ta ken the name of Sack from sacking of Cities. And since it is the nature of light things to ascend, what better way, or more agreeing to nature can be invented, whereby we might ascend to the height of know ledge, then a light head? A light head being as it were allied with heaven, first found out, that the motion of the orbs was circular Like to its own, which motions, teste Arist tele, first found that intelligence: so that I conclude all intelligence, in tellect, and understanding to be the invention of Sack, and a light head; what mists of error had cloaded Philosophy, till the never sufficiently praised Copernicus found out that the earth was moved, which he could never have done, had he not been instructed by Sack, and a light head? This is the Philosophy, the great Stagirite read to his Pupil, Alexander, wherein how great a proficient he was, I call the faith of History to witness. Now, to make our demonstration to prove, no wine, no Philosophy, is that admirable Axiom, in vino veritas, and you know that Sack and truth are the only Buts which Philosophy aims at. Plot summary

Plot — Aristippus, or The Jovial Philosopher
Simplicius asks after the philosopher Aristippus in the Dolphin tavern, but is served the wine of the same name. On finding the right Aristippus, he enrols as a pupil, and is sworn in with an oath taken in drink. Aristippus encourages him to renounce beer and drink sack instead, to stay up at night, and to sleep in the morning. The Wild Man, who favours beer, beats Aristippus out of the tavern with the help of two brewers. However, he is quickly persuaded of the error of his ways, and seeks to make amends by fetching a surgeon to mend the philosopher’s head. The boastful Medico de Campo - who claims that he has treated many a world leader, and could even cure decapitation by making an artificial head - restores Aristippus to health with a draught of sack. For his reward, he asks that the Wild Man be forgiven, and the Wild Man promises that in future he will be Aristippus’ faithful disciple.
