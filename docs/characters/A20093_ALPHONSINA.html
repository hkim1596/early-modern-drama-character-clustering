Is't come to this, have the walls of the Castle been besie ged thus long, lain open for a breach; and dare you not Give fire to once piece? o y'ar a proper soldyor, good Sister, brother follow your game more close, or i'll leave you. Why willow? is the noble Prince forsaken? Why brother, what's the matter? That's not well. Surfeit? you eat some Meat against your stomach. Was it fish or flesh? I'st not the missing of a mark (which you long to hit) Makes you draw sighs in stead of arrows? By casting of thy water thy, I guess thou wouldst Have a medicine for the green sickness. Tent it, tent it, and keep it from rankling, you are Over head and ears in love. There's no hurt in that. And faith what doctress is she must call you patient? How! Darien? can no feather fit you but the broach in and Old men hat? were there so many dainty dishes To fill your belly, and must you needs long for that dish The master of the house sets up for his own tooth. Love? a disease as common with young-gallants as Swaggering and drinking Tobacco, there's not one Of they all but will to day lie drawing on for a Woman, as if they were puffing and blowing at a straight boot, And to morrow be ready to knock at death's door, But I wo'd feign see one of you enter and set in His staff. I shall look so old first, I shall be taken for thy gran dame; come, come it's but a worm between the skin And the flesh, and to be taken out with the point of a Waiting-woman's needle, as well as a great Countesses. Leave thee in sickness? I had more needs give thee a Caudle; and thrust thy addle-head into a nightcap, for look you brother— If what you will might so too, then would you be in Tune: I warrant, if the sucket stood here before Thee, thy stomach would go against it. Despair for a woman? they hang about men's Necks in some places thicker then hope upon poles. Walls of chastity? walls of wafer-cakes, I have Known a woman carry a featherbed, and a man in't In her mind, when in the street she cast up the white of Her eye Like a Puritane. Well brother, since you will needs S sail by such a Star as I shall point out, look you here it is; if she were Your Fether-makers, Tailors or Barbers wife, Bait a hook with gold, and with it— That cunning woman you take me to be, and because I see you dissemble not, here's my medicine. First send for your Barber. Your barber shall not come to rob you of your beard, I'll deal in no concealments— But let him by rubbing of you quicken Your spirits. Then whistle your goldfinches (your gallants) to your fist. Into a Tanerne, Drink stiff, swear stiff; have your music, and your brace, dance, and whiff Tobacco, Till all smoke Again, and split Sir. And do thus, but till the More cuts off her horns; Laugh in the day, and sleep in the night: and this wenching fire will be burned out of you. Alas, poor brother now I pity thee and wo'd do Any thing to help thee to thy longing, but that a Gap must be broken, in another men hedge to rob His orchard, within there Lucam Angelo, give him Music: Music has helped some madmen, let it then Charm him, Love makes fools of the wisest men. Thanks worthy sir. Our humblest duty to my Lord the Duke; If in my Brother's name, and mine, you tender For this his noble love, we both shall rest Highly indebted to you all. Signior, your love. And all fair Fortune's doubled on your self. Plot summary

Plot — The Wonder of A Kingdom
The Duke of Florence has betrothed his daughter, Fiametta, to the Prince of Pisa, and, to facilitate the political marriage, banished her lover, Angelo. Fiametta sickens for her absent love, and he returns to court disguised as a French doctor, offering the Duke his services to save her life. He diagnoses sexual frustration, and proposes a cure which requires the patient to drink a potion made from the beloved’s powdered heart: she will then hate him. In order to obtain the key ingredient, Angelo must be murdered. Angelo reveals his presence to Fiametta, but she insists on identifying him to her father, in the expectation that the Duke will relent. Angelo passes this off as an insane delusion, then flees to sanctuary. He is found and brought to court with a friar. He seems to reject Fiametta, and she agrees to marry Pisa the following day, but asks for the friar to hear her confession overnight. In fact, he secretly marries her to Angelo, pre-empting Pisa; the Duke and Prince are annoyed, but recognize there is nothing to be done about it. In the sub-plot, Lord Vanni has amorous inclinations younger than his physical age, and sends a love-letter and jewel to the chaste Alphonsina Neri. Meanwhile, Alphonsina’s brother Tibaldo has fallen sick for love of Vanni’s wife Dariene. He asks Alphonsina to accept Vanni’s advances, and Vanni duly excuses himself from Dariene’s bed. Tibaldo has arranged for himself to be present disguised as Alphonsina’s sister, and offers himself as an alternative, ‘female’ bedfellow for Dariene. His plan is aborted when he learns that Vanni’s daughter Alisandra loves Trebatio and Trebatio’s son. When Vanni pays his nocturnal visit, he is scared off by Trebatio’s lute-playing, then caught by his wife and blamed for the adultery he never committed. Alphonsina and Tibaldo marry Trebatio and Alisandra. In the third plot, the hospitable Jacomo has built a house in which to entertain his guests: his uncle, who is suspected of getting rich through supernatural agency, gave him a deathbed injunction to spend his inheritance virtuously. Torrenti envies Jacomo his new house, and plans a magnificent lifestyle of his own; but whereas Jacomo entertains the poor and needy, Torrenti is only interested in luxury and guests of a high social standing.
