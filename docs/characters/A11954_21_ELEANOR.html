Why droops my Lord Like ouer-ripen'd Corn, Hanging the head at Ceres plenteous load? Why does the Great Duke Humfrey knit his brows, As frowning at the Favours of the world? Why are thine eyes fixed to the sullen earth, Gazing on that which seems to dim thy sighed? What seest thou there? King Henries Diadem, Enchased with all the Honours of the world? If so, Gaze on, and grovel on thy face, Until thy head be circled with the same. Put forth thy hand, reach at the glorious Gold. What, is't too short? I'll lengthen it with mine, And having both together heaved it up, We'll both together lift our heads to heaven, And never more abase our sighed so low, As to vouchsafe one glance unto the ground. What, what, my Lord? Are you so choleric With Elianor, for telling but her dream? Next time I'll keep my dreams unto my self, And not be checked. What sayest thou? Majesty: I am but Grace. What sayest thou man? Hast thou as yet conferred With Margery Jordan the cunning Witch, With Roger Bolingbroke the Coniuter? And will they undertake to do me good? What dreamed my Lord, tell me, and I'll requite it With sweet rehearsal of my morning's dream? Tut, this was nothing but and argument, That he that breaks a stick of Gloucesters grove, Shall lose his head for his presumption. But list to me my Humfrey, my sweet Duke: Me thought I sat in Seat of Majesty, In the Cathedral Church of Westminster, And in that Chair where Kings & Queen's were crowned, Where Henry and Dame Margaret kneeled to me, And on my head did set the Diadem. Yes my good Lord, I'll follow presently. Follow I must, I cannot go before, While Gloucester bears this base and humble mind. Were I a Man, a Duke, and next of blood, I would remove these tedious stumbling blocks, And smooth my way upon their headless necks. And being a woman, I will not be slack To play my partly in Fortune's Pageant. Where are you there? Sir John ; nay fear not man, We are alone, here's none but thee, & I. It is enough, I'll think upon the Questions: When from Saint Albans we do make return, We'll see these things effected to the full. Here Hume, take this reward, make merry man With thy Confederates in this weighty cause. Well said my Master's, and welcome all: To this gear, the sooner the better. Not half so bade as thine to England's King, Injurious Duke, that threatest where's no cause. Welcome is Banishment, welcome were my Death. Come you, my Lord, to see my open shame? Now thou dost Penance too. Look how they gaze, See how the giddy multitude do point, And nod their heads, and throw their eyes on thee. Ah Gloucester, hide thee from their hateful looks, And in thy Closet penned up, rue my shame, And ban thine Enemies, both mine and thine. Ah Gloucester, teach me to forget my self: For whilst I think I am thy married Wife, And thou a Prince, Protector of this Land, Me thinks I should not thus be led along, Mailed up in shame, with Papers on my back, And followed with a Rabble, that rejoice To see my tears, and hear my deepe-fet groans. The ruthless Flint does cut my tender feet, And when I start, the envious people laugh, And bid me be advised how I tread. Ah Humfrey, can I bear this shameful yoke? Trowest thou, that ere I'll look upon the World, Or count them happy, that enjoys the Sun? No: Dark shall be my Light, and Night my Day. To think upon my Pomp, shall be my Hell. Sometime I'll say, I am Duke Humphrey's Wife, And he a Prince, and Ruler of the Land: Yet so he ruled, and such a Prince he was, As he stood by, whilst I, his forlorn Duchess, Was made a wonder, and a pointing stock To every idle Rascal follower. But be thou mild, and blush not at my shame, Nor stir at nothing, till the Axe of Death Hang over thee, as sure it shortly will. For Suffolk, he that can do all in all With her, that hates thee and hates us all, And York, and impious Beauford, that false Priest, Have all lime Bushes to betray thy Wings, And fly thou how thou canst, they'le tangle thee. But fear not thou, until thy foot be snared, Nor never seek prevention of thy foes. What, gone my Lord, and bid me not fare well? Art thou gone to? all comfort go with thee, For none abides with me: my Joy, is Death; Death, at whose Name I oft have been afeared, Because I wished this World's eternity. Stanley, I prithee go, and take me hence, I care not whither, for I beg no favour; Only convey me where thou art commanded. That's bade enough, for I am but reproach: And shall I then be used reproachfully? Sheriff farewell, and better then I fare, Although thou hast been Conduct of my shame. I, I, farewell, thy Office is discharged: Come Stanley, shall we go? My shame will not be shifted with my Sheet: No, it will hang upon my richest Robes, And show it self, attire me how I can. Go, lead the way, I long to see my Prison. Was't I? yea, I it was, proud Frenchwoman: Could I come near your Beauty with my Nails, I could set my ten Commandments in your face. Against her will, good King? look to't in time, Shee'le hamper thee and dandle thee Like a Baby: Though in this place most Master wear no Breeches, She shall not strike, Dame Elianor unrevenged.
