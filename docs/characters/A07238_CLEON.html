Sir, I have made oft The best use in my power, and hope my gleanings, After the full crop others reapd before me, Shall not when I am called on, altogether Appear unprofitable: yet I left The miracle of miracles in our age At home behind me; every where abroad Fame with a true, though prodigal voice, delivered Such wonders of Pulcheria the Princess, To the amazement, nay astonishment rather Of such as herd it, that I found not one In all the States and Kingdoms that I passed through, Worthy to be her second. In this you shall do me A special favour. You amaze me. With your good favour let me interrupt you. Being as she is in every partly so perfect, Me thinks that all kings of our Eastern world Should become rivals for her. You have given her And admirable character. What means this solemn music? I am all eyes and ears. Yes my good Lord, I was ravished with it. Yet there were some That in their sullen looks rather confessed A forced constraint to serve her, then a will To be at her devotion, what are they? From whence are the Ambassadors To whom she promised audience? I'll wait on you, Exeunt. Cheer her up, she faints, Sir. They are returned from the Temple. That I am in a dream, or that I see A second Pallas. She is strongly guarded In her virgin blushes. The triumphs of and artsman Over all infirmities, made authentical With the names of Princes, Kings and Emperors That were his patients. It may be so, but he swear within three days He will grub up your gout by the roots, and make you able To march ten leagues a day in complete armour, Or if you Like not him He is at hand, march up: now speak for your self? I am gone Sir. Exeunt. Your humble to servant, The Lord Paulinus, as a with ness of His zeal, and duty to your Majesty, Presents you with this jewel. It is Preferred by him. No Sir, I would have said his patrimony, And he intreats, since lameness may excuse His not presenting it himself, from me (Though far unworthy to supply his place) You would vouchsafe to accept it.
