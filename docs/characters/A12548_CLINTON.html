But we'll disturb it, & your amorous joys. I feel and Earthquake in my trembling flesh, And my well boding Gems bids me draw A sword of vengeance on this hasty Lord, Ere suffer him to be a pariside. No, but restrain you from a wilful murder. I stand not here to offend, but to defend Your lives and honour gainst so vile and act Would blur the Conquerors fame perpetually, Making your swords the furies firebrands, Bathed so unnaturally in others blood. Where neither Honour, nor Religion springs, 'Tis better far such combats be unsought: I know your pleasant, her father has my grant, You her affection God against my will: The place whereon you stand is our own ground, And here 'tis fit I abritrate the cause. Amongst reasonable men peace is held good: None love dissension, but they thirst for blood. Will you subscribe to what we shall enjoin? In brief 'tis this. You must forbear my house, And never more be seen within my gates. Never regard the passion of a woman, They are wily creatures, and have learned this wit, Where they love most, best to dissemble it. The Bride not up, and the Archbishop come, Some call her down. What was your dream to night? Fear it not, see where she comes. How now my Lord, have you surprised your Bride? You highly wrong me. Threaten me? Not in cold blood. Here are their cases. 2. Sailors canvas Suits. Under the which I think were better clothes, And for their Steeds, thought could not be more quick, Or we had took them. The best that ere I saw. What a brave Horse the Palsgrave rid upon, And with what courage, nimbleness, and strength, Did he unhorse his valiant opposites? Spears flew in splinters, half the way to heaven, And none that ran against him kept this saddle, Except the King, and he demand him well: It joys my soul, that he has yet in store Such manly vigor; and the peoples hearts Were not a little glad. Who was the cause of this, speak; Where my Daughter? Pleases my Liege to lay aside your sorrow, And with your Royal person grace my house, Clynton will hold it and exceeding favour. Lets go seek him. Take several streets, but let your meeting be At the Waterside, least they should flee to Sea. In choler, passion and a crazed brain, But when you have slept upon your menaces, You will not then make good a noble challenge, And enter single combat Like a Lord. The Bishops, Mainz, & Trier, sent from ̄ France, By the French King desire to be admitted. With them associate comes the bold Poytiers, But as I think he's no Ambassador. Or return no more. Such as our Swords by a glorious victory set free. Daughter come from him, least I force you hither. So shall you. I'll take your gage, and meet you when you dare. Clinton kneels with him.

Plot summary

Plot — The Hector of Germany, or The Palsgrave, Prime Elector
. Defeated in his attempt to seize power in Spain, Henry the Bastard attempts to get himself elected Holy Roman Emperor; he secures the support of three of the seven Electors, promising that Saxony will be the power behind his throne. But the other Electors, including the sickly Palsgrave, prefer the Duke of Savoy’s candidature, so the Bastard uses force: he defeats the opposing Electors in battle, imprisons two of them, and has himself crowned, then breaks his word and excludes Saxony from power. News of the ===== PAGE 311 ===== 1613 usurpation cures the Palsgrave, who prepares to mobilize an army. Spain is the theatre of war for their first confrontation. The Palsgrave is victorious, and the Bastard is captured, but rescued by Saxony. The warring potentates seek alliances against one another: the Palsgrave goes to England, the Bastard to France. The Palsgrave competes in an English tournament and is installed in the Order of the Garter. King John of France is reluctant to fight the English again (they have too good a track record of victory in France), and hopes to use diplomacy to secure Edward III’s neutrality. Refused the chance to conduct the mission, Saxony goes to England in disguise, but is identified by the Palsgrave, who dishonours him by pulling off a lock of his hair. Diplomacy fails, and Edward and the Palsgrave invade France, dispatching troops to free the imprisoned Electors. The Palsgrave defeats and kills Saxony in single combat. King John withdraws his support from the Bastard, and Savoy is formally crowned Emperor. In the sub-plot, Lord Fitzwaters is betrothed to Floramell, but discovers that she loves his son. He witnesses a secret assignation between the lovers in the garden of Lord Clinton, Floramell’s father. Clinton orders Young Fitzwaters off his land; Lord Fitzwaters goes further and banishes him from England. Floramell goes with him, leaving in her place a cross-dressed page, whom Lord Fitzwaters ‘marries’. The lovers are separated in a shipwreck: Floramell is washed up on the French coast and becomes a maid of honour at court; Young Fitzwaters is marooned on a barren rock and subsists on a fish diet until he is rescued by Saxony. King John is attracted to Floramell and offers to make way for her by having the Queen murdered. Floramell will not consent to adultery or murder, but agrees to give him a single kiss.
