If Jove espy Sapho, he will devise some new shape to entertain her. Mother, they say she hath her thoughts in a string, that she conquers affections, and sends love up and down upon arrandes, I am afraid she will yerk me, if I hit her. The Gods are amorous and therefore will ling to be pierced. I dare not. My mother bade me draw mine arrow to y e head. I was blind, and could not see mine arrow. That came by the nature of the head, which be king once let out of the bow, Can ̄ find none other ligh ting place but the heart. You gave him a face to allure, then why should not I give him eyes to pierce? You will ever be playing with arrows, Like chil dre ̄ with knives, & the ̄ when you bleed, you cry, go to Vulcan, entreat by prayers, threat ̄ with blows, wow with kisses, ban with curses, try all means to rid these extremities. That he might make me new arrows: for no thing can root out the desires of Phao, but a new shaft of inconstancy, nor any thing turn Saphoeshart, but a new arrow of disdain. And then they disliking one the other, who shall enjoy Phao but Venus? My mother will make much of you, when there are no more men then Vulcan. I warrant you I will cause Phao to languish in your love and Sapho to disdain his. That my mother commanded, Sapho. Then hath mine arrow his effect. I dare not. My mother is in love with Phao, she willed me to strike you with disdain of him, and him with desire of her. I could be even with my mother: and so I will, if I shall call you mother? I have and arrow, with which if I strike Phao, it will cause him to loath only Venus. I will about it. Yea, and left Phao railing on Venus, and cur sing her name: yet still sighing for Sapho, and blafing her virtues. I will not. I will be Saphoes son, I have as you command dead stricken her with a deep disdain of Phao, and Phao as she entreated me, with a great despite of you. Yes. Yes. Yes. Not I. But what will you do for Phao?

Plot — Sappho and Phao
Menus plans to visit Syracuse to make SAppH6 fall in love, and travels there on PHa0’s ferry-boat. She makes Phao beautiful; and, unknown to her, @iipi@ makes him irresistibly attractive to women, even though he is not interested in them. Sibyllaj whom the gods have given long life but not eternal youth, counsels Phao not to waste his prime years. He falls in love with Sappho, and Sibylla advises him on the technique of wooing. Sappho is taken ill; Phao, who has some pharmaceutical knowledge, is summoned to minister to her. In fact, her malady is love for Phao, whose base origins make him unsuitable for her. Venus too falls for Phao, and procures from Wailea new arrows for Cupid, with which he can break her rival Sappho’s infatuation and turn Phao’s affections her way. Cupid carries out the first part of his mission, but also tells Sappho what he has done; so she persuades him to use another of the new arrows to make Phao hate Venus. Cupid decides to be Sappho’s son instead of Venus’, and gives her his arrows: now she, not Venus, will be the lady of love. Venus swears to get even, and, convinced of love’s pointlessness, Phao leaves Sicily.
