Good son, a way. A father gives command. Dear husband, I am yet clear; O do not you Force me to sin, I'll be for over true. Heaven pardon your black slanders. Who made you? I'll work, or beg for you. Any thing. Will you forsake me then? Take place, thou tyrant will. Thick woes here hover. My state is lower then fate can recover. My obedience waits your pleasure. About some six and forty. I can skip lighter then the wanton Do, And jerk it through the Dale, I cannot hold, neither my tongue, nor heals, (Nor nails from scratching out a Leachers eyes) Sure, I am composed most of the nimbler elements: But little water in me, far less earth, some air, To keep me humid, mutable, and tender, And apt for convolution: but their mixture Is scarce discernible, the are so dispersed. For my predominant quality is all fire, Pure, radiant, subtle fire. Till you are weary of me. I'll but take leave of my brother, and follow you. Am I not worthy of one kiss? Be gone? Death could not speak a word more fatal. Yet one more—so now farewell— Unjust—unkind—my woe-diuining heart. By this we first embraced, by this we partly. I herd this way some men distressed voice, Crying for help: some robbery. O 'tis no wonder! A thief and bawdy house are never far asunder. Would you turn hangman, Sir? O pity them: necessity has no law. Perhaps want forced them; though it was not good. What Horseleeches are they, that full, suck blood! There is and Inn, enter, refresh your selves. Their lose is money, yet I moan their state. Who pities me most, most unfortunate! Robbed of a husbands love, now of himself. How far is this beyond all lose of pelf! He sold me hither; may that sinful price Of my deep sorrow never prejudice His happiness, what climate ever holds him. Be blessed, sweet husband; let my ruin buy Thy wished content, though I forsaken die. This witch has tired me with her customers, Whom I have all sent home with bettered minds. Against her vicious will, I force her strive By virtue rather, then by lust to thrive. I know, I am expected. More robberies yet? 'tis strange, how villains swarm! Mischiefs hold close to keep each other warm. Three rank corruptions make their near abided. And Abbey, Bawd'house, and a Thievish road. Where be these men distressed?—how? my Usurer? Sir, do not think to enter my chaste fort, Encouraged by this parley. You presume— Then use it well. How's wealth abused, when it conducts to hell! Sir, I will set no price on your desires. You must: Then pay me for my virtue: so I'll take it. What starves lust, is well bought; not what it feeds. It's follies dregs, with coin to buy ill deeds. These are some incitations to a heart Tainted with malice, or that thinks a heaven In glorious ostentation; or would stand Affected with the bane of prurient lust. I'm of another temper. Pray you leave me. Thy language does affright me. O my stars! Sir, I have no desire, To take your lust, but pity. Some what prompts my credulous heart, there is some goodness in you. Plot summary

Plot — The Honest Lawyer
Richard Vaster’s lands are mortgaged to Gripe the usurer for £300. Jealously believing himself to have been cuckolded, he sells his wife Florence to the bawd and innkeeper Marmaid, claiming her to be his sister. He then challenges Benjamin Gripe, the usurer’s son, to a duel, and pretends to be killed; the remorseful Benjamin is determined to make amends by looking after Vaster’s family. Valentine sets himself up as a phoney doctor and is asked to cure Gripe’s gout, which entails driving a large nail through the affected toe. While Gripe is inconvenienced by the nail, Valentine steals his purse and keys. Gripe also sends Benjamin to foreclose on Vaster’s mortgage; when Benjamin asks him to give the bereaved family a three-month respite, he is disinherited. Surprisingly, the nail does indeed drive out the gout. Curfew has passed himself off as the deputy Abbot of Newnham after the death of the real deputy, his brother, and he uses the abbey as a base for highway robbery. His first two victims are Valentine and the disguised Vaster, who both prove as rascally as he; the three of them end up going into partnership. They proceed to rob travellers: Vaster happily relieves Gripe of his money, but lets Benjamin off with an injunction to be good to the Vaster family. Whenever they leave anyone tied up on the road, Florence comes out of the nearby inn and releases them. She is proving a 1614 disappointment to Marmaid ~ all her clients seem to end up with a new moral outlook on prostitution — so the bawd allows Benjamin, the landlord, to take her away. He lodges her at his father’s house under an assumed name, Widow Sorrow. Valentine makes an affray at the inn, and is arrested. The justice before whom he is brought happens to be Gripe, who lets him off, because he has developed another medical complaint: inability to urinate. Valentine proposes using gunpowder to blow open the urinary tract, and again the treatment is surprisingly successful. Robert and Anne, Vaster’s son and daughter, are destitute, but nobody will give them any money, not even Bromley, Vaster’s former steward who owes everything to the family. Benjamin sends Robert to see ‘Widow Sorrow’, and falls in love with Anne. Robert soon identifies the widow as his mother, but suspects that her presence in the usurer’s household confirms his father’s suspicions of her adultery.
