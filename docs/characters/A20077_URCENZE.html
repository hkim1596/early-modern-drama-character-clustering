But Sirrah Rice, when's the day? will not thy master Sir Owen and Signior Emulo fight? Then heel never be saved by his book. Would, would, adue Rice, Sir Owen belike keeps a lean Kitchen. That long coat makes the master a little king, for wheresoever his piece of a follower comes hopping after him, he's sure of a double Guard. I shall feel them no more then so many fleas, therefore I care not: but Farnese yowl prove a most ac complisht coxecombe. He's Steel to the back you see, for he writes Challenges. What be the accontreme ̄ ts now of these galla ̄ ts? Whats their qualities? They cannot choose but ride well, because every good wit rides them. No more of these Jadith tricks: here comes the Hobby horse. He would jangle villanously. Well encountered S. Owen. His leg graces the boot. Sweet widow even your countryman here. I think Salomon was wiser then Sir Owen. If she miss his crown 'tis no matter for crack king, Peace here comes our fair mistress. So you may, but the hardness is to hit her. Every courteous mouth will be a stage for that, rather tell her of the welch tragedy that's towards. Would you not have men love you sweet mistress? Nor I the sweetness of her looks shall make me relish any punishment. The other I have taken up with the fine finger. Come Signior, one fine lie now to apparel all these former, in some light sarsenet robe of truth: none, none, in this mint? Fie Emulo, nothing but wardrobe, yet hear all your trunks of suits? She's wondrous beautiful & wondrous kind. What says she sir Owen? So does Vrcenze. He had needs for his head is very poor. And your beauty the colours we fight under. I obey though I taste no pleasure in it. Trust me nor I.
