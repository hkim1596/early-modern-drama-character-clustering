All well my sovereign Liege, except some few, That are deceased by fortune of the war. Victory my Liege, and that with little lose. Tribute and wonted homage therewithal. Where Spain and Portingale do jointly knit Their frontiers, leaning on each others bound: There met our armies in their proud array, Both furnished well, both full of hope and fear: Both menacing alike with daring shows, Both vaunting sundry colours of device, Both cheerly sounding trumpets, drums and fifes. Both raising dreadful clamours to the sky, That valleis, hills, and rivers made rebound, And heaven it self was frighted with the found. Our battles both were pitched in squadron form, Each corner strongly enst with wings of shot, But ere we joined and came to push of Pike, I brought a squadron of our readiest shot, From out our rearward to begin the fight, They brought another wing to encounter us: Mean while our ordinance played on either side, And Captains strove to have their valours tried. No peace my Liege, but peace conditional, That if with homage tribute be well paid, The fury of your forces will be stayed. And to this peace their Viceroy hath subscribde. And made a solemn vow that during life, His tribute shall be truly paid to Spain. This tells me that your graces men of war, Such as wars fortune hath reserved from death, Come marching on towards your royal seat, To show themselves before your Majesty, For so I gave in charge at my depart. Whereby by demonstration shall appear, That all (except three hundred or few more) Are safe returned and by their foes enriched. It was my Liege, the Prince of Portingale.
