Room shall not lose its name, the world's Commander, Till this knot unties; perpetual be it, till Room erects Our golden Statues, placed by Saturn, and great Jupiter, And there be deified, to bless all those which may succeed But in these designs, let us remember high deserving Albon, whose valuor was not seconded this day By any single Arm. O Brother Caesar, in this British calm weel pass again Over to stormy France, the Goths, and Vandals have outpast The bounds, and over the Rhine passed into Burgandy, our work Must be to reverberate, and drive them to Confined Germany, while you persever, with and awful hand, To keep our conquered foes beneath your feet; give not those Frighted Welch-men time to breath: But if again you doubt of what you can, you know your Friend called Dioclessan ? A careful Policy, be my Tutor to teach me Military Discipline, Fly Brason Oratrix, all lingued fame, And tell at Room of Maximinus name: Say Dioclesian too will bring a Crown, To bind thy seven fold Head with high Renown: Flourish. Say Like two Jove's, when our Dread Thunders burled, Our sable Eagle strikes through all the world. Exeunt omn. Advance the Roman Eagle, and command Our armed Legions to troop close, and stand. Death blur their parley, we'll not answer The thunder of their Drums: our Eagle shall not nesell by base Ravens, but to peck out their eyes; our Swords shall answer The Thunder of their Drums, the Roman Caesar holds scorn To parley with such servile Nations, as you the barbarous Vandals And Goths, poor frozen Snakes, that from the Northern cold Creep to the warmth of the Suns Western fires, Troubling our fertile Lands, and Like starved sheep, You spoil the Countries with a line you keep in Regions beggarly. What croaks the Raven? Of two barbarous Kings. Would, would, would; must Lyons be enforced to league with Wolves. Poor Flies, behold the Eagle, and give over; Strive not to cope with strength beyond your power, For us she spreads her wings as far and bright, As in a Day the Sun rides with his light, And that's the universal Globe of Earth: Europes proud throat we tread on: Afric and Asia our Eagles talents gripe, The Lords of Room fathom both Land and Deep. We'll hear no more; call up the Britain Soldiers Our Brother Maximinus sent unto our aid, let they begin the battle, Fight Like Roman: Remember this, your enemies are base; Let your Swords work Like Sithes confound these swarms, And sweep these Locusts hence with conquering Arms. Exen to. Turn thee base Vandal. And thou hast found me; He teach thee speak the Roman Language. A Fencer 'tis agreed. The School trick thou shalt learn at first blow. Alarnm. What art thou that hast saved me? And Emperor. I thank thee: follow thy Fortune's, and go on; The gods of Room fit on thy weapon still: The battle ended, see me in my Tent. Immortal gods! How crept a Kingly spirit into a breast so low! How now, how goes the day? Enter a Roman. How Traitor? The Roman gods forbid: Let a Trumpet call up the Britain's to recover it. Exit. Beside our gift, the ransom of this King I freely give; and that thy same may sing a lofty note, Back to thy Country led these Britain Soldiers, Over whom I make thee head; and to the Emperor Maximinus thou shalt bear such Letters from our self, As he shall rear and swell thine honours, And when we in France have laid These Whirlwinds that now shake the State, Weel cross the seas to Britain after thee. In what circumference? This in the South: Brave Crispianus, to requite thy deed, Great Dioclesian's hand shall Crown thy head. A Crown presented, Even with their bloods they have: Their daring and their downfalls fill one grave, And yet our Conquest had not spread such wings But for those Britain forces you sent over: They from the French Field plucked the noblest Flower, And of them all, a Soldier too, whose Fame I cannot sing too much, carried the name Of Honour from us all: his good sword flew Like Lightning, And where it went, overthrew: the King of Goths Called me his prisoner, but then this brave Opponent Fetched me off in ransom with his blood, and that being done, He Like a Lion on the Vandal run: Took him, and closed the battle in his fallen, The work was bloody, rough, and Tragical; And therefore for my love pray crown his head That twice saved mine: It is a man, whose Fate Upheld the glory of the Roman State. Plot summary

Plot — A Shoemaker a Gentleman
The Roman Emperors, Diocletian and Maximinus, bring an army to Britain to crush the Christians. Alured, the British King, is killed in battle. His sons, Eldred and Offa, change their identities and get themselves apprenticed to a shoemaker. They see their mother taken to prison after being captured on the battlefield; their sympathy for her plight impresses their new master. Alban is appointed to rule Britain and continue the persecution. Amphiabel comes from Wales and converts him to Christianity. Maximinus arrests Alban, and Amphiabel flees back to Wales, pursued by the Roman army. Meanwhile, the Welsh Queen Winifred, also converted by Amphiabel, asks her suitor Sir Hugh for a moratorium on his wooing efforts; when he completes the agreed period, however, she announces that she has given herself wholly to heaven and cannot marry him. One of the Romans pursuing Amphiabel mocks the power of Winifred’s holy well, and smears the water on his eyes; it blinds him. His sight is restored, but he ascribes the miracle to the pagan gods. Winifred and Amphiabel are both arrested and brought to see the martyrdom of Alban. Leodice, Maximinus’ daughter, becomes enamoured of her new shoemaker, Crispin, who is really Offa; she continually finds reasons to asks for new shoes and new fitting sessions. She offers to show him the image of his future wife in a magic mirror; but in fact it is an ordinary mirror, and he sees her reflection. He confides that he is not really base-born, but a disguised prince, and they are secretly married. The Romans are mustering an army to resist an invasion of the Empire by Goths and Vandals. Eldred volunteers, and the shoemaker employs Sir Hugh to fill his place. Eldred distinguishes himself in battle: he saves Diocletian’s life, takes back the eagle standard after it has been seized by the enemy, and captures the Vandal king Roderick. The barbarians are expelled, and Diocletian knights Eldred. Sir Hugh is arrested after he sees Winifred a prisoner and reviles the Roman gods. Alban and Amphiabel are tortured in an attempt to make them renounce Christianity, but they remain steadfast and are sent for execution; Alban becomes Britain’s first Christian martyr ~ but not the last. Winifred is allowed to choose the manner of her own execution; she opts to bleed to death. Sir Hugh is made to drink her blood, mixed with poison.
