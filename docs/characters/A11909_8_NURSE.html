Oh worthy Oueene among the Greeks that bears the swinging sway. And born of Leda's royal blood, what muttering dost thou say? What fury fell enforceth thee, bereaved of thy wits. To rage and rave with bedlam brains, to fret withfranticke fits? Though madam thou do counsel keep, and not complain thy case, Thine anguish plain appears in thy pale and wanny face. Reveal therefore what is thy grief, take leisure good and stay, What reason could not remedy, oft cured hath delay. In silent shore thou sailest yet thy trespass we may hide, If thou thy self detect it not, nor cause it be descried. This desprat dotage does declare, and rashness rude and blind, To choose out chance to be the guide and ruler of thy mind. Thy former fait with pensive heart and sorrow thou dest rew. And fondly yet thou goest about, to set abroach a new, The thing he fears he does augment who heapeth sin to sin. There is no man who at the first extremity will try. The sacred name of wedlock once revoke and have in mind. Consider yet thy son and heir whom he of thee begotten. When as our navy might not pass by wind nor yet by stream, Thy daughters blood in sacrifice their passage did redeem: She sturd and brake the sluggish seas, whose water still did stand, Whose Feeble force might not hoist up, the vessels from the land.
