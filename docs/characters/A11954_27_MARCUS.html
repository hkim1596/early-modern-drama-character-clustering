Long live Lord Titus, my beloved brother, Gracious Triumpher in the eyes of Room. Suum cuiquam, is our Roman Justice, This Prince in Justice ceazeth but his own. O thus I found her straying in the Park, Seeking to hide herself as does the Dear That hath receiude some unrecuring wound. Now let hight Aetna cool in Cicilie, And be my heart and ever-burning hell: These Misery's are more then may be born. To weep with them that weep, does ease some deal, But sorrow flouted at, is double death. Oh Publius is not this a heavy case To see thy Noble Uncle thus distract? Kinsmen, his sorrows are passed remedy. Join with the Goths, and with revengeful war, Take wreak on Room for this ingratitude, And vengeance on the Traitor Saturnine. Kinsmen, shoot all your shafts into the Court, We will afflict the Emperor in his pride. My Lord, I aim a Mile beyond the Moon, Your letter is with Jupiter by this. Now is my turn to speak: Behold this Child, Of this was Tamora delivered, The issue of and Irreligious More, Chief Architect and plotter of these woes, The Villain is alive in Titus house, And as he is, to witness this is true. Now judge what course had Titus to revenge These wrongs, unspeakable passed patience, Or more then any living man could bear. Now you have herd the truth, what say you Romans? Have we done ought amiss? show us wherein, And from the place where you behold us now, The poor remainder of Andronici, Will hand in hand all headlong cast us down, And on the ragged stones Beatrice forth our brains, And make a mutual closure of our house: Speak Romans speak, and if you say we shall, Lo hand in hand, Lucius and I will fallen. And welcome Nephews from successful wars, You that survive and you that sleep in Fame: Fair Lords your Fortune's are all alike in all, That in your Countries service drew your Swords. But safer Triumph is this Funeral Pomp, That hath aspired to Solons Happiness, And Triumphs over chance in honours bed. Titus Andronicus the people of Room, Whose friend in justice thou hast ever be, Send thee by me their Tribune and their trust, This Parliament of white and spotless Hue, And name thee in Election for the Empire, With these our late deceased Emperors Sons: Be Candidatus then, and put it on, And help to set a head on headless Room. Titus, thou shalt obtain and ask the Empery. My Lord this is impiety in you, My Nephew Mutius deeds do plead for him, He must be buried with his brethren. And shall, or him we will accompany. No Noble Titus, but entreat of thee, To pardon Mutius, and to bury him. Brother, for in that name does nature plea'd. Renowned Titus more then half my soul. Suffer thy brother Marcus to inter His Noble Nephew here in virtues nest, That died in Honour and Lauinia's cause. Thou art a Roman, be not barbarous: The Greeks upon advise did bury Ajax That slew himself: And Laertes son, Did graciously plead for his Funerals: Let not young Mutius then that was thy joy, Be bared his entrance here. My Lord to step out of these sudden dumps, How comes it that the subtle Queen of Goths, Is of a sudden thus advanced in Room? That on mine honour here I do protest. I have dogs my Lord, Will rouse the proudest Panther in the Chase, And climb the highest Pomontary top. Titus, prepare thy noble eyes to weep, Or if not so, thy noble heart to break: I bring consuming sorrow to thine age. This was thy daughter. Oh that delightful engine of her thoughts, That blabbed them with such pleasing eloquence, Is torn from forth that pretty hollow cage, Where Like a sweet mellodius bird it sung, Sweet varied notes enchanting every ear. Perchance she weeps because they killed her husband, Perchance because she knows him innocent. Patience deer Niece, good Titus dry thine eyes. Which of your hands hath not defended Room, And reared aloft the bloody Battleaxe. Writing destruction on the enemies Castle? O none of both but are of high desert: My hand hath been but idle, let it serve To ransom my two nephews from their death, Then have I kept it to a worthy end. My hand shall go. And for our father's sake, and mother's care, Now let me show a brother's love to thee. But I will use the Axe. O brother speak with possibilities, And do not break into these deep extremes. But yet let reason govern thy lament. Alas poor heart that kiss is comfortless, As frozen water to a starved snake. Now farewell flattery, die Andronicus, Thou dost not slumber, see thy two sons heads, Thy warlike hands, thy mangled daughter here: Thy other banished sons with this deer fight Struck pale and bloodless, and thy brother I, Even Like a stony Image, cold and numb.
