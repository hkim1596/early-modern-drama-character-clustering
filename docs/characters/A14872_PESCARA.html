Full speech

Bosola arrived? what should be the business? Some falling out amongst the Cardinals. These factions amongst great men, they are Like Foxes, when their heads are divided They carry fire in their tails, and all the Country About them, goes to wrack for't. Mark Prince Ferdinand, A very Salamander lives in's eye, To mock the eager violence of fire. The Lord Ferdinand laughs. These are your true pangs of death, The pangs of life, that struggle with great statesmen, Doctor, he did not fear you thoroughly. Knows your grace What accident hath brought unto the Prince, This strange distraction? We'll leave your grace, Wishing to the sick Prince, our noble Lord, All health of mind, and body. So sir, we shall not, Let out honours bind this trifle. How now (my Lord?) How now (my Lord?) How fatally (it seems) he did withstand, His own rescue? The noble Delio, as I came to palace, Told me of Antonio's being here, and showed me A pretty gentlemen his son and heir. To me. You are my friend: But this is such a suit, Nor fit for me to give, nor you to take. I will give you ample reason for't, Soon in private: Here's the Cardinals Mistress. He entreats for you The Citadel of Saint Bennet, that belonged To the banished Bologna. I could not have thought of a friend, I could Rather pleasure with it: it's yours: Why. Do you know what it was? It was Antonios land: not forfeited By course of law; but ravished from his throat By the Cardinals entreaty: it were not fit I should bestow so main a piece of wrong Upon my friend: it's a gratification Only endue to a Strumpet: for it is injustice; Shall I sprinkle the pure blood of Innocents To make those followers, I call my friends Look ruddier upon me? I am glad This land, (taken from the owner by such wrong) Returns again unto so fowl and use, As Salary for his Lust. Learn, (good Delio ) To ask noble things of me, and you shall find I'll be a noble giver. Prince Ferdinand's come to Milan Sick (as they give out) of and Apoplexy: But some say, it's a frenzy; I am going To visit him. Exit. Now Doctor; may I visit your Patient? Pray-thee, what's his disease? What's that? I needs a Dictionary to't. I am glad on't, Rise good my Lord. It's a noble Virtue; Your Doctor.. He wished you should not come at him: but beleeu't, The accent of the voice sounds not in jest. I'll down to him, howsoever, and with engines Force open the doors.

Plot summary

Plot — The Duchess of Malfi
There are two new arrivals at the Duchess of Malfi’s court. One is Antonio, her steward, who has returned from an extended visit to France. The other is Bosola, an ex-convict who committed a murder for the Cardinal, and who is now placed at court by the Duchess’s brother Ferdinand, with instructions to supply information. Ferdinand and the Cardinal leave the court, enjoining their sister not to marry again, but she defies them and secretly woos and marries Antonio. She clandestinely gives birth to a son, which Bosola discovers after Antonio accidentally drops the baby’s horoscope. The news is duly reported to the infuriated Ferdinand. The Duchess has two more children. Ferdinand visits Malfi, planning to marry her to Count Malateste. He visits her private chamber and catches her talking to Antonio, whose identity remains unknown because he has hidden as part of a practical joke. She banishes Antonio as a pretext to get him out of court, and follows him on a feigned pilgrimage to the state of Ancona. Bosola wins her confidence by speaking well of Antonio, unlike the other court flatterers; she entrusts him with the secret of their marriage, which he duly reports to his paymaster. The Cardinal is commissioned to fight in the war, and ceremonially puts off his ecclesiastical office. By papal decree, the Duchess and Antonio are banished ===== PAGE 347 ===== 1613 from Ancona and the Duchy of Malfi is seized by the Holy See; Antonio flees to Milan with his eldest son, and Bosola arrests the Duchess. Acting on Ferdinand’s orders, he imprisons and torments her, shows her the dead bodies of Antonio and her son (which turn out to be waxworks), and finally supervises her and her younger children’s murder. Ferdinand disowms him, then goes mad with lycanthropic delusions. The Cardinal schemes to have Antonio’s lands confiscated, poisons his mistress, and inadvertently reveals to Bosola his part in the Duchess’s death. Seeking revenge, Bosola mistakenly kills Antonio in the dark. In his madness, Ferdinand kills the Cardinal and Bosola, who kills him in turn. Delio decides to advance the rights of Antonio’s surviving son.
