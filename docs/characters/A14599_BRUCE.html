May it please my Sovereign Confirm his grant touching— Gracious Liege. Following the execution which we held Three English miles in length. Yes for Scottish blood, I never shall have Enough on't, the Kings health. What's that you all laughed at? Nay, good Sir tell me. No more, I have drunk too much, Wallace and I did parlee. No Percy, I'm no prater, 'twas with swords, Your laughing jest was not at me? I would done sir. As you are a soldier, as you are noble I charge you and conjure you to unclasp A book in which I am graveled. Yes, if you dare you can. Then tell me sir, what was that bitter scorn, Which I Like poison tasted in my wine? You Scot drinks his own blood, which Scot? Who brake the jest upon me? The Oracle I'll wake is here, o Wallace, I never had eyes till now, they were closed up By braving English, witchcraft drinks his own blood, England my stepdame take my bitter curse, Thy own nails tear thy own bowels, o my parent Dear Scotland, I no more will be a goad, Pricking thy sides, but if ere I draw a sword, It shall be double-edged with blood and fire, To burn and drown this Kingdom and this King. Thanks noble Clifford, what did he bid thee say? A pair of Spurs, Bruce never was runaway, Twelve silver pence, o bitter scorn, with Judas, I have betrayed my Master, my dear Country, And here's the emblem of my treachery, To hasten to some tree, and desperate die, Twelve sterling silver pence, sterling, would sterling, It's a limb of Scotland, spurs for flight, Clifford, i'll thither, comment I wrong or right. None that dare oppose your King, O my impostumed spleen, Will fly into their faces, what command Has England now? against whom? I will not, I'm not his Butcher, Gainst the Scots I will not fight. No, will not Clifford. My Lord, I dare not, In this last battle I received some wounds That yet bleed inward, I will no more banquet strangers With my native blood. English Edward commands not Like a King, Thrice honoured Clifford, I'll trust you with my bosom. Let him have noble trial. Me hence? What is King Edwards meaning? You may. Exeunt Bruce, North and Clifford. England is full of honour, Bruce does bend To thy command. They crown him. Stand back, a Serpent shall not with his breath Infect our Kingly ears, die slave, for he That would betray his friend shall never serve me. A sacrifice of honour and revenge, no traitors hand Shall help to lift a Crown up to my head, Thou didst betray, then die unpitied. I have no hand in't though. Here. They are Colours. To hell if I can chase him. Though I never stooped unto a traitors lure, I scorn thine, why dost thou single me, Yet turnest thy weapon downward to the earth? I'll parley with no traitor but with blows. Why against thy Sovereign lifts thou then thy sword? Tell Edward so thy King. This I'll try. Thou hast no treason towards me? In Glasco-moore I'll meet thee, fare thee well. Some two hours hence. Help to disguise me Soldier, in exchange Take these for thine, and here's some gold to boot. Phew, I warrant thee, Seal up thy lips and eyes, thou neither seest Nor canst tell where I am. Here quick mine own again, and get thee gone. Who calls for Bruce? It shall not, I will but, Spare me, the air hath ears no more, You sent to me, I will but tell bold Clifford Not a word, My thoughts owe as much honour as their Lord. Within traitor, traitor. Plot summary

Plot — The Valiant Scot
PLOT Scotland is under English rule. An attempt is made to dispossess Old Wallace of his office and lands; but Old Wallace partially circumvents it by transferring ownership of the land to his son. King Edward plans to make war on France, and appoints Bruce his general. Scotland is left in the charge of Queen Elinor and a panel of commissioners. Young Selby, son of one of the commissioners, abducts Peggy Graham, intending to marry her by force. Wallace intervenes and kills Young Selby in a duel. Commissioner Selby arrests Peggy and threatens her with execution for the murder. Wallace gives himself up, and Peggy is released. His own execution is forestalled when the Queen orders him brought to court for interrogation. He escapes en route, marries Peggy, and becomes a rebel. Friar Gertrid prophesies that he will be victorious until betrayed by his own blood. The English capture Peggy, Gertrid, and Old Wallace, and Selby has them killed. King Edward sends Wallace an ultimatum: if he will surrender and beg for mercy, he may be spared. Wallace sends an alternative ultimatum back: if Edward will admit himself to be a tyrant, he may be spared. Upon discovering that one of the ambassadors, the Queen’s nephew, has no diplomatic accreditation, he puts the young man to death. He then cuts out an ambassador’s tongue and blinds another, and accompanies them back to King Edward disguised as a lame soldier. The English plan to entice Wallace into a trap, and send another messenger to invite him to a parley; but the stratagem fails because the man appointed to escort the messenger to the Scottish camp is the lame soldier — Wallace himself. Wallace sails to seek help from France, but his ship is wrecked. On the shore, he meets first Bolt, from whom he forcibly takes possession of a washed-up chest of provisions, then Selby, who is now banished from England and penurious, and regrets the execution of Old Wallace. Wallace decides not to take revenge, but while he is away fetching food, another of the fallen commissioners turns up and kills Selby in an argument. Bolt returns and kills the murderer, taking him for Wallace. He is presented to King Edward and rewarded, but later discredited when Wallace turns out to be alive. The Scots prepare to fight, but Wallace is offended to be placed in the rearguard, and withdraws his services.
