Sweet sister, I know not how it comes to pass, but I find in my self passion more than brotherly. Our parents are poor, our love unnatural, what can then happen to make us happy? It is good counsel fair sister, if the nieces sity of love could be relieved by counsel, yet this is our comfort, that these unnatural heats have stretched themselves no further than thoughts, un happy me that they should stretch so. So it is Serena, the nearer we are in blood, the further we must be from love, and the greater the kindred is, the less the kindness must be, so that be tween brother's & sisters superstition hath made of fection cold, between strangers custom hath bred love exquisite. Nor any good I think Serena, yet to satisfy thy mind we will see what she can say. who is within? She might have said the beldame, for her face and years, and attire. This is my sister, marry we cannot: our pa rends are poor and have no land to give us, each of us is a fool to come for counsel to such and old fool. Content sweet sister, and learn of me hereaf ter, that these old saws of such old hags, are but false fires to lead one out of a plain path into a deep pit. How happy is Maestius thou blessed Serena, that being neither children to poor parents, nor bro there and sister by nature, may enjoy their love by con sent of parents and nature.
