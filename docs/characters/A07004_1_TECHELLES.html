His deep affections make him passionate. With duty not with amity we yield Our utmost service to the fair Cosroe. Oh my Lord, 'tis sweet and full of pomp. Then shall we send to this triumphing King, And bid him battle for his novel Crown? See you Agidas how the King salutes you. He bids you prophesy what it imports. Vsumeasane, see how right the man Hath hit the meaning of my Lord the King. Agreed Casane, we will honour him. The more he brings, the greater is the spoil, For when they perish by our warlike hands, We mean to seat our footmen on their Steeds. And rifle all those stately Ianisars. Puissant, renowned and mighty tamburlain, Why stay we thus prolonging all their lives'? You must devise some torme ̄ to worse, my Lord To make these captives reine their lavish tongues. I pray you give them leave Madam, this speech is a goodly refreshing to them. 'Tis Like he will, when he cannot let it. 'Tis enough for us to see them, and for tam burlaine only to enjoy them. They have my Lord, and on Damascus walls Have hoist up their slaughtered carcases. The Sultan and the Arabian king together March on us with such eager violence, As if there were no way but one with us. As princely Lions when they rouse themselves, Stretching their paws, and threatening herds of Beasts. So in his Armour looks Tamburlaine: Me thinks I see kings kneeling at his feet, And he with frowning brows and fiery looks , Spurning their crowns from off their captive heads. We are his friends, and if the Persean king Should offer present Duke domes to our state, We think it lose to make exchange for that, We are assured of by our friends success. And that made us the friends of Tamburlaine. To lift our swords against the Persean King. What now? In love? No: cowards and fainthearted runaways, Look for orations when the foe is near. Our swords shall play the Orators for us. Come let us march. Welcome renowned Persean to us all. We have their crowns their bodies strow the field. My hand is ready to perform the deed, For now her marriage time shall work us rest: The town is ours my Lord, and fresh supply Of conquest, and of spoil is offered us: I, if I could with all my heart my Lord. I hear them come, shall we encounter them?
