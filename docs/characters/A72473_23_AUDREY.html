Oh their naked weapons! Oh my Father, and Mother! But is not Puppy hurt? nor the tother man? Father— He that shall be My husband— Yes, I shall anon be married: That's he. Come Father; I would we were married: I am a cold. Mother, I'll go with you, and with my Father. What have you To do to ask, I pray you? I am a cold. You lie; I am as cold as Ice is: Feel else. Done with me? I do defy you. So I do, to say You have done with me: you are a awcy Puppy. Meant you not knavery; Puppy? No: not I. Clay meant you all the knavery, it seems, Who rather, then he would be married to you, Chosen to be wedded to the Gallows first. I thought he was a dissembler; he would prov A slippery Merchant in the frost. He might Have married one first, and have been hanged after, If he had had a mind to't. But you men, Fie on you. You are disposed to jeer one, Mr. Hannibal. Enter Hilts. Pity of me! the angry man with the beard! What Puppy? Puppy? Alas Sir, what is't you would have me do? I'd feign do all for the best, if I knew how. No Mistress, Sir, my name is Awdrey. Squire Tub, you mean? I know him: he knows me too. I, so he told me: in his wits, I think. But he's too fine for me; and has a Lady Tub to his Mother. Here he comes himself! You are a merry man, Squire Tub, of Totten! I have herd much of your words, but not of your deeds. Yet, I was never so straightlaced to you, Squire. Love you? I cannot tell: I must hate no body, My Father says. It shall be for your sake then. He offers to kiss her. She puts him back. Soft, and fair, Squire, there go two word's to a bargain. Nay, I cannot tell. My Mother said, zure, if you married me, You'd make me a Lady the first week: and put me In, I know not what, the very day. A velvet dressing for my head, it is, They say will make one brave: I will not know Bess Moale, nor Margery Turne-up: I will look Another way upon them, and be proud. Was ever silly Maid thus posted off? That should have had three husbands in one day; Yet (by bade fortune) am possessed of none? I went to Church to have been wed to Clay; Then Squire Tub he seized me on the way, And thought to have had me: but he missed his aim; And Justice Bramble (nearest of the three) Was well nigh married to me; when by chance, In rushed my Father, and broke off that dance. Husbands, they say, grow thick; but thin are sown, I care not who it be, so I have one. Now out on me! what shall I do then? His worship now, and then, please you, takes pains To see my Father, and Mother: But for me, I know my self too mean for his high thoughts To stoop at, more then asking a light question, To make him merry, or to pass his time. They go out. Mother, the still Sow eats up all the draff. I love not to be led: I'ld go alone. You are too finical form; speak plain Sir. Forbear Squire Tub, as mine own mother says; I am not for your mowing. Yowl be flown Ere I be fledge. Now, out upon you, are you not ashamed? What will my Lady say? In faith I think She was at our house: And I think she asked for you: And I think she hit me in the teeth with you, I thank her Ladyship, and I think she means Not to go hence, till she has found you. How say you? Yes, twenty words. No Sir, what's that? If't be a toy, it's good to play withal. If you do mean to stand so long upon it; I pray you let me give it a short cut, Sir. You are disposed to ask. Nay, now I see you are disposed indeed. In as plain terms, I tell you who would have me. John Clay would have me, but he hath too hard hands; I Like not him: beside, he is a thief. And Justice Bramble, he would feign have catched me: But the young Squire, he, rather then his life, Would have me yet; and make me a Lady, he says, And be my Knight; to do me true Knights service, Before his Lady Mother. Can you make me A Lady, would I have you? Is the Squire gone? I am thine.

Plot summary

Plot — The Magnetic Lady, or Humors Reconciled
Probee and Damn-Play are sent on stage by the audience to bargain for a good play; what they get is The Magnetic Lady. They watch the play and comment on it between the acts. Visitors flock to Lady Loadstone’s house, some brought by her lodger, Compass, others to pay court to her sickly but wealthy niece, Placentia Steel, on her birthday. By the terms of her parents’ will, Placentia’s marriage portion of £16,000 is to be paid when she marries, but until then is held by her uncle, Interest. There are several suitors, including Practice the lawyer and Silkworm the courtier. Lady Loadstone initially prefers Practice, but the prospect of being made a countess through a friend at court converts her to Silkworm’s suit. Palate also promotes Silkworm, pointing out that Practice is already engaged to the servant Pleasance. Interest is trying to arrange a marriage between Placentia and Bias, who is a younger brother and will be content to accept less than the full dowry. Ironside, Compass’s brother, wounds Silkworm in a violent altercation at dinner, and Compass persuades the courtier to seek redress in a duel rather than at law; Bias hopes that Silkworm will be killed and the court rid of a nuisance, but in the event he merely talks to Ironside about valour. Placentia turns out to be pregnant, and the suitors back off; Practice asks Compass to help him marry Pleasance instead. Compass learns that Pleasance and Placentia were swapped for one another in their cradles, and uses the blank licence obtained by Practice to get himself married to ‘Pleasance’. His bride then disappears, and turns out to have been abducted by Polish, her supposed mother, who is trying to conceal the baby swap. The steward Needle pretends to suffer from walking and talking in his sleep, and persuades Interest that a ghost, in love with him, has hidden treasure in the garden. Interest goes looking for it and falls into a well. The false Placentia gives birth. Convinced that the pregnancy was only malicious gossip, Bias agrees to marry her, and Interest promises to pay him the dowry. Compass makes his peace with Practice with the gift of a court office and has Interest arrested. Polish confesses the exchange of infants, which leaves Interest obliged to pay the money twice over, to Compass in respect of the true Placentia and to Bias in respect of the false one.
