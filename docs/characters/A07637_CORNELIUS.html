I think deer wife thou speakest more then thou thinkest. Thou wouldst be loath to adventure him so much. Thanks, good mine Host, is Phœbus passed his height, Or be times change, is't Noon beforte be night? Your house is altered, its grown a School Of good discourse; of martial discipline. Some Claret Wine; No Beer, I seldom use to drink betwixt meals, Observe good diet, to preserve mine health. Drink fasting in the morning strong March Beer, Small Beer at Meals, and when my stomachs raw, A Cup of Spanish Wine: Eat light Suppers, Near fit up late at night: and rise betimes; Oft walk abroad, and use much exercise: These midnight Revels, Surfeits, Wine, and Whores, And private quarrels, have deuourde more men Then have the wars of late. Wondrous well, But not to take it; It does purge mine head, And makes me sneeze, as though I tookt my self. Its well done Sergeant, you have held him up With good discourse: All times not lost, I see, Nor yet ill spent: place makes not men or good Or bade, its lewdness, ill condition, As vice or virtue does in men abound; Virtue from Heaven, Vice it comes from Hell, And drags men's souls where monstrous Furies dwell. Please you sir, I will drink one hearty draught Unto your Worships welcome to the town; In generous Claret, sparkling; this for me, The only drink. Well spoke, brave Lass, I think fair Pallas shine, Begirt thy temples with her glorious rays, At thy days birth, the wonder of thy sex. What courting callest thou them, thou rubst me up, To think upon the times forepast, I saw In England's Court so famous and renowmde Of great Eliza's blessed memory. In Latin land when Trojan wars were passed, To live in peace we are arriude at last. Blessed be that hand which brought this blessed peace; And blessed be those that pray it never cease. Plot summary

Plot — Hans Beer-Pot (See Me and See Me Not)
Hans is sent to town to sell corn for his master, Cornelius; his mistress, Hanneke, gives him money to give to her younger son, a student, anda shilling on top for beer-money. Cornelius suspects that Hans is cheating him, and follows him to town. There he finds his son and son-in-law drinking with Hans at a tavern; they have been losing bets with Sergeant Goodfellow about his linguistic ability, and discussing the relative merits of infantry and cavalry (with extensive reference to Turkish history). Hans sneaks away, thinking he has not been spotted; but in fact he has. The town characters go to the country as Cornelius’ guests at Christmas dinner. There they discuss the decline in public respect for soldiers, making extensive reference to the great military men of the Elizabethan era, including some with whom Cornelius served in his younger days.
