Why my Lord? You will overthrow the state Of that deer health which so much cost and time Have been a building up, your pores lying open Colds, Agues, and all enemies to pure bloods Will enter and destroy life. Pray my Lord stir not forth. The Air will pierce you. When sir? Nay that was well, how slept you the next night? The Admiral of Castille! It's my certain death to do it. I will by these two hands. When you please. This hour. Fast. For speaking. O my Lord! O my Lord, I am beaten to these things. Cordial. It strikes sure. He's gone. Some of his men attending with his Caroach This I can safely swear. Your gracious pardon to call back a life That's half lost with despair. Poisoned a man. The Queens Father in law. I must have company with me then: Done John Your Highness Brother, set against my throat— A word in your Highness ear: I in stead of poison, Gave him a sleepy Potion, he's preserved Done John thinks not: the noble Admiral Fears plots against his life, forbears the Court But sends me to your Grace, to bid you set Your foe oting stiff and strongly, for Done John Trips at your life and Kingdom, to his throat Valasco this will justify. All the better. Marry I'm glad on't. Still the better. Yes my good Lord, in Purgative and Expulsive. Your Lordship's merry. His armed sword; I had died, had I not done't. I am bound to your Lordship. O my good Lord.

Plot summary

Plot — Match Me in London
It is midnight, and Tormiella is missing from her father Malevento’s house. He wonders if she is visiting Gazetto, her fiancé, but in fact she has eloped to Seville with the man she loves, Cordolente. There they get married and open a shop. The bawd Dildoman recommends her to the King, who comes to the shop on the pretext of buying a glove. Tormiella is taken to court and her escort disposed of by a ruse; Dildoman tries to persuade her to yield to the King. The Queen becomes jealous of Tormiella and attempts to stab her. Cordolente realizes he has been cuckolded; he and Malevento are both offered places at court, but only Malevento accepts. Don John intends to depose the King, his brother, with the help of the Portuguese nobility. He persuades his doctor to poison Don Valasco, the Queen’s father, having first told him that the King is having an affair with Tormiella. Valasco eats some grapes, falls sick, and is then poisoned with the medicine the doctor administers; Don John sends the body home. In fact, it was only a sleeping potion, and the King confronts Don John with the living Valasco; when their quarrel escalates to the point of a duel, the King puts Don John under house arrest. He discovers documentary proof of the conspiracy, and sends Valasco to supervise Don John’s execution, which is to be done in private and using a method selected by Don John himself from an illustrated book of deaths. Valasco reports him dead. The King uses Martines, who is implicated in Don John’s plot, to facilitate a charge of adultery against the Queen: he writes her a love-letter to the King’s dictation and delivers it, only to be arrested. The King engages Gazetto, who has come to Seville in disguise, to murder both the Queen and Cordolente, thereby clearing his path to marry Tormiella. Gazetto has scruples about killing the Queen, but is quite ready to dispose of his successful rival in love. The King hears that Tormiella has gone mad; then Gazetto reports that the Queen is dead. The King intends to go through with marrying Tormiella anyway, and Gazetto offers to cure her. When he visits her disguised as a doctor, he learns that the madness is only a ruse to facilitate her escape from court with Cordolente. Gazetto reveals his identity to her, and she agrees to kill the King on their wedding night.
