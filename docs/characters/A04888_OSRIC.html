Your Honour shall repose you here to night, And early as you please, begin your task, Time serves not now, come Ethenwald, As welcome as the King himself to me. DAughter, see that you entertain the Earl, As best beseems his state and thy degree: He comes to see whither Fame have worthily Be niggard, in commending thee or no, So shall thy virtues be admired at the court. And thou be praised for kind and debonair, For courtesy contents a Courtier oft: When nothing else seems pleasant in his eyes. How long have you be troubled with the pain, Or is it a pain that you have usual? Or is it some water, that by taking cold, Is fallen into your eyes, and troubles you? On Monday next, till then you are my guest. Ethenwald, my son, what news? Not year, I promise you my Lord. And welcome shall his Majesty be to me, That in the wane of my decreasing years, Vouchsafes this honour to Earl Osricks house. What else my Son? Why, men that bear of this will make a scorn of you. Earl Ethenwald, welcome, how fares our friends at court What cause constrains your Honour, that thus late You visit us, that dream not of your coming? How fares my L. of Cornwel, what displeased Or troubled with a mood thats male content? I am sorry that my house should cause your grief Daughter, if you have any skill at all, I pray you use your cunning with the Earl, And see if you can ease him of his pain. My Lord with all my heart, you have my consent If so my daughter please to condescend. My gracious Lord, as welcome shall you be, To me, my Daughter, and my son in Law, As Titus was unto the Roman Senators, When he had made a conquest on the Goths: That in requ tall of his service done, Did offer him the imperial Diadem: As they in Titus, we in your Grace still find, The perfect figure of a Princely mind.

Plot summary

Plot — A Knack to Know a Knave
. . Concerned for the good order of his kingdom, King Edgar commissions Honesty to seek out and expose knaves. The dying Bailiff of Hexham advises his four sons to live by fraud and deceit in their various: professions, and to enrich themselves at the expense of the commonweal and the poor..He dies and is carried off by the devil. While Edgar deals with the case of 1592 Philarchus, a courtier who has abused his father, Honesty observes the corrupt dealings of the Bailiff’s sons: Cuthbert, a cony-catcher, who bears false witness in an attempt to take possession of a farm; Walter, a farmer, who oppresses his neighbours and exports corn to feed the enemy; John, a priest, who refuses to give to charity; and Perin, a courtier, who misuses the royal seal and sells Walter an export licence for his corn under pretext of raising revenues for the crown. Meanwhile honest members of the state, from knight to ploughman, complain of their economic decline. Edgar sends Ethenwald to woo Alfrida by proxy, but Ethenwald falls in love with her himself and marries her. The King forgives him and visits the couple (receiving a petition from the mad men of Gotham on the way), but the jealous Ethenwald is convinced that Edgar plans to cuckold him, and has Alfrida and the kitchen maid swap identities during the royal visit. An outraged Edgar sees through the subterfuge and plans to take revenge, but Bishop Dunstan, with the help of the devil, persuades him to pardon Ethenwald instead. Honesty exposes the various knaves he has observed, and, with royal permission, sentences them all to gruesome and unusual punishments.
