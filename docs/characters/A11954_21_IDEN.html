Lord, who would live turmoiled in the Court, And may enjoy such quiet walks as these? This small inheritance my Father left me, Contents me, and worth a Monarchy. I seek not to wax great by others warning, Or gather wealth I care not with what envy: Suffices, that I have maintains my state, And sends the poor well pleased from my gate. Why rude Companion, whatsoever thou be, I know thee not, why then should I betray thee? Is't not enough to break into my Garden, And Like a Thief to come to rob my grounds: Climbing my walls inspight of me the Owner, But thou wilt brave me with these saucy terms? Nay, it shall never be said, while England stands, That Alexander Iden and Esquire of Kenned, Took odds to combat a poor famished man. Oppose thy steadfast gazing eyes to mine, See if thou canst outface me with thy looks: Set limb to limb, and thou art far the lesser: Thy hand is but a finger to my fist, Thy leg a stick compared with this Truncheon, My foot shall fight with all the strength thou hast, And if mine arm be heaved in the Air, Thy graven is dug already in the earth: As for words, whose greatness answer's words, Let this my sword report what speech forbears. Is't Cade that I have slave, that monstrous traitor? Sword, I will hallow thee for this thy deed, And hang thee over my Tomb, when I am dead. Never shall this blood be wiped from thy point, But thou shalt wear it as a Heralds coat, To emblaze the Honour that thy Master God. If one so rude, and of so mean condition May pass into the presence of a King: Lo, I present your Grace a Traitors head, The head of Cade, whom I in combat slew. I was, an't Like your Majesty. Alexander Iden, that's my name, A poor Esquire of Kenned, that loves his King. May Iden live to merit such a bounty, And never live but true unto his Liege. How much thou wrongest me, heaven be my judge; Die damned Wretch, the curse of her that bore thee: And as I thrust thy body in with my sword, So wish I, I might thrust thy soul to hell. Hence will I drag thee headlong by the heals Unto a dunghill, which shall be thy graven, And there cut off thy most ungracious head, Which I will bear in triumph to the King, Leaving thy trunk for Crows to feed upon.
