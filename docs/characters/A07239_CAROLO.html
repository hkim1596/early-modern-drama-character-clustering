YOu bring your welcome with you. Again most welcome. Yet give me leave to wish (and pray you excuse me) For I must use the freedom I was born with) The great Dukes pleasure had commanded you To my poor house upon some other service, Not this you are designed to; but his will Must be obeyde, how ere it ravish from me The happy conversation of one As deer to me as the old Roman held Their household Lars, whom they believed had power To bless and guard their Families. I have discharged, To the utmost of my power, the trust the Duke Committed to me, and with joy perceive The seed of my endeavours was not sown Upon the barren sands, but fruitful glebe, Which yields a large increase; my noble Charge, By his sharp wit, and pregnant apprehension Instructing those that teach him; making use Not in a vulgar and pedantic form Of what's read to him, but it's straight digested And truly made his own. His grave discourse, In one no more indebted unto years, Amazes such as hear him; horsemanship And skill to use his weapon are by practice Familiar to him; as for Knowledge in Music, He needs it not, it being born with him, All that He speaks being with such grace delivered That it makes perfect harmony. Sir, he is no less, And that there may be nothing wanting that May render him complete, the sweetness of His disposition so wins on all Appointed to attend him, that they are Rivals even in the coursest office, who Shall get precedency to do him service. Which they esteem a greater happiness Then if they had been fashioned, and built up To hold command over others. He is now Running at the ring, at which he's excellent. He does allot for every exercise A several hour, for Sloth the Nurse of vices And rust of Actium, is a stranger to him. But I fear I am tedious, let us pass If you please to some other subject, though I cannot Deliver him as he deserves. And how I pray you (For we that never look beyond our villa's Must be inquisitive) are State affairs Carried in Court? Does Lodovica Sanazarro hold Weight, and grace with him? It's said nobly. For Princes never more make known their wisdom Then when they cherish goodness, where they find it, They being men, and not Gods, Contarino; They can give wealth and titles, but no virtues; That is without their power. When they advance (Not out of judgement, but deceiving fancy) And undeserving man, how ere set of With all the trim of greatness, state, and power, And of a creature even grown terrible To him from whom he took his Giant form, This thing is still a Comet, no true star; And when the bounties feeding his false fire Begin to fail, will of it self go out, And what was dreadful, proves ridiculous. But in our Sanazarro it's not so. He being pure and tried gold, and any stamp Of grace to make him currant to the world The Duke is pleased to give him, will add honour To the great bestower, for he though allowed Companion to his Master, still preserves His Majesty in full lustre. Is He yet married? So I think too. But break we off. Here comes my Princely charge. Make your approaches boldly, you will find A courteous entertainment. Your Excellence Must give me liberty to observe the distance And duty that l'owe you. Pray you observe The frequent changes in his face. Now he appears Collected and resolved. I conceive you: And something I would say, but I must do it In that dumb rhetoric, which you make use of; For I do wish you all. — I know not how My toughness melts, and spite of my discretion I must turn woman. This acknowledgement Binds me your debtor ever. Here comes one In whose sad looks you easily may read What her heart suffers, in that she is forced To take her last leave of you. And me to bring you To the one half of your journey. I grow young When most I serve you. I will entertain him. As fits his worth, and quality, but no farther. Most noble Sir Excuse my age subject to ease, and Sloth, That with no greaver speed I have presented My service with your welcome. Offend my Lord? I feel my self much younger for the favour. How is it with our gracious Master? I am rich In his acknowledgement. Sits the wind there? It's a plain Village Girl Sir, but obedient, That's her best beauty Sir. You are that way My Lord free from suspicion. Her own manners (Without and imposition from me) I hope will prompt her to it. As she is She's come to make a tender of that service Which she stands bound to pay. How he falls off! This is strange; my Lord. Pray you my Lord make trial: She can speak I can assure you, and that my presence May not take from her freedom, I will leave you. For know my Lord, my confidence dares trust her Where, and with whom she pleases. If he be Taken the right way with her, I cannot fancy A better match; and for false play I know The tricks, and can discern them. Petronella! I have employment for you. As I live I know not what to think on't. Is't his pride, Or his simplicity? They are come to parlee, I must observe this nearer. Oh, they are at it. A homely breakfast does attend your Lordship. Such as the place affords. Gone so abruptly? It's very strange. Take heed Lidia! I do advice you with a Father's love, And tenderness of your honour: as I would not Have you course and harsh in giving entertainment, So by no means be credulous. For great men Till they have gained their ends are Giants in Their promises, but those obtained, weak Pigmies In their performance. And it is a maxim Allowed among them, so they may deceive They may swear any thing; for the Queen of love As they hold constantly, does never punish, But smile at Lovers perjuries. Yet be wise too, And when you are sued to in a noble way, Be neither nice, nor scrupulous. So shall you keep Your fame untainted. Here she is Sir, Suddenly sick I grant, but sure not drunk, Speak to my Lord the Duke. And my heir Sir, Nor keep I any woman in my house (Unless for sordid offices) but one, I do maintain trimmed up in her cast habits, To make her sport. And she indeed loves wine, And will take too much of it. And perhaps for mirth She was presented to you. Here's a storm soon raised. That is and oath I long since took. These are strange turns, But I must not dispute they. My Lord. In my judgement Sir He suffers more for his offence to you, Then in his fear of what can follow it. For he is so collected and prepared To welcome that, you shall determine of him, As if his doubts and fears were equal to him. And sure he's not acquainted with much gilded, That more laments the telling one untruth Under your pardon still (for 'twas a fault Sir) Then others that pretend to conscience, do Their crying secret sins. My Honor's pawned for it. And that hope Sir Hath wrought a miracle. She is recovered, And if you please to warrant her, will bring The penitent Prince before you. If ever She was handsome, as it fits not me to say so, She is now much bettered. Are not these Strange gambols in the Duke? There's some hope yet Since they have such gentle Judges. Giovanni writes to give Lydia advance warning of what he understands to be Cosimo’s dishonourable intentions, and they contrive to have her drunken servant Petronella introduced in her place. Cosimo is duly repelled, and criticizes Carolo for giving her inadequate fatherly guidance. The ruse collapses when Carolo summons the real Lydia. Cosimo has Sannazaro and Giovanni confined and summons the court. Sannazaro manages to get a message to Fiorinda, scratched onto a pane of glass which he drops to her as she arrives. She asks Cosimo to restore him to favour, and agrees to accept him as her husband even after she has heard of his interest in Lydia. Cosimo appoints Lydia and Fiorinda to judge the miscreants, reserving to himself the role of prosecutor. He reminds them that he has sworn not to marry again, and pardons Giovanni and Sannazaro upon ascertaining that Lydia and Fiorinda really do love them.
