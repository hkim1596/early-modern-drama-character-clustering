Plot summary

Plot — Othello, the Moor of Venice
Before the start of the action, the Moorish general Othello visited Brabantio’s home, told of his exotic military life, and wooed Desdemona; now she has run away with him. Iago has been building up Roderigo as a hopeful suitor to Desdemona, whilst pocketing the gifts Roderigo asks him to deliver. He is angry after Othello has passed him over for promotion in favour of Cassio, who has no practical battlefield experience; so he uses Roderigo to tell Brabantio about the elopement. There is no chance of scoring a direct hit, because Othello is indispensable: there is about to be a war in Cyprus, and he is the only competent commander. When Brabantio complains to the Senate that his daughter has been seduced by magical means, Othello defends himself, is exonerated, and is sent to the war. Since her father will not take her back into his house, Desdemona is allowed to go with him. The Turkish attack is halted by a storm which also delays Othello’s arrival on Cyprus. Believing that both Othello and Cassio have cuckolded him, Iago plans to make Othello believe that Desdemona is having an affair with Cassio. To that end, he gets Cassio drunk during the celebration of the Turkish repulse, and uses Roderigo to provoke a brawl in which Montano is wounded. Othello cashiers Cassio. Iago advises Cassio that Desdemona may persuade Othello to reinstate him, and he duly sues to her. Iago then persuades Othello that there is something improper behind Desdemona’s advocacy of Cassio. Desdemona loses her handkerchief, a love-token from Othello. Iago acquires it and puts it in Cassio’s room; upon finding it, Cassio gives it to his girlfriend Bianca to copy the distinctive embroidery before the owner asks for it back. Iago leads Othello to believe that Desdemona has given it to Cassio, and sets up an apparently incriminating conversation with Cassio for Othello to watch. By chance, Bianca angrily returns the handkerchief to Cassio as he is talking to Iago. Othello is convinced in his jealousy, and his thoughts turn to murder. Othello is recalled to Venice and Cassio appointed as his successor on Cyprus. He accuses Desdemona directly, and she denies adultery. Roderigo confronts Iago and demands the return of the misappropriated gifts. Iago advises him that the only way to keep Desdemona in Cyprus is to murder Cassio, so that Othello will have to stay. Roderigo wounds Cassio, but is himself wounded; Iago finishes him off, but Cassio survives. Othello smothers Desdemona in her bed.…
