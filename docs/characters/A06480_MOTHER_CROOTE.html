¶ He did send me to you, and I may say to you, Chaue brought you hither the price of a good what cow: Have here is four old Ryalles, iche would they were a score, Before yesterday cham did not see them this twenty year and more. ¶ When ych put my hand in my coffer, ych might then feel them, But chaue be blind this thirty year, so that ych could not see them: Therefore chill conclare the cause of my coming hither. ¶ You can conclout the matter much better then I. ¶ Chaue you may see be brought up amongst swine and kye. Chill now declare the cause of my coming, ¶ Because cham rich and have something to take too, There came of late a young sprignall me to woo: Nay he is a wholesome young man, of twenty year old and three, It does me good to think on him by our blessed lady. He made full much on me, and loved me God woote, And cham love him again even at the heart root: So that we plighted our troths eke to other. And so at pervenient time to marry together. ¶ You may see Sir, old women have much clitter clatter. But I pray you good Sir bear with my budenes, ¶ What soeuerich say my meaning is no less, But my husband, my husband was so well favoured, That a young drab of him was enamoured, Who said unto him that it was a great shame For him to marry such and old beldame: And thus this young drab my husband enticed, To whom she doth hope right shortly to be married. But chad rather the young whore were quick at the devil Than she should have my husband, my sweet heart and jewel. My love in my youth was never so fervent As it is on my sweeteheart, now at this present: Shall I never buss my sweet heart again, Nothing in the world would make me so fain. ¶ I wish but one night with him for to lie, Ohe, he would make me look young by and by. ¶ Yea, but he is good in my bed to keep my back warm, And now and then chill kiss him and clip him in my arm: Therefore my Lord for your friend Moneys sake, That I may have my husband I pray you undertake. ¶ Yea, but the younger he is, the better he is for me, ¶ There will no such be got my deer heart root, ¶ Now Gods deer blessing light on that soot face of yours, Chaue tarried to long by two or three hours. Now chill desire from hence to depart. ¶ Marry chill my life son but iche do not hear you well. Every one that knows me do call me mother Croote, Cham and hundredth years old cham can skantlie go on foot.

Plot summary

Plot — All for Money
The play satirizes the domination of money in a sequence of discrete episodes. Theology, Science, and Art lament that they are now primarily studied out ofa desire for money. Money becomes ill and vomits up Pleasure, who in turn gives birth to Sin in the same manner, whose progeny in turn is Damnation. Satan is pleased that Money’s wide popularity will consign so many souls to hell, and asks Sin to assist in the process. The relative values of money and learning are assessed in an encounter between four characters who have or lack them in various permutations: learning is shown to be superior to money, and poverty in this world carries the promise of a heavenly reward in the next; the clinching point is that Learning with Money gives alms to the poor where Money without Learning refuses. Money feels overworked and Sin gives him an associate, All for Money, who presides over a corrupt court: he pardons theft and assault, infanticide, and bigamy, but hangs a poor thief who is unable to bribe him. He also advises two suitors to procure false witnesses to support their causes, and protects a corrupt and incompetent cleric. Judas and Dives, two souls damned for monetary sins, lament their situation; Damnation drives them off to hell. Godly Admonition warns the audience not to repent too late, and to give up cupidity.
