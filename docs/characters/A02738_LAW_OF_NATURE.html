We consider that, for as concerning Man, Four several times, are Much to be respected. Of Innocency first, of his transgressyon than, Than the long season, wherein he was afflicted, Finally the time, wherein he was redeemed. Of pleasure is the first, the second of exile. The law in effect. is a teacher general, What is to be done. & what to be laid aside But as touching me the first law natural A knowledge I am whom God in Man does hide, In his whole working, to be to him a guide, To honour his God and seek his neybers health, A great occasion, of peace and public wealth, A soar charge I have, Mankind to over see. And to instruct him, his lord God to obey. That lord of heaven grant, I may so do my duty That he be pleased, and Man brought to a stay. His bryttle nature, his slyppernesse to way, Much does provoke me. But if God set to hand, He shall do full well. For non may him withstand, That might have done you smart. Why dost thu call me knave? Thu art disposed to mock, Soon mayest thu have a knock, If thu with me so game. I am the law of Nature. If thu use soch vyllanye. I shall displease the truly. Why dost thu me blaspheme, And so ungodly deem? You are disposed to dally, To leap and ouersallye, The compass of your wit? I counsel you yet in season, Somewhat to follow reason, And gnaw upon the byrte, God sent me unto Man, To do the best I can, To cause him to amend. Such creatures as want reason. My rules obye yche season, And that in every bondre. The sun and moan does moan, With the other bodies above, And never break their order. The trees and herbs does grow, The see does ebb and flow, And varieth not a nail. The floods and wholesome Springs, With other natural things, Their course do never fail The beasts and birds engender, So do the fishes tender, According to their kind Alonely man does fallen, From good laws natural, By a forward wicked mind. Plot summary

Plot — The Three Laws of Nature, Moses, and Christ, Corrupted by the Sodomites, Pharisees, and Papists
God gives mankind three laws to guide him one by one through the three stages of his history. The Law of Nature has an altercation with Infidelity, who then raises Sodomy and Idolatry to confound the law and pervert mankind’s body and soul. He equips them with the paraphernalia of the Catholic church, and through their work the Law of Nature is afflicted with leprosy. Infidelity next engages Covetousness and Ambition to defeat the Law of Moses: they will exhaust the clergy with long services, and have devotions in Latin to inhibit understanding by the laity; laymen will also be deceived with false interpretations of the Bible, and strict laws against heresy will be enforced. The Law of Moses becomes a blind, lame cripple. Next Infidelity summons Hypocrisy and False Doctrine, who provide religious schism and popish relics. They raise up the clergy, the universities, and local government in opposition to the Law of Christ, who comes to preach. Infidelity competes with a sale of indulgences, and the Law of Christ is arrested, stripped, and taken to be burnt for heresy. Infidelity’s triumph is cut short by the arrival of a vengeful God, who ignores his claim that he is not responsible for the corruption of the laws, and smites him with water, the sword, and fire. God banishes the vices, curing the three Laws, and assigns them to teach Christian Faith, who then goes out to see those laws obeyed in all nations and the Catholic church extirpated.
