I will my Lord, but I must speak with him. I hope your highness will not so disgrace me, and dash the virtue of my seal at arms, He hath my never broken name to show, Carectred with this princely hand of mine, and rather let me leave to be a prince, Than break the stable verdict of a prince, I do beseech you let him pass in quiet, To that condition I agree my Lord, And will unfeignedly perform the same. Not for his sake my gracious Lord so much, Am I become and earnest advocate, As that thereby my ransom will be quit, No good my Lord except the same be joust, For profit must with honour be commixed, Or else our actions are but scandalous: But letting pass these intricate objections, Wilt please your highness to subscribe or no? Why then I know the extremity my Loid, I must return to prison whence I came, Changed Return, I hope thou wilt not, What bird that hath escaped the fowlers gin, Will not beware how she's insnard again: Or what is he so senseless and secure, That having hardly passed a dangerous gulf, Will put him self in peril there again. Ah but it is mine oath my gracious Lord, Which I in conscience may not violate, Or else a kingdom should not draw me hence. In all things that uprightly he commands: But either to persuade or threaten me, Not to perform the covenant of my word, Is lawless, and I needs not to obey. To kill my Lord when war is once proclaimed, So that our quarrel be for wrongs receaude, No doubt is lawfully permitted us: But in and oath we must be well advised, How we do swear, and when we once have sworn, Not to infringe it though we die therefore: Therefore my Lord, as willing I return, As if I were to fly to paradise. I humbly thank your grace, I must dispatch, And send this passport first unto the Earl, And then I will attend your highness pleasure.
