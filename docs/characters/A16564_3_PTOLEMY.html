To think of this, it makes my soul ashamed, That we should serve a base Barbarians brood, What should we bear the yoke that have framed? To buy disgrace, have we bestowed our blood? Our ancestors whose glory we obscured, Would get some vantage of their offspring thus: That peoples bondage they would have procured, And have we warred to make them Lords over us? Ah, bury this as a most odious thing, Which may bring danger, and must breed our scorn. Though (in effect) descended from our King, They (come of Captives) are basely born. Oh! brave Leonides, I Like thy strife, Who with so few performed so glorious things, And death prefer worser before the infamous life, Which bondage still from a Barbarian brings. Those (loath to take a stranger for their Lord) Did with their blood renown a foreign field, And shall we honour them whom they abhorred, And even (though victor's) to the vanquished yield?
