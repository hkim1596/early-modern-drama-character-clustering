C Ome, prithee tell me brother, why art sad. I do; and wish that I could tell you such a secret of mine own; for of all men living, I think you most happy. How can that be! is not Clarinda yours? In which (were I a man) I should believe More happiness consisted, then for to be a Monarch. Nothing can take her from you but the graven, I hope she is not sick. Be plain sweet brother. Why who compels it? He is now at liberty. How came you by this miserable knowledge. Then he does no way suspect there's love betwixt you; But tell me Brother how poor Clarinda Did receive her Father's deadly proposition. And having said so, did she not cast her watery eyes Upon you, and in this sad, yet pleasing language, Tell you, that she would not forsake you for the Duke. Brother it's true; but I so seldom see you, That I'll not go, unless you promise to come and see me. That is but your excuse; I have told you how often the Princess Earnestly hath desired to see you; yet you would never go. Will you not come six miles to see a Sister That so dearly loves you? I wonder how the Dear escaped; the follow-dog Once pinched him. Why will you course among the bushes? Gerard the Keeper would have brought you To a fairer course; but you will never let Him go along. Madame, it's very hight, will you go bath your self In the River. Madame, the breath hath quite forsaken this body, as I think: Oh my deer Brother! Dear Brother speak, who hath hurt you? Brother it is the Princess. Dear Brother, what was your quarrel? But Madame, is it possible that (You) should love him thus? Madame, I'll go see. Oh Madame 'we are undone, it is the King, Who threatens to hang up Gerard for concealing Of my Brother. Why, love you any one so well to give away your heart I know she's dearer to you? Why, think you shee'l prove false? Madame, it's true, we not alone in Princes See the bitter effects of such forced Marriages; But even in private Families, Murders and Adulteries, do often wait upon those Couples Whose Bodies are compelled by Parents or Friends To join for worldly respects, without the souls consent. Oh deer Madame, what a sad object's this? Oh Madame say not so, had you but even now So great a care to save his life, and are you now So cruel to say that he must perish by the hand Of Justice, though he should escape these wounds? Would not the Duke have killed him if he could? I'll pawn my life upon it, my Brother killed him fairly. I want words to express how much I love And honour you. Brother, I know you see it your self, Though you will not take notice of it. I'll see who it is. Exit. Exceeding well Madame; Brother, here is the Princess. Madame, I rather wish My Brother never had been born, Then that the King should know you love him, Nay, I hope you know it not your self: Shall I believe that your great heart, that ever Yet contemned love, can on a sudden in four Or five days knowledge, be struck by my unworthy Brother's slender merits, and one that must Be perjured too, if he should love you. Brother, me thinks now your wounds being well. It were good to quit this Country for a while: For it is impossible but by some means or other, If you stay hear, you will be discovered. The time hath been, that you without Clarinda's leave would have done nothing. Madame, it were in me presumption To censure any of your actions. Oh Madam yes, my poor Brother must die to day. Madame, I could wish that I might not outlive him. Madame, how strangely do you talk. Yet you must have Your husband chosen to your hand; the King your Brother Will take that pains for you. When she shall know you have another Mistria, She will call back her judgement, and quickly Free her self: but Brother, I do fear You love her too; you look and speak to her With more affection then well becomes your faith, Being promised to Clarinda. Madame, we must not go until the Gods do call us, Yet I bylieue it is the better place.

Plot summary

Plot — The Deserving Favorite
Before the start of the action, Utrante and Orsinio were suspected of poisoning a member of the royal family: Orsinio was banished and, for a time, Utrante imprisoned, his lands confiscated. Nevertheless Orsinio's son, Lysander, has prospered: the Duke saved his life after a hunting accident, took him into favour, and intervened to secure his estate when his uncle mounted a legal claim against him. He has become secretly betrothed to Utrante's daughter, Clarinda; but now the Duke has fallen in love with her too. Lysander feels he must stand aside in his patron's favour, not only because of his personal obligations to the Duke but also because he knows that, if balked, the Duke could make life difficult, or short, for Clarinda's father. Clarinda, however, will not accept Lysander's withdrawal or the Duke's suit. Not knowing of her prior engagement, Utrante pressurizes her, and she asks to postpone the decision for a month, intending to force a fait accompli by marrying Lysander in the interim. Jacomo discovers the secret romance and arranges for the Duke to witness an assignation between the lovers. He offers to murder Lysander, against whom he has a grudge, but the Duke refuses, preferring the honourable option: a duel. The rivals fight in the forest; both are wounded, but the Duke gets the worst of it. They are found by the Princess, Cleonarda, who takes Lysander back to her hunting lodge, leaving the Duke's body for later collection. It has gone when her servants come back for it. Meanwhile the King receives a letter from the Duke, which was to be delivered in the event of his death, in which he names Clarinda as his heir and asks forgiveness for his killer. The King ignores the request and swears that Lysander will be swiftly executed when caught. A forest hermit arrives with news of the Duke's death: his body was stolen by pirates and disposed of at sea. Cleonarda falls in love with Lysander, but accepts that Clarinda has a prior claim on him, and tells her that he is hiding out at the hunting lodge. Clarinda plans to go there disguised as a boy, and asks Jacomo to escort her, but on the way he attempts to rape her. She is saved by the intervention of the Duke, who is still alive and staying at the hermit's cottage; not knowing her gender, he spends the night there with her.
