Ever I begin Let me entreat your grace forgo all passion Which may be raised by my free discourse: It is a wonder to your noble friends, That you have as 'twere entered the world, With a free Sceptre in your able hand, And have to use of nature well applied High gifts of learning, should in your prime-age Neglect your awful throne, for the soft down Of and insatiate bed. o my Lord, The Drunkard after all his lavish cups, Is dry, and then is sober, so at length, When you awake from this lascivious dream, Repentance then will follow; Like the sting Placed in the Adders tail: wretched are Princes When fortune blasteth but a petty flower Of their unwieldy crowns; or rauesheth But one pearl from their Sceptre: but alas! When they to wilful shipwreck lose good Fame All Princely titles perish with their name. Oh good Madame. Here is and Emblem nephew pray peruse it. 'Twas thrown in at your window, Do not fear it, I'll be your ranger. I expect good from you, Your parting is so merry. Why that was it; To what scorned purpose else should we make choice Of him for a sea Captain, and beside, Count Lodowick which was rumoured for a pirate. Is now in Padua. Most certain. I have letters from him, which are suppliant To work his quick repeal from banishment, He means to address himself for pension, Unto our sister Duchess. Forbear my Lord, here is no place assing'd you, This business by his holiness is left To our examination. Your business? He was. Who made you overseer? Your lust. Would? Your Champions gone. Go to, go to. After your goodly and vainglorious banquet, I'll give you a choke pear. How does the Duke employ you, that his bonnet Fell with such compliment unto his knee, When he departed from you? Take you heed: Least the Jade break your neck. Do you put me off With your wild horse-tricks? Sirrah you do lie. Oh, thou'rt a foul black cloud, and thou dost threat A violent storm. Wretched creature! I know that thou art fashioned for all ill, Like dogs, that once get blood, they'll ever kill. About some murder? wast not? You have oretane me. To the Duke of Florence? Enough to give you taste How far I am from flattering your greatness? My Lords, you shall not word it any further Without a milder limit. My Lord. No more my Lord, hear comes a Champion, Shall end the difference between you both, Your son the Prince Giovanni, see my Lords What Hope's you store in him, this is a casket For both your Crowns, & should be held Like deer: Now is he apt for knowledge, therefore know It is a more direct and even way To train to virtue those of Princely blood, By examples then by precepts: if by examples Whom should he rather strive to imitate Then his own father: be his pattern then, Leave him a stock of virtue that may last, Should fortune rend his sails, and split his mast. Witty Prince. That is. Plenty of horns hath made him poor of horns. I'll tell you, it's given out You are a Cocould. Look you cossin. Go change the air for shame see if your absence, Will blast your Cornucopia, Marcello Is chosen with you joint commissioner For the relieving our Italian coast From pirates. It may be objected I am dishonourable, To play thus with my kinsman, but I answer. For my revenge I'de stake a brother's life, That being wronged durst not avenge himself. Curse of greatness, Sure he'll not leave her. At your pleasure Sir. Stand to the table gentlewomen: now Signior Fallen to your plea. Go on Sir: O for God sake: gentlewoman, your credit Shall be more famous by it. These are your Pirates: and these following leaves, For base rogues that undo young Gentlemen, By taking up commodities: for politic bankrupts: For fellows that are bawds to their own wives, Only to put off horses and slight jewels, Clocks, defaced plate, and such commodities, At birth of their first children. Plot — The White Devil (Vittoria Corombona)
Count Lodovico is banished from Rome after a career of drunkenness, riot, and murder; but, he comments, not all malefactors fall foul of the law, for the Duke of ===== PAGE 257 ===== 1612 Bracciano seems to be getting away with his designs against the honour of Vittoria Corombona. In this, Bracciano is abetted by Vittoria’s brother Flaminio, who persuades her husband Camillo that their sleeping apart will make her more amorous. Vittoria tells Bracciano of a dream which seems to suggest the murder of Camillo and the Duchess Isabella; but the liaison is forestalled by the intervention of Vittoria’s mother, Cornelia. Flaminio inveighs against Cornelia for compromising his chances of preferment. Isabella comes to Rome to effect a reconciliation with her husband. Cardinal Monticelso and the Great Duke of Florence also put pressure on him to fulfil his responsibilities, both marital and political. In a private interview with Isabella, Bracciano vows never again to sleep with her, but she tries to limit the political damage by making the same vow herself in public. Florence and Monticelso plan to tempt Bracciano into adultery by getting Camillo out of Rome on public business, but Bracciano plots murder: Isabella is killed when she kisses a poisoned picture of Bracciano, and Flaminio breaks Camillo’s neck. Vittoria is arrested and arraigned before an ecclesiastical court; unable to prove her complicity in Camillo’s murder, it convicts her of adultery and commits her to a house of convertites. Flaminio feigns madness to evade interrogation. Lodovico witnesses Isabella’s murder. He returns to Rome illegally to bring Florence the news, but his pardon is signed after the event. He and Flaminio meet and quarrel. Florence borrows from Monticelso a list of villains, in order to select an agent of vengeance. By writing a letter feigning love for Vittoria, he induces Bracciano to contrive her escape from the house of convertites, disguised as a page. Monticelso is elected Pope; his first act is to excommunicate Bracciano and Vittoria. Florence sends Lodovico to Padua to assassinate Bracciano, and by trickery makes it appear that Monticelso supports the conspiracy. Bracciano marries Vittoria and accepts into his court Mulinassar, a Moorish soldier, who is actually Florence in disguise.
