Oppose me Scythia to ambitious Room, Alarbus goes to rest, and we survive, To tremble under Titus threatening looks, Then Madam stand resolved, but hope withal, The self same Gods that armed the Queen of Troy With opportunity of sharp revenge Upon the Thracian Tyrant in his Tent, May favour Tamora the Queen of Goths, (When Goths were Goths, and Tamora was Queen) To quit the bloody wrongs upon her foes. Chiron thy years wants wit, thy wit wants edge And manners to intru'd where I am graced, And may for ought thou knowest affected be. Why Boy, although our mother (unadvised) Gave you a dancing Rapier by your side, Are you so desperate grown to threat your friends? Go too: have your Lath glued within your sheath, Till you know better how to handle it. How now deer Sovereign And our gracious Mother, Why does your Highness look so pale and won? This is a witness that I am thy Son. See how with signs and tokens she can scowl. She hath no tongue to call, nor hands to wash. And so let's leave her to her silent walks. If thou hadst hands to help thee knit the cord. Who is this, my Niece that flies away so fast? Cousin a word, where is your husband? If I do dream, would all my wealth would wake me; If I do wake, some Planet strike me down, That I may slumber in eternal sleep. Speak gentle Niece, what stern ungentle hands Hath lopped, and hewed, and made thy body bore Of her two branches, those sweet Ornaments Whose circling shadows, Kings have sought to sleep in And might not gain so great a happiness As half thy Love: Why dost not speak to me? Alas, a Crimson river of warm blood, Like to a bubbling fountain stirred with wind, Does rise and fallen between thy Rosed lips, Coming and going with thy honey breath. But sure some Tereus hath deflowered thee, And least thou shouldst detect them, cut thy tongue. Ah, now thou turnest away thy face for shame: And notwithstanding all this lose of blood, As from a Conduit with their issuing Spouts, Yet do thy cheeks look read as Titan's face, Blushing to be encountered with a Cloud, Shall I speak for thee? shall I say it's so? O that I knew thy heart, and knew the beast That I might rail at him to ease my mind. Sorrow concealed, Like and Oven stopped, Does burn the heart to Cinders where it is. Fair Philomela she but lost her tongue, And in a tedious Sampler sowed her mind. But lovely Niece, that mean is cut from thee, A craftier Tereus hast thou met withal, And he hath cut those pretty fingers off, That could have better sowed then Philomel. O had the monster seen those Lily hands, Tremble Like Aspen leaves upon a Lute, And make the silken strings delight to kiss them, He would not then have touched them for his life. Or had he herd the heavenly Harmony, Which that sweet tongue hath made: He would have dropped his knife and fell asleep, As Cerberus at the Thracian Poets feet. Come, let us go, and make thy father blind, For such a sighed will blind a father's eye. One hours storm will drown the fragrant meads, What, will whole months of tears thy Father's eyes? Do not draw back, for we will mourn with thee: O could our mourning ease thy misery. Why do the Emperor's trumpets flourish thus? Advise thee Aaron, what is to be done, And we will all subscribe to thy advise: Save thou the child, so we may all be safe. Show me a Murderer, I'll deal with him. Madam depart at pleasure, leave us here. I Boy, grow you so brave? Not I, till I have sheathed My rapier in his bosom, and withal Thrust these reproachful speeches down his throat, That he hath breathed in my dishonour here. Why, makest thou it so strange? She is a woman, therefore may be wooed, She is a woman, therefore may be won, She is Lavinia therefore must be loved. What man, more water glides by the Mill Then wots the Miller of, and easy it is Of a cut loaf to steal a shive we know: Though Bassianus be the Emperors brother, Better then he have worn Vulcan's badge. Then why should he despair that knows to court it With words, fair looks, and liberality: What hast not thou full often struck a Do, And born her cleanly by the Keepers nose? Aaron thou hast hit it. Not me, so I were one. Sij fas aut nefas, till I find the streams, To cool this heat, a Charm to calm their fits, Per Stigia per manes Vehor. Chiron we hunt not we, with Horse nor Hound But hope to pluck a dainty Do to ground. Stay Madam here is more belongs to her, First thrash the Corn, then after burn the straw: This Minion stood upon her chastity, Upon her Nuptial vow, her loyalty. And with that painted hope, braves your Mightiness, And shall she carry this unto her graven? Away, For thou hast stayed us here too long. So now go tell and if thy tongue can speak, Who to was that cut thy tongue and ravished thee. Gramercy lovely Lucius, what's the news? For villainy's marked with rape. May it please you, My Grandsire well advised hath sent by me, The goodliest weapons of his Armory, To gratify your honourable youth, The hope of Room, for so he bade me say: And so I do and with his gifts present Your Lordship's, when ever you have needs, You may be armed and appointed well, And so I leave you both: Like bloody villains. What's here? a scroll, & written round about? Let's see. Integer vitae scelerisque purus, non egit maury jaculis nec ar cus. But me more good, to see so great a Lord Basely insinuate, and send us gifts. I would we had a thousand Roman Dams At such a bay, by turn to serve our lust. Come, let us go, and pray to all the Gods For our beloved mother in her pains. Soft, who comes here? Villain what hast thou done? And therein hellish dog, thou hast undone, Woe to her chance, and damned her loathed choice, Accur'st the offspring of so foul a fiend. I'll broach the Tadpole on my Rapiers point: Nurse give it me, my sword shall soon dispatch it. Wilt thou betray thy noble mistress thus? By this our mother is for ever shamed. How many women saw this child of his? What meanest thou Aaron ? Wherefore didst thou this? For this care of Tamora, Her self, and hers are highly bound to thee. Youngling, Learn thou to make some meaner choice, Lavinia is thine elder brother's hope. Listen fair Madam, let it be your glory To see her tears but be your heart to them, As unrelenting flint to drops of rain.
