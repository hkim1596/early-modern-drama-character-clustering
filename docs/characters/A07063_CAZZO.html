Your honour with a paugh? slid, now every Jack and Apes loads his back with the golden coat of ho nou_r_; every Ass puts on the Lyons skin and roars his honour, upon your honour. By my Ladies panta ble, I fear I shall live to hear a Vintners boy cry; 'tis rich neat Canary, upon my honour. I think thou art hungry. I'll stop the barrel thus; God Dildo, set not fire to the touchhole. And I will suck the juice of the capon, to the health of the Idiot thy master Balurdo. Right dutch blades. But was't not rare sport at the sea-battle, while rounce robble hobble roared from the ship sides, to view our Master's pluck their plumes and drop their feathers, for fear of being men of mark. The brief and the semiquaver is, we must have the descant you made upon our names, ere you depart. Yes, when your throat's perfumed; your very words Do smell of Amber Greece. Oh stay sir, stay; Sprinkle some sweet water to your shoes heals, That your mistress may swear you have a sweet foot. The belly hath no ears Dildo. No capon, no not a bit ye smooth bully; Can pon's no meat for Dildo: milk, milk, ye glibbery ur chin, is food for infants. Buxom Flaviam: can you sing? song, song. Troth not a whit, if you seem to come off quickly. All, beyond all, no peregal: you are wondered at, for and ass.

Plot summary

Plot — Antonio and Mellida
The Genoese navy is routed by the Venetians, and the defeated Duke and his son Antonio are banished; the victorious Venetian Duke Piero proclaims a price on both their heads. Meanwhile, aristocratic suitors have come to Venice to woo Mellida, Piero's daughter and Antonio's love. Shipwrecked in Venetian territory and believing his father dead, Antonio disguises himself as an Amazon. He meets Mellida and is received at court; at first he maintains his alias, pretending to have seen Antonio die, but then reveals himself to Mellida. He makes arrangements to abscond with her to England; the plot is discovered, but they escape, separately, in disguise. Andrugio too is alive, believing Antonio to have perished, and he too has been washed up in Venice. Father and fugitive son are reunited in the Venetian marsh; then Antonio meets the disguised Mellida. Piero's search party captures Mellida; she is imprisoned and will be forced to marry her suitor Galeazzo. The courtiers perform a wedding masque, during which Andrugio arrives, to claim Piero's proffered reward for bringing his own head. Piero is astounded at his courage and renounces their enmity. Antonio prompts further remorse by feigning his own death; he rises from his coffin when Piero wishes Mellida's love could bring him back to life. Galeazzo agrees to give up Mellida (he was never that fond of her anyway), and Piero gives her to Antonio, along with the promised gold.
