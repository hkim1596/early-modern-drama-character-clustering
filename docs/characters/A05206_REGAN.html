Not all the world could lay a better plot, I long till it be put in practice. Oh, that my simple utterance could suffice, To tell the true intention of my heart, Which burns in zeal of duty to your grace, And never can be quenched, but by desire To show the same in outward for wardnesse. I then, my deeds should prove in plainer case. Dost thou not blush, proud Peacock as thou art, To make our father such a slight reply? Nay, here is one, that will confirm as much As she hath said, both for my self and her. I say, thou dost not wish my father's good. I was not much behind to do the Like. In truth, I think, the same that moves you; Because she does surpass us both in beauty. But we will keep her low enough, I warrant, And clip her wings for mounting up too hie. She were right fit to make a Parsons wife: For they, men say, do love fair women well, And many times do marry them with nothing. I mean, no money. As gold is welcome to the covetous eye, As sleep is welcome to the Traveller, As is fresh water to sea-beaten men, Or moistened showers unto the parched ground, Or any thing more welcomer then this, So and more welcome lovely Morgan is. How may I bless the hour of my nativity, Which bodeth unto me such happy Stars! How may I thank kind fortune, that vouchsafes To all my actions, such desired event! I rule the King of Cambria as I please: The States are all obedient to my will; And look what ere I say, it shall be so; Not any one, that dareth answer no. My eldest sister lives in royal state, And wants nothing fitting her degreet Yet hath she such a cooling carded withal, As that her honey savoureth much of gall, My father with her is quartermaster still, And many times restrains her of her will: But if he were with me, and served me so, Idem send him packing some where else to go, Idem entertain him with such slender cost, That he should quickly wish to change his host. No, I mistake not, sure it is my father: I must dissemble kindness now of force. Father, I bid you welcome, full of grief, To see your Grace used thus unworthily, And ill befitting for your reverend age, To come on foot a journey so endurable. Plot summary

Plot — The True Chronicle of King Leir (King Lear and his Three Daughters)
King Lear deems it a good moment to marry off his three daughters: with his wife recently dead, they no longer have a mother to direct them. His plan is to retire and divide the kingdom between them as their dowries, and to marry them to neighbouring kings in order to forestall foreign aggression. Cordella, however, is determined to marry for love rather than for diplomatic convenience, so Lear plans to circumvent her by laying on a test: the daughters will make a public declaration of how much they love their father, and he will then ask Cordella to show that love by complying with his wishes over her marriage. Scaliger warns the other daughters, Goneril and Regan, of what Lear has in mind, and misleads them into believing that their shares of the kingdom will reflect the extent of their declared love. Jealous of their younger sister’s beauty and renown, they flatter Lear so that Cordella’s simple statement of due filial love pales in comparison. Lear gives the kingdom wholly to them, summons the Kings of Cornwall and Cambria to be their husbands, and assigns them their shares by lot. Against the advice of the good counsellor Perillus, Lear banishes Cordella from court, and she plans to make her living working as a seamstress. By chance, she meets the King of France, who has come to Britain disguised in search of a wife, and he takes her back to France as his Queen. Lear goes to live with Cornwall and Goneril, but annoys her with his criticisms of her extravagance. Scaliger advises her that halving his allowance will persuade him to take himself off to Regan, and so indeed it does. Cornwall sends a messenger to Regan asking for news of Lear, but Goneril bribes him to deliver instead a libellous letter about her father’s behaviour; she also encourages him to murder Lear. Regan has the same idea, and sets up an opportunity, but at the last moment the messenger is struck by conscience, admits the truth to his intended victim: and runs away.
