See, see, I think the Captain hath been a Cook in his time, h and fit sweet meat with sour sauce. But what a fool is our Captain, to prescribe Laws to Outlaws? If we would have kept the Laws before in the City, we needed not to have be driven now to lead our lives in the Country. But Captain, since you are our Captain, we will resign unto you all our treasures and prisoners, and our spoils. Take pos session of them in Gods name, that came to us in the devils name. And I another? Old man is your purse afloat? I have vowed to cut his throat, but to have it every groat. Nay you old Jack a lent, six weeks and upwards: though you be our Captains father, you cannot stay there, and for furety that you shall not go back, and tell him what we have done to you, we'll kill you, and fling you into some Coalpit. Whom have we here? a Gentleman and his water spaniel? Let's robs them too, and after kill thee. Captain, a prize! we two were assailed by two hundred, and of them two hundred. we killed all but these two. These are the remainder of them that are left alive. I go. What do you mean Gentlemen to fight among your selves, that should be friends, and had more needs to take one another's partly, to fight against your enemies. We shall all be slain, killed, murdered, Massacred. For my own partly, if I had nine lives Like a cat; they were all sure to die one dogs death. O noble Captain, we shall all be slain. Tankard a Prince of Italy, with and Army hath beset the foot of the Mountains, and hath vowed to make Venison of all us poor Outlaws, and kill us Like Dear. God be with you: I'll go shift for one. It's hard to teach them manners that are Clowns. But for my own partly, here's a leg, here's a cap, here's a knee, All these sweet half Captain, I reserve for thee. Nay you continued ardly Lady, that run away from the Camp, and dare not stand to it, I am glad I have light on you; choose your weapon, choose your weapen; I am a Soul dier, and a martial man, and I will offer you the right of Ar : If you vanquish me, I'll be your captine, if you be Can down I'll carry you back prisoner. I was never oh overreached; and, but for shame, and that I am a man at arms, I would run away, and take me to my legs. Have at thee sweet Lady. You think you have a fool in hand; no by my faith, not I. If you have any business to the Camp, farewell, I am running thither as fast as I can. Brave Captain courageous whom death can not daunt; we have be all Gentlemen and Householders; But I was banished for nothing but getting of Bastards; but this fellow ed from Venice, for killing a man cowardly on the Ri alto; some for one villainy, and some for another. Our Captam e that you killed, and now supply his place, poisoned a worthy Merchant in the City with rates-bane; and flying hither, for his valour we made him our General. But now brave Canal lero, to thee alone we sing Honononero. Captain, what shall he be done withal, that lies with a wench with her will, if he be hung that lies with one against her well. Is this our Captains father? what villains were we to use him so roughly? Give v the gold my Captain you, you old Anatomy. I had rather some body else should attempt them then I now: But since their is no other remedy, Give me three or four of the stourest of our crew, and then God and S. Anthony.

Plot summary

Plot — The Four Prentices of London
Deposed and banished by the King of France, the Earl of Bouillon flees to England, where he helped to put King William on the throne. Planning to end his life on a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, he takes his leave of his four sons, who have found work as London apprentices. Dissatisfied by their jobs and itching for the military life, the four brothers join Robert of Normandy's army embarking on a crusade; their sister Bella Franca follows them in disguise. The army is struck by a storm at sea, and the brothers are separated, variously washed up in Bouillon, France, Italy, and Ireland, each believing the others to be dead. Godfrey frees the town of Bouillon from its usurping Spanish occupiers, and the people acclaim him their earl. Guy is spotted on a raft by the Princess of France, who falls in love with him and persuades her father to make him a courtier - a dangerous situation, since the King is the enemy of Guy's father and would execute him if he knew his true identity. Uninterested in love, Guy joins the army which the King is sending to the crusade, but the princess follows him disguised as a page. In Ireland, Eustace acquires a servant. In Italy, Charles is set on by banditti, but kills their captain and becomes their leader. He attempts to impose civility on them, and discovers that they have captured his father. Charles releases him, tells him of his brothers' death, and gives him money for his continuing pilgrimage. The banditti appointed to escort the old Earl try to rob and murder him, but Eustace and his Irish servant intervene in his defence. Now the divided family begins to meet up, but the mutual assumption of death at sea prevents them from recognizing one another. Charles wants Eustace to join the outlaws, and challenges him to a fight, wagering their purses; Bella Franca runs between them and stops the battle. Both are attracted to her, not realizing that she is their sister. Prince Tancred leads soldiers to dispose of the banditti, but Charles and Eustace persuade him to deploy his forces in the crusade instead. Since both brothers want to marry Bella Franca, Tancred takes her into his custody until the end of the war; she is attracted to him. Godfrey rejoins Duke Robert's troops and meets Guy, now in command of the French army. They quarrel over precedence, but finally agree to go side by side.
