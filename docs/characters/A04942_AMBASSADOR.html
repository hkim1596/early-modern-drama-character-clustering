I'll make the motion to my sovereign Liege, And work it if my counsel may prevail. Renowned King he hath received and read, Thy kingly proffers, and thy promised league, And as a man extremely overjoyed, To hear his Son so Princely entertainde, Whose death he had so solemnly bewailde. This for thy further satisfaction, And kingly love, he kindly lets thee know: First, for the marriage of his Princely Son, With Bel-imperia thy beloved Niece, The news are more delightful to his soul, Then myrrh or incense to the offended heavens. In person therefore will he come himself, To see the marriage rites solemnized, And in the presence of the Court of Spain, To knit a sure inexecrable band, Of Kingly love, and everlasting league, Betwixt the Crowns of Spain and Portingale. There will he give his Crown to Balthazar, And make a Queen of Bel-imperia. Wilt please your grace continued mmand me ought besid? That is perfourmd already my good Lord. Between us theres a price already pitched, And shall be sent with all convenient speed. Farewell my Lord of Castille and the rest. Know sovereign L. that Balthazar does live. Your highness son, L. Balthazar does live. And well entreated in the Court of Spain: Humbly commends him to your Majesty. These eyes beheld, and these my followers, With these the letters of the Kings commends. Are happy witnesses of his highness health. The King looks on the letters, and proceeds. These are his highness farther articles. Now last (dread Lord) her hath his highness sent, Although he send not that his Son return, His ransom endue to Done Horatio. Therein your highness highly shall content, His Majesty, that longs to hear from hence. Sad is our King, and Portingale laments, Supposing that Done Balthazar is slain. This is and argument for our Viceroy, That Spain may not insult for her success, Since English warriors likewise conquered Spain, And made them bow their knees to Albion.
