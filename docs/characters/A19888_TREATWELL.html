What in a place so public? A place more private would become my message, And give it gentle hearing. From the young Lord Scales. The Lord my Master, to whom the fame of Your fair Daughters beauty hath thronged by infinite Tongues, hath in his breast Took such a strong impression, that he's pleased (So your consent and his may parallel) To make her his fair Bride. Sir, I have full Commission from his mouth To treat so much; and what I undertake He hath engaged his honour to make good. Pray sir, your answer. But Donna Anne, Madonna, Madam, Lady, What breadth those Titles bear? Me thinks the motion might be fair and even, To make her mistress of her own free thoughts And choose where she best likes. Your Mother sir, that must be. His Lordship asks no more. Sir, I shall. A bold fellow; give him scope, my Lord. Nay good my Lord have patience; hear him speak. This fellows bluntness Does somewhat better than at first, Whom wilt thou follow now? I do not think but under that rough brow Is lodged and honest heart; they are best servants Whom want, penurious need, and poverty Cannot fright from their Master's. Ent. Chan. and his Wife. That meant by me? But Trencher friend? But eat well. It's most strange: sure it's some vexed spirit that hath Deceased of late from out the house: who died last in that Chamber? Perhaps hers; who having hid some treasure in her Life time must, till that be discovered, walk of force, The Like I have read in Legends. But hath he done such strange things? These things so well succeeding, pray resolve us In what form does this Devil haunt your house. So I told you, sure your Mother's. Your reason sir? As you would purchase us your lasting friends. I'll aid him in the search to make That sweet conclusion. If his pockets lining suit but to the outside Of his Clothes, he hath small needs of them. Still, still misprision; might I advice you, proffer What you purpose, and if refused, so leave him. Sir, I had conference with him. By no entreaty or persuasive skill, Nay were he pressed by menace or command, He vows to have no hand in these designs; I could not make him think such things can be; He counts them mere impostures, falacyes, Or, let a man receive them at the best, Illusions of the Devil; that Ghosts walk He says directly it's impossible, And in that faith he'll die; further discourse I could by no means get him listen to, but half displeased he left me. I know not how you could dispose him better And empty all your wishes. The maid talks idly. I am but one and I my self will venture.

Plot summary

Plot — A New Trick to Cheat the Devil
Anne Changeable wants to marry Slightall, with her father’s consent, but her mother persuades her that marriage to Lord Scales will be better for her social standing. Slightall takes rejection badly: he turns to misogyny and voluptuous hedonism. One of his servants, Roger, tries unsuccessfully to dissuade him from squandering his money, while the other, Geoffrey, helps make an arrangement with a usurer to supply him with a further £1,000 — the usurer’s objective being to acquire his land. Lord Scales visits Anne, and she finds that he is not a good kisser: Slightall is the better catch in every respect save his lack of a title; but when she tries to reactivate the romance, Slightall rejects her. He has been ruined by prodigality and pays off his servants; Geoffrey is taken on by Lord Scales, but Roger declines a similar offer. Anne insults everyone who persuaded her to jilt Slightall, but this only makes Lord Scales more attracted to her. Changeable promises Anne that he will get Slightall back for her. Driven out of his wits with melancholy, Slightall conjures up Beelzebub, and makes a contract: the devil will give him enough money to pay his debts in exchange for the right to take his soul when he no longer owes anyone anything. On the day his debt to the usurer falls due, he turns out to have ample money to pay, so the usurer gets his money back rather than the lands he coveted. Asked by Anne to give Slightall some financial support, Lord Scales finds his offer rejected with contempt. The Changeables’ house is troubled by a female spirit; Slightall agrees to help exorcise it by spending the night in the haunted room. When he does so, he is visited by the ghost in Anne’s form. Beelzebub comes for Slightall’s soul, but Slightall demonstrates that he is not yet clear of debt: he paid the usurer with money loaned by Beelzebub. The devil accepts the situation and offers to marry him to the she-devil who haunts the Changeables’ house. Anne’s mother and Lord Scales give their consent, and watch as a friar performs the ceremony. In fact it is not a ghost in Anne’s form, but Anne herself; and ‘Beelzebub’ was really Changeable: Anne has finally got her man back. In the sub-plot, two friars are detained until late by charitable visitation, and find themselves locked out of their abbey. They ask Nan to give them a bed for the night.
