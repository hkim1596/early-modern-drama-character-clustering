Or eyes? We come to spy. It may be. We may spy out, that, which you never meant. Hath been oft called from it, To be their Lord of misrule. Yes. We needs no interpreter, on, what of Time? His days of feast, and liberty again. Slaves of their lords. This will be better: I spy it coming, peace. All the impostures, The prodigies, diseases, and distempers, The knaveries of the Time, we shall see all now. My four eyes itch for it. That which the Time is presently to show us. Yes Fame must know him, all the Town admires him. We know, Sir. But the Time's now come about. We shall do what we list. You'll see. That he ha favourers, Fame, and great ones too. That unctuous Bounty, is the Boss of Belinsgate, She labours, and lies in of his inventions, And is no sooner foaled, but will neigh sulphur: The ragged rascals? Mere rogues, you'd think them rogues, but they are friends, One is his Printer in disguise, and keeps His press in a hollow tree, where to conceal him, He works by glowworm light, the Moon's too open. The other zealous rag is the Compositor, Who in and angle, where the ants inhabit, (The emblem's of his labours) will fit curled Whole days, and nights, and work his eyes out for him. This Man of war, i'the rear, He is both Trumpet And Champion to his Muse. Now Fame, how Like you this? And these will deify him, to despite you. What gambols? what devises? what new sports? Pox, then it's nothing. I had now a fancy We might have talked o'the King. Censured the counsel, ever they censure us. No, the Man I'the Moon dance a Corranto, his bush At's back, a fire; and his dog piping Lachrimae. What? And talking of all men they never herd of, And, when the matter is at hottest, then All fallen asleep. Oh, that we shall never do. Or impossible. To little, or no purpose. But see, there be no cause, as you will answer it. Why, if not those, then something to make sport.

Plot summary

Plot — The Masque of Augurs
Some citizens have heard rumours that the royal poet and architect have run out of ideas. Realizing that this may mean there will be no masque for the King’s entertainment, they come to the buttery at court to offer one of their own, featuring dancing bears. The artist Van Goose believes he can do better, and produces, on request, an antimasque of pilgrims, who dance until they are scared off by Apollo. Apollo descends and raises his mortal children from the dead. He has come to court to establish a.college of augurs, presided over by the King’s own son. The augurs arrive and dance. Their augury is interpreted as signifying Britain’s excellent prospects of peace, freedom from faction, and undisturbed royal power.
