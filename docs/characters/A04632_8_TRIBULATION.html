I think him a profane person, indeed. Good Brother, we must bend unto all means, That may give furtherance, to the holy cause. Not always necessary. The children of perdition are ofttimes, Made instruments even of the greatest works. Beside, we should give somewhat to men nature, The place he lives in, still about the fire, And fume of metals, that intoxicate The brain of man, and make him prove to passion. Where have you greater Atheists, then your Cooks? Or more profane, or choleric then your Glasse-men? More Antichristian, then your Bell-founders? What makes the Devil so devilish, I would ask you, Satan, our common enemy, but his being Perpetually about the fire, and boiling Brimstone, and arsnike? We must give, I say, Unto the motives, and the stirrers up Of humours in the blood. It may be so. When as the work is done, the stone is made, This heat of his may turn into a zeal, And stand up for the beauteous discipline, Against the menstruous cloth, and rag of Room. We must await his calling, and the coming Of the good spirit. You did fault, t'vpbraid him With the Brothers' blessing of Heidelberg, weighing What needs we have, to hasten on the work, For the restoring of the silenced Saints, Which never will be, but by the Philosophers stone. And, so a learned Elder, one of Scotland, Assured me; Aurum potabile being The only medicine, for the civil Magistrate, T'incline him to a feeling of the cause: And must be daily used, in the disease. Let us call on him, then. Sir, be appeased, he is come to humble Himself in spirit, and to ask your patience, Verily, it's true. We may be temporal lords, our selves, I take it. I pray you, sir. Let me find grace, sir, in your eyes; the man He stands corrected: neither did his zeal (But as your self) allow a tune, somewhere. Which, now, being to'ard the stone, we shall not needs. Mind him not, sir. I do command thee, spirit (of zeal, but trouble) To peace within him. Pray you, sir, go on. Truly, sir, they are Ways, that the godly Brothers have invent For propagation of the glorious cause , As very notable means, and whereby, also, Themselves grow soon, and profitably famous. Peace. ANANIAS. It is and ignorant zeal, that haunts him, sir. But truly, else, a very faithful Brother , A butcher: and a man, by revelation, That hath a competent knowledge of the truth. But how long time, Sir, must the Saints expect, yet? What will the orphans goods arise to, think you? How! Can you so? I, but stay, This act of coining, is it lawful? Would? you distinguish well. Casting of money may be lawful. Truly, I take it so. I'll make a question of it, to the Brothers. The doors are shut against us. You shall do well. Profane as BEL, and the Dragon. Be patient ANANIAS.
