Full speech

We mourn in black, why mourn we not in blood? Henry is dead, and never shall revive: Upon a Wooden Coffin we attend; And Death's dishonourable Victory, We with our stately presence glorify, Like Captives bound to a Triumphant Car. What? shall we curse the Planets of Mishap, That plotted thus our Glories overthrow? Or shall we think the subtile-witted French, Conjurers and Sorcerers, that afraid of him, By Magic Verses have contrived his end. How were they lost? what treachery was used? Were our Tears wanting to this Funeral, These Tidings would call forth her flowing Tides. The Dolphin crowned King? all fly to him? Oh whither shall we fly from this reproach? Remember Lords your Oaths to Henry sworn: Either to quell the Dolphin utterly, Or bring him in obedience to your yoke. To Eltam will I, where the young King is, Being ordained his special Governor, And for his safety there I'll best devise. I, we may march in England, or, in France, Not seeing what is likely to ensue: This late dissension grown betwixt the Peers, Burns under feigned ashes of forged love, And will at last break out into a flame, As festered members rot but by degree, Till bones and flesh and sinews fallen away, So will this base and envious discord breed. And now I fear that fatal Prophecy, Which in the time of Henry, named the Fifth, Was in the mouth of every sucking Babe, That Henry born at Monmouth should win all, And Henry born at Windsor, lose all: Which is so plain, that Exeter does wish, His days may finish, ere that hapless time. It grieves his Highness, Good my Lords, be Friends. Well didst thou Richard to suppress thy voice: For had the passion of thy heart burst out, I fear we should have seen deciphered there More rancorous spite, more furious raging broils, Then yet can be imagined or supposed: But howsoever, no simple man that sees This jarring discord of Nobility, This shouldering of each other in the Court, This factious bandying of their Favourites, But that it does presage some ill event. It's much, when Sceptres are in Children's hands: But more, when Envy breeds unkind division, There comes the ruin, there begins confusion. What, is my Lord of Winchester installed, And called unto a Cardinals degree? Then I perceive, that will be verified Henry the Fifth did sometime prophesy. If once he come to be a Cardinal, He'll make his cap coequal with the Crown. Beside, his wealth does warrant a liberal dower, Where Reignier sooner will receive, than give.

Plot summary

Plot — 1 Henry the Sixth
King Henry Vis dead, and England's French lands are lost. After a military setback, the French accept the offered aid of Joan Pucelle , who claims supernatural powers from the Virgin Mary; but they also release Talbot in an exchange of prisoners. With Joan 's help the French recapture the occupied town of Orleans, but the English take it back in a night attack led by Talbot. The Countess of Auvergne invites Talbot to visit her. intending treachery, but he second-guesses her and brings soldiers with him. In England, feuds brew between Gloucester and Winchester, and between Richard Plantagenet and the Duke of Somerset, whose supporters show their allegiance by picking different coloured roses. Richard , who has been deprived of his titles through the attainder of his father, learns from the imprisoned and dying Mortimer that he has a claim to the throne . King Henry restores him to his inheritance, creating him Duke of York. Joan takes Rouen by a ruse , but Talbot recaptures it the same day; she then persuades England's ally Burgundy to join the French. After King Henry's coronation in Paris, York is left in command of Prance, but personal tension between him and Somerset means that troop reinforcements arrive late; in consequence, Talbot is defeated, surviving only long enough to learn that his son has perished in battle. Joan conjures devils, but they will not help her: she is captured by York and burned at the stake. The French and English agree a peace which will entail King Henry making a diplomatic marriage, but this is pre-empted when Suffolk captures Margaret and (having fallen in love with her but being alr eady married) plans to install her as Queen of England . Henry agrees to marry her, and Suffolk goes to France to mili the arrangements.
