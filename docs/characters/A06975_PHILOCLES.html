Envy will say she's rare, then truth must vow, She is beyond compare, sith in her looks, Each motion hath a speaking majesty, She is herself, compared with her self: For but her self, she hath no companion. But when I think of beauty, wit and grace, The elements of active delicacy, Those all eie-pleasing harmonies of sighed Which do inchant men's fancies, and stir up The life blood of dull earth, Oh then methinks, Fair Mariana hath and equal place And if not outshine, it shows more beautiful. More in the gloss of beauty, less in worth, Of wisdom and great thoughts, the one I find was made for wonder, th'other for admire. I am less then my thoughts, more then my self, Yet nothing but the creature of my fate, By name my nature only is obscured, And yet the world baptised me Philocles. My entrance here is proof of holy zeal, And to maintain that no severe disdain, False shape of chastity, nor woman's will, Neglective petula ̄ ce, or uncertain hope, Foul vizard coins, nor seducing fame Should rob the royal temper of true love From the desired aim of his desires, Which my best blood shall witness, or this field Entomb my body made a recreant. I crave the Queens conditions, or this blow Sends this afflicted soul to heaven or hell. Speak madame, will you yield or shall he die? Oh t'is but glass, And cannot bear this axes massiness. Duke, thy brave words that second thy brave deeds, Fills me with emulation, only we two Stand equal victor's; then if thou hast that tie And bound of well knit valour, which unites Virtue and same together, let us restore Our captives unto freedom, and we two, In single combat try out the mastery. Where whoso falls each other, shall subscribe To every clause in each condition. What says his majesty? My stars are writ in heaven, nor death nor fate Are slaves to fear, to hope or human state. Alphonso, here my hand, Thy fortune lends thy peace no infamy. And now thou glorious issue of Jove's brain, That burned the Telamonian Ravisher, Look from thy sphere, and if my heart contain And impure thought of lust, send thy monsters forth And make me more then earthly miserable. Yield, recant or die. Night clad in black mourns for the lose of day, And hides the silver spangle's of the air, That not a spark is left to light the world, Whilst quiet sleep the nourisher of life Takes full possession on mortality. All creatures take their rest in soft repose Save malecontents, and we accursed lovers, Whose thoughts perturbed, makes us passion slave: And robs us of the juice of happiness. Dear Mariana, shaped in and Angels mould, Thou thral'st my senses, and inflamest my blood, Love, power, by wisdom cannot be withstood. But see the morning star breaks from the East, To tell the world her great eye is awaked To take his journey to the western vales: And now the court begins to rise with him. There goes the Physician, the waiting maid, And a fine straight leg'd Gentleman Usher, The preface to a kirtill all puff passed. One that writes sonnets in his Ladies praise, And hides her crimes with flattering poesy. But peace amazement, see the day of life, Nature's best work, the world's chief paragon. Madame one word. You do mistake me. Dear Lady lend and ear unto my voice Sith each were made for others happiness: My tong's not oiled with courtly flatterings, Nor can I paint my passion to the life; But by that power which shaped this heavenly form, I am your bondslave, forced by loves command, Then let soft pity with such beauty dwell. Madame I love you. But Madame whom? But will you love me? I hope you do but jest. Judge not so hard of all for some offenders; For you are subject to the self same crimes, Of men and women always have be had Some good of each. That humour Like a flower soon will fade, Once did mine own thoughts sing to that delight, Till love and you reformed my barbarousness: Therefore deer Lady, pity my wounded heart. T'is but a kiss I ask, a minutes joy. I have obtained my heaven, and in this touch, I feel the breath of all delitiousnesse: Then freely give the sentence of my work, Muster up all the engines of your wit, Teach Juno rules beyond malitiousnesse, What ear it be, i'll die but i'll perform it. Plot summary

Plot — The Dumb Knight
The King of Cyprus, in love with the Queen of Sicily; lays siege to her city, but accepts the Queen’s proposal that they settle the affair with a trial by combat: the King and his champion Philocles versus the Duke of Epire and Lord Alfonso. Epire defeats the King but is bested in a play-off with Philocles; the Queen accepts the outcome and submits to the King’s love, but Epire swears to avenge his dishonour. Philocles woos Epire’s sister, Mariana, but gets the brush-off. Asked to set him a task to prove his love, she demands that he remain silent for a year; when he swears to do so, she is pleased not to have to listen to any more love-talk from him. His dumbness is taken to be a malady, which medicine fails to remedy. The King offers a reward for a successful cure ~ but those who try unsuccessfully will be executed. Mariana offers to achieve the cure, but Philocles will not speak at her command and she is duly sentenced to death. Philocles waits until the headsman is about to strike before speaking out. She is spared, but Philocles refuses to marry her until she demonstrates that she cares as much for him as he for her. Epire’s vindictive vexation grows. Epire suggests to the King that the Queen is having an affair with Philocles: he takes his opportunities to put the Queen into Philocles’ company, and arranges for the King to see them dancing and playing cards together. They are arrested and imprisoned, and parliament condemns them to death. Epire’s plan is to proclaim the Queen’s innocence after she has been executed, and then see the King deposed by an outraged populace. Mariana bribes Philocles’ jailer to allow her a visit. They swap garments: Philocles escapes posing as her, and she joins the Queen on the scaffold on the day of execution. Epire agrees to defend his accusation as the King’s champion in a trial by combat. Philocles comes forward to fight for the Queen, and defeats Epire, who admits malicious slander. The Queen forgives him, but only for Mariana’s sake. In the sub-plot, Lollia is unhappily married to the choleric orator Prate. Alfonso attempts to seduce her, using her neighbour as a go-between; Lollia agrees to receive him when her husband is next away. Lord Méchant has had his lands confiscated for abetting the Cypriot invasion; the King has sent him and others to Prate, but Prate neglects these cases in favour of the King’s own business. Méchant plans to avenge himself by cuckolding Prate, but finds that Alfonso has got there first.…
