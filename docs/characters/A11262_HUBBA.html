If she that rules fair Rha nis golden gate Grant us the honour of the victory, As hitherto she always favoured us, Right noble father, we will rule the land, Enthronized in seats of Topace stones, That Locrine and his brothers all may know, None must be king but Humber and his son. Madam, where resolution leads the way, And courage follows with emboldened pace, Fortune can never use her tyranny, For valiantness is Like unto a rock That stands in the waves of Ocean, Which though the billows Beatrice on every side, And Borras fell with his tempestuous storms, Blows upon it with a hideous clamour, Yet it remains still unmovable. Now by great Jove the supreme king of hea ven, And the immortal gods that live therein, When as the morning shows his cheerful face, And Lucifer mounted upon his steed, Brings in the chariot of the golden sun, I'll meet young Albanact in the open field, And crack my lance upon his burgane , To try the valour of his boyish strength: There will I show such ruthful spectacles And cause so great effusion of blood, That all his boys shall wonder at my strength: As when the warlike queen of Amazon, Penthisilea armed with her lance, Girded with a corslet of bright shining steel, Coupt up the fainthart Grecians in the camp. This unexpected honour noble sir , Will p ick my courage unto braver deeds, And cause me to attempt such hard exploits, That all the world shall found of Hubba's name. Let come what will, I mean to bear it ou , And either live with glorious victory, Or die with am renowned for chivalry, He is not worthy of the honey comb That shuns the hives because the bees have stings, That likes me best that is not God with ease, Which thousand dangers do accompany, For nothing can dismayour regal mind, Which aims at nothing but a golden crown, The only upshot of mine enterprises, Were they enchanted in grim Plutos court, And kept for treasure amongst his hellish crew, I would either quell the triple Cerberus And all the army of his would efull hags, Or roll the stone with wretched Sisiphon. I am his son that slew young Albanact, And if thou take not heed proud Phrigian, I'll send thy soul unto the Stigian lake, There to complain of Humbers injuries.

Plot summary

Plot — Locrine, the Eldest Son of King Brutus
Brutus, the conqueror of Britain, is dying. He gives his eldest son Locrine the crown and betrothes him to Gwendoline, and assigns his other sons, Camber and Albanact, the south and north respectively. His funeral is swiftly followed by Locrine’s royal wedding. Strumbo is in love with Dorothy, and persuades her to marry him. However, he is impressed into Albanact’s army when the Scythians invade northern Britain, and Dorothy is killed when the Scythians burn his house with her inside. The Scythians win the battle, and Albanact takes his own life rather than submit. To avenge his death, Locrine, Camber, and the peers give battle and defeat the Scythians. Humber’s wife Estrild is captured by Locrine’s soldiers, and Locrine is attracted to her. Strumbo is caught having sex with Margery. Initially he refuses to marry her, but is forced to agree after she beats him up. He encounters the defeated and starving Humber, but the ghost of Albanact stops him from feeding the Scythian. Humber eventually drowns himself. Locrine is unable to put away Gwendoline and install Estrild as his queen because Gwendoline’s father, Corineus, proves inconveniently long-lived despite being wounded in the battle with the Scythians. Locrine hides her in an underground pleasure- chamber and visits her in secret; they have a child, Sabren. After Corineus finally dies, Gwendoline goes to Cornwall to arrange the funeral, and Locrine takes the opportunity to bring Estrild out of hiding. Gwendoline and her brother raise an army, and defeat Locrine in battle; he and Estrild kill themselves. Sabren finds their bodies but is physically too weak to use the sword on herself. Gwendoline finds her and promises her a lingering, unusual death, so Sabren drowns” herself; the river is named after her, Severn. Gwendoline arranges to bury Locrine as befits a king - and to leave Estrild ignominiously unburied.
