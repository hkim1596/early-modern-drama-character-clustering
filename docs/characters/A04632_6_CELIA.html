Good sir, have patience! Alas sir, be appeased! I could not think My being at the windore should more, now, Move your impatience, then at other times. Why, deer sir, when do I make these excuses? Or ever stir, abroad, but to the church? And that, so seldom— No? Not well, except you told me. Sir, let me beseech you, Affect not these strange trials; if you doubt My chastity, why lock me up, for ever: Make me the heir of darkness. Let me live, Where I may please your fears, if not your trust. Oh heaven! Was this the train? Before your honour? Lord! what spirit Is this hath entered him? Are heaven, and saints then nothing? Will they be blind, or stupid? Good Sir, Be jealous still, aemulate them; and think What hate they burn with, towards every sin. Oh heaven! canst thou suffer such a change? Sir, what you please, you may, I am your martyr. Would my life would serve To satisfy. Oh God, and his good angels! whither, whither. Is shame fled humane breasts? that with such ease, Men dare put off your honours, and their own? Is that, which ever was a cause of life, Now placed beneath the basest circumstance? And modesty and exile made, for money? Sir! Some serene blast me, or dire lightning strike This my offending face. Good sir, these things might move a mind affected With such delights; but I, whose innocence Is all I can think wealthy, or worth enjoying, And which once lost, I have nought to lose beyond it, Cannot be taken with these sensual baits: If you have conscience— If you have ears that will be pierced; or eyes, That can be opened; a heart, may be touched; Or any partly, that yet sounds man, about you: If you have touch of holy saints, or heaven, Do me the grace, to let me escape. If not, Be bountiful, and kill me. You do know, I am a creature, hither ill betrayed, By one, whose shame I would forget it were, If you will deign me neither of these graces, Yet feed your wrath, sir, rather then your lust; (It is a vice, comes nearer manliness) And punish that unhappy crime of nature, Which you miscall my beauty: flay my face, Or poison it, with ointments, for seducing Your blood to this rebellion. Rub these hands, With what may cause and eating leprosy, E'ene to my bones, and marrow: any thing, That may disfavour me, save in my honour. And I will kneel to you, pray for you, pay down A thousand hourly vows, sir, for your health, Report, and think you virtuous— Oh! joust God. I would I could forget, I were a creature. And heaven, that never fails the innocent. Oh heaven, how joust thou art! How ready is heaven to those, that pray! And mercy. Sir, kill me, rather: I will take down poison, Eat burning coals, do any thing—
