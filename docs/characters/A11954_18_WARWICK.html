Many good-morrows to your Majesty. It's One a Clock, and passed. We have (my Liege.) It is but as a Body, yet distempered, Which to his former strength may be restored, With good advice, and little Medicine: My Lord Northumberland will soon be cooled. There is a History in all men's Lives, Figuring the nature of the Times deceased: The which observed, a man may prophecy With a near aim, of the main chance of things, As yet not come to Life, which in their Seeds And weak beginnings lie entreasured: Such things become the Hatch and Brood of Time; And by the necessary form of this, King Richard might create a perfect guess, That great Northumberland, then false to him, Would of that Seed, grow to a greater falseness, Which should not find a ground to root upon, Unless on you. It cannot be (my Lord:) Rumor does double, Like the Voice, and Echo, The numbers of the feared. Please it your Grace To go to bed, upon my Life (my Lord) The Powers that you already have sent forth, Shall bring this Prize in very easily. To comfort you the more, I have received A certain instance, that Glendour is dead. Your Majesty hath been this fortnight ill, And these unseasoned hours perforce must add Unto your Sickness. Both which we doubt not, but your Majesty Shall soon enjoy. My gracious Lord, you look beyond him quite: The Prince but studies his Companions, Like a strange Tongue: wherein, to gain the Language, It's needful, that the most immodest word Be looked upon, and learned: which once attained, Your Highness knows, comes to no farther use, But to be known, and hated. So, Like gross terms, The Prince will, in the perfectness of time, Cast off his followers: and their memory Shall as a Pattern, or a Measure, live, By which his Grace must mere the lives of others, Turning past-euills to advantages. Be patient (Princes) you do know, these Fits Are with his Highness very ordinary Stand from him, give him air: He'll straight be well. Speak lower (Princes) for the King reco vers. Can for the Music in the other Room. Less noise, less noise. Not so much noise (my Lords) Sweet Prince speak low. The King, your Father, is disposed to sleep. Wil't please your Grace to go along with us? What would your Majesty? how fares your Grace? This door is open, he is gone this way. When we withdrew (my Liege) we left it here. My Lord, I found the Prince in the next Room, Washing with kindly Tears his gentle Cheeks, With such a deep demeanour, in great sorrow, That Tyranny, which never quafft but blood, Would (by beholding him) have washed his Knife With gentle eye-drops. He is coming hither. Indeed I think the young King loves you not. Here come the heavy Issue of dead Harry: Oh, that the living Harry had the temper Of him, the worst of these three Gentlemen: How many Nobles then, should hold their places, That must strike sail, to Spirits of vild sort? We do remember: but our Argument Is all too heavy, to admit much talk. Here comes the Prince. How now, my Lord Chief Justice, when there away? Exceeding well: his Cares Are now, all ended. He's walked the way of Nature, And to our purposes, he lives no more. It's called Jerusalem, my Noble Lord.
