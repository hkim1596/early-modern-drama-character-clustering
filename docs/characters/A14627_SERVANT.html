Nay by gisse I thought he would not be heavenly long: For that to his nature were clean contrary and wrong. Yonder are such a sort of Ruterkins lively and jolly: That all that can be got is little enough for their belly. Soul we work, we labour, and that night and day: Yet can we scant have meat and drink the truth to say. And that which we have, is ill enough for Dogs: And we are served with all, Like a many of Hogs. By my truth Father Tenant even as you see. Kept Like no men servant, but rather Like a slave: That I am weary of my life I tell you so God me save. My master takes on Like the Devil of hell: There was never one so hasty, cruel and fell. But so covetous Lord, Lord you will not believe: I think all his mind and study to bribery he doth give. I know the occasion of all this géer: But I would not for twenty pound it should come to his ear He says, that he will never leave his extortion: Till of riches he have got and innumerable portion. He will build, plant, set, and sow: Till such a fame of him abroad there grow. That there is none Like to him in all the Country: And so by that means he shall come to authority. Plot summary

Plot — Enough Is as Good as a Feast
Remembering his father’s bankruptcy, the Worldly Man hoards treasure for the future, seeing such behaviour as provident thrift, whereas the Heavenly Man relies on God to look after him and regards wealth as a distraction from spiritual things. He and Contentation persuade the Worldly Man to change to a godly lifestyle, but the vices, using innocuous aliases to inveigle their way into his confidence, cause him to backslide into covetous materialism. He becomes a miser and maltreats his tenants, servants, and hirelings: he raises rents, fails to pay the wages he owes, and seizes property adjacent to his own. After a bad dream about God’s punishment, he falls ill and dies. Satan carries him to hell on his back, advising the audience to live their lives in the same way. The virtues advise the contrary.
