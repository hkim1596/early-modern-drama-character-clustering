Full speech

TO is true great King, this woman, Nature's monster, By her base treachery betrayed my state, You have my goods, my sceptre and my person; But look, sir, who it is that gives them you, See from what hand you do receive these presents, The same put her brother in these fetters, It is my sister, or some Devil rather, That took that name upon her to betray me. How am I ravished with this sweet revenge, What pleasure 'tis to see thee in this case Where thy best hope is to fit down with nothing, Now Lady you may triumph, though you wear not These fetters, you're no less a slave then I. Friend, Which name I rather choose to call you by Then brother, (since you cannot be her husband With safety of your honour) could you think That she can spare her modesty, whose falsehood Spared not her brother, and her Country too, No sir, believe, she that is base her self Cannot love virtue in and other person: For how should she love that she never knew; Her then put out your flame, and think Spheranti That this conformity of our lives and fortune Which makes me share in all your passion May link our hearts so fast we shall not needs Such and alliance to confirm the knot. Which to confirm, no ceremonious oath The band of doubtful, and inconstant souls Shall I now take; thee friendship, I invoke, When I do leave this Prince, let me be left Without a friend, I and without a name. No more Dear friend of her; come, come forget her name. You are in love it seems. Is there a beauty can oppose your Courtship, What quality is higher then your own? Why should you then despair? Consider that your fortune was to yield His to overcome, yet did you long dispute it And bravely too; what can you fear Spheranti? To be too rash, let hope Remove those thoughts, and you will not displease her, A virtuous love was never odious. But tell me sir, what name has this your Mistress, Honour my freedom with this trust. Hope better sir, I am your caution She shall receive the offer of your vows. I'll wait him sir. Exeunt. Greatest Monarch, Who may dispose my life, you know that here, I am no less without a will then power I must obey, what ever you impose, But yet her crime joined with the insolence In facing it is such, that giving me My Kingdom back with her, is worse to me Then to live here your captive, with what pleasure Can I behold that power redelivered Which with my enemies I must receive. Such is her want of worth, the Prince you meant To Join her to, despises her, and truly I should believe him to be worth my pity, Of whom so base and object were considered. Sir, I beseech you think how great the distance Is between their conditions Done Sancho Has much worth in him, but he is no Prince, And though Seriffa has disgraced her birth, She's never the less descended from a King. Sir, I am yours, and since this marriage Is welcome to your thoughts: Done Sancho may In Celimant meet a brother, and Seriffa Being looked on by me, through you and him, Shall see I can forget what she has done. TO is the sum Of his desires, your Majesty will pardon the abruptness of my parting, he's my friend And such a happiness he longs to hear As much as I to tell him.
