Did he not write his name, Sim Valentine? Warrant I have met no Sin in Finsbury books; And yet I have writ them six or seven times over. You should do well to study Records, Fellow Ball, both Law and Poetry. There's another reading now: My Mr. reads it Son, and not Sin Valentine. And he will know't, and shew't too by his place Of being high Constable, if no where else. I can give you Sir, A Roman story of a petty-Constable, That had a Daughter, that was called Virginia, Like Mrs. Awdrey, and as young as she; And how her Father bore him in the business, against Justice Appius, a Decemvir in Room, And Justice of Assize. I can tell you A thousand, of great Pompei, Caesar, Trajan, All the high Constables there. Lord! what a world of business The Squire dispatches! Those were verses now, Your worship spoke, and run upon vive feet. Let them have leaves first. There's nothing green but Baize, and Rosemary. The Coloni. Sir, Colonus is and Inhabitant: A Clown original: as you'd zay a Farmer, a Tiller o'th' Earth, Ere since the Roman planted their Colony first, Which was in Midlesex. Dictator, and high Constable Were both the same. One Rasis was a great Arabic Doctor. Vaith, I cannot tell If mine were kyrsind, or no. But, zure he had A kyrsin name, that he left me, Diogenes. A mighty learned man, but pest'lence poor. Warrant, h'had no house, save and old Tub, to dwell in, (I vind that in records) and still he turned it In the winds teeth, as't blue on his backside, And there they would lie routing one at other, A week, sometime. That was avore Sir Peter Tub, or his Lady. Two words, Cyning and Staple, make a Constable: As wee'd say, A hold, or stay for the King. I, upon record! The Clock dropped twelve at Maribone. And's Governor, Mr. Basket. Hilts, do you know 'hun, a valiant wise vellow! Az tall a man on his hands, as goes on veet. Bless you Mass' Basket. And zome a thousand, when the Muster day comes. A mumming, or a show. With vizards, and fine clothes. Hee'll do't alone Sir, He will join with no man: Though he be a Joiner, in design he calls it. He must be sole Inventer: In-and-In. Draws with no other in's project, hee'll tell you, It cannot else be feazeable, or conduce: Those are his ruling words? Pleaze you to hear 'hun? He scorns the motion, trust to him alone. Are you revised o'that? A man may have wit, and yet put off his hat. All their colours. He has his whistle Of command: Seat of authority! And virge to interpret, tipped with silver, Sir You know not him. We will not back, And leave our Dame.

Plot summary

Plot — The Magnetic Lady, or Humors Reconciled
Probee and Damn-Play are sent on stage by the audience to bargain for a good play; what they get is The Magnetic Lady. They watch the play and comment on it between the acts. Visitors flock to Lady Loadstone’s house, some brought by her lodger, Compass, others to pay court to her sickly but wealthy niece, Placentia Steel, on her birthday. By the terms of her parents’ will, Placentia’s marriage portion of £16,000 is to be paid when she marries, but until then is held by her uncle, Interest. There are several suitors, including Practice the lawyer and Silkworm the courtier. Lady Loadstone initially prefers Practice, but the prospect of being made a countess through a friend at court converts her to Silkworm’s suit. Palate also promotes Silkworm, pointing out that Practice is already engaged to the servant Pleasance. Interest is trying to arrange a marriage between Placentia and Bias, who is a younger brother and will be content to accept less than the full dowry. Ironside, Compass’s brother, wounds Silkworm in a violent altercation at dinner, and Compass persuades the courtier to seek redress in a duel rather than at law; Bias hopes that Silkworm will be killed and the court rid of a nuisance, but in the event he merely talks to Ironside about valour. Placentia turns out to be pregnant, and the suitors back off; Practice asks Compass to help him marry Pleasance instead. Compass learns that Pleasance and Placentia were swapped for one another in their cradles, and uses the blank licence obtained by Practice to get himself married to ‘Pleasance’. His bride then disappears, and turns out to have been abducted by Polish, her supposed mother, who is trying to conceal the baby swap. The steward Needle pretends to suffer from walking and talking in his sleep, and persuades Interest that a ghost, in love with him, has hidden treasure in the garden. Interest goes looking for it and falls into a well. The false Placentia gives birth. Convinced that the pregnancy was only malicious gossip, Bias agrees to marry her, and Interest promises to pay him the dowry. Compass makes his peace with Practice with the gift of a court office and has Interest arrested. Polish confesses the exchange of infants, which leaves Interest obliged to pay the money twice over, to Compass in respect of the true Placentia and to Bias in respect of the false one.
