Full speech

On you and her I'll wait, until my dying day. My misery, sir Doncaster? why? I thank God, I never was in better state than now. What wrong have I? Is't wrong to be relieved? I am his vassal, and I will be still. Neither himself, nor any that he keeps, Ever upbraided me, since I came last. And what of that? Why should you hate him? why should you or you Envy this noble Lord, thus as you do? What shall I think? but think upon my needs, When men fed dogs, and me they would not feed: When I despaired through want, and sought to die, My piteous master, of his charity, Forgave my fault, relieved and saved me: This do I think upon, and you should think (If you had hope of souls salvation) First Prior, that he is of thy flesh and blood, That thou art uncle unto Robin Hood: That by extortion thou didst get his lands: God, and I know how it came to thy hands: Now thou pursu'dst him in his misery, And how heaven plagued thy hearts extremity: Think Doncaster, when, hired by this Prior, Thou camest to take my master with the Friar, And Wertenberg thy self taken, how he set thee free, Gave thee and hundred pound to comfort thee, And both bethink ye how but yesterday, Wounded and naked in the field you lay, How with his own hand he did raise your heads, Poured balm into your wounds, your bodies fed, Watched when ye slept, wept when he saw your woe, Even from my soul, I villainy defy. Oh forgive me, God, And save my master from their bloody hands.
