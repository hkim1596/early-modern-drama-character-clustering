Does fame of people nought Adverse thee fear? Whom fear does so compel To praise, the same his foes to be, does fear enforce agoyne: But who indeed the glory seckes of favour true obtain He rather would with hates of each be praised, then tongues of all Let first the king will honest things and none the fame dare nill. And where y shame is none, Nor care of right, faith, piety, nor holiness none stays, That kingdom swarves. The brother's hurt a mischief count though he be never so ill. Through pierced w to sword let him be slain & yield his hateful breath. Does piety in thee prevail no whit? What mischief new do'ste thou in rage pro-uide? What sword? what fire? What weapon then shall sorrow such find fit to work thy will? Then ire it self yet that's a greater ill. What thing seekest thou to bring to pass I note what greater thing my mind, and more then wont it was but what disceit may we for him prepare, Whereby betrapt he may be drawn, to fallen into the snare? He wots full well we are his foes. Who shall of peace the promise geeue? Whom will he trust? Yet other messengers to send, in such affairs procure, What thing against their uncle now, you them enstruckt to do, Perhaps with you to work the Like, they will not be a dread. Such mischief wrought hath oft return'de upon the workers head. And shall your sons of this Disceipt beware that work you will? no secretness there is In their so green and tender years: they will your trains disclose, And will ye them, by whom ye would he should beguiled be, Them selnes begull'de? I need not warned be, for these within my bosom deep, Both faith, and fear, but chiefly faith, does shet and closely keep. TO He noble house at length of high renown, The famous stock of ancient Inachus, Apeasd & laid the threats of brethren ̄ down But now what fury stirs & drives you thus Eke one to thirst the others blood again, Or get by guilt the golden Mace in hand? Ye little wot that so desire to reign, In what estate or place does kingdom stand. Not riches makes a king or high renown, Not garnished weed with purple Tyrian die, Not lofty looks, or head enclosed with cr wne, Not glyttring beams with gold and turrets High. A King he is that fear hath laid aside, And all affects that in the breast are bred: Whom impotent ambition does not guide, Nor fickle favour hath of people led. Nor all that west in metals mines hath found, Or channel clear of golden Tagus shows, Nor all the grain that threshed is on ground, That with the heat of libyk harvest glows. Nor whom the flash of lightning flame shall be ate, Nor eastern wind that smights upon the seas, Nor swelling surge with rage of wind replete, Or greedy Gulphe of Adrian displease. Whom not the prick of Soldiers sharpest spear, Or poyncted pike in hand hath made to rue, Nor whom the glimpse of sword might cause to fear, Or bright drawn blade of glyttring steel sub endue. Who in the seat of safety sets his feet, Beholds all haps how under him they lie, And gladly runs his fat all day to meet, Nor ought complains or grudgeth for to die. Though present were the Princes everyone, The scattered Dakes to chase that wonted be, That shining seas beset with precious stone, And read sea coasts do hold, like blood to see: Or they which else the Caspian mountains hie, From Sarmats strong with all their power withhold: Or he that on the flood of Danubye, In frost a foot to travail dare be bold: Or Seres in what ever place they lie, Renownde with fleece that there of silk does spring, They never might the truth here of deny, It is the mind that only makes a king. There is no need of sturdy steeds in war, No need with arms or arrows else to fight, That Parthus wonts with bow to fling from far, While from the field he falsely feigneth flight. Nor yet to siege no need it is to bring Great Guns in Carts to overthrown the wall, That from far of their battering Pellets sling. A king he is that fears nought at all. Eke man him self this king dome geeues at hand. Let who so list with mighty mace to reign, In tyckle top of court delight to stand Let me the sweet and quiet rest obtain. So set in place obscure and low degree, Of pleasant rest I shall the sweetness knoe. My life unknown to them that noble be, Shall in the step of secret silence go. Thus when my days at length are over passed, And time without all troublous tumult spent, And aged man I shall depart at last, In mean estate, to die full well content. But grievous is to him the death, that when So far abroad the bruit of him is blown, That known he is to much to other men: Departeth yet unto him self unknown. His travails now the time hath made to seem to him but small.
