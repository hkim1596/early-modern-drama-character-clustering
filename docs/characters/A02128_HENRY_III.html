Great men of Europe, Monarchs of the West, Ringed with the walls of old Oceanus, Whose lofty surges Like the battlements, That compassed high built Babel in with Towers, Welcome my Lords, welcome brave western Kings, To England's shore, whose promontory cleeues, Shows Albion is another little world, Welcome says English Henry to you all, Chiefly unto the lovely Eleonor, Who darde for Edwards sake cut through the seas, And venture as Agenors Damsel through the deep, To get the love of Henries wanton son. He shall my Lord, this motion likes me well, Weel progress straight to Oxford with our trains, And see what men our Academy brings. And wonder Ʋ andermast welcome to me, In Oxford shalt thou find a jolly Fryer, Called Fryer Bacon, England's only flower, Set him but Nonplus in his magic spells, And make him yield in Mathematic rules, And for thy glory I will bind thy brows, Not with a Poets Garland made of Baize, But with a Coronet of choicest gold, While then we fit to Oxford with our troops, Lets in and banquet in our English Court. Stand to him, Bungay, charm this Vandermast, And I will use thee as a Royal King. Now Monarchs, hath the Germane found his match? Bacon, thou hast honoured England with thy skill, And made fair Oxford famous by thine Art, I will be English Henry to thy self. But tell me, shall we dine with thee to day? Ned, where hast thou been? Let me that joy in these consorting greets, And glory in these honours done to Ned, Yield thanks for all these favours to my son, And rest a true Plantagenet to all. 'Tis, my Lord, Doctor Bacons poor Scholar. What, Madam, hath my son found grace or no? I, Raphe, how then? Whats that, Raphe? 'Tis true, my Lord, 'tis wondrous for to hear, Her beauty passing Marses Paramour: Her virgins right as rich as Ʋ estas was, Lacy and Ned have told me miracles. Lacy, go to the Quiry of my Stable, And take such Coursers as shall fit thy turn, High thee to Fressingfield, and bring home the Lass, And, for her fame flies through the English coast, If it may please the Lady Ellinor, One day shall match your Excellence and her. To horse, my Lord, the day is passing fair, Weel fly the Partridge, or go rouse the Dear. Follow, my Lords, you shall not want for sport. He posted down, not long since from the Court, To Suffolk side, to merry Fremingham, To sport himself amongst my fallow Dear, From thence by packets sent to Hampton house, We hear the Prince is ridden with his Lords, To Oxford in the Academy there, To hear dispute amongst the learned men: But we will send forth letters for my son, To will him come from Oxford to the Court. What say you Royal Lordlings to my Fryer? Hath he not done a point of cunning skill? By Henries honour and the Royal faith The English Monarch bears to his friend, I knew not of the Friars feeble fare, Nor am I pleased he entertains you thus. Gramercy, Lordings, old Plantagenet, That rules and sways the Albion Diadem, With tears discovers these conceived joys, And vows requital, if his men at arms, The wealth of England, or endue honours done To Ellinor, may quite his Favorites. But all this while what say you to the Dams, That shine Like to the crystal lamps of heaven? Seeing the marriage is solemnized, Let's march in triumph to the Royal feast. But why stands Fryer Bacon here so mute? This Prophesy is mystical, But glorious Commanders of Europa's love, That makes fair England Like that wealthy I'll, Circled with Gihen and first Euphrates, In Royallizing Henries Albion, With presence of your prin ely mightiness, Let's march, the tables all are spread, And viands such as England's wealth affords, Are ready set to furnish out the boards, You shall have welcome, mighty Potentates, It rests to furnish up this Royal Feast, Only your hearts be frolic: for the time Craves that we taste of nought but iouysance. Thus glories England over all the West. How now, Ʋ andermast, have you met with your match? Raphe tells all, you shall have a good Secretary of him.
