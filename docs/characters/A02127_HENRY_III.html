Great men of Europe, monarch's of the West, Ringed with the walls of old Oc nus, Whose lo tie surges Like the bat ments, That compassed high built Babel in with towers, Welcome my lords, welcome brave westeme kings, To England's shore, whose promontory cleeues, Shows Albion is another little world, Welcome says English Henry to you all, Chie lie unto the lovely Eleanour, Who darde or Edwards sake cut through the seas, And venture as Agenors worser msell through the deep, To get the love of Henries wanton son. He posted down, not long since from the court, To Suffolk side, to merry Fremingham, To sport himself amongst my fallow deer, From thence by packets sent to Hampton house, We hear the Prince is ridden with his lords, To Oxford, in the Academy there, To hear dispute amongst the learned men, But we will send forth letters for my son, To will him come from Oxford to the court. He shall my lord, this motion likes me well, Weel progress straight to Oxford with our trains, And see what men our Academi brings. And wonder Vandermast welcome to me In Oxford shalt thou find a jolly friar, Called Friar Ba on, England's only flower Set him but Nonplus in his magic spells, And make him yield in Mathematic rules, And for thy glory I will bind thy brows, Not with a poets garland made of Bays, But with a coronet of choicest gold, While then we fit to Oxford with our troops, Lets in and banquet in our English court. Stand to him Bungay, charm this Vandermast, And I will use thee as a royal king. Bacon, thou hast honoured England with thy skill, And made fair Oxford famous by thine art, I will be English Henry to thy self, But tell me shall we dine with thee to day. N worser, where hast thou been. Let me that joy in these consorting greets, And glory in these honours done to Ned, Yield thanks for all these favours to my son, And rest a true Plantagenet to all. 'Tis my lord, doctor Bacons poor scholar. By Henries honour and the royal faith The English monarcke bears to his friend: I knew not of the friars feeble fare, Nor am I pleased he entertains you thus. 'Tis true my lord, 'tis wondrous for to hear, Her beauty passing Marces parramour: Her virgins right as rich as Vesta's was, Lacy and Ned hath told me miracles. Lacy, go to the quirie of my stable, And take such coursers as shall fit thy turn, High thee to Frefingfield and bring home the lass, And for her fame flies through the English coast, If it may please the lady Ellinor, One day shall match your excellence and her, To horse my lord, the day is passing fair, Weel fly the partridge or go rouse the deer, Follow my lords, you shall not want for sport. Gramercy Lordlings, old Plantagenet, That rules and sways the Albion diadem, With tears discovers these conceived joys, And vows requital, if his men at arms, The wealth of England, or endue honours done To Ellinour, may quite his favorites. But all this while what say you to the Dams, That shine Like to the crystal lamps of heaven? Seeing the marriage is solemnised, Lets march in triumph to the royal feast, But why stands Friar Bacon here so mute? This Prophesy is mystical, But glorious commanders of Europa's love, That makes fair England Like that wealthy I'll, Circled with Gihen, and first Euphrates, In royallising Henries Albion, With presence of your princely mightiness, Lets march, the tables all are spread, And viands such as England's wealth affords, Are ready set to furnish out the boards, You shall have welcome mighty potentates, It rests to furnish up this royal feast, Only your hearts be frolic for the time, Craves that we taste of nought but iouysaunce, Thus glories England over all the west. How now Vandermast, have you met with your match. What madam hath my son found grace or no. Raphe tells all, you shall have a good secretary of him, But Lacy haste thee post to Fressingfield: For ere thou hast fitted all things for her state, The solemn marriage day will be at hand.
