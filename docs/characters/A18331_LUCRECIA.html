What old witch is this, that comes thus trailing her tail on the ground? Look how she sweeps the streets with her gown? Fie, what a dust she makes? Mother Celestina, you be welcome. What wind, I trow, drives you this way? I do not remember, that I have seen you in these parts this many a day. What accident hath brought you hither? Is this your only errand from home? Is it possible, you should come so far for this? I promise you, you make me much to marvel; For I am sure you were not wont to stir your stumps, but you knew wherefore; nor to go a foot forth of doors, unless it were for your profit. Did not I tell you so before? I wot well what I said; you never put in a penny, but you take out a pound: Be your pains never so little, you will be sure you will be well paid for it. But to that pass, my old mistress hath begun a web; she hath needs to buy it, and thou hast need to fell it. Come in, and stay here a while, you and I will not fallen out. With that old woman forsooth, with the scotch on her nose, who sometime dwelled hard by here in Tanners Row, close upon the Riverside. Madame! Why, this old woman is better known then the herb Rew. Do not you remember her that stood on the Pillory for a Witch? That sold young wenches by the great and by wh le sale? and that hath marred many thousands of marri ages, by sundering man and wife, and setting them at odds? Plot — The Spanish Bawd cw»
Looking for a lost hawk, Calisto meets Melibea in a garden and falls in love with her, but she rejects his attentions, thinking them presumptuous. His servant Sempronio tries to talk him out of love, and, having failed, agrees to help. He calls in the services of the bawd Celestina (partly to give him an excuse to visit Elicia, a prostitute, whom he himself loves), and she comes lo Calisto's house. There she meets another servant, Parmeno, who served her when he was a boy. She tells him that his dead mother entrusted her with the secret of where his inheritance is hidden, and promises him the love of Areusa, if he will leave his master and patch up his feud with Sempronio.Having paid for Celestina's assistance, Calisto becomes impatient and sends Sempronio to chivvy her. She casts a spell, creating an enchanted clew of yarn which will compel Melibea to love Calisto. She goes to Melibea's house and sells her the clew, but it does not have the desired effect: she tells Melibea about a sick man, and Melibea seems ready to take pity on him until she hears his name, whereupon she orders Celestina out of the house. Celestina insists that she was not procuring under cover of a metaphor: Calisto has a toothache and has sent her to fetch Melibea's magic girdle, which will cure it. Melibea agrees to send the girdle, but cannot write out the accompanying charm straight away; Celestina will have to return and collect it the following morning. Celestina reports to Calisto, asking for new clothes as her reward. Since it is now late at night, she also asks for a man to light her on her way home, and Parmeno is given the job. On the way home, she again tries to reconcile him with Sempronio, tells him she cannot give him his inheritance until he is older, and tells him stories about his mother. Learning of his love for Areusa, she offers to win her for him, and they stop off at Areusa's house. Feeling poorly, Areusa is going to bed, and is reluctant to see Parmeno, but Celestina leaves him there and goes home alone. He spends the night and much of the next morning with Areusa. Sex makes him better disposed towards Sempronio. Celestina is summoned to see Melibea, who is ill. In reality, as Celestina correctly diagnoses, she has fallen in love with Calisto. At her request, Celestina arranges a midnight assignation for them.
