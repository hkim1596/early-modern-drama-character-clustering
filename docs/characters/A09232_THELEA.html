Full speech

Oh fortune cruel, cruel & unkind, Unkind in that we cannot find our sister; Our sister hapless in his cruel chance: Soft who have we here. Father, here is and Alms penny for me, and if I speed in that I go for, I will give thee as good a Gown of grey as ever thou didst wear. I the fairest for white, and the pu rest for red, as the blood of the Dear, or the driven snow: Well if this do us any good, Welfare the white Bear of England's wood. ex. Oh Delya happy are we to see thee here. Oh spare fair Delia she deserves no death. Sister, where art thou? Delya come again He calls, that of thy absence does complain. Call out Calypha that she may hear, And cry aloud, for Delya is near. Brother remember you the white Bear of England's wood: Start not a side for every danger, Be not afeard of every stranger; Things that seem, are not the same. Which way is Delya then, or that, or this? Then brother draw thy sword & follow me. Peace brother peace, this vild enchanter Hath ravished Delya of his senses clean, And she forgets that she is Delya. Stay brother what hast thou descried?

Plot summary

Plot — The Old Wives Tale
PLOT Lost in the woods, three pages encounter Clunch the smith, and accept the hospitality of his cottage. One of them goes to bed with him while his wife, Madge, tells the others a tale. Delia, the daughter of the King of Thessaly, has been abducted by the sorcerer Sacrapant, who can only be killed by a dead man, and whose power depends on a light in a glass which can only be broken by a woman who is neither wife, widow, nor maid. Delia's two brothers come to England in search of her, and meet Erestus. They give him alms and he prophesies to them. He too is a victim of Sacrapant, who has transformed him into an old man by day and a bear by night, and driven his betrothed lady, Venelia, out of her mind. The brothers enter Sacrapant's domain, but are imprisoned by Furies and set to work digging for gold. Delia, forgetting her identity under the influence of a potion, is appointed as their overseer. Others are also searching for Delia. One is the wandering knight Eumenides, another the braggart Huanebango; the former gives generous alms to Erestus, but the latter refuses. Eumenides happens upon a fight between Wiggen and the parish officials, who are refusing to bury Wiggen's dead brother Jack; Eumenides resolves the dispute by giving all his remaining money to pay for the funeral. Huanebango attempts to force his way into Sacrapant's cell, and is ejected by the Furies; Sacrapant strikes him deaf and Corebus blind. Lampriscus has two daughters, one ugly in looks and the other in temperament. Erestus advises that they should fetch water from the well of life and there see their fortune. Zantippa, the ill-tempered one, maltreats the well's spirit, a head, but finds the unconscious, newly deaf Huanebango lying beside it: her future husband will be unable to hear her shrewish words. Celanta, the ill-faced one, becomes the blind Corebus' wife. She treats the head in the well better, and is rewarded with corn and gold. The despairing Eumenides meets Jack, and accepts him as a companion. At a tavern, the knight will not eat because he cannot pay, but finds his purse miraculously full of money. He vows to give Jack a half share in everything he gets on his travels. Jack renders Eumenides resistant to Sacrapant's magic by stopping his ears, then invisibly takes the enchanter's sword and garland. Sacrapant falls dead.
