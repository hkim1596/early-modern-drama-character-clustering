No sir. For Seigneur Stratio, and whence he is, 'tis ignorant to us; we are not correspondent for any, but our own places. All pains are lost in hunting out this Soldier; his fear (and ding wings to his heals) outgoes us as far as the fresh Hare the tired hounds. Who goes there? Ent. 2. soldier another way Oh, your success and mine touching this Sentinel, tells, I suppose, one tale; he's far enough I un dertake by this time. Why should the laws of Paphos, with more rigour, then other City laws pursue offenders? that not appeased with their lives forfeit, exact a justice of them after death? And if a Soul dier in his watch forsooth loose one of the dead bodies, he must die for't: It seems the State needed no soldiers when that was made a law. 'Tis true; he's at the Captains lodge under guard, and 'tis my charge in the morning to unclose the leaden coffin, and dis cover the body; The Captain will assay and old conclusion of ten approved; that at the murderers sighed the blood revives again, and boils a fresh; and every wound has a condemn ning voice to cry out guilty gainst the murderer. Tush man, in this topsy turuy world, friendship and bosom kindness, are but made covers for mischief, means to compass ill. Near-allied trust, is but a bridge for treason. The presumptions cry loud against him; his answers found disjointed; cross legd tripping up one another. He names a Town whither he brought Lysander murdered by Mountainers, thats false, some of the dwellers have been here, and all disclaim it. Beside, the wounds he bears in show, are such as shrews closely give their husbands, that never bleed, and find to be counterfeit. Faith (as the manner of such desperate offenders is till it come to the point) careless, & confident, laughing at all that seem to pity him. But leave it to th'euent. Night fellow Soldier, yowl not meet me in the morning at the Tomb, and lend me your hand to the unrigging of Lysander's hearse. Bring forth the Knight ath' Tomb; have we meet with you Sir? My truant was mich't Sir into a blind corner of the Tomb. For the Corpse Sir? bore misprision, there's no body, no thing. A mere blandation; a deceptio visus. Unless this soul dier for hunger have eat up Lysander's body.

Plot summary

Plot — The Widow's Tears
Renouncing Fortune and devoting himself instead to the goddess Confidence, Tharsalio intends to woo the widowed Countess Eudora. His wooing technique is grossly sexual, and he also quarrels with a rival, Rebus. Rebuffed and mocked by his family, he seeks out Arsace and bribes her to advise Eudora not to marry him, but when he returns to her and throws out Rebus, he is rejected a second time. Finally, however, she accepts him, and their wedding is marked by a masque. ===== PAGE 180 ===== Lysander is concerned about his wife Cynthia’s constancy, and plans to test it by faking his own death: with the help of Lycus he undertakes a journey and sends back a report that he has been murdered by mountain bandits. His body is interred in the family monument; Cynthia and her waiting-woman go to live in the tomb in order to be near him. Lysander returns incognito, disguised as a soldier assigned as the sentinel of the cemetery. He successfully seduces Cynthia, but she insists that their liaison be conducted privately to avoid compromising her reputation. Tharsalio sees them kissing inside the tomb and takes revenge on the ‘sentinel’ by stealing one of the crucified bodies he is supposed to be watching. When this is discovered, Cynthia suggests disinterring Lysander’s body and substituting it for the stolen one. Lysander goes to fetch a crowbar, removes his disguise, and meets Tharsalio, who deduces what is happening and informs Cynthia. She is thus able to pretend that she knew who the ‘sentinel’ was all along. Lycus is arrested for Lysander’s murder, and the tomb is opened to test whether his presence will make the corpse’s wounds bleed; but the coffin proves to be empty, and the ‘sentinel’ is found hiding in the tomb. Both men are sent for trial by the Governor, recently appointed with a hard-line law-and-order policy. Eudora intervenes to explain the truth, and Lycus is released; after Tharsalio replaces the stolen body, so is Lysander. Tharsalio allows him to believe that Cynthia was never tempted to infidelity.
