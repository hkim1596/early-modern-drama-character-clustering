How? dine to day with us. My Lord I am not used to entertainments, Nor is my house fit for so great a presence; To avoid a storm they might obey Necessity, and take it for some shelter, But in so calm a day. My duty is To meet that interpretation, though the new Come unexpected; now it will my Lord Become me to be thrifty of the minute , Their persons being so near; you will excuse If so short summons do expect my care To entertain they; my good Lord you have honoured me. Now Domitilla, is my Lord gone? I expected not These guests to day, they'le take us unprepared. I know not daughter, But I could wish rather to enjoy our selves, Not for the cost, those thoughts are still beneath me. For thy sake Domitilla. It was for thee I chosen this quiet life Upon thy father's death, and left the court; Thou art all my care, sole heir to all my Fortune's, Which I should see unwillingly bestowed. On some gay prodigal. By some hasty marriage. And am honoured. Whats all this Domitilla? I hope you are not dreaming of a Queen; Such wild interpretation f the Kings Favour to us cannot be made without The forfeits of wits and duties which Should teach us to contain our thoughts in their Own Sphere and not to point them upon objects Above our Level. 'Tis not my thought; Thou art young and fair and though I do not Suspect thy mind, thus far bred up to virtue, I would not have it tempted but reservde For a most noble choice, wherein should meet My care and thy obedience. This does please me. I must see How they prepare, things may want method else. You have Humbled your self too much to do us honour. 'Tis a blessing that My roof can boast so great a guest. Please your Majesty. This is a grace I ever must be proud of. It not worth such guest; But prayers and duty must supply. I am studying Domitilla why the King Should send for us to Court. But why To us this favour; to call us from those Cold and obscure shades of a retirement To plant us here near his own beams? It 'tis yet dark to me. Our duties. Leave that to her; it may concern. What's all this To thee? Leaving this Which is but mirth I know since we are fallen Into discourse of love, what would you answer To Lord Montalto if he came a wooing And recommended by the King? Is not his favourite worth your love, if he Descend to be your servant. My daughter is transported, sure you ar No Queen sweet Domitilla. Would we had still been strangers to the Court; Leave us; my daughter is much bound to your grace. I'll loose no time. If you Sir apply no cure, The found impression may I fear endanger Her sense and life; I urged Montalto Sir By your command, before his change of fortune , But she took no delight to hear him nam. You fright me; Without these not a thought within my heart But you have power to summon. How Sir? I know not How to reply to these particulars; But if your meaning be to have me speak Truth of her modest and pare thoughts, she is All that her mother can beseech of heaven To bless a child with of so chased a soul, And virtuous simplicity. Now you bless us; A widows prayers and tears for this great bounty.
