Peace Sir, I know the devil is at your tongues end, to furnish you with speeches? what are the particulars? you charge him with. They deliver a paper to Lucio, who reads. Peace, such hath ever been the clemency of my gracious master the Duke, in all his proceedings, that I had thought, and thought I had thought rightly; that malice would long ere this, have hid her self in her den, and have turned her own sting against her own heart: but I well now perceive; that so forward is the dispositi on of a depraved nature; that it does not only seek re venge, where it hath received injuries; but many times thirst after their destruction, where it hath met with be fits. Peace again, but many times thirst after distructi on, where it hath met with benefits; there I left: Such, & no better are the business, that we have now in hand. But surely me thinks, setting aside the touch of Conscience, and all other inward convulsions. Hold thy peace: thou canst not answer this speech: no Tratour can answer it: but because you can not answer this speech; I take it you have confessed the Treason. I thank his Lordship, carry him away speedily to the Duke. Away with him, I'll follow you, look you pyni on him, and take his money from hurt, least he swallow a shilling and kill himself. Plot summary

Plot — The Woman-Hater Ce»
Oriana is on her way to visit the Duke’s court when she is caught in a hail-storm, and takes refuge in the house of Gondarino. Gondarino is a confirmed misogynist, and makes her duly unwelcome. The Duke, on his way to visit Oriana at home, is also caught in the storm and he too shelters in Gondarino’s house. Gondarino suspects that he is unwittingly being made the host of an illicit assignation (and indeed the Duke is attracted to Oriana). Oriana takes umbrage at Gondarino’s attitude to women, and pretends to pursue him amorously in an effort to torment him out of it. He sends her to stay in a bawdy-house, and tells the Duke that she is unchaste. Her brother Valore disputes this, and Gondarino is suspected of slander. To defend himself, he shows her to the Duke in her new lodgings, but his story is discredited when it emerges that he sent her there himself. The Duke sends the courtier Arrigo to kill Oriana, but he offers to spare her in return for sex. She refuses, and thereby proves her chastity: the Duke was testing her, and she now accepts his proposal
