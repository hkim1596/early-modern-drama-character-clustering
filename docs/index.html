Character Clustering in Early Modern English Drama

An interactive map of dramatic-character archetypes built from the full speech of every named character in the early modern English repertoire. Each point is a character; color encodes cluster.

Cluster map

Archetype clustering

Chunked speech embeddings (1,024-token windows, mean-pooled), leave-one-out play centering, spherical k-means (k=25). Characters with fewer than 150 spoken words are left unclustered. UMAP 2-D projection for display only.

Per-cluster evidence

For each cluster, a dedicated page names the archetype, shows its most typical and most familiar members, then lists every character in chronological order — the earliest members are the candidate prototypes of that voice; later ones follow suit. Each row links to the character's full speech; typicality (cosine similarity to the cluster centre) shows how central a member is. Use these pages to verify the archetypal interpretation against the raw data.

How to read the map

Each point is a single character; color encodes its archetype cluster. Hover for play, author, date, genre, role, and the cluster's distinguishing words. Click a legend entry to hide that cluster, double-click to isolate it. The dropdown menus highlight a single decade, genre, author, play, play type, theater, or company — everything else dims. Characters speaking fewer than 150 words are not shown (too little text for a stable stylistic signature), and only one edition of each play is included.

About

Characters were extracted from EEBO-TCP; play metadata is merged from Wiggins & Richardson's British Drama: A Catalogue of Plays and from DEEP. Speech is typographically normalized, spelling-modernized (MorphAdorner), and proper nouns are masked so clusters reflect register rather than shared names. Each character's speech is embedded with Alibaba-NLP/gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct in ~1,024-token windows, mean-pooled per character; play-level signal is removed by leave-one-out play centering; the typology is spherical k-means (k=25). The visible 2-D map is a UMAP projection for display only — cluster membership comes from the full 1,536-dimensional space.
